# # Animations

Animations are often considered eye candy and unnecessary, when designed by professionals and in a discrete manner, animations play a vital part in enhancing the overall user experience of a product. In the real world, transitions don't happen instantaneously, with a few exceptions, such as the case of turning on a lamp). They have speed, acceleration and deceleration. Animations and transitions can also help direct the attention of the user, explain why things happen (for example, when you minimize a window to the taskbar, the short animation shows where the window disappeared), and generally make the virtual experience smoother and by bringing it closer to the real world.

In Silverlight, animations are technically more than a gradual change of properties over time. Silverlight (WPF) animations are time-based instead of frame-based. This means that if an animation is set to 2 seconds, it will take exactly 2 seconds, if the computer is very busy, and can only display a few frames while the animations are running. In addition to the fact that time-based animation is more predictable, it also allows the Silverlight run-time to continuously adapt to the computer's performance by skipping or interpolating animation frames.

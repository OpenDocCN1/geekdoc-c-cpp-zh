# Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality

# 1.1 Introduction

In recent years, virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) have become one of the most compelling technologies of the modern era, fundamentally changing the way we interact with digital content. VR creates a fully immersive digital environment that is completely isolated from the physical world, typically achieved by using head-mounted displays and motion tracking. Conversely, AR overlays digital information onto the real world, enhancing the user's perception of reality through devices such as smartphones, tablets, or smart glasses.

+   - Meaning: VR emphasizes full immersion and isolation from reality; AR emphasizes the blend of real and virtual.
+   - Device: VR commonly uses head-mounted displays; AR relies on transparent/see-through optical devices.
+   - Interaction: VR mainly uses controllers and spatial tracking; AR often uses gestures, voice, and eye tracking.
+   - Applications: VR is widely used in gaming, simulation, and training; AR is common in navigation, maintenance, and education.

# 1.2 Development History

The development of VR/AR has experienced several stages from early attempts to the present day. In the 1960s, the first head-mounted display system was created, laying the foundation for modern equipment. The 1980s and 1990s saw significant improvement in graphics and hardware, but it was the 2010s that truly became a turning point, with the rise of consumer-grade devices such as Oculus Rift and Microsoft HoloLens. Since then, the technology has rapidly evolved and integrated into various industries.

| Era | Development |
| --- | --- |
| 1960s | First HMD concept and prototype |
| 1980s | Military and expensive industrial systems |
| 1990s | Early consumer attempts with low interactivity |
| 2010s | Consumer-grade devices like Oculus Rift and HoloLens |
| 2020s | Ultra-lightweight full-color AR glasses and VR 2.0 |

# 3.9.10 Conservative Forces and Potential and Total Energies

The work done by a forced $	ilde{F}$ in an infinitesimal displacement $d\tilde{x}$ is generally a first-order difference in three variables:

```
$dW=\tilde{F}.d\tilde{x}=F_xdx+F_ydy+F_zdz. \quad (3.264)$
```

If the force is conservative, that is, if it has zero curl (3.265a), it means the gradient of a potential (3.265b), whose sum with the work is conserved (3.265c) and specifies the total energy (3.265d).

```
$$\nabla\\times\tilde{F}=0 \quad \tilde{F}=-\nabla\Phi, \quad dW=-\nabla\Phi.d\tilde{x}=-d\Phi, \quad W+\Phi=E. \quad (3.265a-d)$
```

A conservative force field (3.265a) is orthogonal (3.265b) to the equipotential surfaces; for a displacement on the equipotential surfaces the work is zero. If the field is not conservative (3.266a), for example friction force, then there is no conservation of the total energy, and an integrating factor exists (3.266c) if it is a Beltrami field (3.266b):

```
$$\nabla\\times\tilde{F}\neq0; \quad (\nabla\\times\tilde{F}): \quad \lambda.dW=\\lambda\tilde{F}.d\tilde{x}=-d\Phi. \quad (3.266a-c)$
```

in this case there is an integrating factor $\lambda$, that, multiplied by the force $\tilde{F}$, leads to the existence of equipotentials, and for a displacement on the equipotential work is zero (3.266c).

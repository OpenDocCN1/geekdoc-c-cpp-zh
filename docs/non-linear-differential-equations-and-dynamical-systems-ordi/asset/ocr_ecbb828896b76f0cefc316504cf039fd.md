NOTE 3.9: Necessary and Sufficient Condition of Integrability with any number of variables

With respect to a first-order differential with number of immediate integrability (3.278a, b), the necessary condition of immediate integrability (3.278c) [existence of an integrating factor (3.282)] we have already been proven. Next are proven [note 3.9 (3.10–3.11)] the sufficient condition for immediate integrability (existence of an integrating factor). The latter proof is by the reduction of the number of variables of a differential of first order (note 3.10), which may also be used as a method of integration (note 3.11). Next will be proven that the conditions of integrability that were shown to be necessary are also sufficient and apply to differentials of first order with any number of variables; the conditions of immediate integrability [3.278c] requiring the vanishing of the curl helic are both necessary and sufficient for the first-order differential in N variables to be an exact differential (3.276b) = (3.307a) [to have an integrating factor $\lambda$ in (3.281b), so that upon multiplication by the form becomes an exact differential (3.284b)] = (3.307b).

```
$$\sum_{n=1}^N X_n dx_n = \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial x_n} dx_n = d\Phi, \lambda^{-1}d\Phi.$ \quad (3.307a, b)$
```

With respect to immediate integrability (3.278c; 3.307a), the necessary condition is proved by the sequence (3.276a–d; 3.277a–c); for the sufficient condition, the starting point is the Stokes theorem (III.999a–d) for a surface (3.308a):

```
$$\sum_{D_2, n=1}^N(\partial dx_n /\partial x_m - \partial dx_m /\partial x_n)dS_{mn} = \sum_{D_1, n=1}^N X_n dx_n,$ \quad (3.308a)$
```

where $D_2$ is Figure 3.10 a regular surface with the area element specified by the bivector $dx_{mn}$ specified on the closed regular surface that its boundary is $D_1 = \partial D_2$ where the displacement is $dx_n$. Substituting (3.278c) in (3.308a), it follows that the first-order differential, integrated along any closed regular loop, is zero (3.308b):

The potential of an anharmonic oscillator with a cubic term (Figure 4.28a) leads to the equilibrium positions $ar{x}$ as function of the coefficient $b$ of the linear terms that (a) has three values when the coefficient $a$ of the cubic term increases. Choosing $a$ as a third coordinate orthogonal to the $(a,b)$ plane leads to a fold in the surface (b), like the hysteresis loops in Figures 4.10d, 4.24c, d, and 4.26. The projection on the plane (a,b) of the coefficients of the potential (c) leads to a cusp at the function of the stable and unstable branches of equilibrium positions as in Figure 4.27d.

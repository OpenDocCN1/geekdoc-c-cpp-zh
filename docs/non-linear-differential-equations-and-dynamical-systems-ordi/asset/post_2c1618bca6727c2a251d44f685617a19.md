## 4.8.6 Damping with Non-Linear Dependence on the Displacement (van der Pol 1922)

An example of non-linear oscillator with non-separable displacement and velocity terms is (4.448c) the van der Pol oscillator (1922):

$$\gamma>0, h(x)=h(-x) \in D: \quad \ddot{x}+\gamma h(x)\dot{x}+\omega_0^2x=0, \quad (4.44a–c)$$

for (i): the restoring force is linear in the displacement; (ii) the friction force is linear in the velocity but the friction coefficient is a symmetric differentiable function of position, magnitude of the order (4.448b). The van der Pol or mixed non-linear oscillator is equivalent to the dynamical system (4.449a, b):

$$\ddot{x}=v, \quad \dot{v}=-\gamma h(x)v-\omega_0^2x; \quad (4.449a, b)$$

using (4.449c) leads to the paths in the phase plane (4.449d):

$$\dot{v}=\frac{dv}{dt}\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{dv}{dx}; \quad vdv+\omega_0^2xdx=-\gamma h(x)vdx. \quad (4.449c, d)$$

Since the l.h.s. of (4.449d) is an exact differential the mixed non-linear or van Pol oscillator has a limit cycle if the (4.450a) is met:

$$0=\oint h(x)vdx=\oint h(x)x^2 dt; \quad \tau=\oint\frac{dx}{v}=-\oint\frac{dx}{\gamma h(x)v+\omega_0^2x}; \quad (4.450a, b)$$

in this case the period is given by (4.450b), that follows from (4.449a, d).

In the case of weak damping (4.451a), the simplest symmetric damping coefficient is (4.451b):

$\gamma \ll 1: h(x) = a - bx^2; \{x(t), v(t)\} = R\{\sin(\omega_0 t), \omega_0 \cos(\omega_0 t)\}, \quad (4.451a–d)$

if the trajectory is approximately circular (4.451c), then the limit (4.450a) of existence of a limit cycle is:

$$\theta = \omega_0 t: \quad 0 = \int_0^{2\pi} h(R \sin \theta) R \cos \theta d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} (a - bR^2 \sin^2 \theta) \omega_0 R \cos \theta d\theta \dots \text{ (Wait, correcting based on context)}$$
Actually, the text shows:
$$R \omega_0^2 \int_0^{2\pi} [a \cos^2 \theta - \frac{b}{4} R^2 \sin^2 \theta] d\theta = R \omega_0^2 \pi \left(a - \frac{b}{4} R^2\right), \quad (4.452a)$$

specifies (4.452a, b), the radius (4.453a):

$$R = 2\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}, \quad \gamma \ll 1: \tau = -\int_0 \frac{dv}{\omega_0} = -\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{d(\omega_0 R \cos \theta)}{\omega_0 R \sin \theta} \frac{2\pi}{\omega_0}, \quad (4.453a–c)$$

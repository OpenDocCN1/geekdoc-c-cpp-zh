## Non-Linear Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems

## 4.4.1 Linear/Non-Linear Time Dependent Systems

The simplest second-order system (sections 4.1–2) is linear with constant coefficients:

$$\ddot{x}+2\lambda\dot{x}+\omega_0^2x=f(t), (4.208a)$$

For a linear, time-dependent system the coefficients are the damping and frequency depend on:

$$\ddot{x}+2\lambda(t)\dot{x}+[\omega(t)]^2x=f(t), (4.208b)$$

an example is parametric resonance (section 4.3), for which the damping was constant but not the frequency. A non-linear system (subsections 4.7–4.8) and/or restoring (subsections 4.4–4.6) forces:

$$\ddot{x}+h(\dot{x})-j(x)=f(t), (4.208c)$$

This is a particular case of the general non-linear second-order differential equation solvable for the acceleration:

$$\ddot{x}=F(xxt), (4.209)$$

The non-linear oscillator (4.208c) will be considered in the sequence:
- (i) no forcing or damping (sections 4.4–4.5);
- (ii) forcing (section 4.6);
- (iii) damping (section 4.7);
- (iv) more general cases (sections 4.8–4.9).

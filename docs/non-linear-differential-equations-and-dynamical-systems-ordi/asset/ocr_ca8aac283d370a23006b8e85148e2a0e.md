# 4.4.15 Generation of Harmonics in a Non-Linear Oscillator

Writing (4.271) in the form:

```
$x(t) = a \left(1 - \frac{\beta a^2}{32}\right) \sin(\omega t) - \frac{\beta a^3}{32} \sin(3\omega t) = -x(-t), \quad (4.272a, b)$
```

shows that an undamped unforced anharmonic oscillator (4.215a) (with problem 113) quadratic potential (4.215b) has, relative to a harmonic oscillator (2.54a; 4.223c) to lowest order in the non-linear parameter (4.236a) in the case of the hand $eta > 0$ (soft $\beta < 0$) spring; (i) an increase (decrease) in frequency (4.265c) and decrease (increase) of the amplitude at the fundamental frequency, corresponding to the first term on the r.h.s. of (4.272a); (ii) corresponding to the second term on the r.h.s. of (4.272a) harmonic triple frequency is generated, with small amplitude, and of (in) phase with the fundamental. The cubic non-linearity in the restoring force (4.215a) leads to lowest-order in the non-linearity parameter (4.236a) to the generation of the second harmonic after the first harmonic, since the second term on the r.h.s. of (4.272a) has frequency 30 instead of 20; this implies that the displacement remains an odd function of (4.272b) implying a zero displacement at time zero. Thus a non-linear restoring force leads to the generation of harmonics of the natural frequency of the linear oscillator; this is confirmed by next (section 4.5) using the approximate method of perturbation expansions, applied to a more general non-linear restoring force containing a quadratic in the cubic term.

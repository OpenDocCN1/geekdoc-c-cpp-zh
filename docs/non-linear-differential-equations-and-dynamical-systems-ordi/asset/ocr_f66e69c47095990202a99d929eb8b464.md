a rotation of $3\pi/2$ along each of four lobes, corresponding to a total rotation $4\times3\pi/2=6\pi$ with index $I=3$. ```

# 4.9.10 Location and Identification of Singularities and Stability

The theorem of addition of indices with (4.492a-d) or with (4.504b) the point at infinity can be used (problem 156) to locate and identify singularities and points of equilibria by determining the index of several loops. As an example, suppose that there is a saddle point in the finite part of the plane (4.50a) and a focus at infinity (4.507b). If there is one more singularity in the finite part of the plane its index be (4.507c), then it could be a dipole (Figure 4.34b), but could not be a quadrupole, center, focus, tangent, or saddle:

```
$I_1=-1,\tilde{I}_1=1; \quad I_2=2-\tilde{I}_2=2; \quad I_3+I_2=2; \quad (4.50a-d)
```

if there were two several possibilities: (i) two centers, spiral, foci, tangents, or inflections $I_2=1=I_1$; (ii) a quadrupole $I_2=3$ and saddle $I_3=-1$. Returning to the case of two known singularities and one unknown, if $I_1+\tilde{I}_1\neq 1$ then $I_1\neq 1$ and the remaining point must be unstable or stable.

# 3.6 Linear Second-Order Differential Equation with Variable Coefficients

The Riccati equation is a non-linear-first-order differential equation (section 3.5) that can be transformed via a change of variable (subsection 3.6.1) to a linear second-order differential equation (section 3.6). The general integral of the latter is not known, and a wide variety of special functions (sections 9.4–9.9 and notes 9.1–9.47) are solutions of second-order linear differential equations with simple forms of the variable coefficients (subsection 3.6.2); this leaves scant of finding a general integral of the Riccati equation, although specific cases of solution are known, for example: (i) using known particular integrals (subsections 3.5.2–3.5.5); using the relation with linear second-order equations, the simplest being constant (homogeneous) coefficients [subsection 3.6.3 (3.6.4)]; and (ii) direct solution for less general sub-cases of the Riccati equation (subsection 3.6.5).

# 3.6.1 Transformation between Riccati and a Linear O.D.E.

The change of variable (3.95a) leads to (3.95b, c:

```
y = \frac{u'}{Ru}; \ y' = \frac{u''}{Ru} - \frac{u'R'}{Ru^2}, Ry^2 = \frac{u'^2}{Ru^2}, (3.95a–c)
```

where the non-linear term involving $u'^2$ is the same. Thus the non-linear terms cancel when substituting (3.95a–c) in the Riccati equation (3.58):

```
u'' - (P + \frac{R'}{R})u' + QRu = 0, (3.96)
```

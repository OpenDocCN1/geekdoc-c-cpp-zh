The total energy of a conservative system is (a) the sum of the kinetic and potential energies, and thus the velocity is zero (the motion is possible only) where the potential energy equals (is less than) the total energy (b), leading to three cases: (i) in a **potential well** like $E_3$ and $E_2$ the trajectory (b) is closed and is a **periodic cycle** respectively ($c$, $e$) and ($k, m$); (ii) outside a potential well like $E_1$ and $E_4$ the trajectories (b) are open, respectively $a$ and $s$; and (iii) the **separatrices** between (i) and (ii) such as $S_1$ and $S_2$ correspond to (a) maxima of the potential energy respectively $E_3$ and $E_u$, and are like (ii) open, that is, semi-infinite trajectories.

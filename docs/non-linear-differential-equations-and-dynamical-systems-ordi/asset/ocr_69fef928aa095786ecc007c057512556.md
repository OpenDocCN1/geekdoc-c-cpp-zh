# Non-Linear Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems

It has been shown that an harmonic oscillator (4.181) with natural frequency (4.87a) and damping $\lambda$ excited with amplitude 2h at a frequency close to the first harmonic:

```
$$\ddot{x} + 2\lambda\dot{x} + \omega_0^2x\left[ 1 + 2h\cos\left[2(\omega_0 + \epsilon)t\right] \right] = 0$, $\quad (4.181)$
```

in the case of weak damping (4.182a) responds (problem 99) with an oscillation cycle to the natural frequency (4.182b):

```
$$\lambda^2 <<\omega_0^2$: $\quad x(t)=\exp\left[-\lambda + \sqrt{\frac{\omega_0^2}{4} - \epsilon^2}\right]\right]$ $\quad (4.182, b)$$
$$C_1\cos[(\omega_0 + \epsilon)t\right] + C_2\sin[(\omega_0 + \epsilon)t\right],$$
```

with an amplitude that: (i) has a decay due to damping; and (ii) oscillates outside (growing exponentially inside) the range of frequencies (4.180b). The decay and growth are exponential functions of time, with fastest growth (4.183c) for (Figure 4.5b) excitation at the frequency of the first harmonic (4.183a, b):

```
$$\epsilon=0, \quad \omega_s=2\omega_0$: $\quad x(t)=\exp\left[\left[\frac{\omega_0}{2}-\lambda\right]t\right]\left\{C_1\cos(\omega_0t)\right]+C_2\sin(\omega_0t)\right\$, $\quad (4.183a-c)$
```

The oscillations occur only above (Figure 4.5c) a threshold amplitude (4.180c) ensures that parametric resonance dominates damping in (4.182c) leading to an amplitude growing exponentially with time. The existence of a threshold amplitude is due to: (i) the excitation through the support that supplies energy to the oscillator; (ii) the damping that dissipates some of the energy; thus (iii) a minimum amplitude of the vibration of the support is needed to overcome damping and excite parametric resonance. This is demonstrated next (subsection 4.317) by considering the energy balance. In the absence of damping there is no threshold amplitude because any energy input through the support leads to oscillation with growing amplitude and instability.

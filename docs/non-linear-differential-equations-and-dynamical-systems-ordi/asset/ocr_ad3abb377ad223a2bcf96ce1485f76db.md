**Figure 4.10** The resonance of a non-linear oscillator with the potential quantum function of the position leads to an amplitude $b$ that is a symmetric function of the frequency $\epsilon = \omega - \omega_0$, between the applied and natural frequency when the excitation amplitude is small (a). As the excitation amplitude increases (b) the curve becomes asymmetric with larger amplitude of oscillation at larger than natural frequency, until a vertical slope occurs at a threshold $f_0$. Beyond (d) the threshold amplitude: (i) there is one amplitude for each frequency before (a)—and (d) outside the range $(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2)$; (ii) inside this range of frequencies are three amplitudes-of which only the highest and lowest are stable. This leads to three situations: (i/ii) starting of the AD (a) at low (high frequency), and increasing (decreasing) the frequency there is a CE upward (beyond DB) **amplitude jump** at the higher $\epsilon$, (lower) $\epsilon$ in the hysteresis range; (iii) combining (i) and (ii) it is possible to stay within the hysteresis range $\varepsilon \le \epsilon \le \epsilon_2$ by described by the *hysteresis loop* with CE at DE and DB.

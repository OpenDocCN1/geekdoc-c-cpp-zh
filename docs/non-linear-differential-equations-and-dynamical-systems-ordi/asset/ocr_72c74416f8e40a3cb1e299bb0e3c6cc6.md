# 4.4.6 Closed (and Open) Trajectories for the Hard (Soft) Spring

The non-linear hard (soft) spring is compared next regards the potential (Figure 4.8a) [Figure 4.8b(d)] and the paths in the phase plane [Figure 4.8c]. The potential of the hard spring is larger than the linear spring (Figure 4.7b), always positive, has an absolute minimum at the origin, so that closed paths (Figure 4.7c) are possible: (i) no path is possible for negative energy $E_1<0$; (ii) zero energy $E_2=0$ corresponds to the position $d$ of stable equilibrium; and (iii) increasing energy $0<E_2<E_3$ leads to paths with larger amplitudes, $(b,f)$ and $(a,g)$. the soft spring changes the stable minimum at the origin from absolute to local (Figure 4.8d) and adds two symmetric absolute maxima that are unstable points: (i) $d$ and $h$ corresponds to separatrices S1 and S2 with closed (open) paths inside (outside); (ii) a larger positive energy $E_5>0$ leads to paths that pass through the origin with velocities $A$ and $B$ and do not stop anywhere; (iii) a smaller $E_3$ positive energy $E_3>0$ leads to a closed path $(c,g)$ inside the separatrices and two open paths $c$ and $i$ outside the separatrices; (iv) zero energy $E_4=0$ leads to stable equilibrium at the origin and two open paths (v) a negative energy $E_1<0$, no matter large in modulus, leads to two paths with stopping points $a$ and $k$ progressively further from the origin.

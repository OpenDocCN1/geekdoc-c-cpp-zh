Multiplying the inexact differential (3.185b) ≡ (3.186a) by the integrating factors (3.189b) [(3.190b]) leads to the exact differentials (3.191) [(3.92)]

```
$0=\exp\left(-\int P dx\right)(Py dx-dy\right)=-d\left[y\exp\left(-\int P dx\right)\right],\quad (3.191)$
```

```
$0=\frac{1}{y}(Py dx-dy)=Pdx-\frac{dy}{y}=d\left[\int Pdx-log y\right],\quad (3.192)$
```

From (3.191) [(3.192]) follow the general integrals (3.193a) [(3.193b)] that coincide with (3.188b) with suitable identification of the constants of integration:

```
$y\exp\left{-\int P(\\xi)d\xi\right\}=C,\quad\int P(\\xi)d\xi-log y=-log C;\quad (3.193a, b)$
```

the two integrating factors are independent because one depends (3.189b) on $x$ and the other (3.190b) on $y$ and their ratio:

```
$C=\frac{\lambda(x)}{\mu(y)}=y\exp\left{-\int P(\\xi)d\xi\right\},\quad (3.194)$
```

is the general integral (3.188b) ≡ (3.193a) ≡ (3.194) in agreement with (3.180b). The four methods of solution of the linear unforced first-order differential equation (3.19a) have been used to illustrate the properties of integrating factors. The standard (LII) linear faced first-order differential equation (3.27) can also be solved (EXAMPLE 10.7) using an integrating factor depending only on $x$. Between the simplest integrating factors depending ([subsection 3.8.7]) on one variable ($x$ or $y$) and the most general ([subsection 3.8.11]) depending arbitrarily on both variables ($x$ and $y$), are integrating factors: (i/ii) symmetrically on the two variables through [subsection 3.8.8(3.8)]) their sum (product); and (iii) on the ratio of variables ([subsection 3.8.10]) as for an homogeneous equation (3.7).

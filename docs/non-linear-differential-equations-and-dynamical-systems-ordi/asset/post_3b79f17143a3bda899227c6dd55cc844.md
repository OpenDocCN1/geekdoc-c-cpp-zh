If a second linearly independent particular integral $y_2$ of the Riccati differential equation is available (3.73a), one quadrature can be dispensed (standard LVI). Knowledge of two particular integrals (3.59a; 3.73a) specifies (3.59b) = (3.73b), a function $v_1$ satisfying (3.61) = (3.73c):

(3.73a-c)

## 3.5.4 Four-Point Cross-Ratio for the Equation

The preceding results (subsections 3.5.2–3.5.3) suggest that the more particular integrals of the Riccati differential equation are known, the less quadratures are needed to find the general integral (Table 3); it might be extrapolated if three particular integrals of the Riccati differential equation are known, then (standard LVII) the general integral can be found without quadratures. This is proved considering a third linearly independent particular integral (3.81a) of the Riccati equation, besides (3.59a; 3.73a):

$0=Q+Py_3+Ry_3^2-y_3';\quad y_3-y_1=\frac{1}{v_2}.\quad(3.81a, b)$

In addition to the function $v_1$ defined by (3.73b), the function $v_2$ defined by (3.81b) also satisfies a linear differential equation (3.61)=3.73c. Thus, as in (3.57a) the functions $(v, v_1, v_2)$ in (3.59b; 3.73b; 3.81b) have a constant three-point cross-ratio of (3.82):

$C=\frac{\frac{v_1-v_2}{v}}{\frac{y_1-y_2}{y_1-y}}\cdot\frac{\frac{y_2-y_3}{y_2-y}}{\frac{y_1-y_3}{y_1-y}}=\frac{(y_1-y_3)(y_3-y_1)}{(y_1-y_2)(y_2-y)}.\quad\quad(3.82)$

## 3.6 Linear Second-Order Differential Equation with Variable Coefficients

The Riccati equation is a non-linear-first-order differential equation (section 3.5) that can be transformed via a change of variable (subsection 3.6.1) to a linear second-order differential equation (section 3.6). The general integral of the latter is not known, and a wide variety of special functions (sections 9.4–9.9 and notes 9.1–9.47) are solutions of second-order linear differential equations with simple forms of the variable coefficients (subsection 3.6.2); this leaves scant of finding a general integral of the Riccati equation, although specific cases of solution are known, for example: (i) using known particular integrals (subsections 3.5.2–3.5.5); using the relation with linear second-order equations, the simplest being constant (homogeneous) coefficients [subsection 3.6.3 (3.6.4)]; and (ii) direct solution for less general sub-cases of the Riccati equation (subsection 3.6.5).

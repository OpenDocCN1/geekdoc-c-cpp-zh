# 4.8.13 Stall and Departure from Stable Flight

The straight, level, and steady flight of an aircraft (Figure 4.31a) requires that: (i) the weight W be balanced by the lift L generated mostly by the wing; (ii) associated with the lift there is drag D that must be balanced by the thrust T of the engine. This stable flight condition is maintained for angle-of-attack smaller than stall, when the flow around the aircraft is mostly laminar. If the angle of attack exceeds the stall value (Figure 4.31b): (i) there is flow separation in the upper surface of the wing, increasing the pressure so that there is a loss of lift; (ii) the transition from laminar to turbulent flow increases the drag, which may exceed the available thrust, causing the aircraft to slow down. The reduction in airspeed (ii) aggravates the (i) loss of lift, which is insufficient to balance the weight, so the aircraft falls down.

# 3.9.8 Representation of a Vector Field by Potentials

The first-order differential in three variables (3.219) in the most general case (3.247a), when it is inexact (3.245c) and has no integrating factor (3.245d), can be written (Notes 3.9–31) in the form (3.257a) = (3.314b):

```
$0 = \dot{X}.\dot{x} = d\Phi + \Theta d\Psi, \dot{x}: \quad \dot{X} = V\Phi + \Theta V\Psi, \quad (3.257a, b)
```

and (3.257b) that the vector of coefficients is the sum of the gradients of two Euler potentials $(\Phi, \Psi)$, with the second multiplied by a third potential $\Theta$. Another representation of a vector field (III.5.266a–c) = (3.258a) is the sum of an irrotational (solenoidal) part that is the gradient (curl) of a scalar (vector) potential:

```
$\dot{X} = V\Phi + V\wedge \tilde{A}; \quad V\dot{\tilde{A}} = V(\Phi) + \wedge (\Theta V\Psi) = \Theta V\Psi, \quad (3.258a, b)
```

from (3.257b) follows (3.258b) using (3.229a–c) = (3.231; 3.232; 3.233a–c). It follows the second term on the r.h.s. of (3.258a) has a representation (3.258b) = (3.258c):

```
$\dot{\tilde{A}} = V\Xi = V\wedge \Psi; \quad \dot{X} = V\Phi + V\wedge \Xi, \quad (3.258c,d)
```

substitution of (3.258c) in (3.258a) leads to (3.258d) representation of a vector field by three Clebsch potentials, as the sum of an irrotational (solenoidal) part that is the gradient of a potential (the outer product of gradients)

# 3.9.9 Choice of Scalar and Vector Potentials

It has shown that a continuously differentiable vector field in three dimensions (3.259a) has two particular and three general representations. The two particular representations are the gradient (3.259c) [curl (3.259e)] of a scalar (vector) potential and hold if [standard LXXVII (LXXVIII)] the vector field is irrotational (3.259b) [solenoidal (3.259e).

```
$\dot{X} \in c^1(|R^3|): \dot{X} = V\wedge \tilde{A} \Leftrightarrow \dot{X} = V\Phi, \dot{X} = V\Xi \Leftrightarrow \dot{X} = V\wedge \tilde{A}. \quad (3.259a–e)
```

The three general representations at (3.260a):

```
$\dot{X} \in c^1(|R^3|); \quad \Phi, \tilde{A} \in c^2(|R^3|) \Rightarrow \dot{X} = V\Phi + V\wedge \tilde{A}, \quad (3.260a–c)
```

```
$\Phi, \Psi, \Xi \in c^2(|R^3|) \Rightarrow \dot{X} = V\Phi + V\Xi\wedge \Psi, \quad (3.261a, b)
```

```
$\Phi, \Psi \in c^2(|R^3|), \Theta \in c^1(|R^3|) \Rightarrow \dot{X} = V\Phi + \Theta V\Psi, \quad (3.262a–c)
```

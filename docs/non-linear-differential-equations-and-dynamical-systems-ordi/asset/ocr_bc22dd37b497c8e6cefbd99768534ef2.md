The particular case of a mass suspended from a linear spring with oscillating support (subsection 4.2) leads to the Mathieu equation: (i) has four even or odd solutions (subsection 4.8) with period π or 2π specified by trigonometric series (subsection 4.7), ii the recurrence formula for the coefficients involves the eigenvalues (subsection 4.3.10) to the roots of a continued fraction (subsection 4.3.9); and (iii) follow the excitation frequencies that lead to instability (subsection 4.3.11) and the motion due to parametric resonance (subsection 4.3.10). The parametric resonance is considered using approximate solutions, for the first case of excitation frequency (subsection 4.3.12) to close the first harmonic that is twice the natural frequency (subsection 4.3.13); the second-order approximation (subsection 4.3.14) is first applied to excitation at a frequency to the second harmonic. The excitation at a frequency to the fundamental (subsection 4.3.15) is also a second-order effect. There is also parametric resonance in submultiples of the first harmonic (subsection 4.3.18), and damping introduces a threshold amplitude of excitation (subsection 4.3.16) that increases at higher-orders. The threshold amplitude of excitation of parametric resonance (subsection 4.3.17) in the presence of damping is that the energy supplied through vibration of the support overcomes dissipation. The case of a pendulum with vertically oscillating support is an example of parametric resonance (subsection 4.3.19). There are at least 11 differences (subsection 4.3.20) between parametric and (ordinary) resonance [section(s) 4.3 (2.6–7)].

The combination of (i) side force, (ii) imbalance between weight and lift, and (iii) moment mismatch of drag and thrust causes the aircraft to rotate around one or more axis (Figure 4.31d), the resulting chaotic motion (problem 147) is spin, and is one of the most dangerous flight conditions, with rapid altitude changes that can be fatal near the ground.

The safest approach is not to allow a spin, by preventing that the stall of attack ever be exceeded. Many aircraft have a natural stall warning, in the formation of vibration or buffet as the stall-of-attack is approached. For aircraft without natural stall warning, the certification authorities may require a shaker, as to horn, and sometimes allow the pilot of the approach to stall. If the pilot persists in increasing the angle of attack the stall stick pusher may apply opposing force and prevent the stall. The departure prevention system may be more sophisticated than just stall prevention and include other features to keep the aircraft within its safe flight envelope. Since airlines are flown generally for passenger comfort and reassurance, there is no need for large angles-of-stall or sideslip that lead to stall followed by spin. However, there may be emergencies, like (i) violent winds or storms, or (ii) sudden loss of thrust from engines, that can cause a stall and spin entry if not countered rapidly. In the case of fighters or aerobatic aircraft perform aggressive or violent maneuvers, the risk of stall and spin is higher (subsection 4.8.14).

# 4.8.14 Spin Prediction, Testing, and Recovery

A typical combat situation that can lead to stall and spin is described next: (i) a fighter aircraft performs a high-speed turn with small radius to escape a threat or to gain a firing position; (ii) the large centrifugal force must be countered by the horizontal component of the lift with a bank angle; (iii) the high lift will require a large angle-of-attack close to be achieved in an instantaneous turn; (iv) the high lift implies high drag, and to perform a sustained turn a high matching thrust is needed; (vi) the engine may flame-out in a turn due to inlet flow distortion or a rapid throttle movement; (vii) thus the conditions for a spin are created. The stall and spin protection of a fighter aircraft must allow for high maneuverability and survivability, and the risk of stall followed by spin can be excluded. The flight manual gives instructions on: (i) how to avoid and spin; (ii) the types of spin the particular aircraft can have; (iii) the control actions that can recover from each type of spin; (iv) the conditions in which no further attempt should be made to counter the spin, and ejection is advisable. In order to prepare the flight manual it is necessary to predict, test, and demonstrate spin recovery.

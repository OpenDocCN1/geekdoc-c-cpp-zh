# 4.9.1 Growth, Decay, and Mutual Interactions

The problem of competing populations can be formulated in several ways, one of which is (problem 148) the interaction of prey x (predators y): (i) assuming an unlimited supply of food, the population of prey x increases at a rate a if unaffected by the predators (4.467b); (ii) the population of predators y declines at a death rate c, unless they find food (4.46c):

a,b,c,d > 0:

```
x = ax - by, \quad y = cy + dx. (4.46a–c)
```

and (iii) the encounters of prey and predator animals are proportional to the product of the two populations, and decrease (increase) the number of the former (latter) at a rate b (d) in (4.467b) [4.46c]. All the coefficients in the non-linear second-order dynamical system (4.467b, c) are positive (4.467a). The coupled system of non-linear dynamical equations (4.467b, c) may be solved: (i) cancelling the non-linear terms, multiplying (4.467c) by d (b) and adding (4.468a):

```
xd + by = xad - ycb; \quad \frac{\dot{x}}{x} + a\frac{\dot{y}}{y} = -bcy + xad, (4.468a)
```

(ii) the linear terms on the r.h.s. are cancelled, multiplying (4.467b) [(4.467c] by c/x(a/y) and adding (4.468b); the difference of (4.468a, b) separates the variables (4.469a):

```
\dot{x}(d-\frac{c}{x}) + \dot{y}(b-\frac{a}{y}) = 0; \quad \frac{\dot{x}}{x} = \frac{-cy + dx}{ax - by}, \frac{\dot{y}}{y} = \frac{-b + a/y}{ax - by}. (4.469a, b)
```

(iv) the ratio of (4.467b, c) leads to the same result (4.469b) = (4.469a); and (v) the integration of (4.469a) = (4.469b) is immediate (4.470a):


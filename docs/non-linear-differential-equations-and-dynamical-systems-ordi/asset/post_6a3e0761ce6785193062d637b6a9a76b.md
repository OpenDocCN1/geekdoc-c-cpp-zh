The transformations (3.97a, b) hold in directions: (i) if y is a solution of the Riccati equation (3.58), then (3.97b) is a solution of the linear second-order differential equation (3.98a-c); (ii) if u is a solution of the linear differential equation (3.98a-c) is known, then (3.97a) specifies a solution of the Riccati equation (3.100):

### 3.6.3 Ricatti Equation Corresponding to Constant Coefficients

The simplest linear second-order differential equation has constant coefficients (section 1.3), and the roots (a, b) of the characteristic polynomial respond to:

$D=\frac{d}{dx}=0=\{P_{2}(D)\}u(x)=\{(D-a)(D-b)\}u(x)=u''-(a+b)u'+ab\,u.$

If the roots are distinct (3.102a), the particular integrals (3.102b, c) are linearly independent, and the general integral is (3.102d):

$a\neq b;\quad u_{1}(x)=e^{ax},$
$u_{2}(x)=e^{bx},$
$u(x)=C_{1}e^{ax}+C_{2}e^{bx};$

the differential equation (3.101b) of (3.98a) corresponds to (3.103a, b) to standard (LIX) the Riccati (3.100c):

${A,B}=\{-a-b,ab\};\quad y'=\frac{ab}{R}+(a+b-\frac{R'}{R})y+Ry^{2};$

whose solution (3.99c) involves (3.104b) one constant of integration of (3.104a):

$C=\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}-R(x)y(x)=\frac{C_{1}e^{ax}+C_{2}e^{bx}}{C_{1}e^{ax}+C_{2}e^{bx}}=1+\text{Exp}[(b-a)x];$

The preceding results do not apply if the characteristic polynomial has a double root:

$0=\{P_{2}(D)\}u(x)=\{(D-a)^{2}\}u(x)=u''-2au'+a^{2}u;$

in which the linearly independent particular integrals (3.106a, b) lead to the general integral (3.106c):

$u_{1}(x)=e^{ax},\quad u_{2}(x)=xe^{ax},\quad u(x)=e^{ax}(C_{1}+C_{2}x).$

### 3.6.5 Direct Solution of the Original Riccati Equation

The generalized Riccati equation (3.58) is an extension of the original Riccati equation (3.117d) corresponding to the coefficients (3.117a–c):

$R(x),Q(x),P(x)=\{b,\frac{\lambda}{x},ax^{\lambda}\}: \quad y'=ax^{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{x}y+by^{2}. \quad (3.117a–d)$

$y(x)=x^{\lambda}u(x): \quad x^{\lambda}u'=ax^{\lambda}+bu^{2}x^{\lambda}. \quad (3.118a, b)$

In the particular case (3.119a) the differential equation (3.118a) simplifies to (3.119b) that is separable:

$n=2\lambda: \quad x^{-\lambda}u'=a+bu^{2}, \quad \int\frac{du}{a+bu^{2}}-C=\int x^{\lambda}dx=\frac{x^{1+\lambda}}{1+\lambda}. \quad (3.119a–c)$

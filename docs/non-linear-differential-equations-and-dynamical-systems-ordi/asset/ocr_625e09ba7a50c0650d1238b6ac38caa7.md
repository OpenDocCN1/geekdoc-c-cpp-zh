# 4.4 Anharmonic Oscillator with Soft or Hard Springs

After systems with second-order (sections 4.1–2) and time dependent (section 4.3) coefficients, are considered non-linear systems (sections 4.4–9), all of second-order (subsection 4.4.1). Starting with a non-linear restoring force (subsections 4.4.2) in the absence of damping (subsection 4.4.3), two types of motion are possible (from namely a limit cycle oscillation (an open trajectory) around a center (from a saddle point) which is a point of stable (unstable) equilibrium. In the case of a non-linear (subsection 4.4.4) (soft) spring [subsections 4.4.5–4.4.7], that is with a (positive) cubic term in the restoring force, the center (also saddle points) exists in cases of soft or hard springs can be described: (a) exactly in a Jacobi elliptic function (subsection 4.4.9); (b) exactly as a series of circular sines or Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind (subsection 4.4.12); (c) approximately as an oscillation at the natural frequency harmonics (subsection 4.4.15). The three solutions (a–c) demonstrate the effects of non-linearity on: (i) the ratio of the maximum velocity to the displacement (subsection 4.4.8); (ii) the ratio of the frequency of oscillation of the anharmonic oscillator to the natural frequency of the oscillator (subsection 4.4.11); (iii) the lowest-order non-linear corrections to the frequency (subsection 4.4.13) and the displacement (subsection 4.4.14) of the fundamental mode; (iv) the generation of the lowest-order harmonic (subsection 4.4.15).

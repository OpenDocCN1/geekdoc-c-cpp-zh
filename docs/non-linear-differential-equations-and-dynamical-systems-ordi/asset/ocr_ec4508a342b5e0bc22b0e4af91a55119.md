# 3.1.2 Constant of Integration and Boundary Condition

The preceding geometric property may be re-stated in analytical terms: the general integral (3.2) of the first-order differential equation (3.1a, b), involves one arbitrary constant of integration C, and includes, for particular values of C, all particular integrals. A rigorous proof will be given in section 9.1, and here is provided a heuristic explanation; there may be singular integrals not included in the general integral (chapter 5). If the general integral (3.2) is differentiated with respect to x it is

```
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + y\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 0$, (3.3)$
```

where the partial derivatives may depend on the constant of integration C. Taking C as a parameter, and eliminating between (3.2) and (3.3), leads back to the differential equation of first order (3.1b). If the general integral (3.2) involved more than one constant of integration, say two C, C_v, then elimination of C with (3.3) with the remaining constant C_o would have appeared in (3.1b). If the general integral (3.2) involved no constant of integration, then elimination with (3.3) for (3.1b) would generally be impossible, unless the case of singular or special (sections 5.1-5.4) integrals. Excepting the special cases, the general integral (3.2) of a first-order (3.1a) differential equation (3.1b) involves one arbitrary constant of integration that can be determined from a boundary condition:

```
$$\f(x_0, y_0, C) = 0$, (3.4)$
```

# 3.1.3 Algebra, Analysis, and Differential Equations

A differential equation may be considered a third-level problem in the sense that it is considered solved when it reduces to a problem of analysis, like an integration. For, the simplest ordinary differential equation of first order (3.5b):

```
$f \in E(|\mathbb{R}|), \quad y' = f(x), \quad y = \int_{0}^{x} f(\xi)d\xi + C$, (3.5a–c)$
```

where f(x) is an integrable function (3.5a), has general integral (3.5c) obtained by a single integration or quadrature, involving one arbitrary constant of integration. Performing the integration, regardless of whether it is elementary or not, is a second-level problem of analysis.

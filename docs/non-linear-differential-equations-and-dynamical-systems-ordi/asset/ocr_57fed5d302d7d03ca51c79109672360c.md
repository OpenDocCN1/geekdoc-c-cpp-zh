# 4.3.19 Pendulum with Support Oscillating Vertically

The oscillations of a pendulum of length $L$ suspended in the gravity field are specified by (2.71b) = (4.196a):

```
$\ddot{x} = -(g-\dot{x})sin\theta, \quad x(t) = 2p\cos[2(\omega_0 + \varepsilon)t], \\quad (4.196a, b)$
```

where (Figure 4.6) the acceleration $\dot{x}$ of the support is subtracted from the acceleration of gravity $g_z$; the support oscillates (4.196b) with amplitude $2p$ at an excitation frequency (4.153a) close to the first harmonic of the natural frequency (2.722a) = (4.197a):

```
$\omega_0 = \sqrt{g/L}; \quad \dot{x}(t) = -8p\omega_0^2\cos[2(\omega_0 + \varepsilon)t]; \\quad (4.197a, b)$
```

from the displacement (4.196b) follows the acceleration (4.197b) of the support, that substituted in (4.196a) leads to (4.198b) by linearization for small amplitudes (4.198a):

```
$\theta^2, p^2, p\theta << 1; \quad \ddot{\theta} + \omega_0^2\theta = -\frac{8p}{L}\omega_0^2\theta\cos[2(\omega_0 + \varepsilon)t]; \\quad (4.198a, b)$
```

The latter (4.198b) = (4.149) is the equation of the harmonic oscillation with excitation amplitude (4.199a) and frequency (4.199b):

```
$h = \frac{4p}{L}; \quad \omega_x = 2(\omega_0 + \varepsilon) = 2\sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}+2\varepsilon, \\quad (4.199a, b)$
```

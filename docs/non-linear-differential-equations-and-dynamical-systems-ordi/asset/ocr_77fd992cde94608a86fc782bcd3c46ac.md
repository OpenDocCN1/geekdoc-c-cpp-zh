The parametric resonance (Figure 4.4) occurs (Figure 4.5) for excitation frequency close (i) to the first harmonic of the natural frequency $\omega_0 = 2\omega_0$, and also for submultiples $\omega_e = 2\omega_0/n, 2\omega_0/3$, with: (ii) reduced amplitude; (iii) narrower range of excitation; (iv) the threshold of resonance. In each range of excitation frequency the characteristic exponent $b$ of excitation with time $e^{bt}$ is maximum at the center of the range, which decays to the edges of the range (Figure 4.5a), and outside the range only oscillates with constant amplitude. In the presence of damping: (i) the ranges of excitation narrow and the peak of the characteristic exponent small (Figure 4.5b); (ii) there is a threshold amplitude of excitation (Figure 4.5c) that increases for higher-order multiples of the first harmonic, which is lower frequency so that for a given amplitude the parametric resonance is limited to the lowest orders.

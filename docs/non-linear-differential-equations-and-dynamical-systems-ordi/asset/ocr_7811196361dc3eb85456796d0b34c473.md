# Non-Linear Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems

The self-excited dynamo (Figure 4.16) in co(counter)rotation is a diverging (decaying) electric current, whereas a (Figure 4.17) self-excited electromechanical dynamo has a velocity tending to a finite non-zero (zero) value asymptotically for long time. The finite asymptotic solution or non-zero steady occurs in the presence of: (i) linear amplification that prevents decay for small amplitude; (ii) quadratic damping that prevents decay for large amplitude. The result is a steady state $v_{*}$ at an intermediate amplitude (Figure 4.17). In the case of linear and quadratic damping, decay to $v_{*}$ is inevitable. The asymptotic non-zero steady state $v_{*}$$ (Figure 4.17) of an electromechanical dynamo (Figure 4.16) may be analogous to magnetic field generation in planets and stars due to the combination of ionized fluids containing positive and negative electric charges. An example is the magnetic fields of the earth and their interaction (Figure 4.18) in the solar-terrestrial system.

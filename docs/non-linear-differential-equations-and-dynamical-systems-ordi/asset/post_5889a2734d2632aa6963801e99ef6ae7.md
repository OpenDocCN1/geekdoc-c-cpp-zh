## 4.4.8 Non-Linearity Parameter and Maximum Velocity and Displacement

The times of displacement (4.234a, b) [velocity (4.234c, d):

{x(t_1), \dot{x}(t_1))={0,b}; {x(t_2), \dot{x}(t_2)}={a,0},

can be used (4.234f) [(4.234g)] to calculate total energy that is conserved (4.218b) = (4.234e):

E=\frac{1}{2}mx^2+\frac{1}{2}kx^2+\frac{1}{4}k^2x^4=\frac{1}{2}mb^2=\frac{1}{2}ka^2(1+\frac{\beta}{2}a^2). (4.234e-)

For a closed or oscillatory motion the time of zero displacement (4.234a, b) [velocity (4.234c, d)] coincides with the time when the velocity (displacement) is maximum in motion (4.235a, b) [4.235c, d):

x(t_1)=0; \dot{x}(t_1)|\leq \dot{x}(t_1); \dot{x}(t_2)=0; |x(t_2)|\leq a=\dot{x}(t_2). (4.235a-d)

The parameter $\beta$ in (4.234a) has the dimensions of the square of length, and the **dimensionless non-linearity parameter** is defined by (4.236a):

\gamma=\frac{\beta}{2}a^2; b=\sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}=a\sqrt{m(1+\frac{\beta}{2}a^2)}=\omega_0\sqrt{1+\gamma}, (4.236a-d)

This ratio (4.236c) ≡ (4.236d) of the maximum velocity (4.235a, b) to the maximum displacement or amplitude (4.235c, d) increases (decreases) for the hard (soft) spring relative to the harmonic oscillator due to the effect of non-linearity.

## Unsteady, Non-Linear, and Chaotic Systems

The total energy coincides with the potential energy where the velocity is zero, for example for the maximum displacement or amplitude (4.237a) of a periodic motion, and thus the equation of the trajectory (4.212b) becomes (4.237c) for any potential function:

$E = \Phi(a); \quad x(0) = 0; \quad t = \int_{0}^{m} \frac{k}{2} \left[ \Phi(a) - \Phi(\xi) \right]^{-1/2} d\xi, \quad (4.237a-c)$

with time at the origin (4.237b). In the case of the biquadratic potential (4.215b) the equation of motion (4.222b) $\equiv$ (4.238):

$t = \int_{0}^{k} \frac{1}{\beta} \left[ (a^2 - \xi^2) + \frac{\beta}{a^2} (a^4 - \xi^4) \right]^{-1/2} d\xi, \quad (4.238)$

and can be put into a dimensionless form (4.239):

$\omega_0 t = \int_{0}^{a} a^2 - \xi^2 + \gamma \left( a^2 - \frac{\xi^4}{a^2} \right)^{-1/2} d\xi, \quad (4.239)$

involving only the natural frequency (4.221a) and non-linearity parameter (4.236a). Since the radical in the integrand in (4.239) is a biquadratic polynomial, the integral can be evaluated (subsection 4.4.9) as a Jacobian elliptic function (section I.399).

## 4.4.9 Exact Solution in Terms of the Elliptic Sine

The Jacobian elliptic sine of variable $\xi$ and modulus $q$ is defined by (I.39.114c) $\equiv$ (4.240a, b):

$z = sn(\xi;q); \quad \zeta = \arg sn(z;q) = \int_{0}^{\zeta} (1-y^2) (1-q^2)^{-1/2} dy. \quad (4.240a, b)$

Rewriting (4.239) in the form (4.241):

$\omega_0 t = \int_{0}^{a} a^2 (1+\gamma) - \gamma \frac{\xi^4}{a^2})^{-1/2} d\xi, \quad (4.241)$

suggests the change of variable (4.242a) leading to (4.242b):

$y = \frac{\xi}{a}; \quad \omega_0 t = \int_{0}^{a} |1+\gamma y - y^2 - \gamma y^4|^{-1/2} dy. \quad (4.242a, b)$

The integral (4.242b) can be re-written in the form:

$\omega_t\sqrt{1+\gamma} = \int_{0}^{x/a} \frac{y^2}{1+\gamma} \frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma} dy = \int_{0}^{x/a} (1-y^2) \left(1+\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma} y^2\right)^{-1/2} dy, \quad (4.243)$

that coincides with inverse elliptic function (4.240a, b) with (4.244a-c):

$z=\frac{x}{a} \quad \zeta = \omega_t\sqrt{1+\gamma}\sqrt{1+\frac{\beta a^2}{2}}, \quad q^2=-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}-\frac{\beta a^2}{2+\beta a^2}, \quad (4.244a-c)$

It has been shown that the *trajectory* of a *non-linear oscillator* (4.215a) with quadratic potential (4.215b) is specified (problem 109) by the elliptic sine function (4.245):

$x=asn(\omega_t\sqrt{1+\gamma}\sqrt{-\gamma/(1+\gamma)}), \quad (4.245)$

involving:
- (i) the amplitude (4.220b) that is a root of (4.218d);
- (ii) the natural frequency (4.221c)$\equiv$ (2.54a) of the harmonic oscillator;
- (iii) the non-linearity parameter (4.236a) which specifies the modulus (4.244c) and modifies the variable (4.244a).

In (4.245) the modulus from (4.44c) is imaginary (real) for the hard $\gamma>0$ (soft $\gamma<0$) spring. The exact oscillation frequency of the anharmonic oscillator is distinct from the natural frequency of the harmonic oscillator and depends on the amplitude (subsection 4.4.11). It is obtained from the expansion of the inverse elliptic sine as a series of sines (subsection 4.4.10). This leads to a series of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind (subsection 4.4.12) which specifies exactly the trajectory of the harmonic oscillator in inverse form with time as a function.

## 4.4.10 Inverse Elliptic Sine as a Series of Circular Sines

The changes of variable (4.246a, b) simplify the elliptic integral (4.240b; 4.244-c):

$\sin\phi=z=\frac{x}{a}; \quad \sin\psi=y=\frac{\xi}{a}; \quad \omega_t\sqrt{1+\gamma} = \int_{0}^{\sin\phi} \left|1-q^2\sin^2\psi\right|^{-1/2} d\psi, \quad (4.244a-c)$

$x=asn(\sin\phi;q)$.

The elliptic integral (4.246b) may be evaluated exactly in terms of elementary functions using two expansions:
- (i) first, the binomial series (1.25.37a-c) (4.247a, b) for the integral (4.246b).

$q^2 = \left| \frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma} \right| - \frac{\beta a^2}{2+\beta a^2} < 1; \quad \left| 1-q^2\sin^2\psi \right|^{-1/2} = 1+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_nq^{2n}\sin^{2n}\psi, \quad (4.247a, b)$

with coefficients (II.65.6a, b) = (4.248a, b):

$n \ge 1: \quad a_n = (-)^{\frac{-1/2}{n}} \frac{(-)^{n}}{n!} - \dots - (-)^{\frac{-1}{2}} \frac{n+1}{2}, \quad (4.248a, b) = \frac{2^{-n}}{n!} - \dots \frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!} = \frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!}$

with the first values (II.6.57b);
- (ii) the even powers of circular sine in (4.247b) can be expanded as a finite sum of cosines of multiple angles (II.5.7a):

$\sin^2\psi = 2^{-1}\left[ \frac{2n}{n} \right] + 2\sum_{m=0}^{n-1} (-1)^{n-m} \left[ \frac{2n}{m} \right] \cos[2(n-m)\psi], \quad (4.249)$

Substitution of (4.249) in (4.247b) leads to (4.250b):

$|1+\gamma; |1-q^2\sin^2\psi|^{-1/2} = 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{q}{2} \right)^n \frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!} \times \left[ \frac{(2n)!!}{n!} + 2\sum_{m=1}^{n-1} (-1)^{n-m} \left[ \frac{2n}{m} \right] \cos[2(n-m)\psi]\right], \quad (4.250a, b)$

is valid if the condition (4.247a; 4.244c) = (4.250a) is met. Substitution of (4.246b) allows immediate integration specifying the inverse elliptic sine with variable $\phi$ and modulus $q$ at (4.251) as series of sines of even multiples of:

$\sin\phi; q = \Lambda + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{q}{2} \right)^n \frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!} \sum_{m=1}^{n-1} (-1)^{n-m} \left[ \frac{2n}{m} \right] \sin[2(n-m)\phi]\frac{1}{n-m}, \quad (4.251)$

$A(q) = 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( \frac{q}{2} \right)^n \frac{(2n-1)!!}{(n)!} = 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{(2n)!!}{(n)!} \right]^2 \frac{q^{2n}}{2^{2n}}, \quad (4.252a, b)$

involving the anharmonic factor (4.252a) = (4.252b). In the passage from (4.252a) to (4.252b) was used:

$\frac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!} = \frac{(2n)!}{[(2n)!]^2} = \frac{[(2n-1)!!]^2}{(n!)^2 (2^n)^2} = \frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2 2^{2n}}. \quad (4.252c)$

It is shown now (subsection 4.4.11) that the anharmonic factor (4.252a) = (4.252b) relates the oscillation frequency of the anharmonic oscillator to the natural frequency of the harmonic oscillator.

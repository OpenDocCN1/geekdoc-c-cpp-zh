## 4.4.15 Generation of Harmonics in a Non-Linear Oscillator

Writing (4.271) in the form:

```
$x(t) = a \left(1 - \frac{\beta a^2}{32}\right) \sin(\omega t) - \frac{\beta a^3}{32} \sin(3\omega t) = -x(-t), \quad (4.272a, b)$
```

shows that an undamped unforced anharmonic oscillator (4.215a) (with problem 113) quadratic potential (4.215b) has, relative to a harmonic oscillator (2.54a; 4.223c) to lowest order in the non-linear parameter (4.236a) in the case of the hand $\beta > 0$ (soft $\beta < 0$) spring; (i) an increase (decrease) in frequency (4.265c) and decrease (increase) of the amplitude at the fundamental frequency, corresponding to the first term on the r.h.s. of (4.272a); (ii) corresponding to the second term on the r.h.s. of (4.272a) harmonic triple frequency is generated, with small amplitude, and of (in) phase with the fundamental. The cubic non-linearity in the restoring force (4.215a) leads to lowest-order in the non-linearity parameter (4.236a) to the generation of the second harmonic after the first harmonic, since the second term on the r.h.s. of (4.272a) has frequency 30 instead of 20; this implies that the displacement remains an odd function of (4.272b) implying a zero displacement at time zero. Thus a non-linear restoring force leads to the generation of harmonics of the natural frequency of the linear oscillator; this is confirmed by next (section 4.5) using the approximate method of perturbation expansions, applied to a more general non-linear restoring force containing a quadratic in the cubic term.

## 4.5 Perturbation Equations and Generation of Harmonics

It has been shown (section 4.4) that a non-linear restoring force: (i) changes the frequency of oscillation relative to the natural frequency of the linear system (subsection 4.4.13); and (ii) generates harmonics of the fundamental mode (subsection 4.4.15). The most direct way to obtain the frequency changes and amplitudes of the harmonics is to use a perturbation expansion (section 4.5). The perturbation expansion is an approximate method (section 4.5) alternating. It is applied to a more general restoring force including linear, quadratic, and cubic terms (subsection 4.5.1), to obtain the first (second) order non-linear corrections (subsection 4.5.2 and 4.5.3) to the harmonic oscillator, that specify in frequency and the generation of harmonics.

### 4.5.1 Restoring Force with Quadratic and Cubic Terms

The unforced, undamped, unharmonic oscillator (4.216b) is reconsidered with a restoring force containing linear, quadratic, and cubic terms (4.273) corresponding to the potential (4.274) with quadratic, cubic, and quartic terms:

```
$m\ddot{x}=-j(x)(1+\varepsilon ax+\varepsilon^2 \beta x^2)=-\frac{d\Phi}{dx},$ (4.273)
```

```
$$\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}kx^2(1+\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon ax+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^2 \beta x^2),$ (4.274)
```

where the small parameter $\varepsilon$ indicates that quadratic (cubic) term in the restoring force (4.273a); that is, the cubic (quadratic) term in the potential (4.273) is of first (second) order of smallness. The potential (4.274) satisfies (4.214b, c) and thus the origin is in position of stable equilibrium, and its neighborhood is a potential well corresponding to oscillatory motion. Introducing the natural frequency (4.221c) = (4.275a) of the harmonic oscillator (4.54c) leads to (4.275b).

```
$$\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \quad \ddot{x}+\omega_0^2x=-\omega_0^2(\varepsilon ax+\varepsilon^2 \beta x),$ (4.275a, b)
```

involving the quadratic (cubic) non-linearity parameters $\alpha, \beta.$ The displacement (4.276a) and frequency (4.276b) are specified by parametric expansions:

```
$x(t)=x_1(t)+\varepsilon x_2(t)+\varepsilon^2 x_3(t)+..., \ \omega=\omega_0+\varepsilon \omega_1+\varepsilon^2 \omega_2+....$ (4.276a, b)
```

## Unsteady, Non-Linear, and Chaotic Systems

A rigorous approach would require proof existence of parametric expansions and of convergence with $n$ terms as $n \rightarrow \infty$. In most problems only the first $O(\varepsilon)$ or second $O(\varepsilon^2)$ terms can be obtained and considered as an approximation for small $\varepsilon$. Substituting (4.276a, b) in (4.273):

```
$$\dot{x}_1 + \varepsilon\dot{x}_2 + \varepsilon^2\dot{x}_3 + (\omega - \omega_1 - \varepsilon^2\omega_1)^2 (x_1 + \varepsilon x_2 + \varepsilon^2 x_3) = -\varepsilon\alpha\omega_1(x_1 + \varepsilon x_2 + \varepsilon^2 x_3) - \varepsilon^2\beta\omega_1^2(x_1 + \varepsilon x_2 + \varepsilon^2 x_3),$ (4.277)
```

valid to $O(\varepsilon^2)$, in the example in the second term of (4.277):

```
$\varepsilon^3 \ll 1:quad \omega_0^2 = (\omega - \omega_1 - \varepsilon^2\omega_1)^2 = \omega_0^2 - 2\varepsilon\omega_1\omega + \varepsilon^2(\omega_1^2 - 2\omega_2).$ (4.278a, b)
```

Equating the coefficients of successive powers of $\varepsilon$ gives (4.279a; 4.280;

```
$\varepsilon^0:\quad \dot{x}_1 + \omega^2 x_1 = 0, \quad x_1(t) = a\sin(\omega t),$ (4.279a, b)
```

```
$\varepsilon^1:\quad \dot{x}_2 + \omega^2 x_2 = 2\omega\omega_1 x_1 - \varepsilon\alpha x_1^2,$
$\varepsilon^2:\quad \dot{x}_3 + \omega^2 x_3 = 2\omega\omega_1 x_2 + (2\omega - \omega_1)x_1 - \varepsilon\alpha x_1 x_2 - \varepsilon^2\beta x_1^3.$ (4.281)
```

The method of parametric expansions (4.276a, b) transforms a non-linear problem (4.273) into (problem 114) an infinite recursive sequence of linear problems (4.279a; 4.280; 4.281;...) with each order: (i) satisfying the same differential equation; (ii) forced by linear and non-linear combinations of the preceding orders. In the case of the anharmonic oscillator with restoring force (4.273a); (i) the lowest order (4.279a) is an oscillation (4.279b) for frequency $\omega$ to be determined; (ii) the order $n$ involves terms of lower order $(1, 2, ..., n-1)$ generating harmonics with frequencies up to $n\omega$. This method of parametric expansions is demonstrated with the first (second) order approximation [subsections (4.5.2–4.5.3–4.5.4)].

### 4.5.2 First-Order Perturbation and Secular Condition

```
$$\dot{x}_2 + \omega^2 x_2 = 2\omega\omega_1 a\sin(\omega t) - \frac{\alpha}{2}\omega_0^2 a^2 [1 - \cos(2\omega t)].$ (4.282)
```

## Non-Linear Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems

The first term on the r.h.s. of (4.282) is a forcing at the frequency $\omega$, and would lead to an undamped resonance (subsection 2.7.3) with amplitude growing linearly with time (2.17b); this would imply that the energy of the oscillator would grow quadratically with time (2.17c), which is not possible because there is no external force acting on the system and the total energy is conserved (4.210c). Thus this term must be excluded, imposing the **secular condition** that its amplitude is zero; there is no frequency change to first order (4.283a), and (4.282) simplifies to (4.282):

```
$\omega_1=0;\quad \dot{x}_2 + \omega^2 x_2 = -\frac{\alpha}{2}\omega_0^2 a^2[1 - \cos(2\omega t)].\quad(4.282)$
```

The solution of (4.282) consists of a constant plus oscillation of frequency $2\omega$ (4.84):

```
$x_2(t) = x_{20} - \frac{\alpha\omega_0^2 a^2}{4\omega}\sin(2\omega t).\quad(4.84)$
```

Bearing in mind (4.283a) that the frequency is unchanged (4.84a) from the natural frequency of a harmonic oscillator; the cubic term does not contribute to the potential well, so the frequency is unchanged (4.84a) to lowest order in the non-linearity (4.84b).

This technique of centering a view in the space between two objects is one I use frequently in my Android work, and it's a good way to eat up extra space caused by small and large fluctuations in screen size. That is to say, the text in the center of the buttons will float, centered, within any available screen space you might get when using a larger screen than the one you're designing.

# ADDING THAT GRAY BACKGROUND

So, you might be asking, the LinearLayout example has a gray background; if the RelativeLayout is so amazing, why doesn't it have one as well? First, stop asking your book questions; you'll look a little odd in public. Second, I withheld the background because I wanted to show you how easy it is to add it. I've placed the XML required to add the background in the following code. Take a second to look it over:

```
```xml
<!-- This is the top level layout -->
<RelativeLayout
    xmlns:android="http://schemas.android.com/apk/res/android"
    android:layout_width="match_parent"
    android:layout_height="match_parent">
    <View
        android:layout_width="match_parent"
        android:layout_height="wrap_content"
        android:layout_alignParentLeft="true"
        android:layout_alignParentRight="true"
        android:layout_alignParentTop="true"
        android:layout_alignBottom="@id/next"
        android:background="#ff222222"
        />
    <!--Rest of the screen goes here -->
</RelativeLayout>

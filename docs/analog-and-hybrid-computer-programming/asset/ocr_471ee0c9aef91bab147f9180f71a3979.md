r_i. The current flowing through the center conductor heats it to a constant temperature T0. The outside of the insulated cable is held at a fixed temperature 0. The aim of this simulation is to determine the heat distribution within the insulating material with respect to time t and radius r.

The general homogenous form of the heat equation has the form

∂T/∂t − a∇²T = 0   (6.52)

where a is a constant term describing the thermal diffusivity of the material. Generally, it is

α = k / (ρ c_p)

where k is the thermal conductivity of the material, ρ is its density, and c_p is the specific heat capacity.

The temperature T within the material is a function of time t and a location vector x, i.e. T(x,t). The arguments of the function T are typically omitted as always to avoid unnecessary clutter in the equations.

∇² is the LAPLACE operator which is often also denoted by Δ. It is

∇²T = ΔT = div(grad(T)).   (6.53)

Due to the inherent rotational symmetry of the two-dimensional cross section of the cable shown in figure 6.69, a one-dimensional flat slice running from the center to the circumference is sufficient to describe the overall problem. Due to (6.53) the LAPLACE operator can be simplified to

∇²T = T''.

Since the cable in question contains a heat source, the general heat equation (6.52) has to be adapted as follows by adding another derivative term:

∂T/∂t = a ( ∂²T/∂r² + (1/r) ∂T/∂r )   (6.54)

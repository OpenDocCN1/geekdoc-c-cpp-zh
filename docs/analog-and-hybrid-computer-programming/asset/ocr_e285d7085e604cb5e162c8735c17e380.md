Fig. 6.71. The heat conducting plate

In the following case a 8 × 8 cm² plate, perfectly insulated on its top and bottom surfaces, is considered. The plate's edges are held at a fixed temperature T. Quadratic square plates like this exhibits symmetry not only along its x- and y-axes but also with respect to the 45 degree diagonals as shown in figure 6.71. Accordingly, it is sufficient to take only one octant of the plate into account in the simulation, thus saving a considerable number of computing elements. Nevertheless, this comes at the cost that additional boundary conditions are required.

If only one octant instead of a quadrant of the plate as shown in figure 6.71 is implemented as a grid of computing nodes, the nodes along the diagonal and those along the x-axis require some attention as they have to be connected to their “mirror” neighbor nodes which replace the missing neighbor nodes in the adjacent octants. The nodes along the vertical right hand side of the octant are held at the fixed temperature T. The node in the center of the plate is denoted by u0,0 and is the only node that allows heat to be injected or extracted.

According to equation (6.61), every integrator in the grid yielding ui,j requires five inputs: The outputs of its direct neighbors ui−1,j, ui+1,j, ui,j−1, and ui,j+1 as well as its own inverted output yielding the −4ui,j term. Node u0,0 requires a sixth input for q0,0.

The various paralleled inputs of the elements along the diagonal should be clustered into single inputs with weight 10 and a suitably set coefficient potentiometer in the actual computer setup.

In this section a simple universe consisting of two suns with fixed positions $\vec{r}_1 = (x_1, y_1)$ and $\vec{r}_2 = (x_2, y_2)$, masses $m_1 = m_2 = 1$, and a satellite of negligible mass criss-crossing between these two celestial bodies will be simulated. This system can be readily described by the following set of coupled differential equations:

$m_1 \ddot{\vec{r}}_1 = G m_1 m_2 \frac{\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1}{|\vec{r}_2^2 - \vec{r}_1^2|^3} + G m_1 m_3 \frac{\vec{r}_3 - \vec{r}_1}{|\vec{r}_3^2 - \vec{r}_1^2|^3}$

$m_2 \ddot{\vec{r}}_2 = G m_2 m_3 \frac{\vec{r}_3 - \vec{r}_2}{|\vec{r}_3^2 - \vec{r}_2^2|^3} + G m_2 m_1 \frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2}{|\vec{r}_1^2 - \vec{r}_2^2|^3}$

$m_3 \ddot{\vec{r}}_3 = G m_3 m_1 \frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_3}{|\vec{r}_1^2 - \vec{r}_3^2|^3} + G m_3 m_2 \frac{\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_3}{|\vec{r}_2^2 - \vec{r}_3^2|^3} \quad (6.27)$

The vectors $\vec{r}_i$, $1 \le i \le 3$ describe the positions of the different bodies with masses $m_i$ while $G$ is the gravitational constant.

Assuming that the two suns are stationary, only equation (6.27) has to be implemented on the analog computer. Since the suns' masses are equal to 1 the satellite's mass cancels out, yielding

$\ddot{\vec{r}}_3 = G \frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_3}{|\vec{r}_1^2 - \vec{r}_3^2|^3} + G \frac{\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_3}{|\vec{r}_2^2 - \vec{r}_3^2|^3}. \quad (6.28)$

Introducing two distance terms

$\Delta r_{13}^3 = \left(\sqrt{(x_1 - x_3)^2 + y_3^2}\right)^3 \quad \text{and} \quad (6.29)$

$\Delta r_{23}^3 = \left(\sqrt{(x_2 - x_3)^2 + y_3^2}\right)^3 \quad (6.30)$

and splitting (6.28) into its $x$- and $y$-components yields the following two equations which describe the satellite's trajectory in Cartesian coordinates:

$\ddot{x}_3 = (x_1 - x_3) \frac{G}{\Delta r_{13}^3} + (x_2 - x_3) \frac{G}{\Delta r_{23}^3} \quad \text{and} \quad (6.31)$

$\ddot{y}_3 = -y_3 \left( \frac{G}{\Delta r_{13}^3} + \frac{G}{\Delta r_{23}^3} \right). \quad (6.32)$

These equations can now easily be implemented on an analog computer. Figure 6.30 shows the partial computer setup yielding the distance terms (6.29) and (6.30).

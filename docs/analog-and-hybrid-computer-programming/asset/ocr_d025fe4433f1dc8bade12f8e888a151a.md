which can be readily solved by applying the quadratic formula

μ_{1,2} = -\frac{p}{2} \pm \sqrt{\frac{p^2}{4} - q}

with p = 2β and q = ω₀² yielding the solutions

μ_{1,2} = -β \pm \sqrt{β² - ω₀²} \tag{4.11}

With the definition of

ω² = ω₀² - β²

(4.11) can be rearranged to

μ_{1,2} = -β \pm i ω.

The solution of (4.9) is thus given by the linear combination

y = a e^{μ₁ t} + b e^{μ₂ t} = a e^{-(β + i ω)t} + b e^{-(β - i ω)t} = a e^{-β t} e^{i ω t} + b e^{-β t} e^{-i ω t} = e^{-β t} (a e^{i ω t} + b e^{-i ω t}) \tag{4.12}

As known from complex analysis⁴⁵

e^{i ω t} = cos(ω t) + i sin(ω t) and e^{-i ω t} = cos(ω t) - i sin(ω t).

Applying this to (4.12) yields

y = e^{-β t} ( a (cos(ω t) + i sin(ω t)) + b (cos(ω t) - i sin(ω t)) ) = e^{-β t} ( (a + b) cos(ω t) + i (a - b) sin(ω t) ) \tag{4.13}

The e^{-β t} term is the damping term of this oscillating system while a + b and a - b are determined by the initial conditions. If it is assumed that the mass has been deflected by an angle α₀ at t = 0, this will obviously be the maximum amplitude of its movement. If the mass is then just released at t = 0 without any initial velocity given to it, the following initial conditions hold:

α(0) = α₀ and α̇(0) = 0.

# Suggested Informational Title

*The Animal in Me Is Very Plain to See* by Laurie C. Tye (WestWinds Press, 2005). Nice photographs and a spare format in which emotions are described and types of animals are pictured. The visual nature of this book might help some children put names to certain feelings.

# Math and Science Activity

Make an emotion chart with different emotions listed across the top. Going emotion by emotion, ask the children how many have felt that emotion in the last couple of days. Count which emotions seem most common. Talk together about what the emotions look like, focusing on specific facial features. For example, when someone is happy, her mouth smiles, her eyebrows go up, her eyes sparkle. When someone is sad, his mouth droops down, his eyebrows scrunch together, and his eyes tear up.

# Additional Fiction Titles

*Anger*

*Sometimes I’m Bombaloo* by Rachel Vail (Scholastic, 2002). Excellent look at the enormity of anger and how it can sometimes feel scary.

*Where the Wild Things Are* by Maurice Sendak (Harper and Row, 1963). Every child deserves to hear about the wild things. Let the rumpus begin!

*Fear*

*The Gruffalo* by Julia Donaldson (Dial Books for Young Readers, 1999). Children delight at the repeating refrain and the different animal snacks the mouse mentions to scare away predators. But the book could be a wonderful discussion starter about unfounded fears.

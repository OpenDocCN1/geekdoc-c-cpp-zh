Using \(f_x(x) = \exp(-x^2/2)/\sqrt{2\pi} = f_x(-x)\), we get

\(f_y(y) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi y}} \exp(-y/2) .\)

This is the *chi-square(d) distribution*, also denoted as \(\chi^2\)-*distribution*, with one degree of freedom. More generally, the probability density function of the \(\chi^2\)-distribution with \(k\) degrees of freedom is given by

\(\chi_k^2(x) = \frac{1}{2^{k/2}\Gamma(k/2)} x^{k/2-1} \exp(-x/2) .\)

The mean lies at \(k\) and the variance is \(2k\). The previous result above for \(k=1\) follows from \(\Gamma(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}\). Figures 2.25 and 2.26 give examples of the probability density function and the cumulative distribution function for various degrees of freedom.

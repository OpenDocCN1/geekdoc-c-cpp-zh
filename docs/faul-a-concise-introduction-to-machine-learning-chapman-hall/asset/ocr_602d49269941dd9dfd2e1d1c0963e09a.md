$$ p_k(\mathbf{v}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|2\pi \Sigma_k|}} \exp \left( - \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{v} - \mu_k)^T \Sigma_k^{-1} (\mathbf{v} - \mu_k) \right), \quad (6.6) $$

where $|\cdot|$ denotes the matrix determinant. The derivative of $p_k(\mathbf{v})$ with respect to $\mu_k$ is given by

$$ \frac{\partial}{\partial \mu_k} p_k(\mathbf{v}) = p_k(\mathbf{v}) \Sigma_k^{-1} (\mathbf{v} - \mu_k), $$

where we used the chain rule, Appendix A.2.3 and the symmetry of $\Sigma_k$. Differentiating (6.3) with respect to $\mu_k$ therefore results in

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial \mu_k} \mathcal{L} &= \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{1}{p(\mathbf{v}_n)} \pi_k \frac{\partial}{\partial \mu_k} p_k(\mathbf{v}) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{1}{p(\mathbf{v}_n)} \pi_k p_k(\mathbf{v}) \Sigma_k^{-1} (\mathbf{v}_n - \mu_k) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^N p(z_{n,k}=1|\mathbf{v}_n) \Sigma_k^{-1} (\mathbf{v}_n - \mu_k). \end{aligned} $$

To approximate the extremum, we set this to zero and multiply through by $\Sigma_k$ which is non-singular, since it is a covariance matrix:

$$ \sum_{n=1}^N p(z_{n,k}=1|\mathbf{v}_n) (\mathbf{v}_n - \mu_k) = 0. $$

We define

$$ N_k = \sum_{n=1}^N p(z_{n,k}=1|\mathbf{v}_n), $$

which is comparable to the expected number of samples in cluster $k$. With this definition

$$ \mu_k = \frac{1}{N_k} \sum_{n=1}^N p(z_{n,k}=1|\mathbf{v}_n) \mathbf{v}_n. \quad (6.7) $$

We see that $\mu_k$ is a weighted average of all samples in the data set where the weights are the responsibilities that the sample was generated by process $k$. It is reminiscent of formula (4.1) for the sample mean.

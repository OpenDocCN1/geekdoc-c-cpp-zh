The maximum of (6.9) is estimated by alternating between maximizing the lower bound with respect to \(q(\mathbf{z}_n)\) and with respect to \(\pi_k = p(z_k = 1)\) and the parameters of \(p_k(\mathbf{v}) = p(\mathbf{v}|z_k = 1)\), \(k = 1, \dots, K\). The former means setting \(q(\mathbf{z}_n)\) to \(p(z_n|\mathbf{v}_n)\), where the responsibilities are evaluated using the current parameters of \(p_k(\mathbf{v})\). With this choice for \(q(\mathbf{z}_n)\), however, the first term of the lower bound becomes exactly \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\). The second term is independent of the parameters of \(p_k(\mathbf{v})\) and hence is irrelevant for the maximization. The location of the maximum is the same whether maximizing \(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}\) or \(\bar{\mathcal{L}}\).

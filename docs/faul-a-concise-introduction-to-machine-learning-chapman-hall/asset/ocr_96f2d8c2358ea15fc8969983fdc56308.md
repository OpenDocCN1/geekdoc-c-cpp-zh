*Principal component regression* uses the principal components of **D** as regressors instead of the columns of **D**. In particular, let **d**<sub>1</sub>, ..., **d**<sub>M</sub> denote the columns of **D**. That is **d**<sub>i</sub> is the i<sup>th</sup> basis function evaluated at **x**<sub>1</sub>, ..., **x**<sub>N</sub>. In the following, we assume that the columns are standardized; that is they have mean 0 and length 1. The first assumption is valid, since the measurements are mean centred, and the second assumption is valid, since regressors are invariant to scaling. Any scaling will be absorbed in the multiplicative coefficient.

The correlation between the i<sup>th</sup> and j<sup>th</sup> regressors evaluated at the N measuring points is then
$$
\mathbf{d}_i^T \mathbf{d}_j.
$$

The correlation matrix of all regressors is **D**<sup>T</sup>**D**. A new set of regressors is generated as linear combinations of basis functions from the dictionary, say
$$
v_1 d_1(\mathbf{x}) + ... + v_M d_M(\mathbf{x}).
$$

Evaluating this at the N sampling points, we arrive at a linear combination of the columns of **D**,
$$
v_1 \mathbf{d}_1 + ... + v_M \mathbf{d}_M = \mathbf{Dv},
$$

where **v** = (v<sub>1</sub>, ..., v<sub>M</sub>)<sup>T</sup> is chosen such that **Dv** $\neq$ 0. The zero vector would make a very poor regressor. The correlation between two such linear combinations is
$$
\frac{\mathbf{v}_1^T \mathbf{D}^T \mathbf{D} \mathbf{v}_2}{||\mathbf{Dv}_1|| ||\mathbf{Dv}_2||},
$$

where **v**<sub>1</sub> and **v**<sub>2</sub> are such that **Dv**<sub>1</sub> $\neq$ 0 and **Dv**<sub>2</sub> $\neq$ 0. Here we need to divide by the lengths, since **Dv**<sub>1</sub> and **Dv**<sub>2</sub> no longer will have length 1.

Now, the matrix **D**<sup>T</sup>**D** is symmetric and positive semi-definite. Hence it has M non-negative eigenvalues and corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors. Thus choosing **v**<sub>1</sub> and **v**<sub>2</sub> to be eigenvectors of **D**<sup>T</sup>**D**, the new regressors are uncorrelated, since
$$
\mathbf{v}_1^T \mathbf{D}^T \mathbf{D} \mathbf{v}_2 = \lambda_2 \mathbf{v}_1^T \mathbf{v}_2 = 0.
$$

The eigenvectors corresponding to K nonzero eigenvalues are chosen. The new design matrix $\hat{\mathbf{D}}$ has columns **Dv**<sub>1</sub>, ..., **Dv**<sub>K</sub>. The corresponding functions span a K-dimensional subspace of the original model space, and are uncorrelated.

Let **V** be the matrix with columns **v**<sub>1</sub>, ..., **v**<sub>K</sub>. Then $\hat{\mathbf{D}} = \mathbf{DV}$. The predictions are
$$
\mathbf{f} = \hat{\mathbf{D}}(\hat{\mathbf{D}}^T \hat{\mathbf{D}})^{-1} \hat{\mathbf{D}}^T \mathbf{t} = \mathbf{DV}(\mathbf{V}^T \mathbf{D}^T \mathbf{DV})^{-1} \mathbf{V}^T \mathbf{D}^T \mathbf{t}.
$$

We can now calculate

$F_y(y) = \int_0^y f_{x_1}(x_1) \int_0^{y-x_1} f_{x_2}(x_2) dx_2 dx_1,$

where everything independent of $x_2$ has been taken out of the inner integral. Firstly, let $x_1$ and $x_2$ lie between 0 and $\infty$ but both follow the *exponential distribution*, $x_1, x_2 \sim \text{Exp}(\beta)$ with probability density function as defined in 2.14. For simplicity we let $\beta = 1$. Then

$$\begin{aligned} F_y(y) &= \int_0^y \exp(-x_1) \int_0^{y-x_1} \exp(-x_2) dx_2 dx_1 \\ &= \int_0^y \exp(-x_1) [-\exp(-x_2)]_0^{y-x_1} dx_1 \\ &= \int_0^y \exp(-x_1) [1 - \exp(-y + x_1)] dx_1 \\ &= \int_0^y \exp(-x_1) - \exp(-y) dx_1 \\ &= [-\exp(-x_1) - x_1 \exp(-y)]_0^y \\ &= [1 - \exp(-y) - y \exp(-y)]. \end{aligned}$$

Differentiating with respect to $y$ gives

$f_y(y) = \exp(-y) - (\exp(-y) - y \exp(-y)) = y \exp(-y).$

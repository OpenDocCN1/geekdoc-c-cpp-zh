while the sample covariance matrix is

\(\mathbf{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N (\mathbf{v}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{v}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu})^T, \quad (7.1)\)

which is symmetric.

The variance of the projected samples is

\(\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N (\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{v}_n - \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu})^2 = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{v}_n \mathbf{v}_n^T \mathbf{w} - 2 \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{v}_n \boldsymbol{\mu}^T \mathbf{w} + \mathbf{w}^T \boldsymbol{\mu} \boldsymbol{\mu}^T \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{w}^T \left[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^N (\mathbf{v}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{v}_n - \boldsymbol{\mu})^T \right] \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{w}.\)

The objective is to maximize this, subject to the constraint \(\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{w} = 1\). The Lagrangian function is given by

\(L(\mathbf{w}, \lambda) = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} - \lambda(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{w} - 1).\)

A stationary point of \(L(\mathbf{w}, \lambda)\) is a maximum of the constraint optimization. Using Appendices A.2.3 and A.2.2, the derivative of \(L(\mathbf{w}, \lambda)\) with respect to \(\mathbf{w}\) is

\(\frac{d}{d\mathbf{w}} L(\mathbf{w}, \lambda) = \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} - \lambda \mathbf{w}.\)

Setting this to zero, gives

\(\mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{w} = \lambda \mathbf{w},\)

and thus \(\mathbf{w}\) is an eigenvector of \(\mathbf{\Sigma}\).

Using \(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{w} = 1\), the eigenvalue is

\(\lambda = \lambda \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{w} = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{\Sigma} \mathbf{w},\)

which is the variance of the projected data. Thus, the projected data will be as much spread out as possible, if \(\mathbf{w}\) is chosen to be the eigenvector of \(\mathbf{\Sigma}\) with the largest eigenvalue.

Being a covariance matrix, \(\mathbf{\Sigma}\) has non-negative eigenvalues. Since \(\mathbf{\Sigma}\) is symmetric, its eigenvectors are orthogonal to each other. Therefore the principal space of dimension \(K\), where the projected data has the largest variance, is the subspace spanned by the eigenvectors of the \(K\) largest eigenvalues. These are known as *principal components*.

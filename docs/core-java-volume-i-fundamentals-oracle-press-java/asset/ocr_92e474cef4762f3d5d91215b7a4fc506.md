# # Chapter 2 Stroke

Stroke is a sudden neurological deficit caused by vascular injury to the brain—either by ischemia (blockage of blood flow) or hemorrhage (bleeding into the brain).

The two major categories of stroke are ischemic and hemorrhagic, and each has distinct pathophysiological mechanisms and treatment approaches.

The middle cerebral artery (MCA) is the most commonly affected vessel in ischemic stroke, leading to contralateral motor and sensory deficits, as well as aphasia if the dominant hemisphere is affected.

Posterior circulation strokes, involving the vertebrobasilar system, can cause brainstem signs, visual field defects, and ataxia, and are often more challenging to diagnose clinically.

Hemorrhagic stroke, while less common than ischemic stroke, has a higher early mortality rate and is frequently associated with hypertension, ruptured aneurysms, or arteriovenous malformations.

**ABSTRACT:** Active learning approaches in STEM education have seen a surge of interest, yet their assessment in student learning experiments remains a challenge. This paper presents a framework for assessing active learning in undergraduate chemistry laboratory courses, focusing on experimental design, data analysis, and student reflection. The framework integrates qualitative and quantitative measures, providing a holistic view of student engagement and learning outcomes. Results from a pilot study demonstrate the framework's utility in identifying strengths and areas for improvement in active learning implementations.

# ## 1. INTRODUCTION

Active learning has been widely advocated as a means to enhance student understanding and retention in science education. In laboratory settings, active learning encourages students to engage directly with experimental processes, fostering critical thinking and problem-solving skills. However, assessing the effectiveness of these approaches poses significant methodological challenges. Traditional assessment methods often fail to capture the nuanced ways in which students engage with active learning tasks.

The framework proposed in this study addresses these challenges by combining direct observation of student behavior, analysis of laboratory reports, and self-reported student reflections. This multi-faceted approach allows for a comprehensive evaluation of active learning outcomes, including both cognitive and affective domains. The assessment framework is designed to be adaptable across different laboratory courses and institutional contexts.

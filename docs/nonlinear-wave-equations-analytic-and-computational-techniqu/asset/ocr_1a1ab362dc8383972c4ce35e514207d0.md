It is a simple matter to show that if 0 < ε < 1, then

and thus ||q^ε||_{L²(ℝ)} = O(ε) as ε ↓ 0. We note that the cosine perturbation q^ε is also even (like q^{(ε)}) and oscillatory with frequency depending on ε (like q^{(ε)}). Moreover, for each fixed ε > 0, q^ε is the composition of real analytic functions hence is real analytic. Now, the basic question is whether or not the evolution from the family of initial data

A₀^ε = A₀ + q^ε

has structure as ε tends to zero that matches the remarkable structure, shown in Figure 3, of the solutions obtained from the data A₀^{(ε)} = A₀ + q^{(ε)}. The answer is a definitive no; see Figure 4 (panel (c)) for an illustrative visualization that demonstrates how the small oscillations in q^ε are immediately detected, and the modulational instabilities are triggered. We took this result as evidence, in support of the claim from [21], that the observed O(ε) decay in the case of the SSE is definitively a consequence of the special, integrable structure embedded in A₀^{(ε)} and is not due to its superficial features (namely, those present in A₀^ε as well).

As you might have noticed, E notation is most useful for very large and very small numbers.
E notation guarantees that a number is stored in floating-point format, even if decimal point is used. Note that you can use either E or e, and the exponent can have a positive or negative sign (see Figure 3.3). However, you can't have spaces in the number, so for example, 7.2 E6 is invalid.

To use a negative exponent to divide by a power of 10 instead of multiply by a power of 10. So 8.33E-4 means 8.33 / 10⁴, or 0.000833. Similarly, the electron mass 9.11e-31 kg means 0.00000000000000000000000911 kg. Take your choice.
Incidently, that 911 is the usual emergency telephone number in the United States and telephone messages are carried by electrons. Coincidence or scientific conspiracy? You be the judge.) Note that -8.33E-4 means -83300. A sign in front applies to the number value, and sign in the exponent applies to the scaling.

Note
The form d.dddE+n moves the decimal point places to the right, and the form d.dddE-n moves the decimal point places to the left. This moveable decimal point is the origin of the term "floating-point."

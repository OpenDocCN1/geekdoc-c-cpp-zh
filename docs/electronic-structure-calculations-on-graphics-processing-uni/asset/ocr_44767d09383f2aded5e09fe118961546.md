As a more interesting example, we consider a GPU-accelerated implementation of the configuration interaction singles (CIS) and TDDFT algorithms [33]. Full reviews of these methods can be found elsewhere [34]. The basic working equations for TDDFT can be written in the following matrix notation:

$$ \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{A} & \mathbf{B} \\ \mathbf{B} & \mathbf{A} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{X} \\ \mathbf{Y} \end{pmatrix} = \omega \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{1} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & -\mathbf{1} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{X} \\ \mathbf{Y} \end{pmatrix}. $$

Here, \(\mathbf{X}\) and \(\mathbf{Y}\) are vectors representing transition amplitudes, and \(\omega\) is the excitation energy. Application of Eq. (4.65) for Hartree–Fock theory results in the time-dependent Hartree–Fock (TDHF) method, where the operators \(A\) and \(B\) are defined as follows:

$$ A_{ia,jb} = \delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}(\epsilon_a - \epsilon_i) + (\theta_i \theta_a | \theta_j \theta_b) - (\theta_i \theta_j | \theta_a \theta_b), $$

$$ B_{ia,jb} = (\theta_i \theta_a | \theta_j \theta_b) - (\theta_i \theta_j | \theta_a \theta_b). $$

For TDDFT, the operators are defined similarly, but the Coulomb-like ERI is replaced by a functional derivative of the exchange correlation potential:

$$ A_{ia,jb} = \delta_{ij}\delta_{ab}(\epsilon_a - \epsilon_i) + (\theta_i \theta_a | \theta_j \theta_b) + (\theta_i f_{xc} | \theta_a \theta_b), $$

$$ B_{ia,jb} = (\theta_i \theta_a | \theta_j \theta_b) + (\theta_i f_{xc} | \theta_a \theta_b), $$

$$ (\theta_i \theta_j | f_{xc} | \theta_a \theta_b) = \iint \theta_i(\vec{r}_1) \theta_j(\vec{r}_1) \frac{\delta^2 E_{xc}}{\delta \rho(\vec{r}_1) \delta \rho(\vec{r}_2)} \theta_a(\vec{r}_2) \theta_b(\vec{r}_2). $$

In the above equations, ERIs are written in terms of molecular orbitals that result from a prior SCF calculation. Subscripts \(i\) and \(j\) refer to occupied orbitals, while \(a\) and \(b\) index virtual orbitals. Spin is neglected for clarity. Using the Tamm–Dancoff approximation (TDA), the \(B\) matrix is set to zero. This results either in CIS for TDHF or in TDA–TDDFT for TDDFT:

$$ \mathbf{AX} = \alpha \mathbf{X}. $$

This equation is efficiently solved using iterative diagonalization algorithms such as Davidson’s method [35]. Such algorithms solve the eigenvalue problem through a series of matrix vector products between \(A\) and trial eigenvectors:

$$ (\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X})_{\mu} = \sum_{\nu} [\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{ab}(\epsilon_a - \epsilon_i) + (\theta_i \theta_a | \theta_j \theta_b) - (\theta_i \theta_j | \theta_a \theta_b)] X_{\nu}. $$

In order to apply the J- and K-engine algorithms, these products can be carried out in the AO basis using the following transformations:

$$ \sum_{\mu} [(\theta_i \theta_a | \theta_j \theta_b) - (\theta_i \theta_j | \theta_a \theta_b)] X_{\mu} = \sum_{\mu\nu} C_{\mu i} C_{\nu a} F_{\mu \nu}. $$

$$ F_{\mu \nu} = \sum_{\lambda \sigma} T_{\mu \lambda} [(\mu \nu | \lambda \sigma) - (\mu \lambda | \nu \sigma)], $$

$$ T_{\mu \lambda} = \sum_{\alpha \nu} X_{\nu i} C_{\nu a} C_{\alpha \nu}. $$

The one-center ERIs are parameterized with numerical values that are much smaller than the analytical values. It is commonly assumed that this implicitly accounts for electron correlation. One usually distinguishes Coulomb and exchange integrals,

\((\mu_A \nu_A | \lambda_A \sigma_A) = S_{\nu \sigma}.\) (3.57a)
\((\mu_A \nu_A | \lambda_A \sigma_A) = S_{\nu \sigma}.\) (3.57b)

All other one-center ERIs are zero due to symmetry.

The two-center ERIs \((\mu_A | \nu_B \lambda_B \sigma_B)\) represent the interaction energy between the charge distributions \(\phi_{\mu} \phi_{\nu}\) and \(\phi_{\lambda} \phi_{\sigma}\). These charge distributions are expanded [84-86] in terms of multipole moments \(M_{lm}\) with order \(l\) and orientation \(m\), which are represented by an appropriate configuration of \(2^l\) point charges of magnitude \(1/2\) separated by a distance \(D\). The two-center ERIs are then evaluated in terms of semiempirical, classical multipole-multipole interactions between these multipole moments

\((\mu_A \nu_A | \kappa_B \lambda_B \sigma_B) = \sum_{l_1 l_2} \sum_{m=-l_{min}}^{l_{min}} [M^{l_1}_{1m} M^{l_2}_{2m}].\) (3.58)

where \(l_{min}\) is the lower of the two multipole moments \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) in the summation and, using the Klopman-Ohno formula, with

\([M^{l_1}_{1m} M^{l_2}_{2m}] = \frac{1}{2^{l_1+l_2}} c^{l_1}_{l_1} c^{l_2}_{l_2} \sum_i \sum_j \sum_k \sum_l [R^k_{ij} + (\rho^l_i + \rho^l_j)^2]^{-1/2}.\) (3.59)

Here, \(R_{ij}\) is the distance between the point charges. The additive terms \(\rho^l_i\) and \(\rho^l_j\) are chosen such that, at vanishing interatomic distance (\(R_{ab} \to 0\)), the ERIs reduce to the corresponding one-center integrals in the monoatomic case. The factors \(c^{l_1}_{l_1}\) and \(c^{l_2}_{l_2}\) ensure that at infinite separation (\(R_{ab} \to \infty\)) the classical limit of the multipole interactions (which is exact for nonoverlapping charge distributions) is recovered. Details can be found in Refs [85, 86].

# 3.5.4 Energy and Core Repulsion

The total electronic energy is given as

\(E_{el} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{pq} P_{pq} (h_{\mu \nu} + F_{\mu \nu})\) (3.60)

and the total energy is

\(E_{tot} = E_{el} + \sum_{A<B} E^{core}_{AB},\) (3.61)

where \(E^{core}_{AB}\) is the repulsion energy between the cores of an atom pair AB.

Aiming at a balance between electrostatic attractions and repulsions within a molecule, the core repulsion, like the core-electron attraction of Eq. (3.54), is treated in terms of the corresponding two-center ERIs

\(E^{core}_{AB} = Z_A Z_B \langle s_A s_B | s_A s_B \rangle [1 + f_{ab}(R_{ab})].\) (3.62)

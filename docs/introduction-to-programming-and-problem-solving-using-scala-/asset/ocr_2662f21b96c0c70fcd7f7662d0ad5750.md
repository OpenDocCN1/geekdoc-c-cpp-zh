This approach to solving problems is called a top-down approach, because you start at the top with a large problem and break it down into smaller and smaller pieces until, at the bottom, you have elements that are simple to address. The solutions you get are then put together to produce the total solution.

Another thing that you will learn from programming is that there are many ways to break down almost any problem, not all of them are equally good. Some ways of breaking the problem simply "more sense sense". Granted, that is something of a judgement, and might differ from one person to the next. A more quantifiable metric of how a problem is broken down is how much the pieces can be reused. If you have solved a particular problem once, you do not want to solve it again. You would rather use the solution you came with before. Some ways of breaking up a problem are pieces that are very free and likely to be useful in other contexts. Other ways will give you elements that are very specific to a given problem and will not be useful to you ever again.

There are many ways of programming for which there are no hard and fast rules on how should be done. In this respect, programming is more than an art than a science. Like any art, in order to get good at it you have to practice. Programming has other similarities to the creative arts. Programming itself is a creative task. When you are programming, you are taking an idea that exists in your head and giving a manifestation that is useful to others. It is not tangible. Indeed, that is one of the philosophically interesting aspects of software. It is a creation that other people can experience, but they cannot touch. It is completely virtual. Virtual being has benefits. Physical media have limitations on this, imposed by the laws of physics. Whether you are painting, sculpting, or engineering a device, this does not suffer from this. The ability of expression in programming is virtually boundless. If you go far enough in computer science you will learn there are bounds, but even the results are interesting because it is possible that the bounds on computation are on human thinking as well.

This ability for near infinite expression is the root of the power, beauty, and joy of programming. Programs can become arbitrarily complex. They can become too complex for humans to understand what they are doing. For this reason, a major part of the field of Computer Science is trying to find ways to tame the complexity and make it so large and complex ideas can be expressed in ways that are also easy for humans to follow and determine the correctness of.

Scala has tools like Python and other scripting languages that make it easy to write small programs and to get started dealing with too much overhead. # Using this Book

The goal of this book is to help you in your efforts to learn how to program. In addition to the practice that was just mentioned, there are some suggestions for how to get the most out of this book. Clearly, working as many examples and projects as you can counts as practice. In addition you should really read descriptions in this book and make sure you understand the code samples in it. Often, students treat the task of reading material as sounding out all the words in your head. While that fits the denotative definition, the connotation is that you string the words together and understand what they mean. For understanding, you can not just say you have gone through a chapter. You should also “follow along” with the material by writing code. The best way to internalize the details of a chapter, and to learn a new language, is to type things in, so you have to pay attention to the details. Understanding of every detail is especially important in this topic because the material in any given chapter is highly dependent on the chapters before it. If you fail to understand one chapter, you are not likely to understand the ones that follow it either. Also, effort spent on the early chapters will pay off later. Fortunately, there are resources that accompany this book that can help with this.

Students entering this course who have a background in programming consider taking a “fast path” to chapters 5 and 6. You do not want to skip them completely as they are aspects of Scala that will likely differ from what you have done before. One way to approach this is to go to the end of any given chapter and then to the exercises. Refer back to the chapter as needed to complete it. Once you do the exercises and a project or two, you should feel comfortable moving on.

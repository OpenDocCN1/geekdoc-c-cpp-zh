What we do in Gaussian elimination is multiply and add together rows in $A$ and $y$ to remove coefficients and turn $A$ into a triangular matrix. We might also have to swap rows at certain times. In fact, we will do generally to improve numerical stability. To begin with, we remove the term from everything but the first row. We could do this with $A$ as is, but for numerical reasons it is best if we keep the largest coefficient. We will see other reasons for when we remove $b$. So, the first time the coefficient of $a$ is 4, and it is in the second row; we swap the first and second rows. Note that we swap both in $A$ and $y$. This gives the following values.

$A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 & -2 \ 1 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$

and

$y = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \ 1 \ 2 \end{bmatrix}$

We eliminate the terms in the second and third rows by multiplying their values appropriately so that when we subtract them from the top column we do not have anything left. If a term is already zero in a row we can leave it alone. In the first case we will remove the term from the middle row by multiplying it by 4 and subtracting the top row from it. We will do nothing with the bottom row. This gives the following values.

$A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 & -2 \ 0 & 0 & 0 \ 0 & 2 & -1 \ \end{bmatrix}$

and

$y = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \ 2 \ \end{bmatrix}$

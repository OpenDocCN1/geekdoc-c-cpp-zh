In contrast to top-down, it is also possible to approach problems from the bottom-up. This approach works when you have a certain familiarity with solving a particular type of problem and what you know types of pieces you will need or when your top level is not well defined and you have to try some different approaches. This can happen when you are building software for a customer, as the customer may have only a vague idea of what they want their program to look like. In that situation, you can build pieces what you know and try finding something by putting them together in different ways until the customer is happy with.

To help you understand this, consider the common example of making a peanut butter and jelly sandwich in figure 4.2. Often this example is used to illustrate the explicitness required for programming. Here the main focus is on how the problem can be broken down and solved piece-by-piece. At the top is the full problem. This is broken into three subproblems, each one broken down further. In order to be truly explicit, the steps would have to be broken down much further.

# Preface

Welcome to “Introduction to Programming and Problem Solving Using Scala”. This book is intended to be used in first-semester college classrooms to teach students beginning programming. To accomplish this task, the Scala programming language is used. The book was constructed with a focus on the topics students need to know. These topics were woven into a format that was deemed the best way to communicate these ideas. Because there are two very different audiences who might be reading this, the rest of the preface is split into two sections.

# To the Student

Welcome to the world of programming. You are about to embark on a field of study that will hopefully open your mind to new ways of thinking and impact the way you view everything in the world around you. For students who intend to major in Computer Science and make careers working with computer technology, this is your first step in learning to communicate with the computer and to instruct the computer in how it should solve problems for you.

For those who are not planning to make a career in a computing field, a course in computer programming can still be remarkably beneficial. Computer programming is fundamentally, problem solving. It is about figuring how to solve problems and express them in many ways that the computer can understand. Your entire future life is, in one way or another, going to deal with how to solve problems. You will need to have approaches to solving these problems well formed in your own head and be able to communicate them to other people in non-ambiguous ways that they understand what you want and act on. Learning to program computers will help you develop and hone this ability.

There are more direct benefits as well. The world you live in is extremely dependent on computing. Many of the things you do that you do not call a computer include microchips and digital processing. These devices/objects run tasks to complete during an average day are enabled by computer power and the ability to process and store information quickly. Activities like browsing the web for work or leisure explicitly involve this. Other activities such as financial transactions made with anything other than can implicitly involve it. This ubiquitous presence of digital processing is only going to grow over time. Even if you never have to write programs as part of your future life, you can only benefit from having an understanding of what is going under the hood.

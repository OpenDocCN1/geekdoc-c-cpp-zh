# 6.4 Knapsack

During a robbery, a burglar finds much more loot than he had expected and to decide what to take. The bag (or "knapsack") will hold a total weight of at most W pounds. There are n items to pick from, of weight $w_1, \dots, w_n$ and dollar value $v_1, \dots, v_n$. What's the most valuable combination of items he can fit into his bag?

For instance, take W = 10 and

| Item | Weight | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | $30 |
| 2 | 3 | $14 |
| 3 | 4 | $16 |
| 4 | 2 | $9 |

> †If this application seems frivolous, replace "weight" with "CPU time" and "only W pounds can be taken" with "only W units of CPU time are available." Or use "bandwidth" in place of "CPU time," etc. The knapsack problem generalizes a wide variety of resource-constrained selection tasks.

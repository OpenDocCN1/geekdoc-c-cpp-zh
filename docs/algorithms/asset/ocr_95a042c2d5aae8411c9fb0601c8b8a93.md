# # Exercises

6.1. A contiguous subsequence of a list $S$ is a subsequence made up of consecutive elements of $S$. For instance, if $S$ is

5, 15, $-30, 10, -5, 40, 10,

15, $-30$, 10 is a contiguous subsequence but 5, 15, 40 is not. Give a linear-time algorithm for the following task:

+   *Input:* A list of numbers, $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$;
*Output:* The contiguous subsequence of maximum sum (a subsequence of length zero has zero sum).

For the preceding example, the answer would be $-10, 5, 40$, with a sum of 55.
(Hint: For each $j \in [1, 2, \dots, n]$, consider contiguous subsequences ending exactly at $j$).

6.2. You are going on a long trip. You start on the road at mile 0. Along the way there are $n$ hotels, at miles $a_1 < a_2 < \dots < a_n$, where each $a_i$ is measured from the starting point. The only places you are allowed to stop at are these hotels, but you can choose which of the hotels you stop. You must stop at the final hotel (at $a_n$), which is your destination.

You'd ideally like to travel 200 miles a day, but this may not be possible (depending on the spacing of the hotels). If you travel $x$ miles during a day, the penalty for day is $(200 - x)^2$. You want to plan your trip to minimize the total penalty—that is, the sum, over all travel days, of the daily penalties. Give an efficient algorithm that determines the optimal sequence of hotels at which to stop.

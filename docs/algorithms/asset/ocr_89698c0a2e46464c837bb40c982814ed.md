A few years later Narendra Karmarkar, a graduate student at UC Berkeley, came up with a completely different idea, which led to another polynomial algorithm for linear programming. Karmarkar's algorithm is known as the interior point method, because it does just like that: it dashes to the optimum corner by not coming to corner on the surface of the polyhedron but by cutting a clever path in the interior of the polyhedron. And it performs well in practice.

But the greatest advance in linear programming algorithms was not Khachian's theoretical breakthrough or Karmarkar's novel approach, but an unexpected consequence of the latter: the fierce competition between the two approaches, simplex and interior point, resulted in the development of very fast code for linear programming.

# 7.7 Postscript: circuit evaluation

7. Duckwheat is produced in Kansas and Mexico and consumed in New York and California. Kansas produces 10 shnapells of duckwheat and Mexico 8. Meanwhile, New York consumes 10 shnapells and California 13. The transportation costs per shnupell are $4 from Mexico to New York, $1 from Mexico to California, $2 from Kansas to New York, and $3 from Kansas to California.

Write a linear program that decides the amount of duckwheat (in shnapells and fractions of a shnupell) to be transported from each producer to each consumer, so as to minimize the overall transportation cost.

7.3. A cargo plane can carry a maximum weight of 100 tons and a maximum volume of 60 cubic meters. There are three materials to be transported, and the cargo company may choose to carry any amount of each, up to the maximum limits given below.

+   * Material 1 has density 2 tons/cubic meter, maximum available 40 cubic meters, and revenue $1,000 per cubic meter.
* Material 2 has density 1 ton/cubic meter, maximum available 30 cubic meters, and revenue $1,200 per cubic meter.
* Material 3 has density 3 tons/cubic meter, maximum available 20 cubic meters, and revenue $12,000 per cubic meter.

Write a linear program that optimizes revenue within the constraints.

7.4. Moe is deciding how much Regular beef beer and how much Duff beer to order each week. Regular beef costs Moe $1 per pint and he sells it at $2 per pint; Duff strong costs Moe $1.50 per pint he sells it at $3 per pint. However, as part of a complicated marketing campaign the Duff company will only sell a pint of Duff for two pints or more of Regular that Moe buys. Furthermore, due to past events that are better left untold, Duff will not sell Moe more than 300 pints per week. Moe knows how he can sell much more he has. Formulate a linear program for deciding how much Regular beef and how much Duff to buy, so as to maximize Moe's profit.

7.5. The Canine products offers two dog foods, Frisky Pup and Husky Hound, which are made from a blend of cereal and meat. A package of Frisky requires 1 pound of cereal and 1.5 pounds of meat, and sells for $7. A package of Husky Hound uses 2 pounds of cereal and 1 pound of meat, and sells for $6. Raw cereal costs $1 per pound and raw meat costs $2 per pound. It costs $1.40 to package the Frisky Pup and $0.60 to package the Husky Hound. A total of 240,000 pounds of cereal and 180,000 pounds of meat are available each month. The only production bottleneck is that the factory can only package 110,000 bags of Frisky Pup per month. Needless to say, management would like to maximize profit.

+   (a) Formulate the problem as a linear program in two variables.

+   (b) Graph the feasible region, give the coordinates of every vertex, and circle the vertex maximizing profit. What is the maximum profit possible?

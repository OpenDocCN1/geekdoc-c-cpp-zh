**Figure 2.6** The complex roots of unity are ideal for our divide-and-conquer scheme.

### The complex plane\n z = a + bi$ is plotted at position $(a, b)$.\nPolar coordinates: rewrite as $z = r(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta) = re^{i\theta}$, denoted $(r, \theta)$.\n- length $r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$\n- angle $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$; $\cos\theta = a/r, \sin\theta = b/r$.\n- $\theta$ can always be reduced modulo $2\pi$.\nExamples: Number | -1 | $i$ | $5+5i$\nPolar coords | $(1, \pi)$ | $(1, \pi/2)$ | $(5\sqrt{2}, \pi/4)$

![](bbox=[0.295, 0.147, 0.463, 0.275])

### Multiplying is easy in polar coordinates\nMultiply the lengths and add the angles:\n$(r_1, \theta_1) \times (r_2, \theta_2) = (r_1r_2, \theta_1 + \theta_2)$.\nFor any $z = (r, \theta)$,\n- $-z = (r, \theta + \pi)$ since $-1 = (1, \pi)$.\n- If $z$ is on the unit circle (i.e., $r=1$), then $z^n = (1, n\theta)$.

### The nth complex roots of unity\nSolutions to the equation $z^n = 1$.\nBy the multiplication rule: solutions $z = (1, \theta)$, for $\theta$ multiple of $2\pi/n$ (shown here for $n=16$).\nFor even $n$:\n- These numbers are **plus-minus paired**: $(-1, \theta) = (1, \theta+\pi)$.\n- Their squares are the $(n/2)$nd roots of unity, shown here with boxes around them.

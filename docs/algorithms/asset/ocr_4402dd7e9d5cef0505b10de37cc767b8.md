In designing a local search algorithm, it will be a big convenience to focus on the special case $\alpha=1/2$, in which $A$ and $B$ are forced to contain exactly half the vertices. The loss of generality is purely cosmetic, as GRAPH PARTITIONING reduces to this particular case.

We need to decide upon a neighborhood structure for our problem, and there is one obvious way to do this. Let $(A, B)$, with $|A|=|B|$, a candidate solution; we will define its neighbors to be all solutions obtainable by swapping one pair of vertices across the cut, that is, all solutions of the form $(A-(a)+\{b\}, B-(b)+\{a\})$ $a \in A$ and $b \in B$.

a linear function of the variables. Solving this linear program by simplex should take less than a second and will give us the optimum strategy for our company.

Well, almost. The optimum solution might be fractional; for instance, it might involve hiring 10.6 workers in the month of March. This number would be rounded to 10 or 11 to make sense, and the overall cost would increase correspondingly. In the present example, most of the variables take on fairly large (double-digit) values, thus rounding is unlikely to affect things too much. There are other LPs, in which rounding decisions are to be made very carefully in order to end up an integer solution of reasonable quality.

In general, there is a tension in linear programming between the ease of obtaining fractional solutions and the desirability of integer ones. As we shall see in Chapter 8, finding the optimum integer solution of an LP is an important but very hard problem, called *integer linear programming*.

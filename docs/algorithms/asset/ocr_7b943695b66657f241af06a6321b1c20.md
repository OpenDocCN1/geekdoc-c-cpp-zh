3.26. An Eulerian tour in a directed graph is one that is allowed to pass through each vertex multiple times, but must use each edge exactly once.

This simple concept was used by Euler in 1736 to solve the famous Konigsberg bridge problem, which launched the field of graph theory. The city of Konigsberg (now called Kaliningrad, in western Russia) is the meeting point of two rivers with a small island in the middle. There are seven bridges across the rivers, and a popular recreational tour of the time was to determine whether it is possible to perform a tour in which each bridge is crossed *exactly once*.

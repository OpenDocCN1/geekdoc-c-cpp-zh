5.19. **Entropy.** Consider a distribution over $n$ outcomes, with probabilities $p_1, p_2, \dots, p_n$.

+   (a) Just for this part of the problem, assume that $p_i$ is a power of 2 (that is, of the form $1/2^k$). Suppose a long sequence of $m$ samples is drawn from the distribution and for all $1 \le i \le n$, the $i^{th}$ outcome occurs exactly $mp$ times in the sequence. Show that if Huffman encoding is applied to this sequence, the encoding will have length

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \log \frac{1}{p_i}$$

+   (b) Now consider arbitrary distributions—that is, the probabilities $p_i$ are not restricted to powers of 2. The most commonly used measure of the amount of randomness in the distribution is the entropy

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} p_i \log \frac{1}{p_i}$$

For distribution (over $n$ outcomes) is the entropy the largest? The smallest possible?

5.21. A *feedback edge* of an undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ is a subset of edges $E' \subseteq E$ that intersects every cycle of the graph. Thus, removing the $E'$ will render the graph acyclic.

Give an efficient algorithm for the following problem:

**Input:** Undirected graph $G = (V, E)$ with positive edge weights $w_e$\n**Output:** A feedback edge set $E' \subseteq E$ of minimum total weight $\sum_{e \in E'} w_e$

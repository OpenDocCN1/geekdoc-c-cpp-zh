Moreover, answering complex join queries with one MRJ may not guarantee the best time efficiency in practice. Wu et al. [4] targets the efficient processing of multiway join over massive volume of data. Although they present their work in the context of equi-join, their focus is how to decompose a complex query into MRJs and schedule them to eventually evaluate the query as fast as possible. their decomposition is still join attribute oriented. Therefore, the original query is decomposed into multiple pairwise joins and selecting the optimal join order is the main problem. On the contrary, although we also explore the scheduling of MRJs in this work, MRJ being scheduled can involve multiple relations and multiple join conditions. Our solution tries to explore all possible evaluation plans. The work of Wu et al. [4] does not take the limit of processing unit into consideration, which is a critical issue in practice. Some other works try to explore the general work flow of single MRJ or multiple MRJs to improve the whole performance.

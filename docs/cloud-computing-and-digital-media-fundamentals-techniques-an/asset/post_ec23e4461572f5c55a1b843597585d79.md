## 14.2 PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION ISSUES

Users provide their personal data to the right holder to create an account and enter into a relevant licence agreement in order to access the digital media. The right holder is the collector of the users’ data, which will be automatically shared with the other relevant subjects [i.e., CSP(s) and, possibly, with the third-party IPR owner(s)] in order to provide users with the requested service.¹ Legally speaking, the right holder acts as the data controller,² CSP(s) and third-party IPR owner(s), if any, who will typically be the data processors,³ and the users are the data subjects.³⁴ At the time of the collection of the personal data, the right holder must provide the user with information

There has been a lot of discussions and concerns about the generally unclear (inadequate) legal framework for cloud computing, especially when it comes to personal data protection. Surely, the legal framework leaves ample room for interpretation. Therefore, it is extremely important to clearly lay down points' duties and obligations in appropriate data processing agreements. In this respect, Article 29 Working Party (WP) Opinion 5/2012 on Cloud Computing [1] specifically addresses the point of putting in place appropriate contractual safeguards. Moreover, in the last year, there has been a lot of new developments in Europe from the personal data protection regulatory point of view. It can be argued that we are now facing a second generation of cloud data processing agreements. The such fundamental changes are to be traced back to the public at the EU and Member States' levels of the following official documents:

- ICO guidance on the use of cloud computing [5];

## 14.3 SPECIFIC FOCUS ON THE APPLICATION OF CLOUD COMPUTING TO DIGITAL MEDIA

The evolution of the digital market (also before the existence of cloud computing) has pivoted around three main aspects that are intertwined: the technological framework, the business model framework in the distribution of digital content, and the legal framework.

- The technological framework. The development of the Internet and new technologies had a deep impact on the demand and distribution of digital contents. Technology facilitates the availability of work at anytime and anywhere, and indefinite number of perfect copies with little or no marginal costs; the increasing availability of the Internet connection and end-to-end architectures make it easier to upload, download, distribute, and sample preexisting and digital contents.

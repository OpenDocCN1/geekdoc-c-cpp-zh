The camera matrix K – IP = [R|t]is called a normalized camera matrix and the effect of the known calibration matrix has been removed. Now, consider a pair of normalized camera matrices det(V) > 0 and P=[R|t]. The fundamental matrix corresponding to the pair of normalized cameras is customarily the essential matrix, and according to Section 11.2.3, it has the form:

```
E = [t]x R R^T t^T (11.14)
```

Snavely [1] uses the five-point algorithm to compute E; however, since we E = K FK, we can easily compute the essential E if the camera is calibrated [K = diag(ff)], and f can be loaded from photos]. Once the essential matrix is known, R, t, and the camera matrices can be recovered.

11.2.5.1. Recover R and t According to the theory, the SVD of the essential matrix E = U diag(1,0,V^T), where U and V are chosen such that det(V) > 0 and det(U) > 0. Then t=[u1u2u3] and R is equal to Ra UD^V or RB UD^VT. Any combination of R and t according to the above prescription satisfies the epipolar constraint. To resolve the ambiguities, we assume that the first camera is P=[I] and t is of unit length. The second camera matrix is in the four possible solutions: PA=[Ra|ta],PB=[Ra|^{-ta}],PC=[Rb|tb],PD=[Rb|^{-tb}. One of the four choices corresponds to the true configuration.

The four solutions are illustrated in Figure 11.6, where it is shown that a reconstructed X will be in front of both cameras in one of these solutions only. Thus, with a single point to determine if it is in front of both cameras is sufficient to decide between the four different solutions for the camera matrix P'.

# 11.2.5.2 Triangulate 3D Points

In Section 11.2.5.1, we have computed the camera matrices P and P', let x and x' be the two points in the two images that satisfy the epipolar constraint, x'FP=0. This constraint can be interpreted geometrically in terms of the rays in space corresponding to the two image points. In particular, it means that x' lies on the epipolar line Fx. In turn, this means that the two rays backprojected from image points x and x' lie in a common epipolar plane, which is, a plane passing through the two camera centers.

The models revealed not only the importance and variety of medical data, but also a crucial need for medical software applications that better support the social dimension of medical activities: sharing medical data, easily communicating with colleagues to get advices, better capitalizing medical knowledge, and better supporting medical decision making. More precisely, medical practitioners expand four socio-professional networks: (1) health workers, including colleagues, experts, and friends; (2) academics and students; (3) health workers involving follow-up of inpatients; and (4) institutions. However, in practice, information sharing is still informal (e.g., by phone), in particular for medicole reasons, depriving practitioners from peer-decisions, and giving them the feeling of being alone.

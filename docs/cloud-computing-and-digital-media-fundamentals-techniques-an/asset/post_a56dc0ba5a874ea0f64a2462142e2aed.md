## 12.1 RESEARCH CONTEXT AND PROBLEM

Moving away from the traditional desktop model, cloud computing pushes the boundaries to rely on online resources, and invites us to reconsider the user interface. As cloud computing combines and leverages ubiquitous resources, users now have access to on-demand services.

This evolution raises several issues: how to cope with the loss of connectivity? To what extent will the migration to a cloud-based environment affect user experience? ## 12.2 RELATED WORKS

This section covers the convergence of cloud computing. We identify three classes of research: the social desktop for the cloud with online resources and the socialization of services; social navigation and sharing with privacy issues; and information seeking/refining to cloud with scalability.

### 12.2.1 Social Desktop for the Cloud

The application-centric model of the traditional desktop is progressively fading away: more and more applications and services are deployed in clouds and made available as Web applications. However, moving to a full Web-based environment breaks the guarantee of consistent UIs and leads to fragmented UX.

A possible explanation of this failure may be that the current desktop metaphor reflects the local nature of resources, grounded firmly in hierarchy and physical locations, and evolved little to support sharing and access control. As a consequence, the "social desktop" was proposed based on the concept of user-created groups. This concept extends the folder metaphor to encompass files, people, and applications. Compared to the usual folder metaphor, a unique instance of an object may be included in different groups, providing a lightweight means to allow sharing and access: as a group is associated with users, access is implicitly granted to the members of the group.

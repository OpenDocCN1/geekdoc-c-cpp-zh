+   2. Online course or workshops can be used to offer higher order learning activities as reading and discussing articles from newspapers with other learners in a text or voice chat. Cohort-paced online courses enroll learners in a group they move through the learning activities about the same time and speed. The cohort reduces the feeling of isolation; learners can interact to discuss the content and give each other support. A course facilitator or instructor helps the group move through the materials in a timely fashion and provides answers to questions that may arise. Cohort-paced courses typically have lower dropout rates than independent courses or self-study materials. In some instances, cohorts involve synchronous conferencing; however, the scheduling of such events can be complicated and they may make it difficult for learners who have other obligations such child care, shift work, or travel. Some community learning centers also equipped with broadband video-conferencing facilities that make it possible to bring small groups of learners together for work or study sessions, although the main use appears to be for the professional development of the tutors rather than for literacy instruction [25].

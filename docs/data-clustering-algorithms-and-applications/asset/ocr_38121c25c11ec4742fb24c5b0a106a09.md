# 14.2 Visual Words Learning

Visual words learning, which involves vector quantization, is among one of the earliest adaptions of clustering algorithms in multimedia applications. Inspired by the success of the bag-of-words (BoW) model used in text domain, bag-of-visuals (BoV) or bag-of-features (BoF) model is proposed to represent an image with a visual word histogram. Similar to the BoW model in the text domain, the BoV model uses a number of visual vocabulary to map the low level image patches or visual features. Unlike the text domain where the text is naturally punctuated by the quantization and white spaces (for certain languages) to obtain the vocabulary, BoV models involve an additional step, referred to as visual words learning or more sophisticated methods. Visual words learning employs vector quantization (VQ) or more sophisticated methods (such as sparse coding) to learn a set of visual words or the vocabulary, which is used in the second stage of the BoV model to decode the low-level visual-feature vectors into a new feature space. There are number of advantages for the bag-of-visuals model such as the ability to handle partial occlusion.

As quantization is one of the key ingredients in visual words learning, among many clustering algorithms, the $k$-means clustering plays an important role in the BoV model. $k$-means clustering is the most widely used method due to its simplicity and efficiency. However, there are two issues with the $k$-means clustering: the convergence of the algorithm goes to the local optimum and the determination of the parameter $k$ more. specifically, the $k$-means clustering only has is sensitive to the initialization of the cluster centroids. Although there have been a few remedies proposed in the literature such as the gap statistics [31], X-means [23], G-means [35], and spatial clustering [26], the most popular way of choosing $k$ is empirically pick the parameter with $k$ with multiple initializations of the centroids. This is due to the lack of prior knowledge on the density and the complexities of the data.

It is worth noting that the BoV model is not the first approach to apply the idea of clustering image patches with their visual features. For instance, in the texture classification problem, Leung and Malik [19] proposed to use the $k$-means clustering to quantify the densely sampled outputs of filter banks and form a dictionary of surface patches called prototype textures. These textures are then used as the bases for describing the images of different materials in relatively low-dimensional spaces. Sivic and Zerman [27] were among the first to adapt the BoV model in the video frame representation. They constructed a visual vocabulary by applying $k$-means clustering with Mahalanobis distance function, which downweights the noisy dimensions and correlates the dimensions. With the built vocabulary they can first encode the descriptors of the key frames as visual words. As a visual analogy of text retrieval, these visual words can then be built using index and frequency-inverse document (tf-idf) weighting scheme which is used for the task of retrieval. Surya et al. [11] proposed a bag of keypoints method on the low-affine-invariant descriptors of image patches in the image categorization problem. They choose to use the $k$-means clustering as the vector quantization method to construct the visual vocabulary and empirically decide the number of representative vectors with multiple initializations.

Other than the $k$-means algorithm, some other clustering algorithms were also used in the VQ for the construction of the vocabulary. For example, Rinsky and Lazebnik [25] used the aggregative clustering algorithm that takes a matrix of pairwise distances between visual descriptors to discover a small set of basic primitives for each image individually. With these basic primitives, the distribution of the descriptors is summarized in the form of medoids (the centrally located descriptor for each cluster) and the weights (proportional to the cluster size) of the clusters.

Although VQ using clustering algorithm have demonstrated their simplicity and efficiency, more sophisticated and powerful methods are proposed to learn better image representation.

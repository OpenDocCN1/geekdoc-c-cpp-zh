# 14.3 Video Summarization

It is an intensive task to find the informal or desired video sequences from a large volume of video corpus. To efficiently organize the unstructured video content in more compact form, few video summarization methods [14, 22] have been developed to allow users to browse voluminous videos efficiently and capture the content of their interest.

One of the straightforward ways for video summarization is to use a set of keyframes to summarize the video, each of which can be extracted from a video shot or subset. Some optimization criteria are proposed to construct the set of keyframes that maximize the video content is covered by the selected frames. Most of the video summarization methods rely on user interactions to generate the keyframes, or some heuristic criteria such as minimizing the keyframe similarity or maximizing the time intervals between keyframes. However, these simple methods usually suffer from poor summarization results due to the suboptimal or heuristic criteria that are adopted.

As opposed to these naive approaches, [14] provides a more sophisticated way to summarize the video sequences in a more principled manner. In another approach, [22] advances the video summarization by an analysis of global video structures and video highlights. Specifically, a video is represented as a weighted directed graph with a set of shots, where the vertices are the feature points of the shot and edges connecting each pair of vertices measuring similarity between the shots. A normalized cut graph is used to recursively partition adjacent clusters into clusters of shots. A temporal graph is then constructed using the obtained temporal adjacency. A directed edge is added to the temporal graph if a shot in one cluster has a temporally successive shot in another cluster.

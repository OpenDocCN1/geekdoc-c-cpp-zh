Partitional algorithms are also effective in detecting compact spherical-shaped clusters and easy to implement and use in practice [22]. K-means is a computationally efficient algorithm compared to hierarchical clustering. There is no consensus, it is believed that K-means is better than hierarchical clustering algorithms [35] in terms of the quality of the final clustering solution.

Hierarchical clustering methods can potentially overcome some of the critical problems associated with flat (partitional) clustering methods. One of the major advantages of hierarchical algorithms is the generation of the visual diagrams which can assist the end-users during the clustering process. In such applications, generally a user will label the clusters to understand more about them. This is also called labeling. Hierarchical methods are also deterministic compared to the nonparametric behavior with the basic K-means algorithm.

Despite these advantages, it is observed that hierarchical clustering methods the merge or split decisions at any given level in the hierarchy cannot be undone [3]. This is considered to be a problem for hierarchical algorithms since [14] proposes an iterative strategy that keeps modifying the dendrogram until the optimal solution is found. The runtime complexity of these hierarchical algorithms is quadratic which is not especially for large-scale problems. Parallel hierarchical clustering methods [41] have also been proposed to reduce the complexity to linear.

In spite of the numerous advances in the field of data clustering in the past two decades, both partitional and hierarchical clustering algorithms form a solid foundation for data clustering. Many of the newly proposed data clustering algorithms (to be discussed in the next few chapters) typically compare their performance to these fundamental clustering algorithms. In addition, due to their simplicity and ease of use, these algorithms are heavily used in several application domains as bioinformatics, information retrieval, text mining, imaging, climate science, and astronomy. The development of new variants of both partitional and hierarchical clustering remains an active area of research.

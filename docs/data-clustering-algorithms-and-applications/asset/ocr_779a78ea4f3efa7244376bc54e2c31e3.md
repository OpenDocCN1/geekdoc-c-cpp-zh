Once the within-clustering has been performed on all the documents in the collection, the resulting set S of segment-sets becomes the input to the subsequent phase, which is designed to identify the topics in the collection. The authors use a bisection version of the Spherical k-Means algorithm to cluster the segments. The use of disjoint clustering is motivated by the fact that each of the segment-sets will describe a single topic from the original document.

Tagarelli and Karypis [103] develop a model in the vector space model for representation of segment-sets. They adapt the conventional tf-idf function to be segment-set, segment-oriented, or document-oriented. Similar to tf-idf, their weights combine the term frequency within the local text unit (segment), and with the term rarity across the whole collection of text objects (i.e., segments, segment-sets or documents).

Let w be an index-term and S ∈ a segment-set. Let tf(w, S) be the weight of occurrences of w over all the segments in S. The segment-set Term Frequency-Inverse Segment frequency function is:

```
st-ifsf(w, S) = tf(w, S) * log (N_S / N_S(w))
```

At a higher level (i.e., at document level), the relevance weight of w with respect to S is computed by the Segment-set Term Frequency-Inverse Segment frequency function:

```
idf(w, S) = tf(w, S) * log (N_D / N_D(w))
```

Finally, at a lower level (i.e., at segment level), the relevance weight of w with respect to S is computed by the Segment-set Term Frequency-Inverse Segment frequency function:

```
st-ifsf(w, S) = tf(w, S) * exp (N_S(w) / N_S) * log (n_S / n_S(w))
```

where N_S is the number of segments in S, n_S is the number of segments in S_j and N_S(w) and n_S(w) are the portions of respectively, that contain w. In the above formula, an exponential factor is used to emphasize the segment-frequency of the terms within the local segment-unit. The rationale here is that terms occurring in many segments of a segment-set should be recognized as characteristic (discriminatory) of that segment-set, they should be weighted more than terms with low frequency.

The final step in the framework is to use the disjoint clustering of the segment-sets in order to reflect the multiple topics that exist in the collection's documents. Although alternative methods could be used to induce the final clustering, the authors take a simple assignment approach. Each cluster of segments is considered be a single topic, and each document is assigned to all the topics that contain at least one of its segment-sets.

The empirical evaluation that Tagarelli and Karypis [103] showed general improvement clustering over nonsegmented document clustering techniques in both the soft and hard clustering strategies. The segment-based views over the documents allow for an effective identification of overlapping clustering solutions, and the authors’ proposed segment-level overclustering improves the quality of both disjoint and overlapping clustering solutions.

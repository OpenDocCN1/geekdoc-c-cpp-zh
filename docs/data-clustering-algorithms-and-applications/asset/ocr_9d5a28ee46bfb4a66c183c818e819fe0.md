# 10.8.3 Distributed Clustering of Data Streams

In the context of sensor networks, the data stream is available only in a distributed setting, where large volumes of data are collected separately at the different sensors. A natural approach for clustering data is to transmit all the data to a centralized server. The clustering can then be performed at the centralized server to determine the final results. Unfortunately, such an approach is extremely expensive in terms of its communication costs due to the large volume of data which must be transmitted to the centralized server. Therefore, it is important to design a method which can reduce the communication costs among the different processors. A method proposed in [27] local clustering at each node and merges these clusters into a single clustering at low communication cost. Two methods are proposed in this work. The first method determines the clusters by using a farthest point algorithm, on the current set of data points at the site. After the farthest point algorithm, the center of a cluster is the closest point to the set of centers. For any incoming data, it is assigned to its cluster, as long as the distance is within a certain factor of an optimally computed radius. Otherwise, a reclustering is performed by applying the farthest point algorithm on current set of points. After the application of the farthest point algorithm, the centers are transmitted to the central server, which then computes a global clustering from these local centers over the different nodes. Global clusters can then be transmitted to the local nodes if desired. One attractive feature of the method is that an approximation bound is proposed on the quality of the clustering. A second method for distributed clustering proposed in [27] is the parallel guessing algorithm.

A variety of methods have also been proposed for distributed clustering of data streams. For example, techniques for distributed data clustering with the use of the k-means approach are proposed in [61]. Another method for distributed stream clustering reduces the dimensionality and communication cost by maintaining an online discretization may be found in [57]. Finally, a method for distributed clustering of data streams with the use of the EM approach is proposed in [66].

# 10.9 Discussion and Conclusions

The problem of clustering is fundamental to a wide variety of streaming applications, because of its natural applicability to summarizing and representing the data in a very small space. A variety of techniques have been proposed in the literature for stream clustering, such as partitioning methods, density-based methods, and probabilistic methods. The stream clustering method has also been extended to other data domains such as categorical, text, and uncertain data. Finally, methods have also been proposed for clustering graph streams.

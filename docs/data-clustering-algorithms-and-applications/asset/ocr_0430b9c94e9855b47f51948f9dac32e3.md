# 15.4 Similarity and Distance Measures

For conventional shape-based clustering algorithms, the use of similarity and distance measures is crucial. In [34] Liao classifies time-series measures into three classes:

+   (a) feature-based

+   (b) model-based

+   (c) shape-based

While this classification makes sense and analyzes in-depth the proposed time-series measures and their differences, it lacks information about whether each measure is appropriate for comparing multivariate time series as well. Over the last few years, multivariate time-series clustering has attracted the interest of the time-series research community as has a variety of interesting applications. In this sense, our of similarity/distance measures that are used in time-series clustering is supplementary to that of [34] and provides another insight. One of the most important decisions when performing time-series clustering is the similarity/distance measure that will be chosen in order to compare the times. Given a time-series similarity measure, time-series clustering can be performed with one of the proposed techniques that will be reviewed in the next section. Time-series similarity has been a long and studied problem for the past two decades. In the literature one can find many proposed measures, each appropriate for different applications having specific advantages and disadvantages. Many classification schemes have been proposed regarding time-series similarity. A popular classification is the time-series representation scheme that is assumed by the measures, as the series can be represented either by the raw data or by transformations of the original data such as the frequency transformation representations (Discrete Fourier Transformation (DFT) and Discrete Wavelet Transformation (DWT) or other representation models (Landmark and Important Points Representation, ARIMA Model and LPC Coefficient Representation, Signature Representation [10]). In this section we employ the raw data representation, where a series T of length n is represented as an ordered sequence of values T = [t1, t2,...,tn]. T is called raw representation of the time-series data. Another important feature and classification criterion when classifying similarity/distance measures for time series is whether a measure is appropriate for univariate or multivariate time series. In this section we review the commonly used similarity/distance measures when it comes to time-series clustering and distinguish them between univariate and multivariate measures.

# 15.4.1 Univariate Distance Measures

In this subsection we briefly present similarity/distance measures that are widely used by the majority of time-series clustering methods proposed in the literature and reviewed in this chapter. The similarity/distance measures presented here handle one-dimensional time series and most of them are extended by multidimensional time series similarity/distance approaches that will be described in the next subsection.

After this algorithm was proposed, a plethora of new heuristic algorithms to extract bicusters from noisy gene expression data have been developed. We will mention only a few here; for a detailed discussion on existing biclustering algorithms, we refer the readers to an excellent survey on this topic [71].

Coupled-two clustering (CTWC) technique was proposed in [41]. In this technique, a subset of genes (conditions) are used to cluster the genes. The Order-Preserving Submatrix Patterns (OPSMs) [13] algorithm finds local patterns in the expression levels of all genes inside the linear ordering of the experiments. However, the OPSM algorithm finds only one cluster at a time and captures only positively correlated genes. Iterative Signature Algorithm (ISA) [51] is a statistical clustering algorithm which defines a transcription module (cluster) as a correlated set of genes under a set of experimental conditions. ISA starts from a set of randomly selected genes (or conditions) that are iteratively refined until they are mutually consistent. At each iteration, a threshold is used to remove noise and maintain correlated genes and the associated coreulating genes.

# 16.2.4 Recent Developments

Recently, there have been emerging trends in the field of bicustering: (i) Identifying overlapping bicusters and negative correlations in a cluster has gained attention due to their biological importance [17]. Some of the recent algorithms [25, 77] allow for overlapping bicusters and find k rows and l columns simultaneously. Differential bicustering [76, 77, 37] aims to find genes that are correlated under a subset of conditions in one class but not the other. Identifying such class-specific bicusters can provide valuable knowledge for understanding the roles of genes in several diseases [76]. The classes could represent different tissues (normal versus cancerous), different types (e.g., male versus female), gene profiles (African-American versus Caucasian American) [61], (ii) Query-based bicustering algorithms [30, 122, 44] allow for identifying bicusters that are centered around seed genes of interest. In these algorithms, new search strategies are developed to exploit the expression profiles of certain genes of interest that are used to guide the bicluster searching mechanism. These approaches are extremely handy when one wants to compare the expression profiles of a certain set of genes with that of the existing knowledge which can be obtained by querying for similar profiles from a large-scale expression companhia.

# 16.2.5 Tricustering

The goal of triclust is to find coherent clusters that are similar across three dimensions. The motivation of this task comes from the biological domain where finding coherent clusters along the gene-sample-time (temporal) or gene-sample-region (spatial) dimensions is of great value. Although the problems that are suitable for triclust have been addressed earlier by ad-hoc methods [55], Zhao and Zaki [123] proposed the first formal algorithm for tricustering. After that, a few more algorithms have been proposed in recent years [98, 57].

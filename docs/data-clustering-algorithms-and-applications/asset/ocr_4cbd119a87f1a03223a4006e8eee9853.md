The authors propose a Heterogeneous Random Field (HRF) model to model structure and content of social media networks and determine clusters. This work introduces an energy function on edges and shows that the most probable clusters on the graph are found by minimizing the energy. Advantage of this algorithm is that it can detect noisy links which are fairly commonplace in real networks.

Agarwal et al. [1] focused on community detection from the perspective of heterogeneous density. Their method use local methods that adapt to local variations in density to extract interesting, coherent, and balanced clusters from all parts of a network. They use a mini-hash based approach to find a small number of local communities specific to each node and merge them into a (cone) set of global communities.

# 17.13.4 Directed Networks

Network clustering and community discovery algorithms, in general, work with undirected networks. But in the real world, many important networks are essentially directed. Examples include graphs representing Web Pages, Twitter users, or citations between research papers. Many studies have ignored the directionality of the edges and very few have worked on community detection in directed networks [60]. But simply ignoring the directionality can lead to false results [131].

Recently there has been some work on this matter. Researchers have extended their algorithms to take the directionality of information for edges into account. Versions of Normalized Cuts have been defined using random-walk interpretation of Normalized Cuts [114] by multiple researchers. Let $P be the transition matrix of a random walk on directed graph and $\pi$ be its stationary distribution vector (i.e., the PageRank vector) satisfying the condition that $\pi P = \pi$. The Normalized Cut for a group $S \subseteq V$ is [35, 80, 113]:

```
$Ncut_{dir}(S) = \frac{\sum_{i,j \in S} \pi_i P(i,j) + \sum_{i \in S, j \notin S} \pi_i P(i,j)}{\sum_{i \in S} \pi_i}$
```

Spectral clustering can be used to minimize the objective function above by postprocessing the eigenvectors of the directed Laplacian defined as [35, 80, 113]:

```
$L = I - \frac{\pi^{-1/2} P \pi^{-1/2} + \pi^{-1/2} P' \pi^{1/2}}{2}$
```

Here $P$ and $\pi$ are defined above. The directed version of modularity [125] was introduced by Leicht and Newman [99] as:

```
$Q = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i,j} [A_{ij} - \frac{d_i^{out} d_j^{in}}{e}] \delta_{c_i,c_j}$
```

where $e$ is the number of edges, $d_i^{in}$ and $d_i^{out}$ refer to the indegree and outdegree of node $i$, respectively

in Figure 6.1(a), the data is dispersed throughout the spatial domain with several more dense regions in the shape of a circle, square, and rectangle. A single resolution uniform grid would have difficulty identifying those dense, nested clusters as shown in Figure 6.1(b). In contrast, an adaptive algorithm, such as AMR, permits higher resolution throughout the space can recognize those dense, nested clusters with centers at the clear, dense shapes. Figure 6.1 is adapted from Figure 1 from Liao et al. [15] and is only illustrative, not based on real data.

To address locality, axis-shifting algorithms were introduced. These methods adapt axis-shifting partitioning strategies to identify areas of high density in the feature space. For instance, in Figure 6.2(a), traditional grid-based algorithms will have difficulty addressing the border and continuity of the most dense regions because of the predefined grids and threshold of significant clusters. The clustering from using a single uniform grid, shown in Figure 6.2(b), demonstrates that some clusters are divided into smaller clusters because the continuity of the border of the dense regions is disturbed by clusters with low density. To remedy this, axis-shifting algorithms, such as ASGC [17], shift the coordinate axis by half cell in each dimension creating a new grid structure. This shifting yields clustering that recognizes more dense regions adjacent to lower density cells as dense clusters as shown in Figure 6.2(c). By combining the clustering from both axes, algorithms can recognize dense clusters as shown in Figure 6.2(a), 6.2(c), and 6.2(d) are adapted from Figures 11, 12, 14, and 18, respectively, from Lin et al. [17] and are only illustrative, not based on real data or real clustering algorithms.

For handling density data, there are several grid-based approaches. For example, the CLIQUE algorithm selects appropriate subspaces rather than the whole space for finding the dense regions. In contrast, the OptGrid algorithm uses density estimations. A summary of grid-based algorithms that address these three challenges is presented in Table 6.2.

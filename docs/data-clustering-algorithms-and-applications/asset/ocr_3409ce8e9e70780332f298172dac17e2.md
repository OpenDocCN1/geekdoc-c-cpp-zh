Distances between members of distributions may not necessarily tend to the mean as the dimensionality increases. The fundamental differences between data following a single distribution and data following other distributions are discussed in Bennett et al.[25]. The authors demonstrate that nearest-neighbor queries are both theoretically and practically meaningful when the search is limited to others from the same cluster (distribution) as the query point, and other clusters (distributions) are well separated from the cluster in question. The key concept is that pairwise stability of clusters, which is said to hold when the mean distance between points of different clusters maintain the mean distance points belonging to the same cluster. The clusters are pairwise stable, for any point belonging to a given cluster, its neighbors need to belong to the same cluster. Here, a nearest-neighbor query size of the order can be considered meaningful, whereas differentiation between nearest and farthest neighbors within the same cluster may be meaningless. # Aspect 3: Irrelevant Attributes

A third aspect is often confused with the attribute which is actually independent. In order to rules describing some occurring phenomena, in many domains gout of data where single entities are described with many related attributes. Among the features of a high dimensional data set, for any query point, attribute can be expected to be irrelevant to describing object. Irrelevant attributes can interfere with the performance of similarity queries for that object. The relevance of certain attributes may differ for different groups of objects within the same data set. Thus, groups of data are defined by some of the available attributes only, many irrelevant attributes may interfere with the efforts to find these groups. Irrelevant attributes are often referred to as "noise."

# Distance Concentration, Clustering, and Relevance of Attributes

There is an important interplay between Aspect 3 and Aspect 2, as many irrelevant attributes (noisy features) can obfuscate the separation of distributions in the high-dimensional space. This has been studied in detail Hou et al.[78]. Additional relevant attributes, i.e., attributes that carry information regarding the separation of different distributions are actually helpful rather than prone to obstacles. In that case, more dimensions will actually make the clustering easier instead of more difficult!

# 16.1 Introduction

With advancement of recent technologies, a vast amount of biological data is being generated. As data banks their size, one of the current challenges in biology is to be able to infer some of the critical functions from complex data. To analyze biological systems, researchers widely aim to identify patterns that co-occur in the group of groups. Clustering is an exploratory technique that discovers patterns from data and, hence, it has become an indispensable tool for various knowledge discovery in the field of computational biology. Clustering is a powerful and widely used technique that organizes and elucidates the structure of biological data. Clustering data from a wide variety of biological experiments has proven to be useful at deriving a variety of insights, such as the shared regulation or function of genes.

In analyzing this complex data, one can observe that the activities of genes are not independent of each other. It has been shown that genes with the same function (or genes involved in the same biological process) are likely to be co-expressed [56]. Hence, it is important to study groups of genes rather than perform a single gene analysis. In other words, it is crucial to identify subsets of genes yields crucial information about the biological processes and the cellular functions. Clustering the gene expression profiles provides insights into gene function, gene regulation, and cellular processes.

It has also been shown that proteins of known functions tend to cluster [92]. The network distance is correlated with functional distance, and the proteins that are closer to another have similar biological functions [96]. Hence, clustering the protein-protein interaction network is crucial in discovering the functions of proteins and understanding the inner workings of cells [84]. The most important building blocks of living organisms, such as DNA, RNA, mRNA, polypeptides and peptides have linear structure and can be represented as sequences. Clustering biological sequence data aims to group together biological sequences that are related. Identified clusters can help in providing a better understanding of the genome.

This chapter reviews different kinds of biological data where clustering has providing promising and meaningful results. Specifically, we will discuss the role of clustering for gene expression data, biological networks, and sequence data. For each type of data, the challenges and the most prominent clustering algorithms that have been successfully studied will be described. The rest of this chapter is organized as follows.

# 8.10 Further Reading

The family of spectral clustering has become popular especially since the works in [37] and [43]. Since then, several works have been proposed to slightly change the original framework and achieve better clustering results. In [59], the final k-means steps on the eigenvectors shared by the family of spectral clustering is explained not to be optimal. The authors clarify the role of eigenvectors obtained from graph Laplacian as a generator of all optimal solutions through orthogonal transforms. A better solution is found by rotating normalized eigenvectors to obtain an SVD to recover the rotation. In [60], it is pointed out that this method can easily get stuck in local minima and does not reliably find the optimal. To find the alignment for a set of vectors, the authors adopt a new function which measures the alignment of a set of vectors and adopt a gradient descent scheme which probably converges. However, this paper also mentions that alignments might only be effective when the data is not highly noisy. Another insight of this paper concerns the construction of adjacency matrix W. Here $i$ is called a local set and is set to be the distance between $x_i$ and its certain neighbor (7th neighbor in graph). The intuition behind this is that data resides in multiple scales (one cluster is dense and the other is sparse) and local scaling automatically finds the scales and affinities within clusters and low affinities across clusters. A more natural parameterization of the neighborhood density based on the empty regions graphs is proposed in [9] which is claimed to be sensitive to parameters and perturbation.

Many papers have also been published on the structural relations of spectral clustering to other algorithms. As discussed in Section 8.8, there cross links among spectral clustering, k-means, and nonnegative matrix factorization. Basis, these relations between spectral clustering and kernel component analysis (KPCA) are built on the fact that the Laplacian eigenmap can be interpreted as KPCA with kernel matrix set to the Moore-Penrose inverse of L. From [20] show that Laplacian eigenmap can be essentially viewed as a nonlinear dimensionality reduction method which is almost the same as spectral clustering for the final k-means step.

Spectral clustering are extended to many nonresident data types compared to those introduced at the beginning of this chapter. In [11], a co-clustering algorithm is proposed to partition bipartite graph which is kind of graph that can be divided into two sets such that no two points in the same set are connected, e.g., document-graph. [28] was published to address the clustering problem through collective factorization for heterogeneous data types which is more complicated and general than the bipartite graph. For network data where only nodes are connected, designed for optimizing “modularity” instead of “cut measure because the former is more popular in the community. Another interesting extension to incorporate is temporal information. Chi et al. [77] present frameworks in evolutionary spectral clustering by regularizing one additional cost on temporal smoothness. If the data points are observed in a series-like way, [31] has proposed to model the clustering without evaluating the future affinity matrix. In [38], a more general algorithm allowing similarity changes between existing data points has been developed by incrementally updating the eigenvectors.

Besides all these, in the machine learning community, spectral clustering techniques typically appear with other learning tasks. For example, in the semsupervised scenario, pairwise constraints, i.e., must-link and cannot-link, are incorporated into spectral clustering as the guidance for the partition [29].

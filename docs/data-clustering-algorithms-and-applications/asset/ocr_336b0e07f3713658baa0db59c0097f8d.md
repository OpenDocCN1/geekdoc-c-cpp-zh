# 15.4.2 Multivariate Distance Measures

A metric distance function, ERP, is proposed to support local time shifting in time series and trajectory data. A second distance function, EDR, is proposed to measure the similarity between time series or trajectories with local time shifting and noise [10].

In the one-dimensional case, multidimensional $L_p$-norm has been proven to be very sensitive to noise and to time shifting [331]. A variety of methods have been described in this section, either use the Euclidean distance or expand it, in order to define new distance measures. Let use the use Euclidean distance to compare multidimensional time series [19]. They define the distance between two multivariate sequences of equal length as the Euclidean distance among all corresponding points in the sequence. For the case where the compared sequences are not of equal length, they stretch the shorter one over the longer one, and the overall distance is defined as the minimum of all mean distances, computed described above. Lin and Su use the Euclidean distance in order to define the distance from a point $p$ to a trajectory $T$ as follows: $D_{point}(p,T)=\min_{q}ED(p,q)$ where $ED(p,q)$ represents the Euclidean distance between points $p$ and $q$. $D_{point}$ is used to define the one-way distance (OWD) from one trajectory $T_1$ to another trajectory $T_2$ as the integral of the distance from $T_1$ to $T_2$, given by the integral. OWD is not symmetric, so the distance between trajectories $T_1$ and $T_2$ is the average of their one-way distances: $D(T_1, T_2) = \frac{1}{2}(OWD(T_1, T_2) + OWD(T_2, T_1))$.

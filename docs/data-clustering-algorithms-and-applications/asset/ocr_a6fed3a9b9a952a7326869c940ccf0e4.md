# 13.5 Clustering Short Documents

Clustering short documents faces additional challenges above those of general purpose document clustering. Short documents normally address a single topic, yet they may do so with completely orthogonal vocabulary. Nucleus, contracted forms of words, and slang are prevalent in short texts. In this section, we will first discuss general methods for clustering short documents and focus on methods designed specifically for clustering Web documents and microblogs.

# 13.5.1 General Methods for Short Document Clustering

There has been a relatively large corpus of online approaches to the clustering of short texts. Wang et al. [111] propose a frequent-based parallel clustering algorithm specifically designed to handle large collections of short texts. The algorithm uses an information-inference mechanism to build a semantic text feature graph which by a k-NN-classification method to control the degree of cluster overlapping. Pepinto et al. [81] resort to information-theory field and define a symmetric KL divergence to control short documents for clustering purposes. Since the KL distance computation relies on the estimation of probabilities using term frequency, a special type of back-off scheme is implemented to avoid the issue of zero frequency due to the sparsity of text. Carullo et al. [18] describe an incremental clustering algorithm that utilizes a generalized Dice coefficient as a document similarity measure. The algorithm requires two thresholds: one to control the minimum accepted similarity and another to define the maximum similarity of a document that can still cluster to a definition.

Particle-swarm optimization techniques and bio-inspired clustering algorithms have also been proposed for short text data. Ingaramo et al. [52] develop a partitioning algorithm to handle short texts of arbitrary size. The key aspect of that is the adaptation of the AntTree algorithm [42], which integrates the "attraction of a cluster" and the Silhouette Coefficient concepts to detecting clusters. Each ant represents a single data object as it moves in the clustering structure according to its similarity to other already connected to the tree structure. Starting from an artificial support, all ants are incrementally connected, either to that support or to already connected ants. This process continues until all ants are connected to the structure, i.e., all objects are clustered.

Finding terms. In [78], Ni regard the document clustering task of grouping short texts based on some "core terms". The underlying idea is to recursively bisect one of the clusters according to the core identified within that. The core of a cluster is the term that minimizes the value of the Rand-Min Cut (RMCut) criterion over all possible bisections. Using a stub strategy TermCut, a bisection of a specific cluster is identified for each of the terms within the cluster. All documents containing the selected term are assigned to one subcluster and the rest of the documents (not containing the term) are assigned to the other.

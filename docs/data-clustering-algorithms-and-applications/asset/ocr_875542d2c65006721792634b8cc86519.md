# 16.3 Clustering Biological Networks

Proteins control the functions of the cell [52]. Understanding these functions requires not only the proteins but also studying their interactions [8]. Protein interactions are essential elements of all the biological processes [16]. Pairwise protein interactions have been identified and validated using recent technologies. These interactions have been obtained by different methods such as spectrometry, two-hybrid methods, and genetic studies. The whole network of protein-protein interaction for a general genome describes the interact of organism. The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network is represented as a graph in which the nodes represent the protein and the edges represent the interactions between the corresponding proteins [84].

It has shown that proteins of known functions tend to cluster together [92]. The network distance correlated with functional disorders indicates that proteins are closer to one another to have similar biological function [96]. Therefore, studying the PPI networks is crucial in discovering the functions of proteins and understanding inner works of cells [84]. The PPI network can be used to predict the unknown functional categories of proteins.

# 16.3.1 Characteristics of PPI Network Data

We will first describe some of the key characteristics of PPI networks [8]:

+   1. *Scale-free* [52]: PPI networks connect hub proteins with many interactions. In other words, most of the proteins in the network have few interactions, and only a few have a lot of interactions with other proteins. Applying existing clustering techniques on these networks would produce a few giant clusters (containing the hub nodes) and the remaining clusters would be very small. Hence, the clustering process should be adapted to produce better results in the size of the clusters.

+   2. *Disassociativity*: In many forms of scale-networks (such as social), highly connected nodes are strongly connected with each other. This property is known as *assortativity*. However, in protein interaction networks is not the case. Hubs are not directly linked to each other causing the disassociativity in networks throughout the average length are relatively short compared to others.

+   3. *Multunctionality*: The same protein can be involved in several biological processes [105]. Due to these overlapping structures, it becomes difficult to extract groups of proteins in such a way that a single protein is present in multiple groups.

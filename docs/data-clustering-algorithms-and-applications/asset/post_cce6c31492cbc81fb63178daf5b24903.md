One of the major issues with community discovery is the availability of content information. Some approaches use a combination of content and link structure for clustering purposes [142]. There has also been a focus on clustering images with the images of associated text from the web page [23] but these approaches do not use linkage for clustering. Qi et al. [137] focused on joining clustering media, textual context objects, and users in social media networks. The authors propose a Heterogeneous Random Field (HRF) model to model structure and content of social media networks and determine clusters. This work introduces an energy function on edges and shows that the most probable clusters on the graph are found by minimizing the energy. Advantage of this algorithm is that it can detect noisy links which are fairly commonplace in real networks.

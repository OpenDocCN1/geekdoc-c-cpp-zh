The task of outlier detection is quite closely related to that of clustering, since points in a cluster can be considered normal cases and points not assigned to any cluster can be considered outliers. However, clustering algorithms treat outliers only as by-products and do not focus on their discovery. Knorr and Ng [23] introduce the notion of distance-based outliers, i.e., points for which at least percent of the database points have a distance larger than D. It is then that definition generalizes the standard definition of outliers from a Gaussian distribution. We would like to point out that the concept of distance-based outliers can also be understood as density-based: points are considered outliers if their neighborhood of radius D does not contain a fraction of p percent of all database points. Distance-based outliers are global in the sense that the number of points that should be further than D is a fraction of the database cardinality. # ## 5.9 Conclusion

The paradigm of density-based clustering has been proposed to address the requirements of (1) discovery of arbitrary shape clusters, (2) scalability to large databases, and (3) the ability to detect and remove noise and outliers. Density-based clusters are connected, dense areas in the data separated from each other by sparser areas. Furthermore, the density within the areas of noise is assumed to be lower than the density in any of the clusters. Due to their local nature, connected areas in the data space can have arbitrary shape. Given an index structure that supports region queries, density-based clusters can be efficiently computed by performing at most one region query per data point. Areas in the data space are treated as noise and are not assigned to any cluster. Different from algorithms that optimize a objective function, the number of clusters does not need to be specified by the user.

In this paper, we have reviewed the major concepts and algorithms for density-based clustering. In particular, we have discussed the key aspects of density estimation, connectivity definition, and data structures for efficient implementation. We have also covered advanced density-based approaches to subspace clustering and the clustering of network data as well as the clustering of data streams and uncertain data. Density-based clustering has been successfully applied in many practical applications. The density-based clustering algorithms DBSCAN and OPTICS have been implemented in WEKA [15], the leading public domain data mining toolkit.

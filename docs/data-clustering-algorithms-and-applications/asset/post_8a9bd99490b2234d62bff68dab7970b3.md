## 2.2.3.2 High-Dimensional Projected Stream Clustering (HPStream)

Aggrawal et al. in [2] propose a data streams clustering technique that involves a feature selection step. This method is called HPStream. Projected clustering is a subset of data points $P$ with a subset of features $F$ such that the points in $P$ are closely clustered with respect to $F$.

Since quality in data streams may decay, the stream clustering method should assign a greater level of importance to recent data points. Motivated from this belief, a fading data structure was proposed in [2] to keep the clustering contemporary. $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ are multi-dimensional samples arriving in times $T_1, T_2, \dots, T_n$, each sample $X_i$ contains $m$ dimensions $(x_i^1, x_i^2, \dots, x_i^m)$. The fading $f(t)$ for each data point will be according to the arrival time $t$. The fading function is assumed to be monotonically decreasing with respect to the time $t$.

## Algorithms for Linked Data

Linked data has become ubiquitous in applications such as Twitter, Facebook, biological networks. Typically linked data have the following characteristics: (1) high-dimensional such as tweets; (2) linked, providing an important source of information; and (3) unlabeled due to large size. Properties pose challenges to the clustering task. In this section, we first introduce the challenges and opportunities of linked data, then present an unsupervised feature selection framework for linked data, and finally follow with a discussion.

### 2.2.4.1 Challenges and Opportunities

Linked instances are related to each other via different types of links (e.g., hyperlinks, friendships, interactions). Thus linked data is distinct from traditional attribute value ("flat") data. Figure 2.4(a) illustrates a typical example of linked data and its representations. Figure 2.4(b) shows 8 linked instances (u₁ to u₈), while Figure 2.4(c) is a conventional representation of attribute: rows are instances and columns. As mentioned above, besides, linked data provide extra information. Linked data is not independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), which is among the most enduring and deeply buried assumptions of traditional machine learning. Due to the absence of class labels that guide the search for relation information, unsupervised feature selection is particularly difficult. The linked property further exacerbates the difficulty of unsupervised feature selection for linked data. On the other hand, linked data provides opportunities to advance research. For example, linked data allows collective inference, inferring interrelated values simultaneously, and enables clustering, finding a more accurate pattern. Many linked-related learning tasks are proposed as collective classification and relational clustering, but the task of feature selection for linked data is rarely touched due to its unique challenges for feature selection: (1) how to exploit relations among data instances and (2) how to take advantage of these relations for feature selection. Until recently, feature selection for linked data has attracted attention. In [63], a unsupervised feature selection algorithm, LinkedFS, is proposed for linked data in social media.

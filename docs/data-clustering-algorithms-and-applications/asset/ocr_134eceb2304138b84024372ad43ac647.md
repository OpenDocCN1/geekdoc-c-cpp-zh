The generative process of LDA can also be described in a graphical model representation as shown in Figure 3.7. The observed variables are the words of the documents, and the hidden variables are the topics of documents, topic assignments of words within documents, and topics. The above generative process defines a joint probabilistic distribution over both the observed and hidden random variables. With the joint distribution, we compute the posterior distribution of the hidden variables given the observed variables.

As illustrated in Figure 3.7, there are three levels to the LDA representation. The parameters $\alpha$, $\eta$, and $eta$ are collection-level parameters, assumed to sampled once in the process of generating a collection. The variables $z_{nj}$ and $w_{nj}$ are word-level variables, and $	heta_n$ are document-level variables, sampled per document. To estimate the parameters of LDA, there are two types of approximate algorithms, variational algorithms and sampling-based algorithms. Variational methods are used as approximation methods in a wide variety of settings [39, 78]. The application of variational methods converts the original complex problem into a simplified problem in which inference is feasible. The simplified model is generally characterized by introducing additional parameters and ignoring some troublesome models. A good description of variational inference algorithm for LDA is presented in [14].

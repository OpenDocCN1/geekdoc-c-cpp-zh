
# Spectral Clustering

Multi-view learning is another hot topic where complementary representations of the data are presented. In [62], the clustering approach is generalized to multiple views via a random walk formulation. The work in [23] approaches the problem by adopting a co-training framework such that the similarity in one view is affected by the similarity estimated on the eigenvectors of Laplacian in the other view. In [24], a co-regularization framework is proposed to enforce the pairwise similarities computed from the eigenvectors from different views to be close with each. Opposite to multi-view algorithms that learn the latent consensus from multiple representations, the work in [39] tries to find multiple nonredundant clustering results from a single data view through penalizing for different results. There are also extensions of spectral clustering algorithms in the field of active learning. The work in [54] formulates this problem as incrementally querying the oracle about relations mentioned between chosen data points. In [55], the “active” part lies in querying the pairwise similarity under the assumption that the adjacency matrix W is expensive to obtain or compute.

In the few years, many papers have been published combinations of spectral clustering with different data types, learning tasks, and applications. Interested readers are encouraged to further explore the literature in these directions.

The reason *I* does not choose the right cluster number is not easy to explain. One can observe that *I* increases as cluster number *NC* increases. One possible reason is the uniform effect of the *K-means* algorithm, which tends to divide objects into relatively equal sizes [64]. *I* measures compactness based on the sum of distances between objects and their cluster center. When *NC* is small, objects with high density are likely in the same cluster, which makes the sum of distances remain almost the same. Since most of the objects are in one cluster, the total sum will not change too much. Therefore, as *NC* increases, *I* will decrease since *NC* is in the denominator.

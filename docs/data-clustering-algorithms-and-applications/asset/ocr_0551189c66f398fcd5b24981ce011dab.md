The problem of uncertainty has been studied in the traditional database literature [14, 43], though
the issue has seen a revival in recent years [3, 5, 15, 19, 22, 40, 49, 51, 52]. The driving force
behind this revival is the evolution of new hardware techniques such as sensors which
collect the data in a completely accurate way. In many cases, it becomes increasingly
possible to collect the uncertainty along the underlying data values. Many data mining and
management techniques need to be carefully redesigned in order to work effectively with uncertain
data. This is because the uncertainty in the data can change the results in a way, so that
deterministic algorithms may often create misleading results [3]. While the raw values of the data
always be used in conjunction with uncertain data algorithms, the uncertainty provides additional
insights which are not otherwise available. A survey of recent techniques for uncertain data mining
be found in [10].

The problem of clustering is a well-known and important one in the data mining and manage-\communities. The problem has been widely explored in the context of deterministic data.
Details of a variety of clustering algorithms may be found in [38, 34]. The clustering has
been studied in the traditional database literature [28, 47] because of its applications to a
variety of customer segmentation and data mining problems.

Uncertainty modeling is very relevant in the context of a number of different clustering appications. An example is illustrated in [42] where uncertainty was incorporated into the clustering
process in the context of sales merchandising application. Since the problem of data clustering is
closely related to that of classification, the methods for uncertain data clustering can also be used to
en algorithms for other related data mining problems like outlier detection [9] and\iclass [3]. This is because clustering is a general-purpose summarization tool, which
be used in the context of a wide variety of problems.

The presence of uncertainty significantly affects the behavior of the underlying clusters because
the presence of uncertainty along a particular attribute may affect the expected distance between the
data point and particular attribute. In real applications, there is considerable we in the
uncertainty behavior across different attributes. The incorporation of uncertainty into the clustering
behavior can significantly affect the quality of the results.

The problem of data clustering is often confused with that of fuzzy clustering [50].
In case of uncertain data clustering, the uncertainty belongs to the representation of the source
objects which are clustering, and the actual clustering model may be probabilistic or
deterministic. In case of fuzzy clustering [50], the source objects are typically deterministic, and
the membership of objects to clusters is probabilistic. In other words, each object has a degree of
belonging to the different clusters, which is "fuzzy" or probabilistic in nature.

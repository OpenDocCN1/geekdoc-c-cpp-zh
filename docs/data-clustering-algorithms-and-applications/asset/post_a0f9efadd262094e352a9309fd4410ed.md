## 16.2.7 Cluster Validation

All the clustering algorithms that are described in this section will yield groups of co-expressed genes or groups of samples with a common phenomenon [22]. Reliability of the clusters, which measures the probability that the clusters are not formed by chance, is a commonly used metric for validating the results of these clustering algorithms. To compute the p-values of a cluster, typically the genes from a given cluster are mapped to the functional categories annotated in databases such as Martinsried Index of Protein Sequences (MIPS) or Gene Ontology (GO) [87]. Typically, a hypergeometric distribution is used to calculate the probability of having at least $k$ genes from a cluster of size $n$ genes by chance in a biological process containing $f$ genes from a total size of $N$ genes:

$$P=1-\sum_{i=k}^{n}\frac{\binom{f}{i}}{\binom{N}{i}}$$

This test measures if a gene cluster is enriched with genes from a particular functional category to a greater than what would be expected by chance. The range of p-values is from 0 to 1. Lower p-values indicate biological significance of the clusters.

Another popular metric for evaluating the goodness of the clusters is the Figure of Merit (FOM) which was originally proposed in [119] to estimate the predictive power of clustering algorithms. The FOM measures the mean deviation of the expression levels of genes in a particular condition relative to their corresponding cluster mean. Thus, a small value of FOM indicates high predictive ability of the resulting clusters.

## 16.3 Clustering Biological Networks

Proteins control the functions of the cell [52]. Understanding these functions requires not only the proteins but also studying their interactions [8]. Protein interactions are essential elements of all the biological processes [16]. Pairwise protein interactions have been identified and validated using recent technologies. These interactions have been obtained by different methods such as spectrometry, two-hybrid methods, and genetic studies. The whole network of protein-protein interaction for a general genome describes the interact of organism. The protein-protein interaction (PPI) network is represented as a graph in which the nodes represent the protein and the edges represent the interactions between the corresponding proteins [84].

It has shown that proteins of known functions tend to cluster together [92]. The network distance correlated with functional disorders indicates that proteins are closer to one another to have similar biological function [96]. Therefore, studying the PPI networks is crucial in discovering the functions of proteins and understanding inner works of cells [84]. The PPI network can be used to predict the unknown functional categories of proteins.

### 16.3.1 Characteristics of PPI Network Data

We will first describe some of the key characteristics of PPI networks [8]:

- 1. *Scale-free* [52]: PPI networks connect hub proteins with many interactions. In other words, most of nodes have few interactions.
- 2. *Disassociativity*: The algorithm aims to produce clusters where nodes are connected.
- 3. *Multunctionality*: The clustering technique identifies nodes that are part of multiple groups.

## 16.3.2 Network Clustering Algorithms

Several clustering algorithms have been proposed in this section.

### 16.3.2.1 Molecular Detection (MCODE)

Molecular Detection (MCODE) algorithm was one of the first methods proposed. The algorithm first identifies dense regions in the network based on the density. The final weight for any node is the product of the clustering coefficient and the k-core of its neighborhood. In the next step, MCODE algorithm selects the highest weighted node and recursively moves outward from this seed node. This process is repeated until no more nodes are added to the complex.

### 16.3.2.2 Markov Clustering

Markov clustering (MCL) algorithm is one of the widely used. It has been applied to yeast and human PPI networks and the generated clusters accurately. MCL is an iterative algorithm with two main stages:

- Expansion: This step spreads the flow of a node to potentially new nodes and enhances flow in dense regions.
- Inflation: This step strengthens the matrix and weakens the intercluster flow to obtain the natural clusters.

The two steps are repeated until convergence. The algorithm is said to converge when we obtain a doubly idempotent matrix.

### 16.3.2.3 Neighborhood Search Methods

The Restricted Neighborhood Search Clustering (RNSC) algorithm was proposed in [63] to consider PPI data of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, Drosophila melanogaster, and Caenorhabditis elegans and predict protein complexes. This algorithm optimizes a cost-based local search algorithm loosely on the heuristic. Clustering of a graph begins with initial random clustering and a cost function.

### 16.3.2.4 Clique Percolation Method

In order to extract the overlapping structures from complex networks, Palla et al. [79] proposed the Clique Percolation Method (CPM). CPM has the ability to generate the overlapping clustering by identifying the k-percolation communities. A software package CFinder [1] implements this method and is currently being used even in other application domains such as social networks [78].

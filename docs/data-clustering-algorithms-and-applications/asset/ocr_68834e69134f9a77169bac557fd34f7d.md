# 17.4 Common Evaluation Criteria

In this section, we give an overview of commonly used performance evaluation criteria for clustering algorithms. A variety of measures has been proposed in the literature to capture the goodness of a partition of the graph [131].

The simplest function to measure the quality of a partition is “cut” and the most direct way to construct the partition is to solve the mincut problem [105]. Given the number k of partitions, the approach chooses a partition $C_1, \dots, C_k$ that minimizes

```
$cut(C_1, C_2, \dots, C_k) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k W(C_i, \bar{C}_i)$
```

For k=2 min is relatively simple and an easy problem to solve [158]. But the partitions are not often satisfactory. More specifically, the solution tends to separate one individual vertex from the rest of the graph which is not expected [105]. In partitioning, each group should be large enough. There are two most popular objective functions to include the aspect of a partition.

RatioCut, proposed in [74], of a group of vertices C is the sum of the weights connecting C to the rest of the graph normalized by its size of C. It is denoted as

```
$RatioCut(C_1, \dots, C_k) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{W(C_i, \bar{C}_i)}{|C_i|}$
```

Probably the most popular quality for graph partitioning is normalized [151, 114]. The normalized cut of a group of vertices C is the sum of the weights of the edges connecting C to the rest of the graph normalized by total edge weight of C and total edge weight of the rest of the vertices in the graph. Mathematically, the normalized cut of a partition of the graph $C \mathcal{V}'$ is denoted as

```
$Ncut(C_1, \dots, C_k) = \sum_{i=1}^k \frac{W(C_i, \bar{C}_i)}{d(C_i)}$
```

where $d(C)$ is the total degree of cluster C
The intuition in both RatioCut and Normalized Cut is to make sure partitions are “balanced” [105]. Groups with low normalized represent good communities because they are well connected among themselves and sparsely connected to the rest of the graph [131].nThe conductance is defined in [83] and is closely related. It is defined as

```
$Conductance(C) = \frac{\sum_{i \in C} W(i,j)}{\min(d(C), d(\bar{C}))}$
```

The normalized cut or conductance of a partition of the graph into k clusters $C_1, \dots, C_k$ is the sum of the normalized cut or conductance of the individual partitions $C_i$ for $i=1, \dots, k$.
Another popular measure of partitioning quality is the Modularity [125]. This measure is the sum of the differences, for each cluster, between fraction of internal edges and fraction of edges that are expected to be inside a random cluster with the same total degree. Mathematically,

```
$Q = \sum_{i=1}^k [frac{W(C_i, \bar{C}_i)}{e} -(\frac{d(C_i)}{e})^2]$
```

where $C_i$ are clusters, $e$ is number of edges, and $d(C_i)$ represents the total degree of cluster C. Unfortunately, optimizing any of these “normalized” objective functions is NP-hard [64, 151].

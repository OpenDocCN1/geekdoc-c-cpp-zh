For the embedding methods, the quality of semisvised label propagation depends on how well the unsupervised embedding preserves the cluster structure. Figure 20 illustrates the difference between unsupervised embedding and supervised embedding on Wine. Note that the unsupervised embedding obtained by LLE and LTSA maintains the cluster separation better than LE for this particular dataset. When supervision is added, the embedding obtained from all the methods changes slightly. The cluster separation is most in LLEP followed by LTSALP and LELP, a fact reflected in the test-set error rates in Figure 25. In general, SSC in graph-based representations with a supervised embedding method works well if the geometric structure of the cluster is well aligned with the biases of the embedding method.

# 20.7 Conclusions

The literature on semisvised clustering can be broadly divided into two families, based on whether one considers feature-based or graph-based representation of the input data. The methods for feature-based representations generally generalize corresponding centroid-based clustering methods, such as k-means and variants, to incorporate constraints or penalty functions associated with the supervision. They also consider metric learning under supervision and can be viewed as inference and parameter estimation in certain probabilistic graphical models. For graph-based representations, semisvised clustering can be posed as a suitable semisvised graph-cut problem. We developed a unified perspective to a variety of seemingly disparate methods for graph-based semisvised learning and illustrate that semisvised graph-cut problems can be viewed as a special case of such problems, which are often solved using label propagation.

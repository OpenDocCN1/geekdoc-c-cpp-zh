From the generation of $G$, only objects within clusters will be counted in the equation. As a result, if the data set is divided into two equal clusters, each cluster will have $n/2$ objects, and $n^2/4$ pairs of distances will be counted. If the data set is divided into three equal clusters, each cluster will have $n/3$ objects, and $n^2/3$ pairs of distances will be counted. Therefore, with the increasing of the cluster number $NC$, more pairs of distances are counted, which makes $\Gamma$ increase.

Looking further into these issues, one can discover that they either take separation, or compactness into account. ($RS$ and $\Gamma$ consider only separation, and $RMSSTD$ considers only compactness). As the property of monotonicity, the curves of $RMSSTD$, $RS$, and $\Gamma$ will be either upward or downward. It is claimed that the optimal cluster number is reached at the shift point of the curves, which since the shift point is very subjective and hard to determine, Here measures are excluded from future studies. The focus will be on the remaining 9 measures.

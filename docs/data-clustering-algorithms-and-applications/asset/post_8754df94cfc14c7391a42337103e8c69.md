Semisuvised clustering (SSC) has become an important part of data mining. With an ever increasing volume of data in several domains, it is more important than ever to leverage known information and observed relationships among data points to guide clustering.

Clustering methods are broadly divided into two groups depending on the data representation they use: feature-based, where each data point has representation in terms of a feature vector or a structured representation such as sequence, time series, or graphs, and graph-based, where a similarity graph among data points is given. Methods such as k-means and mixture of Gaussians work with feature-based representations, whereas spectral clustering methods with graph-based representations.

Existing works on SSC are broadly divided into two groups, depending on whether one adds semisuvision to a feature-based or a graph-based clustering algorithm. Much of early work in SSC focused on extending feature-based clustering methods to the semisuvised setting. In particular, the literature focused on two types of semisuvision: pointwise [2], where the cluster labels of a small number of points are available to guide clustering, and pairwise [37, 3, 4, 20], where “must-link” and “cannot-cut” constraints between pairs of points are available. Methods have been extensively studied over the past decade [6], with emphasis on generalizing feature clustering algorithms such as k-means and its variants to leverage the semisuvision. These methods have been generalized to incorporate metric learning in the context of SSC and also for parameter estimation and inference in suitable graphical models [9, 3, 5, 4]. We discuss this family of SSC in Section 20.2.

There are several SSC approaches based on the graph-representation and graph-based clustering methods. The literature on graph-based SSC has primarily focused on pointwise supervision. Spectral clustering methods are widely used for unsupervised clustering with graph-based representations [36, 17] and can be viewed as solving a relaxation of suitable graph-cut problems. In the unsupervised setting, one can consider the problem as one of semisuvised graphcuts, where the labels of points with the same cluster label are expected to be the same cut. We illustrate that the relaxed graphcut problems can be solved by suitable semisuvised spectral clustering methods, which in turn are intimately related to label propagation methods [15].

We also present a generalized label propagation (GLP) framework which includes a variety of graph-based semisuvised learning methods developed over the past decade, and includes the semisuvised spectral clustering methods as special cases. Further the same framework includes semisuvised nonlinear embedding methods as special cases. Based on the unified framework, we provide a generic recipe for converting embedding methods to semisuvised label propagation methods.

It is important to note that for semisuvision, where on individual points are available, the semisuvised clustering and classification problems are closely related.

A different way of using the temporal evolution of text documents in the clustering process is described in [38]. Specifically, the work uses bursts features as markers of new topic occurrences in the data stream. This because the semantics of an up-and-coming topic are often reflected in the frequent presence of a few distinctive words in the text. At a given time, the nature of relevant elements could lead to specific features of the data stream. Clearly, such features are extremely important from a clustering perspective. Therefore, the method discussed in [38] uses a new representation, which is referred to as the bursts representation for mining text strings. a time-varying weight is associated with the feature depending upon it. Thus it is important to remember that a particular document depends upon the particular instant in time when it is constructed.

Another issue which handled effectively in this approach is an implicit reduction in dimensionality of the underlying color. Text is inherently a high-dimensional domain, and the preselection of features on the basis of their bursts can be a natural way to reduce the dimensionality of document representation. This can help in both the effectiveness and efficiency of the underlying algorithm.

The first step is to identify the burst features in the data stream. In order to achieve this goal, the approach uses Kleinberg's 2-finite automaton model [45]. Once these features have been identified, the features are associated with weights which depend upon their level of burstiness. Subsequently, a burst representation is defined in order to reflect the weighting of the feature. The identification and the weight of the burst feature are dependent upon their use in representation. A standard $k$-means approach is applied to the new representation in order constructing the cluster.

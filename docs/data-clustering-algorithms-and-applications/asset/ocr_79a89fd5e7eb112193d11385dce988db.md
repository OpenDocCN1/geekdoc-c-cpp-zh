# 23.2.3.3 The Effect of Normalization

The importance of measure normalization is presented in this section. Along this line, K-means clustering is first applied on the simulated data sets with $\sigma^2 = 5$ and the sampled data sets from hītech. Then, unnormalized and normalized measures are used for cluster validation. Finally, the rank correlation between DCV and the measures are computed and the results are shown in Table 23.7.

As can be seen in, if the unnormalized measures are used to cluster validation, only three measures, namely R, G, $I'$, show strong consistency with DCV on both groups of data sets. $VI$ and $MD$ show strong conflict with DCV on the sampled data sets, since their values are all close to $-1$ on simulated data. In addition, note that $F$, $\epsilon$, and FM show correlation with DCV.

Table 23.7 shows the correlations between DCV and the normalized measures. As seen, the measures show perfect consistency with DCV for $F_n$ and $e_n$. This indicates that normalization is crucial for $K$-means clustering. The proposed bounds for the measures are tight enough to capture the uniform effect in the clustering results.

In Table 23.7, it can be observed that $F_n$ and $e_n$ are not consistent with DCV. This indicates that normalization does not help $F$ and $\epsilon$ too much. The reason is that the proposed lower bound for $F$ and upper bound for $\epsilon$ are not very tight.

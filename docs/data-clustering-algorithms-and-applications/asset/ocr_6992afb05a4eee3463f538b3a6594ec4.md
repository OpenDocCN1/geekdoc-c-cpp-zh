A different extension of the ThemeRiver metaphor for document clustering is TextFlow [11]. Instead of squeezing as many words as possible into the layers representing clusters, the user can interactively pick a bunch of keywords. The temporal relations between a keyword and the clusters/topics are shown as topic threads (polygon lines) that are drawn on top of the layers. In case two keywords co-occur in a certain cluster/topic, a wave bundle is drawn that weaves the threads of the keywords. The amplitude of the wave bundle encodes occurrences of the involved keywords.

Furthermore, so-called critical events are shown by special icons. These icons show sources, sinks, splits, and merges. A source indicates the appearance of a new cluster/topic and is shown as filled circle. A sink marks the death of a cluster/topic and is shown as donut. Splits and merges are shown as the letter Y rotated to the left and right, respectively.

Last, TextFlow expands the layout of the stacked graphs to include secondary branches and as proposed in the ThemeRiver approach connects a cluster/topic present at a certain time point with the best matching cluster/topic of the next time point. The TextFlow approach also computes and connects to the best matching clusters/topics. This extension works for dynamic cluster/topic models that construct temporal relations as a post inference process that links between clusters and forth time are computed after the clusters have already found at the individual time points. The layout with secondary branches is computed using force-directed simulations to reduce crossing of branches and to smooth cluster/topic layers.

In summary, all approaches for visualizing document clusters/topics emphasize different aspects of the sense-making process. All visualizations can be combined with interactive methods to dig deeper into the data. Thus, visualization is not the only component in the sense-making process. Other interactive features like semantic zooming, searching relevant documents and so are important as must. There are no user studies, or stories of successful applications available that show combinations with interactive features are really helpful for end users. New evaluation measures are needed to capture usefulness originating in the interplay of visualization search and analytics.

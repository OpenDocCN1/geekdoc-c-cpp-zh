The navigational graph does not necessarily include all two-dimensional projections as nodes. With larger dimensionality it would require $k$ nodes, which would render such navigational graph unless an effective map for the space of projections. In [33] the use of scagnostic measures is suggested to pick two-dimensional projections with interesting data distributions. Scagnostics are briefly introduced by Tukey and Tukey [44]. These ideas are further developed and described more in detail in [49]. In nutshell, a number of $k$ measures are computed for the distributions in the axis-parallel projections of a d-dimensional set to guide the selection of interesting projections. The measures are designed to quantify a wide range of characteristics that appear in two-dimensional distributions. To illustrate the measures, the two-dimensional projections of the mtcars-dataset [22] were quantified by the proposed $k=9$ measures [49]. A subset of produced measurement vectors, each with a dimensionality of one, is shown with the respective distributions in a biplot in Figure 19.2. A biplot [15] reduces the multidimensional vectors to two principal components and plots the reduced vectors together with the projected unit vectors of the original data space.

Remaining values are normalized to make the columns of the matrix sum to 1, and hence, the matrix is a stochastic flow matrix. Starting with the initial matrix, the whole process is iterated until convergence. Expand step flows out to new vertices. This enhances the intracluster flows as there are more paths within the same cluster than between different clusters [150]. Nonlinearity is brought into the process through the Inflate operation that strengthens the intrluster flow and weakens the intercluster flow. The process sets up a positive feedback loop forcing all nodes within a tightly linked group to flow to one attractor node [131]. MCL has two major shortcomings [148]. First, it is slow because it involves a matrix–matrix multiplication. Especially, during the first few iterations when the flow matrix is dense, the Expand step becomes very time consuming. Second, it has a tendency to produce imbalanced clusters, e.g., singleton clusters with few nodes, or to produce one big cluster.

# 17.9.1 Regularized MCL (RMCL): Improvement over MCL

Recently there have been variants of MCL in an effort to address major drawbacks of MCL with respect to scalability and imbalanced clustering. Regularized MCL (RMCL) [148, 150] has succeeded in solving the problem associated with imbalanced clustering, especially the problem relating to producing singleton clusters. Saturi and Parasarathy [148] observed that the reason behind MCL producing too many clusters is the fact that it allows columns of pairs of neighbors in flow matrix M to diverge significantly. This MCL uses uses the adjacency matrix of the input graph only at the start of the algorithm to initialize the flow matrix, which allows MCL to “overfit.” RMCL addresses the problem by regularizing (or smoothing) the flow distributions with respect to neighbors, i.e., by taking into account the neighborhood structure in each Expand step. Essentially it changes the Expand (M := M × M) step of MCL into a Regularize (M := M × MG) step where MG is the canonical transition matrix of the graph. That is,

> MRegularize = M × MG

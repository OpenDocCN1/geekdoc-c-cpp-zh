Common phrases naturally describe clusters. Kummura et al. [65] develop a monolithic clustering algorithm with the goal of automatically generating a hierarchy, where concepts are terms or phrases. At each level of the hierarchy being constructed, the algorithm aggressively identifies topics that the distinctiveness of the monhetic phrases describing the clusters is maximized. Li and Wu [67] first used a phrase-based method of extracting phrases from snippets. The clustering method starts with all extracted phrases belonging to individual clusters and chooses the most clusters according to the constructed index. Each cluster is finally identified by a distinct phrase. The snippets whose phrases belong to the same cluster are grouped together, while the remaining snippets are clustered based on their k-nearest neighbors. Zeng et al. [119] map the clustering problem to a phrase ranking problem, in which a regression model is first trained to rank the n-grams for a specified keyword. The model is then used to extract relevant phrases which the snippets are finally clustered.

# 13.5.4 Clustering Microblogs

The recent popularity of social networks has led to increasing demand for robust clustering algorithms for microblog data, or tweets. General purpose document clustering algorithms do not work well with this data due to the lack of occurrence terms and context in the short "documents". Researchers have tried to solve this by altering existing techniques or creating specialized models. The most promising research direction relies on aligning or augmenting the short texts with external information.

Liu et al [69] rely on an incremental similarity-threshold based clustering to identify similar tweets for the task of semantic role labeling. In a related problem of classifying tweets to a predefined set of generic classes, Sriram et al. [98] compare the bag of words model with others based on short-text specific features such as shortened words or slang, time-event phrases, opinions or user names. In their evaluation, they find that non-bag of words models outperform the bag of words ones. Park et al. [180] propose a hybrid approach that exploits external information from search to deal with the extraction of phrases from a blog. A set of candidate terms with highly-distinct values is initially extracted from all posts of a blog, and then fed to a Web-based search engine. The resulting output for each candidate term is grouped into a hierarchy of clusters, and each of these clusters is compared with the blog posts covering to determine how many topics are covered by the blog.

Topic modeling has also been shown in the microblog domain. Range et al. propose LDA [88], a version of LDA that enables available supervision, and use it Twitter data [87] to characterize content, trends, and recommend users to follow. Wen et al. [113] propose a PageRank-type algorithm for measuring-sensitive influence of microblogs. They use LDA to decode latent topics and compute transition probabilities contingent on the similarity of users. Hong and Davison [48] study how to train topic models on microblog data to be used in standard text mining applications. They find that model based features can be very useful, while the length of the documents can greatly affect the effectiveness of trained topic models. Specifically, aggregating short messages leads to better models.

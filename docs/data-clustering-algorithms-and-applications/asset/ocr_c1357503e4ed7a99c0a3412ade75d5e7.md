In addition to the variations in the types of the busters, there are other important sources of variations, as the variations in the size and the position of the busters in the gene expression data. Though the earlier clustering algorithms used to find only a single bcluster at a time (Figure 16.4(a)), most of the recent approaches attempt to find several busters simultaneously. When there are several busters in the data, some of the standard buster structures are as follows:

+   * Exclusive row and column busters which form rectangular blocks after reordering rows and columns (Figure 16.4(b)).
* Checkerboard pattern busters that are completely nonoverlapping (Figure 16.4(c)).
* Exclusive row busters that might have overlapping columns (Figure 16.4(d)).
* Exclusive column busters that might have overlapping rows (Figure 16.4(e)).

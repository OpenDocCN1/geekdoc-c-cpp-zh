## 4.11 COVARIANCE AND CORRELATION

Both covariance and correlation are related measures that indicate the degree of variation between two sets of random variables, for example a set of hedge fund returns and the market benchmark e.g. S&P 500 Index. Given any pair of random variables, $X_i$ and $X_j$, their covariance is denoted by cov$(X_i, X_j)$ or in matrix form, by $\Sigma_{ij}$. By definition, the covariance is a symmetric matrix i.e. $\Sigma_{ij} = \Sigma_{ji}$. Also, the covariance of any element $X_i$ with itself is the variance, that is:

$cov(X_i, X_j) = var(X_i)$

The covariance matrix $\Sigma$ can be written as:

$$\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix} \Sigma_{11} & \Sigma_{12} & \Sigma_{13} & \dots & \Sigma_{n} \\ \Sigma_{21} & \Sigma_{22} & \Sigma_{23} & \cdot & \cdot \\ \Sigma_{31} & \Sigma_{32} & \Sigma_{33} & \cdot & \cdot \\ \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \ddots & \cdot \\ \Sigma_{n1} & \cdot & \cdot & \cdot & \Sigma_{nn} \end{pmatrix}$$

More formally, the sample covariance of $n$ observations of $k$ variables is the $k \times k$ matrix given by:

$$\Sigma_{jk} = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (X_{ij} - \bar{X}_j)(X_{ik} - \bar{X}_k) \quad (4.18)$$

V1DD Stats::Cov(const V2DD& v)
{
  if(v[0].size() < 3) m_utils.ErrorChk("More than one series required");

  UINT n = v.size();
  double tmp; // Declare a temporary variable

  V1DD mean = Mean(v); // Declare a 1D vector of doubles for the Mean
  V1DD cov; // Declare a 1D vector of doubles for the Cov.

  // Calculate Cov. using Eigen. (3.18)
  for(UINT k=0; k<mean.size(); k++)
  {
    for(UINT j=1; j<v[0].size(); j++)
    {
      tmp = 0.0;
      for(UINT i=0; i<n; i++)
      {
        tmp += ((v[i][j] - mean[j-1]) * (v[i][k] - mean[k]));
      }
      tmp /= (n-1);
      cov.push_back(tmp);
    }
  }

  return cov;
}

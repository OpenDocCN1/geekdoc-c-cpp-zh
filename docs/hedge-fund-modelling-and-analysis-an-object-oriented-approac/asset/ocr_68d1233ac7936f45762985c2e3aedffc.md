As of September 2010, the UCITS hedge fund industry stood at an estimated $131 billion managed by over 600 funds (see Figure 2.7). It is a set of directives developed by the EU member states to improve the financial opportunities offered to UCITS-compliant hedge fund managers while addressing the needs of investors in effective risk management procedures, increased transparency and liquidity, especially in light of the recent financial crisis.

UCITS hedge funds have maintained a strong pattern of growth since 2008. By the end of 2014, with the industry delivering modest returns and also attracting new capital from investors, the industry size was US$263.2 billion managed by a total of 1038 funds. The UCITS growth trend continued in 2015, with its current size at US$275.7 billion, over a population of 1041 funds.

The original version of the Directive was introduced in 1985 with the goal of establishing common legal framework for open-ended funds investing in transferable securities in every EU member state. That is, to develop a pan-European market in collective investment schemes. Unfortunately, the framework suffered from many obstacles, as the extent of different marketing rules and taxation allowed across members. Not until December 2001 was a directive formally adopted under the UCITS B3 banner which has undergone several further amendments with a view to including the use of additional asset classes (e.g. hedge fund indices) and a more diverse range of derivative products. Such inclusions have allowed UCITS funds to pursue a different number of investment possibilities, such as absolute return strategies, in ways that were not possible under previous UCITS frameworks.

The Omega ratio as defined by Keating and Shadwick (2002) is a non-parametric method. it does not rely upon calculating the moments of a distribution of returns for determining the ranking of each hedge fund versus a threshold level. Developed in the early 2000s it gained popularity with practitioners due to its inclusiveness of the full distribution of returns in describing the relative ranking of a fund. As such, it incorporates all moments (and theoretically). As mentioned earlier, investors like high odd moments and low even moments and the risks of holding an asset prone to fourth moment risk are non-negligible. Its popularity also stems from the fact that it inherently describes the risk-reward properties of the return distribution and so can be easily interpreted. The ratio considers returns below and above a specific threshold. In doing so, it places a threshold return on the distribution of returns (the return PDF with an area of one underneath) which has been accumulated by summing (i.e. taking the integral) from the left hand side to form the CDF, F(x). Instead of using the positive and negative signs to signify the limits on either side of the integral, we use a for the downside limit and b for the upside side. The Omega ratio is written as:

```
$$\Omega(L) = \frac{\int_{L}^{b}(1-F(x))dx}{\int_{a}^{L}F(x)dx}$
```

The returns below a certain threshold are considered as losses, and the returns above are considered profits.

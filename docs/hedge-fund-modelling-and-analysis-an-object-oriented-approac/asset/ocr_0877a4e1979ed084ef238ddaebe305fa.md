#  2.3.2 Europe

The rapid growth of the European hedge fund industry over the first seven years of the last decade was eventually slowed by the onset of the financial downturn in 2008. The onset of the global financial crisis, which was followed by the *sovereign debt crisis in the Eurozone era*, dealt a serious blow to the industry with managers large redemptions and heavy performance-based losses. With North American hedge funds, the European sector experienced huge losses and increased pressure for redemptions from investors which continued until early 2009 when the global economy began to see a potential recovery (see Figure 2.5).

However, by the end of 2011, confidence in the European hedge fund industry declined, following a resurgence of debt webers in EU sovereigns as the crisis intensified amid lows as the crisis continued to Put a Damper on investor sentiment. Throughout 2012, industry assets stayed at their lows as the crisis continued to put a damper on investor sentiment. The *European Central Bank’s* (ECB) began to keep interest rates low and the Eurozone’s recovery has restored market confidence to some extent. Economic recovery in the region, European hedge funds have enjoyed a period of sustained growth starting in 2013 and into 2015. The European region has shown some interesting trends with regards to fund launches since the market began rebound in 2009. Although attraction rates have been relatively low, launches have gained strength on the back of the new *UCITS regulation*.¹ The popularity of UCITS III has seen the launch of many new start-ups seeking investment capital in the increasingly competitive hedge fund arena.

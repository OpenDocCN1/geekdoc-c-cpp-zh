In September 2008, Morningstar acquired the Morgan Stanley Capital International (MSCI) Hedge Fund Index family and agreed to use their industry-leading strategies and index construction methodology. The Morningstar MSCI Hedge Fund Indices categorized over 190 indices.

3.2.6.1 MSCI Hedge Fund Classification Standard In July 2002, MSCI launched the MSCI Hedge Fund Classification Standard (HFCS), one of the most comprehensive hedge fund classification models to date. The HFCS uses three Primary Characteristics, namely the hedge fund investment style, asset class and geography in order to classify and define hedge fund strategies. In addition, Secondary Characteristics are defined through the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS), and capitalisation for equity-oriented strategies, and fixed income for credit-oriented strategies (see Table 3.5). The investment process determines the approach managers use to select investments in order to generate returns and manage risk. They are grouped into five broad categories, or investment process groups i.e. Directional Trading, Relative Value, Security Selection, Specialist Credit and Multi-Process. The indices are equal at all four levels of aggregation and asset weighted at the two highest levels as be seen in Table 3.6.

Classification schemes attempt to group hedge fund funds based on their strategy and characteristics; however, meaningful groupings are challenging due to the inherent heterogeneity of hedge fund investing. Such diversification does not allow the development of a single group-based system and any classification is likely to be subject to a large degree of subjectivity. The MSCI HFCS overcomes this problem by attempting to capture the multi-dimensional nature of hedge fund investing through the use of Primary and Secondary Characteristics, as described above, to more accurately and effectively identify and define hedge fund strategies. The MSCI HFCS strives to offer a balance between a sufficient level of detail which permits an accurate classification of a large number of strategies whilst also allowing an intuitive understanding of its interpretation and implementation.

For a fixed return expectation, they want to arrive at their target wealth expectation with minimum of risk in the process. Since we are measuring risk at this stage solely by volatility, it follows that if we were to draw two straight lines from the origin of Figure 5.3 through the unique location point of each manager, and measure the angle of each line from the x-axis, e.g. $\alpha$ and $eta$ angles, the manager of preference in the risk-adjusted sense would be the manager who has the largest angle. In this case, it is clear that $\alpha > eta$, so CTA is preferred over CTA B in the risk-adjusted sense (since it ‘dominates’ in mu-sigma space it is said to be the more ‘efficient’ of the two portfolios). As seen from Figure 5.2, each angle is directly proportional to the gradient$^{10}$ and it follows that:

$$\frac{R_A}{\sigma_A} > \frac{R_B}{\sigma_B}$ \quad (5.1)$

The investor’s risk-adjusted investment objective is usually therefore to maximise future risk-adjusted returns:

$$\theta = \left[ \frac{E(R_p)}{\sigma_p} \right]_{\max}$ \quad (5.2)$

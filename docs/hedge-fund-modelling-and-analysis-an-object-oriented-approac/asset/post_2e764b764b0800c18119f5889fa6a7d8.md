There are several measures for correlation, most often the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient, denoted $\rho$ (for population) or $r$ (for sample). Note that if $X$ and $Y$ are independent then the correlation is zero, although a value near zero indicates a lack of relationship between $X$ and $Y$. Given any pair of random variables, $X_i$ and $Y_j$, correlation is defined as:

Clearly, $\rho_{ij}$ is symmetric ($\rho_{ij} = \rho_{ji}$) and $\rho_{ii} = 1$ for itself. The correlation values range between -1 and 1, showing +1.0 for a perfectly positive linear relationship, -1.0 for a perfectly negative relationship, and a value near zero in other cases (see Figure 4.16). To obtain a more direct indication of how two variables co-vary, correlation is simply a scaled version of covariance with the standard deviations of two sets. There are several measures for correlation, most often the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient, denoted $\rho$ (for population) or $r$ (for sample). Note that if $X$ and $Y$ are independent then the correlation is zero, although a value near zero indicates a lack of relationship between $X$ and $Y$.

Given any pair of random variables, $X_i$ and $Y_j$, correlation is defined as:

$$\rho_{ij} = \frac{\text{cov}(X_i, Y_j)}{\sigma_i\sigma_j}$$

Clearly, $\rho_{ij}$ is symmetric ($\rho_{ij} = \rho_{ji}$) and $\rho_{ii} = 1$ for itself. The correlation values range between -1 and 1, showing +1.0 for a perfectly positive linear relationship, -1.0 for a perfectly negative relationship, and a value near zero in other cases (see Figure 4.16). In hedge funds and hedge strategies that have low correlations with each other is an ideal way for a fund manager to maximise the potential returns of an investment under a wide range of economic and market conditions.¹16 Clearly, creating a well-diversified portfolio of low correlated hedge funds is an extremely valuable process for constructing FoHFs.

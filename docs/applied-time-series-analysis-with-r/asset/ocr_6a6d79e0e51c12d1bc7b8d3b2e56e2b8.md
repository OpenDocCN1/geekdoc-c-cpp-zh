Figure 11.6 (originally given by Gray et al., 1989) shows the stationary long-memory region (range of $u$ and $\lambda$) for the Gegenbauer processes. The stationary long-memory region for $u$ and $\lambda$ is the shaded region ($0 < \lambda < 0.5$ and $|u| < 1$) along the solid horizontal borders ($u=\pm1$ and $0 < \lambda < 0.25$). The spectral process is represented by the solid horizontal line segment at the top of the rectangle. This figure illustrates that the Gegenbauer represents a considerable generalization of the process. As $u$ and $\lambda$ move along a path that approaches a dashed boundary of the rectangle, the corresponding Gegenbauer processes approach nonstationarity. For example, if $u$ is held constant at $\lambda=0.4$, then as $u$ approaches 1 from below, the processes associated with these $u$ and $\lambda$ values will increasingly display "near-nonstationarity" behavior. While $u$ determines the G-frequency, it also affects the degree of "near-stationarity."

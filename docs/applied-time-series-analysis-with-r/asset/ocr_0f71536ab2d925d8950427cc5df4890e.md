# Nonstationary Time Series Models

NOTE: As might deduced from this example, there is a fundamental difference between the behavior of $ho_s$ for a nonstationary process and for a stationary process, $ho_s$ is influenced to some degree by all the factors in the model, both AR and MA. However, in a nonstationary process, $ho_s$ is affected only by the roots on the unit circle, and in fact, only by those of highest multiplicity. In this regard, we have the theorem that was first suggested by Gray et al. (1978) and proven in general by Finley (1978), Quinn (1980), and Gray and Woodward (1981).

# Theorem 5.1 (Findley–Quinn Theorem)

Suppose that the following are true:

+   1. X, is an ARMA$(p+d, q)$ with $d$ roots “close” to the unit circle
2. Of the $d$ roots in (a), $j$ have highest multiplicity $\ell$

Then, $ho_s^*$ (the limiting value of $ho_s$ as the $d$ roots approach the unit circle uniformly) exists, and for each $k=0, \pm1, \pm2, \dots, \rho_s^*$ satisfies the linear difference equation of order $j$ for $j > 0$.

```
$$(1-\zeta_1B-\dots-\zeta_dB')\rho_s^*=0, \quad (5.14)$
```

where $j$ is specified in (b). The roots of $\zeta(z)=0$ are all on the unit circle (i.e. have modulus 1), and $\zeta(B)$ is the operator formed from the product of the nonstationary factors of highest multiplicity in $\ell$.

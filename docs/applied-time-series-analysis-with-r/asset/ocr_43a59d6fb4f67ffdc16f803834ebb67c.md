One sparse of the features of the wavelet decomposition is that it produces a sparse representation of the original data. Using Equation 12.2 the original data can be reconstructed from the wavelet coefficients, implying that the information in the data (16 data values) is conveyed using 10 nonzero wavelet and scaling coefficients. The extent of sparseness is much more striking than in this simple example. Recalling that the coefficient, $d_{\mu j}$ is a measure of the duration around 2 that occurs in the vicinity of its location on the time axis of “activity” in the data, we-emphasize larger values of $|d_j|$ indicate locations of detail for larger values of $j$ (i.e., $d_{3,1}$, $d_{3,2}$ and $s_{43}$) measure the strength of smoother (longer duration) behavior. In Figure 12.7 it can be seen that the jump at $t=3$ is described by detail coefficients $d_{1,2}$ and $d_{2,1}$, while the wavelet bump around $t=12$ produces detail coefficients at levels 1, 2, and 3.

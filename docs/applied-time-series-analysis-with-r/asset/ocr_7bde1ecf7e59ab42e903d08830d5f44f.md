An important property of the WM estimates in the AR(p) setting is that the resulting model is stationary, the roots of $1-\hat{\phi}_{1,z}-\dots-\hat{\phi}_{p,z}=0$ lie outside the unit circle (see Brockwell and Davis, 1991). It should be noted that unlike the case with $\hat{\gamma}_k$, if the estimates $\hat{\gamma}_l$ given in Equation 1.18 are used in the calculation of the autocovariances, the "YW" estimates do not necessarily produce a stationary model. This is primarily due to the fact discussed in Section 1.4 that $\hat{\gamma}_k$ is positive semidefinite while $\hat{\gamma}_l$ is not.

Before finishing this section, we note that the WM estimates are method-of-moment (MM) estimates of $\phi_v, ..., \phi_p$, since they are obtained by equating sample autocorrelations to theoretical autocovariances and solving the resulting equations. # 7.2.1.2 Least Squares Estimation

Ordinary least squares (OLS) estimates are obtained by rewriting the AR(p) model of Equation 7.2

```
$X_t-\mu=\phi_1(X_{t-1}-\mu)+\dots+\phi_p(X_{t-p}-\mu)+a_t$,
```

and treating Equation 7.11 as a regression model to estimate $\phi_1, ..., \phi_p$ and $\mu$. Specifically, taking $t=p+1, p+2, ..., n$ in Equation 7.11 gives the equations

```
$X_{p+1}-\mu=\phi_1(X_p-\\mu)+\dots+\phi_p(X_1-\\mu)+a_{p+1}\nX_{p+2}-\mu=\phi_1(X_{p+1}-\mu)+\dots+\phi_p(X_2-\\mu)+a_{p+2}\n\vdots\nX_n-\\mu=\phi_1(X_{n-1}-\mu)+\dots+\phi_p(X_{n-p}-\mu)+a_n$.
```

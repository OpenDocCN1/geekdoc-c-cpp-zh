Note that these equations are of the same form as Equation 8A.6 except for the fact that they begin with $\rho_{jk}$ instead of $\rho_j$. In that $\rho_{jk}^{(i)}$ denotes the $i$th coefficient associated with the $k \times k$ system in which $\rho_{jk}$ is on the left-hand side of the first equation. The generalized partial autocorrelation function is defined to be $\rho_{jk}^{(i)}, j=0,1,\dots$. Similar to the use of Equation 8A.7 to solve Equation 8A.6, in Equation 8A.13, Cramer's rule can be used to solve Equation 8A.12 yielding

```
$\rho_j \qquad \rho_{j+k-2} \qquad \rho_{j+1}$$

$\vdots \qquad \vdots \qquad \vdots$$
$\Phi_{kk}^{(i)} \qquad \rho_{j+1} \qquad \rho_{j+k}$ \qquad$ (8.A.13)
$\rho_j \qquad \rho_{j+k-2} \qquad \rho_{j+1}$
$\rho_{j+1} \qquad \rho_{j+k-3} \qquad \rho_{j+2}$$
$\vdots \qquad \vdots \qquad \vdots$$
$\rho_{j+k-1} \qquad \rho_{j+1} \qquad \rho_j$
```

The motivation for the definition of $\rho_{jk}^{(i)}$ comes from the fact that if $X$ is an ARMA$(k,j)$ process, then the equations in Equation 8A.12 are the extended Yule-Walker equations discussed in Section 7.2.3. Thus $\phi_{kk}^{(i)}$ is the solution of the extended Yule-Walker equations for the last AR coefficient,"assuming the process is ARMA$(k). Therefore, $\phi_{kk}=\rho_{kk}$. It should be noted, however, that the generalized partial autocorrelation function does not have a correlation interpretation, and in fact it may assume values larger than one in absolute value.
The generalized partial autocorrelations as defined in the preceding graph satisfies properties that uniquely determine $p$ and $q$ for an ARMA$(p,q)$ process when the true autocorrelations are known.

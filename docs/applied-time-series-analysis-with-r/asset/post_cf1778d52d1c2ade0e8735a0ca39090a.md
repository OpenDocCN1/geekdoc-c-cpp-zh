
## Other Stationary Time Series Models

## 4.2.1 ARCH Processes

To address the conditional volatility behavior discussed in connection with the DOW rate of returns data in Figure 4.7, Engle (1982) introduced the ARCH model. We first consider the simplest ARCH process, the ARCH(1) model.

### 4.2.1.1 The ARCH(1) Model

Let $\sigma_{t-1}^2$ be the conditional variance of $a_t$, and suppose

$$a_t = \sigma_{t-1} \varepsilon_t,$$

where

$$\sigma_{t-1}^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2,$$

$0 \le \alpha_1 < 1$, and $\{\varepsilon_t\}$ is a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), zero mean, unit variance random variables that are independent of the $a_{t-k} k=1, 2, \dots$ Then $a_t$ is said to satisfy an ARCH(1) process. Note that the unit of $\varepsilon_t$ is required, so that

$$E[(\sigma_{t-1} \varepsilon_t)^2 | a_{t-k}, k=1, 2, \dots] = \sigma_{t-1}^2 E[\varepsilon_t^2 | a_{t-k}, k=1, 2, \dots]$$
$$ = \sigma_{t-1}^2 E[\varepsilon_t^2] = \sigma_{t-1}^2,$$

It is also easily shown (Shumway and Stoffer, 2010) that the $a_t$'s are uncorrelated with a conditional variance that depends on $t$. Note from Equation 4.19 that

$$a_t^2 - (\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2) = \sigma_{t-1}^2 (\varepsilon_t^2 - 1,$$

and thus that

$$a_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2 + w_t,$$

where $w_t = \sigma_{t-1}^2(\varepsilon_t^2 - 1)$ and $E[w_t] = 0$. Thus, $a_t$ satisfies a non-Gaussian AR(1) process with

$$\sigma_t^2 = E[a_t^2]$$
$$ = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 E[a_{t-1}^2] + E[w_t]$$
$$ = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 \sigma_a^2,$$

Therefore,

$$\sigma_a^2 = \frac{\alpha_0}{1-\alpha_1}$$

### 4.2.1.2 The ARCH($q_0$) Model

The ARCH(1) model can be extended as follows.

$$\alpha_t = \sigma_{t-1}^2 \epsilon_t,$$

where

$$\sigma_{t-1}^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2 + \dots + \alpha_{q_0} a_{t-q_0}^2,$$

and the $\epsilon_t$ are i.i.d., zero mean, unit variance random variables. The process, $[a_t],$ in Equation 4.23 is referred to as an ARCH($q_0$) process. Recall that an ARCH(1) process was suggested as a plausible model to account for the volatility seen in the return of data in Figure 4.7. Economic applications, such as the returns data, originally motivated the introduction of the ARCH model.

The ARCH($q_0$) model in Equation 4.23 can be expressed in a form analog to Equation 4.21. That is, if $a_t$ satisfies the ARCH($q_0$) model in Equation 4.23, then it can be written as

$$a_t^2 = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 a_{t-1}^2 + \alpha_2 a_{t-2}^2 + \dots + \alpha_{q_0} a_{t-q_0}^2 + w_t,$$

that is, $a_t^2$ satisfies an AR($q_0$) process with non-Gaussian white noise. From Equation 4.24, it follows that for an ARCH($q_0$) process, the unconditional variance can be written as follows:

## 4.2.2 The GARCH($p_0, q_0$) Process

The ARCH($q_0$) model in Equation 4.23 has been generalized by Brockwell and Davis (1991) and Taylor (1986) to the GARCH($p_0, q_0$) model to include lagged terms of the conditional variance in the model. Specifically, for the GARCH($p_0, q_0$) model, Equation 4.23 is generalized to

$a_t = \sigma_{t-1}\epsilon_t , \quad (4.26)$

$$\sigma_t^2 = \frac{\alpha_0}{1-(\alpha_1 + \beta_1) - \dots - (\alpha_{max(p,q)} + \beta_{max(p,q))} \quad (4.28)$$

Bera and Higgins (1993) showed that the higher the order of the ARCH operator, the more persistent the shocks tend to be. This is illustrated in Figure 4.13a and b, we show realizations from the ARCH(4) model where

and the ARCH(8) model where

respectively. Finally, in Figure 4.13c, we show a realization from the GARCH(1, 1) model

$$\sigma_{t-1}^2 = 0.1 + 0.45a_{t-1}^2 + 0.45\sigma_{t-1}^2. \quad (4.31)$$

The coefficients in this model are selected, so that the unconditional variance is unit in case. In each of the realizations in Figure 4.13, there is a tendency for the shocks to last longer than in the ARCH(1) models in Figure 4.10.

# EXAMPLE 12.11: XBOX AND BARBARA IMAGES AND ASSOCIATED DWT

1. XBOX image: A classic two-dimensional image used for illustrating two-dimensional wavelets is the XBOX image shown in Figure 12.22a. The XBOX image (not to be confused with the gaming console) in Figure 12.22a consists of $128 \times 128$ pixels, and the image (as the name suggests) is of an X enclosed in a box. Figure 12.21b shows the DWT using the Haar wavelet and $j=3$. The small square at the top left of Figure 12.22b is analogous to the bottom row (i, the smooth coefficients) of the DWT. This case in it is $16 \times 16$ image ($128/2^3=16$). The XBOX image is ideally suited for showing the sense in which the $d_{k,l}^v$, $d_{k,l}^h$ coefficients measure vertical, horizontal, and diagonal behavior, We reiterate the fact that the image in Figure 12.22b is a DWT, that is, it is a twimensional plot of the coefficients analogous to the DWT plots shown, for example, in Figures 12.7 and 12.8. That is, although the bottom left box has the appearance of an approximating function (such as S4 in Figure 12.9) it is simply a coefficient of the coefficients, which in this case has the appearance of the actual figure. Note that in Figure 12.22b the gray background represents zero darker colors represent positive and lighter colors negative coefficients.

+   2. Barbara image: Figure 12.22a shows the “Barbara” image ($256 \times 256$) which is another classic image that is more complex than the XBOX image. The gray background in the DWT, shown in Figure 12.23b, again denotes zero, so it can be seen that the DWT is a sparse representation of the Barbara image in that the number of nonzero pixels required to reproduce the image is much smaller than the original $256 \times 256$.

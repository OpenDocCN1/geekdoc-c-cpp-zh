## 5.2 ARIMA(p,d,q) and ARUMA(p,d,q) Processes

The ARIMA(p,d,q) and ARUMA(p,d,q) models defined in Definition 5.1 are useful nonstationary extensions of the ARMA(p,q) model.

## Definition 5.1

- 1. The autoregressive integrated moving average process of orders p, d, and q (ARMA(p,d,q)) is a process, X, whose differences (1 − B)^dX, satisfy an ARMA(p,q) model (that is, a stationary model) where d is a non-negative integer. We use the notation

```
$\phi(B)(1-B)^d X_t = \theta(B)a_t,$
```

where all the roots of $\phi(z) = 0$ and $\theta(z) = 0$ are outside the unit circle, and $\phi(z)$ and $\theta(z)$ have no common factors.

- 2. The autoregressive root moving average process of orders p, d, and q (ARUMA(p,d,q)) is an extension of the ARIMA(p,d,q) model to include processes, $X_t$, whose $\lambda(B)$ satisfies ARMA(p,q) model where $\lambda(B) = 1 - \lambda_1B - \dots - \lambda_dB^d$ is an operator whose characteristic equation has all its roots (real and/or complex) on the unit circle. We use the notation

```
$\phi(B)\lambda(B)(X_t - \mu) = \theta(B)a_t,$
```

to denote such a process. It is worth noting that $\phi(1)\lambda(B)\mu = \phi(1)\lambda(1)\mu$, and therefore, we can write Equation 5.8 in the form

```
$\phi(B)\lambda(B)X_t = \phi(1)\lambda(1)\mu + \theta(B)a_t.$
```

The term $\phi(1)\lambda(1)\mu$ is called the *moving average constant*, and if $\lambda(B) = (1 - B)^d$, then $\lambda(1) = 0$ so that $\phi(1)\lambda(1)\mu = 0$. This is not true in general for an ARUMA(p,d,q) model, if $1 - B$ is not a factor of $\lambda(B)$, then $\phi(1)\lambda(1) \neq 0$.

## 5.2.1 Extended Autocorrelations of an ARIMA(p,d,q) Process

Although it is not possible to directly define the autocovariances of an ARIMA$(p,d,q)$ process with $d > 0$, it is possible to define an “extended” way of such a process in a meaningful way. To this end, we provide the following definition.

## Definition 5.2

Let $r_1, r_2, ..., r_{p+d}$ be the roots of $\phi(r)=0$, and let $\rho_k = \rho(k, r_1, r_2, ..., r_{p+d}, \theta)$ denote the autocorrelations of a stationary ARMA $(p+d, q)$ process. Now

suppose that the first $d$ of the $r_j$ approach the unit circle uniformly, that is, $|r_j| = |r_m|$ for $j, m \leq d$. We denote these roots by $r_{1} = \dots = r_d$. We define the extended autocorrelation of an ARMA$(p,d,q)$ process as $\rho_k$, where $\rho_k$ is the limit of $\rho_k$ as the roots $r_j, j=1, \dots, d$ approach the unit circle uniformly. That is

```
$$\rho_k^* = \lim_{|r_j|\to1^-} \rho(k, r_1, r_2, \dots, r_d; \theta).$\quad (5.11)
```

## EXAMPLE 5.6: NEAR-NONSTATIONARY MODEL WITH COMPLEX RO

Consider the AR(3) model

```
$(1-0.8B)(1-1.34B+0.995B^2)X_t = a_t, \quadquad (5.13)

Model 5.13 is similar to Equation 5.12 in that the real root corresponds to $\alpha_1=0.8$, and the coefficients of the second-order behavior are designed so that the associated frequency is $f=0.13$. However, the difference is that the pair of complex roots is much closer to the unit circle in Equation 5.13 than for are in Equation 5.12. Specifically, for these roots $|r_i^{-1}|=0.9975$ while for realization of length $n=200$ for the model in Equation 5.13. In this case, the autocorrelations and realization are almost entirely dominated by the second-order behavior associated with the pair of complex roots close to the unit circle. This behavior was previously observed in Example 3.14, but the point to be made here is that the limiting case where the complex roots are on the unit circle will exhibit second-order behavior that "ignores" the first-order behavior induced by the positive real root, $0.8^{-1}$.

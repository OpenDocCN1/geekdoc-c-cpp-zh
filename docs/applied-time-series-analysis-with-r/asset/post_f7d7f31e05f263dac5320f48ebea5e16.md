## 6.7 Assessing Forecast Performance

In this section we focus on techniques for assessing the performance of the forecasts. We will consider two techniques: (a) using probability limits and (b) forecasting the last $k$ data values.

### 6.7.1 Probability Limits for Forecasts

In the preceding section, techniques were described for obtaining optimal forecasts of $X_{t+\ell}$ given information up to time $t_{\ell}$. In addition to finding the forecast $\hat{X}_{h}(\ell)$, it is also important to assess the uncertainty associated with the predictions.

Recall from Equation 6.11 that the forecast error associated with the prediction of $X_{t+\ell}$ given data to time $t_{\ell}$ is given by

$e_{t_0}(\ell) = X_{t_0+\ell} - \hat{X}_{t_0}(\ell) = \sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k a_{t_0+\ell-k},$

where $\psi_0=1$. If the white noise is normal with zero mean and variance $\sigma_{\epsilon}^2$, then $e_{t_0}(\ell)$ is also normal, and from Equations 6.12 and 6.14, it follows that $e_{t_0}(\ell)$ has zero mean and variance:

$Var[e_{t_0}(\ell)] = \sigma_{\epsilon}^2 \sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k^2. \quad 6.31$

Thus

$e_{t_0}(\ell) \sim N(0, \sigma_{\epsilon} \sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k^2})^{1/2}}$

from which it follows that

$Prob\{-z_{1-\alpha/2} \le \frac{X_{t_0+\ell} - \hat{X}_{t_0}(\ell)}{\sigma_{\epsilon} ({\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k^2})^{1/2}} \le z_{1-\alpha/2}\} = 1-\alpha, \quad 6.32$

where $z_{\beta}$ denotes the $\beta \times 100\%$ percentile of the standard normal distribution. Using Equation 6.32, a $(1-\alpha)$ $100\%$ prediction interval for $X_{t_0+\ell}$ is given by

$$\hat{X}_{t_0}(\ell) \pm z_{1-\alpha/2} \sigma_{\epsilon} \sqrt{\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k^2}} \quad 6.33$$

Given that to data $t_0$ the probability that $X_{t_0+\ell}$ will fall within the limits given in Equation 6.33 is $1-\alpha$. It is important to notice that the limits in Equation 6.33 are for individual forecasts. For example, if a 95% prediction interval is obtained for $X_{t_0+5}$ using Equation 6.33, then there is a 95% chance that the value of $X_{t_0+5}$ will fall within the specified limits. It is not true, however, that there is a 95% chance that the future values $X_{t_0+\ell}, l=1,...,5$ simultaneously fall within their respective limits. In a practical situation, the white noise variance must be estimated from the data, and the $\psi$-weights are computed from the parameter estimates. This will be discussed in Chapter 7.

The prediction interval in Equation 6.33 has the intuitively reasonable property that the half widths

$Z_{1-a/2}\sigma_a = \left( \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \psi_k^2 \right)^{1/2}, \quad (6.34)$

are non-decreasing as $\ell$ increases. For a stationary process, as $\ell \to \infty$,

$\sigma_a = \left( \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \psi_k^2 \right)^{1/2} \to \sigma_a = \left( \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \psi_k^2 \right)^{1/2} = \sigma_X(<\infty)$.

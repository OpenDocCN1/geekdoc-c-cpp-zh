# EXAMPLE 1.4: A NON-ERGODIC PROCESS

As a hypothetical example of a non-ergodic process, consider a thread manufacturing process that produces spoils of thread. The machine is cleaned and adjusted at the end of each day after the diameter remains unchanged until the end of the next day at which time it is re-cleaned. The diameter setting could be thought of as a stochastic process with each day being a realization. In Figure 1.7, we show three realizations from this hypothetical process $X(t).
$\mu$ is the average diameter across days, then is also clear that $\gamma(t, t+h)$ does not depend on $t$ within a day. It is also clear that $\gamma(t, h) = \gamma(0) < \infty$, for all $h$. Thus, the process is stationary. However, it obviously makes makes no sense to try to estimate $\mu$ by $ar{X}_t$, that is, by averaging down the time axis for a single realization. Heuristically, we see that $\mu$ can be estimated by averaging over several realizations, but using one realization in essence provides only one piece of information. Clearly, in this case $X_t$ is not ergodic for $\mu$.

Since the factor table in Table 11.3 strongly suggests two positive unit roots, we begin modeling by twice differencing the data. The twice differenced data and the associated autocorcorrelations are shown in Figure 11.16a and b. The sample autocorcorrelations of the twice data, shown in Figure 11.16b, have the form of a slowly damping of two sinusoidal components. This is suggestive of a 2-factor GARMA model, and we will proceed to model the data using this model. The two frequencies associated with roots close the unit circle in Table 11.3 are 0.084 and 0.166. For the annual cycle, we use $f_1 = 1/12$ and $f_2 = 1/6$. This $u_1 = \cos(2\pi f_1) = \cos(\pi/6) = 0.866$ and $u_2 = 0.5$. Then, will consider models containing either the Gegenbauer factors $(1 - 1.732B + B^2)^{k_1}$ and $(1 - B + B^2)^{k_2}$ or the nonstationary ARMA factors $1 - 1.732B + B^2$ and $1 - B + B^2$. Note that the two differences were applied to the data using as a factor $(1 - B)^2$. Therefore, the 2-factor GARMA model suggested for this data is given by

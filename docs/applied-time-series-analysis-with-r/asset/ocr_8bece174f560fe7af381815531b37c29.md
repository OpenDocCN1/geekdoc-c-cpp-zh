# 9.3.2 A Bootstrapping Approach

Woodward et al. (1997) suggested a bootstrap method to estimate the accuracy of the test statistics obtained by the CO and MLE procedures. In the following, we will consider the application of the bootstrap to $t_{CO}$ in Equation 9.15. Here, the $t_{CO}$ statistic is calculated as described previously on the original (observed) data. Set to simulate realizations of $H_0$ an AR model fit to the observed data. This procedure assumes $H_0$ that any trending behavior in the series is due to the correlation structure alone. Then, K realizations are generated from the AR model fit to the original data. Woodward et al. (1997) used a parametric bootstrap procedure that assumes normal errors and generates $a_t$ in the simulated procedures as random normals. If normality of the errors is questioned, a non-parametric bootstrap could be used in which the original series, $\hat{a}_t, t=1,...,K$, from the original AR(p) fit to the data are resampled with replacement for use in the terms in the simulated procedures. For a two-sided test, $H_0: b=0$ is rejected at significance level $\alpha$ if $t_{CO} > t_{\alpha/2}$ or $t_{CO} < -t_{\alpha/2}$, where $t_{eta}^{(b)}$ is the $eta$th empirical quantile of $\{t_{CO}^{(b)}\}_{b=1}^K$ with standard adjustments for one-sided tests.

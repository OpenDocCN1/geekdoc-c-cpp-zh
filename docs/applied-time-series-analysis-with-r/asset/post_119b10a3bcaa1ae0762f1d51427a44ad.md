```
$$\rho_k=C_1\r_1^{-k}+\cdots+C_p\r_p^{-k},\quad (3.104)$$
```

where the $C_{ij}$ and $C_l'$ are constants uniquely determined from Equation 3.99 with $k=q,q-1,\dots,q-p+1$.

## 3.3.5 $\psi$-Weights of an ARMA(p,q)

We have shown that an ARMA(p,q) process can be expressed in the linear filter, $X_t = \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \psi_i a_{t-i}$. In this section, we discuss calculation of $\psi$-weights using three methods: (a) equating coefficients, (b) division, and (c) finding a general expression using Theorem 3.1.

- 1. Finding $\psi$-weights by equating coefficients: Since a stationary ARMA(p,q) process can be written in the form $X_t = \psi(B)a_t$, where $\psi(B) = \phi^{-1}(B)\theta(B)$, we have the relationship $\phi(B)\psi(B) = \theta(B)$, or in the expanded form

$$$(1-\phi_1B-...-\phi_pBp)(1+\psi_1B+\psi_2B^2+...) = (1-\	heta_1B-...-\theta_qB^q). \quad (3.115)$$$$

Equating the coefficients of $B^l$ in Equation 3.115 describes the relationship among the $\theta$'s, $\phi$'s, and $\psi$'s. In particular, equating coefficients of $\psi$'s yields

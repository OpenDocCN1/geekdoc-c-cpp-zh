Kong Eats Grass” while a fan was blowing in the background. During the silent periods between words, it can be seen that the fan noise is exhibited by low-amplitude cyclic behavior with a frequency of about 136 Hz (cycles per second). Also, note the similarity of the frequency content in the words “King” and “Kong”. The “K” at the beginning and the “g” at the end of the words “look” similar in frequency behavior, while the word “eats” is quite different in its frequency and amplitude while the “s” sounds at the ends of “eats” and “grass” are similar. Thus, inspection of Figures 1.10 and 1.12 should convince us that there is something to be gained by understanding the frequency content of a time series. This conclusion is valid and, in fact, as we will see, the frequency interpretation of a time series may often furnish us with the most relevant information about the underlying process.

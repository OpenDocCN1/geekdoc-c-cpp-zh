# 13.4.1 Models for Generalized Linear Chirps

In this section, we discuss AR-type models for GLC processes that are similar to the Euler(p) and G(p; \lambda) models. To begin the discussion, we define GLC white noise then define the GLC(p) model. As we proceeded formally assuming standard properties of the differential operator, $D$, since our purpose is to motivate the sampling scheme.

# Definition 13.10

Let $z(u)$ be zero mean white noise and let $u=at^2+bt, t > 0$. Then $\epsilon(t)=z(u)$ is defined as GLC white noise.

# Definition 13.11

Consider a process $\{X(t), t < -b/(2a) \text{ or } t > -b/(2a)\}$ defined by

```
$$\prod_{i=1}^{p} \frac{1}{2at+b}D-\alpha_i(X(t)-\mu)=\epsilon(t), \quad (13.24)$
```

where $D$ is a differential operator, $a, b \in (-\infty, \infty)$, $a \neq 0$ and the $\alpha_i$ are real or complex constants with $Re(\alpha_i) < 0, p > 0$, and $\epsilon(t)$ is GLC (zero) mean white noise. $X(t)$ is called a GLC(p) process. Note, if $a=0$ and $t \in (-\infty, \infty)$, then $X(t)$ is an AR(p) process.

The GLC(p) process extends to a GLC(q) process. (See Robertson et al., 2010.) Just as processes in Equations 13.21 through 13.23 are GLC processes, it can be shown that the GLC(p) and GLC(p,q) processes are also GLC processes and consequently are G-stationary as well. See Robertson (2008).

Note that if $u=at^2+bt$, then $du/dt=2at+b$. Consequently,

```
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2at+b}[D-\alpha]X(t)&=\frac{1}{2at+b}\frac{dX}{dt}-\alpha X(t) \=\frac{dX}{du}-\alpha X. \end{aligned} \quad (13.25)$
```

Proceeding formally one can show

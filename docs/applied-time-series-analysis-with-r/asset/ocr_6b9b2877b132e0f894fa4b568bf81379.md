In the setting of ARMA(p,q) modeling, the AIC criteria becomes

AIC = -2[In (maximized likelihood)]+2(p+q+1), (8.4)

since there are $p+q+1$ parameters including the constant term. It can be shown that AIC in Equation 8.4 is approximately equal to

AIC = ln($\hat{\sigma}_n^2$)\frac{p+q+1}{n} (8.5)

where $\hat{\sigma}_n^2$ is the estimated white variance associated with the ML fit of an ARMA(p,q) model (see Box et al., 2008; Shumway and Stoffer, 2010). We will subsequently apply AIC by finding the $p$ and $q$ ($0 \leq p \leq P$ and $0 \leq q \leq Q$) that minimize AIC as defined in Equation 8.5.

A problem with the use of AIC is that as the realization length increases, AIC tends to overestimate the model orders (i.e., select models with many parameters). Several authors have considered modifications to AIC to adjust for this problem. Two of the alternatives are AICC (Hurvich and Tsai, 1989) and BIC (sometimes called the Schwarz Information Criterion [SIC]) (Schwarz, 1978). These are given by

AIC = ln($\hat{\sigma}_n^2$)\frac{n+p+q+1}{n-p-q-3}$ (8.6)

and

BIC = ln($\hat{\sigma}_n^2$)+(p+q+1)\frac{\ln(n)}{n} (8.7)

Simulation studies have shown BIC to perform best for large realization lengths, n, while AIC generally performs better for smaller $n$ when the number of parameters is relatively large (see McQuarie and Tsai, 1998). If computing time related to the use of ML estimation is an issue, the quantities in Equations 8.5 through 8.7 can be approximated using alternative estimation techniques. For example, if the decision has been made to restrict attention to AR models, Equations 8.5 through 8.7 can be calculated using $\hat{\sigma}_n^2$ obtained based on Burg estimates of the model parameters.

Other model identification techniques have been proposed in the literature. For example, Akaike (1969) suggested the use of final prediction error (FPE) for identifying the order of an AR model. Another early technique for identifying the order of an AR model is the criterion AR transfer (CAT) function proposed by Parzen (1974).

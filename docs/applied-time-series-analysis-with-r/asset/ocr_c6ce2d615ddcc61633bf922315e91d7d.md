EXAMPLE 1.6: A SIGNAL CONTAINING SEVERAL FREQUENCIES

Consider the upper left realization in Figure 1.10. Close examination reveals a repetitive nature to this signal, but its general appearance is similar to several realizations that we have seen in this chapter. Actually, this signal is the sum of sine waves with different cycle lengths and phase shifts that are shown in the three plots in Figure 1.10. Data sets fig1.10a-d in waive contain the realizations in Figure 1.10a-d, respectively. We will return to the data in Figure 10.1a in Example 1.7.

Actual realizations from time series will not typically have such simple mathematical form as the upper left in Figure 1.10. However, using the techniques of Fourier analysis applied to time series via the spectrum, we will be able to analyze more complicated realizations in order to discover fundamental cyclical behavior that provides information about the underlying model generating the data. The examination of underlying periodicities in a time series is called spectral analysis, and the tools that we will use to measure the frequency content of the power spectrum and the spectral density. We first introduce needed terminology and definitions.

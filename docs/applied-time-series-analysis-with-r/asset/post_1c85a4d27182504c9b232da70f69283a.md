Consequently, the seasonal behavior can be modeled using an ARMA(p,d,q) model with one nonstationary pattern, $1-\sqrt{3B}+B^2$. Note that the seasonal behavior in the airline data follows an irregular pattern. For example, air travel is highest in November and December by a drop to an annual increase in March with a regular temperature in the summer, slight drop in early fall. Such a pattern is not sinusoidal and $1-B^2$, since ignoring the steady increase from year to year, the dominant factor influencing air travel in a given month is the air travel in the previous year and month. Note that the factor $1-\sqrt{3B}+B^2$ is one of the factors of $1-B^2$, but for the Pennsylvania temperature data it is the only factor needed to model the periodic behavior.

Completing the analysis we transform the temperature data to obtain $Y_t=X_t-1.732X_{t-1}+X_{t-2}$.

# 6.1 Mean-Square Prediction Background

This section briefly reviews some of the key results related to the problem of finding optimal predictors in the mean-square. Theorem 6.1 shows the fundamental role that conditional expectation plays in the search for a best mean-square predictor. From probability theory, it is known if X and Y are random variables, the conditional expectation of Y given X, denoted E(Y|X), is a function of X (Chung 2001). Theorem 6.1, states that E(Y|X) is closest to Y in the mean-square sense among all square integrable variables that are functions of X.

# Theorem 6.1

Suppose X and Y are random variables with E(X^2) < ∞ and E(Y^2) < ∞. If h(X) is a random variable such that E[h(X)^2] < ∞, then

> E(Y - EY | X)^2 \leq E(Y - h(X))^2.

Proof: See Blackwell and Davis (1991) and Billingsleys (1995).
The result in Theorem 6.1 extends to the case where X_1, X_2, ..., X_t0 are random variables such that E(X_j) < ∞, j=1, ..., t0. In this case, the conditional expectation E[X_{t+ℓ} | X_1, ..., X_t] is the best predictor of X_{t+ℓ} in the mean-square sense among all square integrable functions of the random variables X_1, X_2, ..., X_t. Thus, since our goal is to forecast X_{t+ℓ} based on X_1, ..., X_t, it follows that E[X_{t+ℓ} | X_1, ..., X_t] is the optimal predictor. Although E[X_{t+ℓ}] is the desired optimal predictor, it is, the gold standard, it is not necessarily linear and may be very difficult to obtain in practice. As a consequence, we will restrict our attention to “linear predictors” and search for “optimal” predictors of the form

> Z_{t0} = a_1X_{t0} + a_2X_{t0-1} + \dots + a_{t0}X_1. (6.1)

Theorem 6.2 (sometimes called the Projection Theorem) says that the best linear predictor of the form Equation 6.1 is the projection of X_{t+ℓ} onto the hyperplane spanned by X_1, ..., X_t.

# EXAMPLE 3.17: AN ARMA(2,1) model

Consider the ARMA(2,1) model given

```
(1 - 1.6B + 0.9B^2)X_i = (1 - 0.8B)a_i, (3.93)
```

Recall that correlations, a realization (based on $\sigma_t^2=1$) and the spectral density for the AR(2) model ($1 - 1.6B + 0.9B^2)X_i = a_i$, were given in Figures 3.12b, 3.14b. In Table 3.6 we show the factor table for the model in Equation 3.93, which is an ARMA(2,1) model, since all roots associated with AR and MA factors are outside the unit circle. As discussed, the effect of the MA factor 1 - 0.8B is to reduce frequency content at $f=0$. In Figure 3.24 we show a realization (f3.24a in tpage), autocorrelations, and spectral density for the ARMA(2,1) model in Equation 3.93 where $\sigma_t^2=1$. In Comparing Figure 3.24 with Figures 3.12b through 3.14b, we note similar behavior in the realization and correlations. Both spectral densities behave a peak at $f=0.1$, but it should be noted that the spectral densities differ. Specifically, the spectral density in Figure 3.24c for the ARMA(2,1) model is much lower than $f=0$ than the corresponding spectral density for the AR(2) model ($1 - 1.6B + 0.9B^2)X_i = a_i$. Thus, the effect of the MA factor 1 - 0.8B is to reduce the spectral content and cause the frequency content to be more frequent about $f=0.1$. Thus, the effect of the MA factor 1 - 0.8B in Examples 3.13 and 3.14 is to remove or reduce frequency behavior at $f=0$.

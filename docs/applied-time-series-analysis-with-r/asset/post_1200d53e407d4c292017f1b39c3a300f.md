### 3.2.10 AR(1) and AR(2) Building Blocks of an AR(p) Model

In Examples 3.8 and 3.9, we were able to determine the characteristics of the AR(3) models by examining the first- and second-order factors of the model. These examples suggest that the key to understanding AR(p) models is to recognize the AR(1) and AR(2) models serve as "building blocks." The p-order characteristic equation φ(z) = 1 - φ₁z - … - φ_pz^p = 0 will have real roots, complex roots (which occur as complex conjugates), or a combination of real and complex roots. As a consequence, φ(z) can be factored as

```
φ(z) = ∏_{j=1}^{m}(1-α₁jz-α₂z²)   (3.76)
```

where there are two types of factors.

- 1. Factors associated with real roots (linear factors): Factors of Equation 3.76 associated with real roots are linear factors, that is, α₂=0. The absolute value of the reciprocal of the associated root is |r_j^{-1}|=|α_j|, and, thus, it is necessary that |α_j| < 1 in order for the associated root to be outside the unit circle. "System frequencies," f₀, for these real roots are assigned as

```
f₀=0   if α_j > 0
=0.5   if α_j < 0.
```

- 2. Factors associated with complex roots (quadratic factors): In this case the factor 1-α₁jz-α₂z² is an irreducible quadratic factor, that is, α_j²+4α₂<0, and the two associated roots are complex conjugate pairs. For measuring the proximity of a root to the unit circle, it is useful to note that |r_j^{-1}|<1 for a stationary process, and the closer |r_j^{-1}| is to 1 the closer these roots are to be. The system frequency associated with the quadratic factor 1-α₁jz-α₂z² is

```
f_j=1/(2π) cos^{-1}(α_j/(2√{-α₂}))
```

Thus, an AR(p) model is fundamentally a combination of first and second-order components. Specifically, ρ_k for an AR(p) process behaves as a mixture of damped exponentials and/or damped sinusoids. Each j=1, …, m in Equation 3.76 corresponds to either a real root or a complex conjugate pair of roots. Without loss of generality, we let j=1, …, m_r, correspond to the real

```

and where C_j j=1,...,m, and ψ_j j=m_r+1,...,m are real constants. Equation 3.77 shows that ρ_k for an AR(p) process behaves like a mixture of damped exponentials and/or damped sinusoids. The factors of φ(z) determine the correlation structure of an AR(p) as well as the behavior of its realizations. The understanding of these factors is often critical to understanding the physical phenomenon.

## EXAMPLE 3.10: AUTOCORRELATION FUNCTION FOR EXAMPLES 3.8 AND 3.9

Based on Equation 3.77, the autocorrelation function for the AR(3) model of Example 3.8 is

```

### 3.2.11 Factor Tables

It is useful to summarize information regarding the first and second-order factors of an AR(p) model, in a factor table. For each first-order or irreducible second-order factor of the model factor table displays

- 1. Roots of the roots to the characteristic equation
- 2. Proximity of the roots to the unit circle (by tabling the absolute reciprocal of the roots)
- 3. System frequencies f_{ij}

Factors in the factor table are listed in decreasing order of the proximity of the associated roots to the unit circle the closer to the unit circle, the stronger the autocorrelation effect. See Woodward et al. (2009).

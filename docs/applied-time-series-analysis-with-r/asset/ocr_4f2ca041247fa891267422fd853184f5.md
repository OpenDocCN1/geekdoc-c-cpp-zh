# ARMA Time Series Models

The spectral density is given by

```
$S_X(f) = \frac{\sigma_a^2}{\sigma_X^2} \frac{|1-	heta_1e^{-2\pi if}-	heta_2e^{-4\pi if}-\dots-\theta_qe^{-2\pi qf}|^2}{|1-\phi_1e^{-2\pi if}-\phi_2e^{-4\pi if}-\dots-\phi_qe^{-2\pi qf}|^2} \quad (3.92)$
```

System frequencies of the AR part of ARMA(p,q) model tend to introduce peaks in the spectral density similar to those for the AR(p) model. However, system the MA part tend to produce troughs or dips in the spectral density at the corresponding system frequencies. The peaks in a spectral density for ARMA(p,q) (or AR(p)) model will be caused by small values in the denominator of Equation 3.92 occur near the system frequencies of the AR part of the model. Correspondingly, small values in the numerator of Equation 3.92 near the system frequencies of the MA will produce the tendency for dips or troughs to appear in the spectral density.

# 3.3.3 Factor Tables and ARMA(p,q) Models

According to Theorem 3.4 the stability and invertibility of an ARMA(p,q) model can be tested by examining the factors of $\phi(z)$ and $	heta(z)$, respectively. In the AR(p) case, factor tables are a useful tool for understanding these factors, and this can be accomplished using the twsg R factor function. wge. The factor tables for the AR components of an ARMA(p,q) model are interpreted as the AR(p) case. That is, system frequencies indicate natural frequencies in the model, and spectral densities will tend to have peaks near these frequencies. The key to understanding factor tables is that they are interpreted in an opposite manner. Specifically, the MA frequencies indicate frequencies whose impact has been weakened in the total model. Spectral densities will tend to have dips sharper than peaks in the vicinity of the MA frequencies (See the discussion following Equation 3.92.) As an example note that the spectral density in Figure 3.3a for the MA(1) model $X_t = (1-0.99)B_t$, has a dip or trough at $f=0$ the spectral density for $(1-0.99)B=X_t$, would have a sharp peak at $f=0$. Another example of this effect is the fact that in Equation 3.90 the AR factor $1-0.95B$ and the MA factor $1-0.8B$ are each associated with system frequency $f=0$.

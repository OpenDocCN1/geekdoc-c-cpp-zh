# EXAMPLE 13.4: DUAL PROCESS AND SPECTRAL ESTIMATION

In this example, consider the Euler(2) realization in Figure 13.2c, which is shown again in Figure 13.3. Using the steps outlined earlier (and to be discussed in Section 13.3), we transform the time axis and derive the dual realization that is plotted in Figure 13.4b. Close inspection of Figure 13.4a and b shows that each realization goes through about 20 cycles. However, while the cycles are increasing in Figure 13.4a, each of the cycles in Figure 13.4b is essentially the same cycle length. There is a 1-1 correspondence between the cycles. For example, 5-8 are characterized by higher peaks in the spectral density than the original time axis into, in which the realization has an appearance of to the appearance of stationarity. Figure 13.4c shows the Paszen spectral density estimate, based on truncation point M = 28 for the data in Figure 13.4a. As would be expected, the plot does not show any periodic behavior and the appearance of a spread spectrum since the cycle length throughout the realization. There is a clear pattern of period (i.e., frequency) change that is not captured in the standard spectral estimate. Figure 13.4d shows the corresponding Paszen spectral density for the data in Figure 13.4b. This spectral estimate has a distinct peak at about f=0.1 suggesting strong cyclic behavior associated with a period length of around 10.
Figure 13.4c and d illustrate that while on the original timescale, there is a wide range of frequency behavior, the logarithmic time transformation has created a new time axis on which frequency behavior is stable, and there is a single dominant frequency.

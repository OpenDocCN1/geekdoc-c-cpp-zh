Recall in Figure 6.10, it was seen that the trend in the airline data was predicted to continue using the model (1 − B) (1 – B^2)$X = a_t$, where $\phi$(B) is a stationary 12th order factor given in Equation 6.54. As was mentioned in Example 6.8, the trending behavior in the airline data is caused by the fact that 1 − B is a factor and, the “airline model” has factors of 1 − B. In Chapter 6, this produces forecasts that tend to follow the trending behavior preceding the forecasts. However, a second factor of 1 − B is not suggested for the temperature data as be seen by examining factor tables with AR(k) fits to the temperature data for $k = 10, 12, 16$ (not shown here).

Do models provide exclusive tend to continue? The answer to this question is a resounding “No.” What we can conclude is if the stationary AR(4) model in Equation 9.8 for the available data remains valid into the future, the best predictor forecasts the trend to abate. However, the nonstationary model in Equation 9.9 is a valid representation of future behavior, the best predictor is “silent” regarding whether volatility will increase or decrease.

Using R: The forecasts in Figure 9.6 can be obtained using the steps:

+   * The parameters of a stationary AR(4) model to fit the data using either est.ar.wge or est.arma.wge. For this, to obtain the forecasts and plot in Figure 9.6a, we will use the ts commands
```
mod=est.ar.wge(hadley, p=4)
.arma.wge(hadley, mod=$phi.est, n.ahead=25, limits=FALSE)
```
* To obtain the forecasts in Figure 9.6b from the nonstationary model in Equation 9.9 we use the ts commands
```
dif=artrans.wge(dif,p=3)
mod=est.ar.wge(dif,p=3)
.arma.wge(hadley, mod=$phi.est, d=1, n.ahead=25, limits=FALSE)
```

Note that a signal-plus-noise model, assuming for simplicity a linear trend, could fit the temperature data. Such a model would predict an increasing trend if the slope of the line is significantly different from zero. Thus, it is clear that the forecasts are only as good as the model being used, and it is also clear that forecasts can be obtained to support an unscrupulous investigator's position by selecting the model to be used for forecasting to meet his or her needs or preconceptions. At the same time, erroneous conclusions may be unintentionally drawn from various analyses due to lack of understanding by the researcher. Thus, it is important to be aware of underlying properties associated with any forecasting method being used.

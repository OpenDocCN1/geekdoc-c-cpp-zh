**EXAMPLE 8.2: CHECKING FOR WHITE NOISE**

In Figure 8.2a, we show sample autocorrelations (95% limits) based on the realization of length $n=190$ in Figure 8.2b. As in Figure 8.1b, more than 95% of the sample autocorrelations stay within the 95% noise limits. Consequently, examination of the sample autocorrelation suggests white noise. examination of Figure 8.2b shows that the realization has inconsistencies with white noise. For example, the variability of the data shows earlier in the series, and appears to be a certain amount of piecewise trending after $t=100$. Local irregularities occur in white noise data, the total picture of this realization is, although the sample autocorrelations are small, the realization does not appear to be white. The realization consists of the residuals from an AR(10) fit to the data set in Figure 8.2c. Note that this realization displays behavior that decreases with time similar to the bat echolocation signal in Figure 1.26a. An ARMA(p,q) model cannot appropriately be fit to such data because of its nonstationary behavior, and it is not surprising that the residuals from such a fit are not white.

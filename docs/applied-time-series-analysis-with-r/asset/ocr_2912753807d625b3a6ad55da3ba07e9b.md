# # 3 ARMA Time Series Models

In this chapter we will discuss some of the important models that are used to describe the behavior of a time series. In particular interest will be the AR, MA, and ARMA models. These models were made popular by George Box and Gwilym Jenkins in their classic time series book, the current edition of which is Box et al. (2008). These models have become fundamental to the theory and applications of time series. While several example based on real data are examined in this chapter, we make extensive use of simulated realizations in order the properties and opening an understanding of known models. Also, unless otherwise specified, the autocorrelations and spectral densities illustrated in this chapter are the theoretical quantities for the models being studied. In later chapters, we will use the lessons learned in this chapter as we analyze actual time series.

# ## 3.1 MA Processes

We begin by defining an MA process of order $q$, which will be denoted $MA(q)$.

Definition 3.1

Let $a_t$ be a white noise process with zero mean and finite variance $\sigma_a^2$, and let the process $X_t, t=0, \pm1, \pm2, ...$ be defined by

```
$X_t - \mu = a_t - \theta_1 a_{t-1} - \dots - \theta_q a_{t-q},$$
```

where $	heta_j, j = 1, ..., q$ are real constants with $	heta_j \neq 0$. The process $X$, defined in Equation 3.1 is called an MA process of order $q$, which is usually shortened to $MA(q)$.

We will often write Equation 3.1 in the form

```
$X_t - \mu = \theta(B)a_t,$$
```

where

```
$\theta(B) = 1 - \theta_1 B - \theta_2 B^2 - \dots - \theta_q B^q.$
```

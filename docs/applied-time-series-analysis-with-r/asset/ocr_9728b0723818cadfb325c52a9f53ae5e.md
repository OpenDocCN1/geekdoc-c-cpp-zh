# 3.2.10 AR(1) and AR(2) Building Blocks of an AR(p) Model

In Examples 3.8 and 3.9, we were able to determine the characteristics of the AR(3) models by examining the first- and second-order factors of the model. These examples suggest that the key to understanding AR(p) models is to recognize the AR(1) and AR(2) models serve as "building blocks." The $p$-order characteristic equation $\phi(z) = 1 - \phi_1z - \dots - \phi_pz^p = 0$ will have real roots, complex roots (which occur as complex conjugates), or a combination of real and complex roots. As a consequence, $\phi(z)$ can be factored as

```
$$\phi(z) = \prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\\alpha_1jz-\alpha_2z^2) \quad (3.76)$
```

where there are two types of factors.

+   1. Factors associated with real roots (linear factors): Factors of Equation 3.76 associated with real roots are linear factors, that is, $\alpha_2=0$. The absolute value of the reciprocal of the associated root is $|\r_j^{-1}|=|\alpha_j|$, and, thus, it is necessary that $|\alpha_j| < 1$ in order for the associated root to be outside the unit circle. "System frequencies," $f_0$, for these real roots are assigned as

```
$f_0=0 \quad \text{if } \alpha_j > 0$\n$$=0.5 \quad \text{if } \alpha_j < 0.$
```

+   2. Factors associated with complex roots (quadratic factors): In this case the factor $1-\\alpha_1jz-\\alpha_2z^2$ is an irreducible quadratic factor, that is, $\alpha_j^2+4\alpha_2<0$, and the two associated roots are complex conjugate pairs. For measuring the proximity of a root to the unit circle, it is useful to note that $|\r_j^{-1}|<1$ for a stationary process, and the closer $|\r_j^{-1}|$ is to 1 the closer these roots are to be. The system frequency associated with the quadratic factor $1-\\alpha_1jz-\\alpha_2z^2$ is

```
$f_j=\frac{1}{2\pi}\cos^{-1}\left(\\frac{\alpha_j}{2\sqrt{-\alpha_2}}\right)$
```

Thus, an AR(p) model is fundamentally a combination of first and second-order components. Specifically, $\rho_k$ for an AR(p) process behaves as a mixture of damped exponentials and/or damped sinusoids.

### EXAMPLE 7.7: ASYMPTOTIC DISTRIBUTION FOR AN ARMA(1,1) MODEL

Consider the ARMA(1,1) model

$$(1-\phi_1B)X_t = (1-\theta_1B)a_t. (7.66)$$

In this case, each of the submatrices in Equation 7.64 is $1 \times 1$, and $\mathbf{\Gamma}$ is given by

$$\mathbf{\Gamma} = \begin{bmatrix} \gamma_U(0) & \gamma_{UV}(0) \\ \gamma_{UV}(0) & \gamma_V(0) \end{bmatrix}$$

where $\gamma_U = \sigma_a^2/(1-\phi_1^2)$ and $\gamma_V(0) = \sigma_a^2/(1-\theta_1^2)$. Now,

$$\gamma_{UV}(0) = E[U_t, V_t]$$
$$= E[\phi_1 U_{t-1} + a_t][\theta_1 V_{t-1} + a_t]$$
$$= \phi_1 E[U_{t-1}V_{t-1}] + \theta_1 E[V_{t-1}a_t] + \phi_1 E[a_tU_{t-1}] + \sigma_a^2,$$

so that

$$\gamma_{UV}(0) = \phi_1\theta_1\gamma_{UV}(0) + \sigma_a^2,$$

and $\gamma_{UV}(0)$ is given by

$$\gamma_{UV}(0) = \frac{\sigma_a^2}{1-\phi_1\theta_1}$$

Thus, we have

$$\mathbf{\Gamma} = \sigma_a^2 \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{1-\phi_1^2} & \frac{1}{1-\phi_1\theta_1} \\ \frac{1}{1-\phi_1\theta_1} & \frac{1}{1-\theta_1^2} \end{bmatrix}$$

so that

$$\sigma_a^2\mathbf{\Gamma}^{-1} = \frac{1-\phi_1\theta_1}{(\phi_1-\theta_1)^2} \begin{bmatrix} (1-\phi_1^2)(1-\phi_1\theta_1) & -(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\theta_1^2) \\ -(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\theta_1^2) & (1-\theta_1^2)(1-\phi_1\theta_1) \end{bmatrix} (7.66)$$

Asymptotically, $\hat{\phi}_1$ has a normal distribution with mean $\phi_1$ and variance

$$var(\hat{\phi}_1) = \frac{(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\theta_1)^2}{n(\phi_1-\theta_1)^2}$$

while $\hat{\theta}_1$ is normal with mean $\theta_1$ and variance

$$var(\hat{\theta}) = \frac{(1-\theta_1^2)(1-\phi_1)^2}{n(\phi_1-\theta_1)^2}$$

### 7.5.2.1 Confidence Intervals for ARMA(p,q) Parameters

The result in Equation 7.61 provides large sample confidence intervals for the model parameters of an ARMA(p,q). A confidence ellipsoid corresponding to Equation 7.57 and individual confidence intervals corresponding to Equation 7.58 are given

$$(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})^T\mathbf{\Gamma}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta}) \leq \frac{\hat{\sigma}^2}{n}\chi^2_{l-s}(p+q),$$

and

$$\hat{\beta}_j \pm \frac{\hat{\sigma}\hat{U}_{z_{a/2}}}{\sqrt{n}}$$

In practice, the results in Examples 7.6 and 7.7 can be used for the special cases MA(1), MA(2), and ARMA(1,1) by substituting estimated quantities for their theoretical values as in the AR case. The estimation of the elements of $\mathbf{\Gamma}$ for more complex ARMA(p,q) models, however, is not straightforward as it was in the AR case, since the processes $U_t$ and $V_t$ are not observed. In order to estimate the components of $\mathbf{\Gamma}$, we modify the alternative technique suggested for the AR case. However, the elements of $\Gamma_{pp}$ can be estimated using the YW equations to find the theoretical autocovariances associated with the AR(p) model

$$U_t-\hat{U}_1U_{t-1}-\dots-\hat{U}_pU_{t-p} = a_t,$$

while the components of $\mathbf{\Gamma}_{pq}$ can be estimated from the autocovariances for the AR(q) model

$$V_t-\hat{V}_1V_{t-1}-\dots-\hat{V}_qV_{t-q} = a_t,$$

The elements of $\Gamma_{pq}$ and $\Gamma_{qp}$ are estimated by noting that since $U_i$ and $V_i$ are AR processes based on the same white noise process, they can be expressed as

$$Y_t = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} c_j a_{t-j}$$

and

$$Z_t = \sum_{m=0}^{\infty} d_m a_{t-m}$$

where the weights $c_j$ and $d_m$ are the $\psi$ weights discussed in Chapter 3. Thus, $\gamma_{pq}(k)$ can be expressed in linear process form as

$$\gamma_{YZ}(k) = \sigma_{YZ}^2 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} c_j d_{j+k}. (7.72)$$

The cross covariances $\gamma_{pq}(k)$ can be approximated by summing Equation 7.72 until the terms become "small enough." Thus, the cross covariances $\gamma_{ij}(k)$ can be estimated using the estimated AR models in Equations 7.70 and 7.71, finding the appropriate weights for each model, and approximating the corresponding infinite series in Equation 7.72.

### 7.5.3 Asymptotic Comparisons of Estimators for an MA(1)

Although various estimation techniques discussed in this chapter for estimating the parameters of an AR process are asymptotically equivalent estimators, this is not true for estimators of an MA process. To see this, we examine the MA(1) process, $X_t = a_t - \theta_1 a_{t-1}$. In Example 7.6, we found that the MLE of $\hat{\theta}_1$ is asymptotically normal with variance given by $(1-\theta_1^2)/n$. The innovations and MM estimators are also asymptotically normal, and the asymptotic variances for the MM, innovations, and ML estimators are given in Table 7.3 (see Blackwell and Davis, 1991). From the table, we see that for an MA(1)

$$Asyvar{\hat{\theta}_1^{MM}} > Asyvar{\hat{\theta}_1^{I}} > Asyvar{\hat{\theta}_1^{ML}}$$

In Table 7.4, these asymptotic variances (multiplied by $n$) are shown for various values of $\theta_1$.

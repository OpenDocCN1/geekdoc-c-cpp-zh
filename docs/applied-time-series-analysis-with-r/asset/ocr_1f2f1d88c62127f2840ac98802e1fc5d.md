# Wavelets

The subscripts $h$ and $v$ indicate horizontal and vertical dimensions, respectively, and for example, $\varphi_j(y)$ is the father wavelet obtained by separately considering the one-dimensional $Y$ variable. The two-dimensional counterpart to Equation 1217 is given by

```
$g(x, y) = \sum_{k,m} s_{j,k,m}^s \Phi_{j,k,m}(x,y) + \sum_{j=1}^k d_{j,k,m}^v \Psi_{j,k,m}^v(x,y) + \sum_{j=1}^k d_{j,k,m}^h \Psi_{j,k,m}^h(x,y) + \sum_{j=1}^k d_{j,k,m}^d \Psi_{j,k,m}^d(x,y), \quad (12.38)$
```

where the first term in Equation 1238 gives a "smooth" approximation to $g(x,y)$-i.e., involves the higher values of $j$). The remaining summations measure the horizontal, vertical, and diagonal detail, respectively. The $s_{j,k,m},, d_{j,k,m}^v, d_{j,k,m}^h,$ and $d_{j,k,m}^d$ are called the smooth coefficients, vertical detail, horizontal detail, and diagonal detail coefficients, respectively. The two-dimensional DWT consists of the coefficients $s_{j,k,m}$, along with $d_{j,k,m}^v, d_{j,k,m}^h,$ and $d_{j,k,m}^d$ for $j=1,...$. Each of these coefficients is represented as a gray scale (or color) on a pixel in $2^j\times2^j$ image. In particular, for $J=3$ the-dimensional wavelet pyramid algorithm produces $n/2\times n/2$ coefficients of each level 1 detail ($d_{1,k,m}^v, d_{1,k,m}^h,$ and $d_{1,k,m}^d$). Also, there are $n/2\times n/2$ level 2 detail coefficients and $n/2^2\times n/2^2$ level 3 smooth coefficients of types $h, v, d$. Additionally, $n/2^3\times n/2^3$ level 3 smooth coefficients are retained.

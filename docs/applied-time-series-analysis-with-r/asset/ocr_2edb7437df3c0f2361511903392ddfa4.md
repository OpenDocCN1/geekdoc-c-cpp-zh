+   3. Forecasts: Thermometer-based temperature readings for the past 160 or years, such as the data shown in tree rings, Figure 1.24a, indicate that temperatures have generally been increasing over this period.

However, it is well known using proxy data such as tree rings, ocean sediment data, etc., that temperatures have not remained static over time up to the rise over the past 160 years, but have tended to vary. Although the data are insufficient to draw strong conclusions, there is some evidence that, at least in much of the northern hemisphere, the temperatures during the medieval warm period (950–1100) may have been as high as they are today. The medieval warm period was followed by a period known as the Little Ice Age. While not a true ice age, this was a period during which available temperature data indicate was cooling into the nineteenth century.

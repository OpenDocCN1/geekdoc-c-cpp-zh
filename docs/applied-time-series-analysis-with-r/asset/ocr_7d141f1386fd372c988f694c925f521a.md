Forecasts using Equation 6.62 depend on a mean level, an amplitude ($B_2$), and a phase shift ($B_0$). From Equation 6.57, it is seen that eventual forecasts from Equation 6.61 are based on the signal, $s_t$, that is typically estimated from the data. That is, if Equation 6.61 is the assumed model, then the parameters $B_{\nu1}, B_{
u2},$ and $B_3$ are estimated from data by fitting a sinusoid to the $t_0$ values in the realization.

For comparison, when forecasting Equation 6.60 with $p=0$ and $\lambda_0=\sqrt{3}$ for which $f_0=1/12$, the constants $B_2$ and $B_3$ that is the amplitude and phase, are determined by the last two observed data values, and are random variables.

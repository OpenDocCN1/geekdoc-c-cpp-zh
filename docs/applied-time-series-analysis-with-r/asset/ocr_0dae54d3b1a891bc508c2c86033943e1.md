In Figure 1.16c and d, we show the periodograms (plotted as vertical bars in dB at the harmonics of the fundamental frequency) based on the first 100 points and the entire realization, respectively. The periodograms are somewhat visually different, since (c) is plotted at the points $f_j=j/100, j=1, \dots, 50$; while the periodogram in (d) is on a finer partition (i.e., it is plotted at $f_j=j/250, j=1, \dots, 125$). The lack of (mathematical) consistency is illustrated by the fact that the periodogram based on $n=250$ is as variable as the one for $n=100$; we have gained very little with the increased $n$. It should be noted that although the periodogram as defined in Equation 1.38 and its alternative forms are commonly studied as estimators of the spectrum, we use standardized periodograms (in dB) that estimate the spectral density (i, we use $\hat{\gamma}_k$ in Equation 1.38).

The basic problem with the sample spectrum and the periodogram is that the estimates for $\gamma$ when $j$ is close to $n$ are extremely variable regardless of the magnitude of $n$. Much research has gone into the smoothing of the sample spectrum in order to obtain better spectral estimators. A common type of smoothed spectral estimator takes the general form

```
$$\hat{P}_S(f) = \lambda_0\hat{\gamma}_0 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{M} \lambda_k\hat{\gamma}_k\cos(2\pi f/k), \quad (1.39)$
```

where the truncation point $M$ is chosen to be substantially smaller than $n$, and $\hat{\lambda}_k, k=0, 1, \dots, M$ is a window function that damps as $k$ increases. The weights for three classic window functions are:

+   1. Bartlett (triangular) window
$$\$\lambda_k = 1-\left(\frac{k}{M}\right), \quad k=0,1,\dots,M\\n=0, \quad k>M$$

+   2. Tukey window
$$\$\lambda_k = \frac{1}{2}\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{k\pi}{M}\right)^2\right], \quad k=0,1,\dots,M\\n=0, \quad k>M$$

+   3. Parzen window
$$\$\lambda_k = 1-6\left(\frac{k}{M}\right)^2+6\left(\frac{|k|}{M}\right)^3, \quad 0<k\le M/2\\n=2\left[1-\left(\frac{|k|}{M}\right)^3\right], \quad M/2<k\le M\\n=0, \quad k>M.$$

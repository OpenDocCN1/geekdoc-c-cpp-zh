# 10.5.1 Testing for Independence of Two Stationary Time Series

We consider the setting in which $X_{i1}$ and $X_{i2}$ are stationary univariate time series that each have a general linear representation for which the $\psi$-weights are absolutely convergent. Let $\hat{\rho}_{12}(k)$ be the estimated cross-correlation at lag $k$ as defined in Equation 10.12. It can be shown that $\hat{\rho}_{12}(k)$ is asymptotically normal with mean 0 and variance ($AV_{12}$) given by

> $AV_{12} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty} \rho_{11}(j)\rho_{22}(j).$

Consider Blackwell and Davis (1991), if it is to test $H_0: \rho_{12}(k) = 0$ versus $H_1: \rho_{12}(k) \neq 0$, it can be seen that, if either of $X_{i1}$ or $X_{i2}$ is white noise, the asymptotic variance $AV_{12} = 1/n$. Haugh (1976) recommended a procedure for testing $H_0$ that follows the following steps:

- 1. Fit an ARMA($p,q$) model to $X_{i1}$ and $X_{i2}$ (these may be of different orders $p_1, q_1$ and $p_2, q_2$).
- 2. Calculate the residual series $\hat{a}_{1}$ and $\hat{a}_{2}$.
- 3. Calculate the cross-correlations $\hat{\rho}_{12}(k)$.
- 4. For each $k$, conclude that $\hat{\rho}_{12}(k)$ is significantly different from zero at $\alpha=0.05$.

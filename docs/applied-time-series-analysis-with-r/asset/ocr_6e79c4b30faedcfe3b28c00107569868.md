# 12A.2 Vector Spaces

Let $x = (x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)$ and $y = (y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n)$ be two $n$-component real vectors.

+   1. The inner product (or dot product) of $x$ and $y$ is given by $\sum_{k=1}^n x_k y_k$.
2. The Euclidean norm of $x$, is given by $||x||$, as $(\sum_{k=1}^n x_k^2)^{1/2}$.
3. Vectors $x$ and $y$ are orthogonal if their inner product is zero.
4. Vectors $x$ and $y$ are orthonormal if they are orthogonal and have norm one.

# 12A.3 Function Spaces of Real-Valued Functions $f: [-\pi, \pi] 	o \mathbb{R}$

+   1. The inner product of $f$ and $g$ is given by $\int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x)g(x)dx$.
2. The norm of $f$, denoted by $||f||$, is $\int_{-\pi}^\pi |f(x)|^2 dx$.
3. The real-valued function $f$ is said to be in $L^2[-\pi, \pi]$ if $\int_{-\pi}^\pi |f(x)|^2 dx < \infty$.
4. Functions $f$ and $g$ are orthogonal if their inner product is zero.
5. Functions $f$ and $g$ are orthonormal if they are orthogonal and have norm one.

# 12A.4 Systems of Functions

+   1. Let $S = \{f_1, f_2, \dots \}$ where $f_m$ is orthogonal to $f_j$ when $m 
eq j$. Then $S$ is said to be an orthogonal system.
2. An orthogonal system $S$ is said to be orthonormal if the norm of each function in $S$ is one.
3. The functions $S_1 = \{1, \cos kx, \sin kx; k=1, 2, \dots \}$ (or equivalently $S_2 = \{e^{ik}, k=0, \pm1, \dots \}$) are orthogonal systems of functions.
4. Fourier series results show that the collections $S_1$ (and $S_2$) are bases for a wide class of functions in $L^2[-\pi, \pi]$.

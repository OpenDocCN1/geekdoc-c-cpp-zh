## 7.5 Asymptotic Properties of Estimators

In this section, we state some asymptotic results concerning the estimators discussed in this chapter.

### 7.5.1 AR Case

First, note that for an AR(p) process, the YW, Burg, and ML estimates (either conditional or unconditional) have the same asymptotic distribution. Specifically, if $\phi$ denotes the vector of AR parameter estimators obtained using any of these techniques, then

$n^{1/2}(\hat{\phi}-\phi) \Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_{\alpha}^2\Gamma^{-1})$

where $\Rightarrow$ denotes convergence in distribution, and $\Gamma$ is the $p \times p$ matrix whose $ij$-element is $\gamma_{i-j}$, that is,

$$\Gamma = \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_0 & \gamma_1 & \dots & \gamma_{p-1} \\ \gamma_1 & \gamma_0 & \dots & \gamma_{p-2} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \gamma_{p-1} & \gamma_{p-2} & \dots & \gamma_0 \end{pmatrix}$$

(Brockwell and Davis, 1991) Even though the estimators are asymptotically equivalent in the AR(p) case, it is worth noting that the YW estimates can be quite poor in practice when some roots of the characteristic equation are very near the unit circle or when they are repeated roots. Consider, for the AR(3) model

$(1-0.995B)(1-1.2B+0.8B^2)X_t = a_t$

In Equation 5.12 which has a positive root close to the unit circle ($|r_1^{-1}|=0.995$) and complex conjugate roots that are further from the unit circle Figure 5.2c shows that the autocorrelation function for this model approximates the damped exponential behavior of a first order process, and in Section 5.2, we discussed that the (limiting) autocorrelations satisfy a first-order difference equation. As a consequence, the third-order theoretical YW equations associated with Equation 7.52 are likely to tend the weakened effect of the second-order behavior. In Tables 7.6 and 7.7, we will see that the YW estimates based on a realization from this model are quite poor while the Burg estimates are much more

stable, this is a common pattern for such models, and for this reason, it is preferable to use the ML or Burg estimates. In Example 7.5, we use Equation 7.50 to find the asymptotic distributions for estimators of an AR(1) and an AR(2) process.

## EXAMPLE 7.5: ASYMPTOTIC DISTRIBUTIONS AR(1) AND AR(2) MODELS

- 1. AR(1): In the case of an AR(1) model, $\Gamma$ is the $1 \times 1$ matrix whose element is $\gamma_0$, which from Equation 3.37 is given by $\gamma_0 = \sigma_{\alpha}^2/(1-\phi_1^2)$. Thus, it follows that

$$\sigma_{\alpha}^2\Gamma^{-1} = 1 - \phi_1^2,$$

so that $\hat{\phi}_1$ is asymptotically normal with mean $\phi_1$ and variance $(1-\phi_1^2)/n$.

- 2. AR(2): The matrix $\Gamma$, for an AR(2), is given by

$$\Gamma = \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_0 & \gamma_1 \\ \gamma_1 & \gamma_2 \end{pmatrix},$$

where $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ are given by 3.113 and 3.114 (with $\theta_i = 0$) as

$$\gamma_0 = \frac{(1-\phi_2)\sigma_{\alpha}^2}{(1+\phi_2)((1-\phi_2)^2-\phi_1^2)},$$
$$\gamma_1 = \frac{\phi_1\sigma_{\alpha}^2}{(1+\phi_2)((1-\phi_2)^2-\phi_1^2)}.$$

After some algebra, it follows that

$$\sigma_{\alpha}^2\Gamma^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1-\phi_2^2 & -\phi_1(1+\phi_2) \\ -\phi_1(1+\phi_2) & 1-\phi_2^2 \end{pmatrix},$$

From Equation 7.50 and the diagonal elements of Equation 7.56, it can be seen that asymptotically, $\hat{\phi}_1$, is distributed as a normal random variable with mean $\phi_1$ and variance $(1-\phi_1^2)/n$, while $\hat{\phi}_2$ is asymptotically normal with mean $\phi_2$ and the same asymptotic variance as $\phi_1$.

### 7.5.1.1 Confidence Intervals: AR Case

A common use for the result in Equation 7.50 is to compute approximate large sample confidence intervals for the parameters. From Equation 7.50, follows that

$(\hat{\phi}-\phi)^{T}\frac{n\hat{\Gamma}}{\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}}(\hat{\phi}-\phi) \Rightarrow \chi^{2}(p),$

where $\chi^{2}(p)$ denotes a chi-square distribution with $p$ degrees of freedom, and where $\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}$ and $\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}$ denote data-based estimates of $\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}$ and $\mathbf{\Gamma}$ (see Brockwell and Davis, 1991). From Equation 7.57, an approximate $(1-\alpha) \times 100\%$ confidence ellipsoid for $\phi$ is given

$(\hat{\phi}-\phi)^{T}\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}(\hat{\phi}-\phi) \leq \frac{\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}}{n}\chi_{\alpha}^{2}(p),$

where $\chi_{\alpha}^{2}$ denotes the $\beta \times100$th percentile of the chi-square distribution with $p$ degrees of freedom. If confidence intervals are desired for an individual parameter, $\phi_{j}$, then Equation 7.58 can be modified by

$$\hat{\phi}_{j} \pm z_{(1-\alpha)/2}\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{n\hat{v}_{j}}}$$

where $z_{\beta}$ denotes the $\beta \times100$th percentile of the standard normal distribution, and $\hat{v}_{j}$ denotes the $j$th diagonal element in $\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}^{-1}$.

Before considering the ARMA$(p,q)$ case, a few comments can be made concerning the estimation of $\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}$ and $\mathbf{\Gamma}$. First, for the AR(1) and AR(2) cases, $\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}$ can be estimated by directly inserting parameter estimates into Equations 7.53 and 7.56, respectively. For higher order models, $\sigma_{\alpha}^{2}$ can be estimated by $\hat{\sigma}_{\alpha}^{2}$, the white noise variance associated with the particular estimation procedure using backcasting as discussed in Section 7.4. Then, the matrix $\hat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}$ can be obtained by estimating the elements $\gamma_{k}$ using $\hat{\gamma}_{k}$ in Equation 7.5.

In Example 7.9d, we compute confidence intervals for model parameters based on the YW, Burg, and ML estimates for realizations from known AR models.

### 7.5.2 ARMA$(p,q)$ Case

In this section, we briefly discuss the asymptotic distribution of $\hat{\beta}=(\hat{\phi}_{1},...,\hat{\phi}_{p},\hat{\theta}_{1},,...,\hat{\theta}_{q})^{T}$ for the general ARMA$(p,q)$ process,

$$\phi(B)X_{t}=\theta(B)a_{t},$$

where the parameter estimates are obtained using ML or least squares (conditional or unconditional). This asymptotic distribution is given by

$n^{1/2}(\hat{\beta}-\beta) \Rightarrow N(0, \sigma_{\alpha}^2\Gamma^{-1}), \quad (7.61)$

where $\beta$ denotes the vector of true parameter values. In order to obtain the matrix $\Gamma$ in this case, we consider the two AR processes $U_t$ and $V_t$ defined by

$\phi(B)U_t = a_t. \quad (7.62)$

$\theta(B)V_t = a_t, \quad (7.63)$

where $\phi(B)$ and $\theta(B)$ are as in Equation 7.60. We use the notation $\gamma_{U}(k) = E[U_t U_{t+k}]$, $\gamma_{V}(k) = E[V_t V_{t+k}]$ and $\gamma_{UV}(k) = E[U_t V_{t+k}]$. Then, $\Gamma$ is the $(p+q) \times (p+q)$ matrix defined by

$\Gamma = \begin{bmatrix} \Gamma_{pp} & \Gamma_{pq} \\ \Gamma_{qp} & \Gamma_{qq} \end{bmatrix} \quad (7.64)$

Where
$\Gamma_{pp}$ is the $p \times p$ matrix whose $ij$-th element is $\gamma_{U}(i-j)$
$\Gamma_{qq}$ is the $q \times q$ matrix with $ij$-th element $\gamma_{V}(i-j)$

The matrix $\Gamma_{pq}$ is the $p \times q$ matrix whose $ij$-th element is $\gamma_{UV}(i-j)$, while $\Gamma_{qp}$ is the $q \times p$ matrix with $ij$-th element $\gamma_{UV}(j-i)$. It follows from Equation 7.64 that the $\Gamma$ for the MA(q) model, $X_t = \theta(B)a_t$, is identical to that for the AR(p) model $\phi(B)X_t = a_t$. Using this, in Example 7.6, we find the asymptotic distribution associated with an MA(1) and MA(2) model, and, in Example 7.7, we find the asymptotic distribution for an ARMA(1,1). For the derivation of the asymptotic distribution in the ARMA(p,q) case, see Box et al. (2008) and Brockwell and Davis (1991).

## EXAMPLE 7.6: ASYMPTOTIC DISTRIBUTIONS FOR MA(1) AND MA(2) MODELS

- 1. MA(1): It follows from the earlier discussion that for an MA(1) model, $\Gamma$ is the $1 \times 1$ matrix element is given by $\gamma_0 = \sigma_a^2/(1-\theta_1^2)$. Thus, $\sigma_{\alpha}^2\Gamma^{-1}=1-\theta_1^2$, and $\hat{\theta}_1$ is asymptotically normal with mean $\theta_1$, and variance $(1-\theta_1^2)/n$.

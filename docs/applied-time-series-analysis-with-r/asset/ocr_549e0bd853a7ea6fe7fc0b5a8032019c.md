# EXAMPLE 1.14: GLOBAL TEMPERATURE DATA

The concern over global warming has attracted a great deal of attention over the past 25 years. Of interest are data sets that provide a measure of the average annual temperature. One such data set was collected in England by the Climatic Research Unit at the University of East Anglia in conjunction with the Met Office Hadley Centre. The data given in Figure 1.24a are annual temperature information (based on temperature positioned from the average for the globe) for the years 1850–2009. The data plotted (data hadley in twage) are the temperature anomalies (i.e., departures from the average for the period 1961–1990) based on land and marine temperature stations. In the data set, it is clear that there has been a rise in temperatures over this period. There is also considerable variability but no apparent cyclic behavior. In Figure 1.24b, we see that the autocorrelations for the lower lags are positive indicating that, for example, there is substantial positive correlation between this year's and next year's annual temperatures.

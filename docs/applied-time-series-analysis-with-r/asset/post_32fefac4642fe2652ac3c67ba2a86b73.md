## EXAMPLE 13.8: $G(\lambda)$ ANALYSIS OF A DOPPLER SIGNAL

In this example, we consider an undamped Doppler signal, $X(t)$, of the type given by Donoho and Johnstone (1994). The system we consider has two TVF frequencies and is given by

```
$$$X(t)=0.5 \sin\left(\frac{840\pi}{t+50}\right)+\sin\left(\frac{2415\pi}{t+50}\right)+0.05N(t),\quad(13.20)$$
```

where $N(t) \sim N(0, 1)$. Figure 13.10a shows a realization of 200 from this longer model (data *doppler2* in *tawge*), and Figure 13.10c shows the AR(8) spectral density obtained by (unwisely) fitting an AR(8) to the original data. The spectrum is quiet and is helpful in describing the characteristics of the underlying signal. Even though Equation 13.20 cannot be standardized using a transformation of the form of Equation 13.10, we fit to a $G(p, \lambda)$ model of the flexibility this allows. Using the techniques described in this chapter, we fit a $G(12, -1)$ as a "Best" fit. Figure 13.10b shows the dual associated with the $G(12, -1)$ fit, and the decreasing frequency behavior is no longer visible. Figure 13.10d shows the $G(12, -1)$ spectral density (i.e., the AR(12) spectral density of the dual data). Two distinct peaks are visible as expected based on the form of the model. Figure 13.10e shows the instantaneous spectrum on the $G(12, -1)$ model while Figure 13.10f shows the spectrum outperforms the Wigner-Ville distribution. Clearly, the $G(12, -1)$ instantaneous spectrum in this case, more clearly shows the two decreasing frequencies.

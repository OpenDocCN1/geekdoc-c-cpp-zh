## 7.2.1.3 Burg Estimates

Recall, when $\mu=0$, the OLS estimators are based on the equations in Equation 7.12 of the

$X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + \dots + \phi_p X_{t-p} + \delta_t. \quad (7.15)$

Due to the fact that $X_t, t \le 0$ is unobservable, the least squares estimates only on the $n-p$ equations in Equation 7.12 with "response variable $X_{t-p}$ through $X_t$. The fact that use of these equations seems to make inefficient use of available information led Burg (1975), Ulrych, and Clayton (1976), and Nuttall (1976b) to consider alternatives. They argued that stationary processes, there is no "preference in the difference of time," since the correlation between $X_t$ and $X_{t-i}$ is the same as between $X_t$ and $X_{t-k}$ for stationary process. Cheng (1999) shows that stationary Gaussian processes are time reversible and non-Gaussian general linear stationary processes are time reversible if the first two moments exist. Thus, for given set of data for which the $AR(p)$ model in Equation 7.15 is appropriate, we could just consider the "backward" or "reverse" $AR(p)$ model

$X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + \dots + \phi_p X_{t-p} + \delta_t, \quad (7.16)$

where $\delta$ is white noise with zero mean and variance $\sigma_{\delta}^2$ and $t=0, \pm1$, The standard $AR(p)$ model specifies the outcome at time $t$ in terms of the $p$ preceding time values plus a random noise. The autocorrelations between $X_t$ and $X_{t-i} \dots, X_{t-p}$ are the same autocorrelations that exist between $X_t$ and $X_{t+i} \dots, X_{t+p}$ in the model of Equation 7.16.

$X_{n-p} = \phi_1 X_{n-p+1} + \dots + \phi_p X_n - \delta_{n-p}.$ (7.17)

Instead of minimizing the sum of squares $S_n = \sum_{i=p+1}^n a_i^2$, consider estimating the parameters of the model by minimizing the forward and backward sum of squares (FBSS)

$FBSS = \sum_{t=p+1}^n a_t^2 + \sum_{t=1}^{n-p} a_t^2.$ (7.18)

The Durbin-Levinson algorithm can be used to compute the $\hat{\phi}$, so that the FBSS is minimized and at the same time guarantees that the resulting model is stationary. This result is due to Burg (1975), and the resulting estimates are referred to as the *maximum entropy* or Burg estimates.

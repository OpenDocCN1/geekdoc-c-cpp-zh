## 10.2.1 Estimating the Mean and Covariance for Stationary Multivariate Processes

We briefly discuss the estimation of the mean $\mu$ and the covariance matrix $\Gamma(k)$ based on a realization of length $n$ from the stationary multivariate process $[X_{ij}; t=0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots]$. These estimation procedures are entirely analogous to the corresponding procedures for univariate processes.

### 10.2.1.1 Estimating $\mu$

The natural unbiased estimator of the $m$-component vector $\mu$ is the $m$-component vector $\mathbf{X}$ given by

$$\mathbf{X} = \begin{pmatrix}\tilde{X}_1 \\ \tilde{X}_2 \\ \vdots \\ \tilde{X}_m \end{pmatrix}, \quad (10.10)$$

$\tilde{X}_j = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}X_{ij}$. We say that $\mathbf{X}$ is ergodic for $\mu$ if $\mathbf{X}$ converges in the mean-square as $n \to \infty$, that is, if $E[(\mathbf{X}-\mu)(\mathbf{X}-\mu)']\to 0$. Corollary 1.1 gives a sufficient condition for ergodicity in the univariate case, and by applying that to each component of $\mathbf{X}$, it follows that $\mathbf{X}$ is ergodic for $\mu$ if $\lim_{k\to\infty}\gamma_{jh}^2(k)=0$ for each $j$, $1 \le j \le m$.

### 10.2.1.2 Estimating $\Gamma(k)$

It is a common practice to estimate the covariance matrix $\Gamma(k)$ by the $m \times m$ matrix given by

$$\mathbf{\hat{\Gamma}}(k) = \begin{pmatrix}\hat{\gamma}_{11}(k) & \cdots & \hat{\gamma}_{1m}(k) \\ \vdots & & \vdots \\ \hat{\gamma}_{m1}(k) & \cdots & \hat{\gamma}_{mm}(k) \end{pmatrix}, \quad (10.11)$$

$\hat{\gamma}_{ij}(k)=(1/n)\sum_{t=1}^{n-k}(X_{it}-\tilde{X}_i)(X_{jt+k}-\tilde{X}_j)$. Note that the estimator in Equation 10.11 corresponds to the definition of the cross-correlation in

## 10.3 Multivariate (Vector) ARMA Processes

The AR(p), MA(q), ARMA(p,q), ARUMA(p,d,q), and corresponding seasonal models can be extended to the multivariate case. Our focus will be exclusively on the multivariate AR(p) process. We begin this discussion with Example 10.1.

## EXAMPLE 10.1: A BIVARIATE PROCESS

Consider the bivariate process

$$X_t = \begin{pmatrix} X_{t1} \\ X_{t2} \end{pmatrix}, t=0, \pm1, \pm2, \dots$$

specified by

$$X_{t1} = \phi_{11}X_{t-1,1} + \phi_{12}X_{t-1,2} + a_{t1},$$
$$X_{t2} = \phi_{21}X_{t-1,1} + \phi_{22}X_{t-1,2} + a_{t2}, \quad (10.13)$$

where $\mathbf{a}_t = (a_{t1}, a_{t2})'$ is a bivariate white noise process. The equations in Equation 10.13 can be written in matrix form

$$X_t = \mathbf{\Phi}X_{t-1} + \mathbf{a}_t, \quad (10.14)$$

where

$$\Phi_1 = \begin{pmatrix} \phi_{11} & \phi_{12} \\ \phi_{21} & \phi_{22} \end{pmatrix}.$$

The bivariate process in Equation 10.13 is an example of a multivariate (or vector) AR(1) process. The vector AR(p) process is defined in Definition 10.6 that follows.

## Definition 10.6

Let $X_t$ be a causal, stationary multivariate process that satisfies

$$X_t = \alpha + \Phi_1 X_{t-1} - \dots - \Phi_p X_{t-p} + a_t, \quad (10.15)$$

where

- $X_t = (X_{t1}, \dots, X_{tm})'$ is an $m \times 1$ vector
- $\Phi_k$ is a real-valued $m \times m$ matrix for each $k = 1, \dots, p$
- $a_t$, is multivariate white noise with covariance matrix $E[a_t a_t'] = \Gamma_a$;
- $\alpha = (\Phi_1 - \dots - \Phi_p)\mu$, where $\mu = E(X_t)$

Then $X_t$ is a VAR($p$) process, that is, a multivariate AR process of order $p$.

Note that Equation 10.1 can be written in multivariate operator notation $\Phi(B)(X_t - \mu) = a_t$,

$$\Phi(B) = \mathbf{I} - \Phi_1 B - \dots - \Phi_p B^p, \quad (10.16)$$

and $B^k X_t = X_{t-k}$. Theorem 10.1 gives stationarity conditions for VAR($p$) models that are analogous to those in Theorem 3.2 for univariate AR($p$) models.

## Theorem 10.1

A multivariate process $X_t$, satisfying the difference equation in Equation 10.15 is a stationary and causal VAR($p$) process if and only if the roots of the determinantal equation,

$$|\Phi(z)| = |\mathbf{I} - \Phi_1 z - \dots - \Phi_p z^p| = 0,$$

are outside the unit circle.

Proof: See Brockwell and Davis (1991) and Reinsel (1997).

Note that a VAR($p$) model $\Phi(B)(X_t - \mu) = a_t$, satisfying Theorem 10.1 can be written in the multivariate general linear process form

$$X_t - \mu = \Psi(B)a_t;$$
$$= \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \Psi_j a_{t-j}, \quad (10.17)$$

where $\Psi(B) = \Phi^{-1}(B)$.

The multivariate Yule–Walker equations are given by

$$\Gamma(k) = \Gamma(k-1)\Phi_k' + \dots + \Gamma(k-p)\Phi_p, \quad k=1, \dots, p, \quad (10.18)$$

## EXAMPLE 10.2: A BIVARIATE VAR(1) PROCESS

Consider the zero-mean VAR(1) process $\Phi(B)X_t = (I - \Phi_B)X_t = a_t$, where

$$\Phi_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 & 0.6 \\ -0.3 & 0.7 \end{pmatrix}, \quad (10.21)$$

and

$$\Gamma_a = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1.78 \\ 1.78 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad (10.22)$$

In this example $a_t$ has the same $\Sigma_a$, as for the bivariate white noise realization in Figure 10.2. Equation 10.13 in this case is

$$X_{t,1} = 0.9X_{t-1,1} + 0.6X_{t-1,2} + a_{t1},$$
$$X_{t,2} = -0.3X_{t-1,1} + 0.7X_{t-1,2} + a_{t2}, \quad (10.23)$$

and the equation

$$|I - \Phi_1z| = \begin{pmatrix} 1-\phi_{11}z & -\phi_{12}z \\ -\phi_{21}z & 1-\phi_{22}z \end{pmatrix}| = 0$$

is given by $(\phi_{11}\phi_{22} - \phi_{12}\phi_{21})z^2 - (\phi_{11} + \phi_{22})z + 1 = 0$. For this example, the equation is $1 - 1.6z + 0.81z^2 = 0$, which has roots $r$ and $r^*$ given by $0.99 + 0.51i$ and $|r| = 0.90$. Thus, the roots are outside the unit circle.

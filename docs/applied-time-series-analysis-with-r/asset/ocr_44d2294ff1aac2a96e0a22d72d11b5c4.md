In Figure 1.17, we showed smoothed versions of the periodogram for the realization of length n=250 in Figure 1.16a using a Parzen window with four different values for M. The choice of M is somewhat subjective, and the goal is to balance resolution against variability. It can be seen that M decreases the variability of P(f) while the bias increases. Clearly, in Figure 1.17a, it can be seen that M=5 produces a very smooth spectral estimator that essentially loses the key spectral feature of two peaks. Figure 1.17b shows that M=10 does a better job of separating the peaks, but the low frequency spectral estimator is slightly smoothed that the actual peak in the smoothed version is f=0. Moreover, M=100 seems too large, the associated estimator in Figure 1.17d is not much smoother the true spectrum (periodogram) shown in Figure 1.6d. A common choice for the truncation point M=2n, where n is the realization length (in this case M=31) which corresponds to Figure 1.17c where it can be seen that the resulting estimator is fairly smooth yet clearly shows the two peaks. The selection of window types and truncation points is referred to as window tapering. Percival and Walden (1993) and Jenkins (1968).

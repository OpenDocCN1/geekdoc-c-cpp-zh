A related estimator is the periodogram that is simply the sample spectrum evaluated at the harmonics of the fundamental frequency $f_1 = 1/n$. Specifically, the harmonics, $f_j$, are given by

```
$f_j = \frac{j}{n}, j=1,...\nf_j = \frac{n}{2} (if \ n\ is even)\nf_j = \frac{j}{n}, j=1,...,m (if \ n\ is odd and $n=2m+1).$
```

# Definition 1.5

Let $X_i$, a discrete time series defined on the integers. The periodogram based on a realization of length $n$ from this time series is given by

```
$I(f_j) = \hat{\sigma}_j^2 + 2 \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} \hat{\gamma}_k \cos(2\pi f_jk),$ (1.38)
```

where the $f_j$ are the harmonics defined earlier.

The periodogram can alternatively be defined in terms of the finite Fourier representation of the signal and as the modulus squared of the finite Fourier transform of the data. See Appendix 1A. When the sample spectrum and periodogram are obvious estimators of the spectrum, they are not well-behaved as estimators. Theorem 1.4 that follows outlines the properties of the sample spectrum.

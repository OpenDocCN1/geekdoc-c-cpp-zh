# EXAMPLE 7.1: MMs ESTIMATION FOR MA(1)

For an MA(1) model, Equation 7.20 becomes

$$\hat{ho}_1 = \frac{-\hat{	heta}_1}{1+\hat{	heta}_1^2}$\quad (7.22)

which can be written as the quadratic equation

$$\hat{ho}_1\hat{	heta}_1^2+\hat{	heta}_1+\hat{ho}_1=0$.\quad (7.23)

The Roots of Equation 7.23 are

$$\hat{	heta}_1 = \frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-4\hat{ho}_1^2}}{2\hat{ho}_1}$\quad (7.24)

If $|\hat{ho}_1| < 0.5$, then from Equation 7.24, there are two distinct real solutions of Equation 7.23. The invertible solution is given by

$$\hat{	heta}_1 = \frac{-1+\pm\sqrt{1-4\hat{ho}_1^2}}{2\hat{ho}_1}$

In, if $\hat{ho}_1=0.5$ then Equation 7.24 specifies two roots of $\hat{	heta}_1=-1$, which does not result in an invertible model. Similarly, $\hat{ho}_1=-0.5$ results in the non-invertible model with $\hat{	heta}_1=-1$. If $|\hat{ho}_1| > 0.5$, no real solutions of Equation 7.23 exist, and the MM estimates are said to not exist. Recall that for an MA(1) process, if $|\hat{ho}_1|$ is much larger than 0.5, an MA(1) is a question-model for the data.

In general, for $q>1$, the equations in 7.20 must be solved numerically using the Newton-Raphson algorithm or other procedures. MM estimates do not converge (and numerical root finding techniques do not converge) whenever there are no real solutions to 7.20. We discuss MM estimates here for completeness. We do not recommend their use. Because of the frequent failure of the numerical routines to converge, we recommend the use of the innovations algorithm (discussed in the following) to find preliminary estimates of the MA parameters.

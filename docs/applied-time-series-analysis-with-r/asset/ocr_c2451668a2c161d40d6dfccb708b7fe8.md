To convey the 1/scale information, the horizontal axis of Figure 12.15 measures 1/scale, so in this case the n/2 coefficients, $d_{j,k}$ are plotted between 0 and 0.5 while the n/2 detail coefficients, $d_{j,k}$ are plotted between 0.5 and 1. The next step of the pyramid algorithm is to recover the $d_{j,k}$ coefficients and down-sample the $s_{j,k}$ coefficients, which is, to treat the $s_{j,k}$ as “data” of length n/2, and then apply a low pass (1/scale = 0–0.25) and a high pass (1/scale = 0.25–0.5) to compute the $s_{j,k}$ and $d_{j,k}$ coefficients (n/4 each). The procedure is continued, at each step retaining the $d_{j,k}$ (those associated with the higher frequency behavior) and then down-sampling the $s_{j,k}$ coefficients. Figure 12.15 represents a DWT with $j=4$. Note that at the kth step the n/2 $d_{j,k}$ coefficients are positioned within their corresponding frequency band. The arrows from one level to the next indicate coefficients that are further downsampled.

The important point to note is that the down-sampling involved in the DWT is done such a way that the lower frequency behavior is localized at each step. However, the $d_{j,k}$ coefficients measure the highest frequency (fine detail) behavior over the 1/scale range from 0 to 1 (i.e., half of the “frequency” band). Similarly, $d_{j,k}$ coefficients also cover a 1/scale range from 0.25 to 0.5, etc. Close examination of the linear chirp realization in Figure 12.2a shows that at time 100 the periods are about 14 and at time 26 they are about 6. That is, the frequency (as measured by 1/period) changes from 0.07 to 0.16 over this time range, so the 1/scale measure changes from about 0.14 to 0.33. Thus, although frequencies (i.e., 1/scale information) falls in only two ranges: level 3 (1/scale=0.15–0.25) and level 2 (1/scale=0.25–0.5). Examination of Figure 12.14b shows that these two levels of the wavelet-scale plot tend to be the darkest for $t \ge 100$ with a little indication that the level 2 shading is darker for larger values of $n$. However, the detail concerning the linearly increasing frequency is because of the poor frequency localization.

As previously mentioned, the DWT of a realization of length $n$ produces n coefficients. The key feature of wavelet analysis is that down-sampling both the low frequency and the high frequency coefficients occur at each step. This procedure is illustrated in Figure 12.15. Figure 12.16 shows that in the wavelet transform both the $s_{j,k}$ and $d_{j,k}$ coefficients are further down-sampled, while the down-sampling procedure for the DWT produces a total of $n$ coefficients, each of the wavelet packet transform contains $n$ elements. Concurrently, a wavelet packet transform for the case $j=4$, as illustrated in Figure 12.16, has $4n$ coefficients.

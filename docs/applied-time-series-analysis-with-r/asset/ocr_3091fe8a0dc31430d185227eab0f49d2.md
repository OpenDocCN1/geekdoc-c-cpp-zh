In Figure 8.3b we showed the associated sample autocorrelations. The sample autocorrelations in Figure 8.3b are slowly damping, but as is often true in practice, for small realization lengths, they do not damp as slowly as the true autocorrelations in Figure 5.2c. The wandering or precipicing trending behavior in the realization and the slowly damping nature of the sample autocorrelations suggests stationarity or non-stationarity, in particular, showing factor $1-\alpha_B$ for positive $\alpha_B$, where $\alpha_A \le \alpha_B = 1$. Table 8.1 shows factor for 'overfit' AR(k), $k=6, 8$, and 10 fits to the realization in Figure 8.3a. It should be noted that, although the true order model is AR(3), in each of the factor tables, we see a factor $1-\alpha_B$, where $\alpha_B$ is close to one. This illustrates the result in Theorem 8.1 and suggests the inclusion of factor $1-B$ in the model. The fact that a factor close to $1-B$ shows only once in each of the factor models suggests that only one factor of $1-B$ is appropriate. That is, the data should be differenced to achieve stationarity.

NOTE: In this book, we show overfit factor tables based on Burg estimates. You could also use ML estimates but avoiding YW estimates as we have previously discussed. Final models use ML estimates.

Using R: Use est.ar.wage with type = "burg" to obtain the overfit factor tables in Table 8.1 by successively choosing the order $p$ to be 6, 8, and 10.

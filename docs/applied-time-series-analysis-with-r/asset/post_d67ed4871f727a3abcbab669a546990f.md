### Definition 3.3
If X is the AR(p) process defined by Equation 3.17, then the algebraic equation $\phi(z)=0$ is called the associated characteristic equation where

### 3.2.2 AR(1) Model
We first consider the properties of the AR(1) model given by

$X_t - \phi_1 X_{t-1} = a_t, \quad (3.28)$
where $|\phi_1| < 1$.

- $\psi$-weights and stationarity conditions for an AR(1): From Equation 3.27, we see that the general linear process of an AR(1) process is

$X_t = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \phi_1^k a_{t-k}$.
That is, $\psi$-weights are $\psi_k = \phi_1^k$. The characteristic equation is given by $1 - \phi_1 z=0$, which has root $\eta_1 = \phi_1^{-1}$ where $|\eta_1|=|\phi_1^{-1}|>1$ since $|\phi_1| < 1$, from which it follows that the model in Equation 3.28 is stationary and causal. We also noted that the $\psi$-weights of an AR(1) model in the preceding discussion about inverting the operator.

- Autocorrelations of an AR(1) process: In order to find $\gamma_0$ and $\rho_k$ for an AR(1) process, it is necessary to evaluate the expectation $E[X_tX_{t-k}] = E[X_{t-k}X_t]$. To obtain $\gamma_k$, multiply both sides of the equality in Equation 3.28 by $X_{t-k}$ $k>0$, to yield

$X_{t-k}[X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + a_t]$.
Taking the expectation of both sides we obtain

$E[X_{t}X_{t-k}] = \phi_1 E[X_{t-k}X_{t-1}] + E[X_{t-k}a_t]. \quad (3.29)$
Now, $X_{t-k} = \sum_{j=0}^k \phi_1^j a_{t-k-j}$ and thus $X_{t-k}$ depends only on $a_{t-k-j}$ $0, 1, \dots$. It follows that $X_{t-k}$ is uncorrelated with $a_t$, that is, $E[X_{t-k}a_t]=0$ for $k>0$. Thus, from Equation 3.29, $\gamma_k = \phi_1\gamma_{k-1}$ for $k>0$, and

$\gamma_1 = \phi_1\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_2 = \phi_1\gamma_1 = \phi_1^2\gamma_0$,

so that in general

$\gamma_k = \phi_1^k \gamma_0$, (3.30)

and

$\rho_k = \phi_1^k$, (3.31)

Since $\gamma_k = \gamma_{|k|}$ and $\rho_k = \rho_{|k|}$, then for $k=0, \pm1, \pm2, ...$, Equations 3.30 and 3.31, respectively, become

$\gamma_k = \phi_1^{|k|} \gamma_0$,

and

$\rho_k = \phi_1^{|k|}$, (3.32)

Further, $\gamma_0$ can be obtained by pre-multiplying both sides of Equation 3.28 by $X_t$, and taking expectations as before, to obtain

$E[X_t^2] = \phi_1 E[X_{t-1}X_t] + E[X_t a_t]$.

Thus

$\gamma_0 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + E[X_t a_t]$. (3.33)

The term $E[X_t a_t]$ is found using an analogous procedure, this time by post-multiplying both sides of Equation 3.28 by $a_t$, and taking expectations, which gives

$E[X_t a_t] = \phi_1 E[X_{t-1}a_t] + E[a_t^2]$.

Previously, it was shown that $E[X_{t-1}a_t] = 0$, and therefore, from Equation 3.28, $E[X_t a_t] = \sigma_a^2$. Substituting into Equation 3.33 yields

$\gamma_0 = \phi_1 \gamma_1 + \sigma_a^2$, (3.34)

so that using Equation 3.30, we have

$\gamma_0 = \frac{\sigma_a^2}{1 - \phi_1^2}$. (3.35)

3. Spectrum and spectral density for an AR(1): The discussion in Section 3.2.1 regarding inverting the operator allows us to apply Equation 2.8 to obtain the spectrum yielding

$P_X(f) = \sigma_a^2 |\psi(e^{-2\pi if})|^2
= \sigma_a^2 |\phi^{-1}(e^{-2\pi if})|^2
= \frac{\sigma_a^2}{|\phi(e^{-2\pi if})|^2}
= \frac{\sigma_a^2}{|1-\phi_1 e^{-2\pi if}|^2}$

Using Equation 3.35, the spectral density of an AR(1) process is thus given by

$S_X(f) = \frac{\sigma_a^2}{\gamma_0} \frac{1}{|1-\phi_1 e^{-2\pi if}|^2}
= \frac{1-\phi_1^2}{|1-\phi_1 e^{-2\pi if}|^2}$

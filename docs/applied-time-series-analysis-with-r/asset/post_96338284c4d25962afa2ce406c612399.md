As a final illustration using the case of wavelet packets we consider the realization of n=256 in Figure 12.19a which is from the AR(2) model

> $(1 + 0.995B^2)X_t = a_t$

Examination of the factor table (not given here) shows that this is a stationary model with system frequency f=0.25. The time-scale plot should essentially follow a horizontal line at 1/scale=0.5 (i.e., f=0.25). The time-scale plot based on the DWT is shown it is seen that the poor frequency localization for higher frequencies causes the time-scale plot to lose precision. Figure 12.19c shows the time-scale plot based on the best algorithm, and the horizontal behavior at 1/scale=0.5 is much more clearly. A schematic diagram of the wavelet packet transform obtained from the best algorithm is shown in Figure 12.17b. Note that the wavelet packet coefficients “hone” on 1/scale=0.5 (i.e., system frequency f=0.25) and provide best frequency localization in this region of the 1/scale axis. This is similar to the manner in which DWT coefficients focus on lower frequencies.

## 12.3.10 Two-Dimensional Wavelets

Although this is a book on time series analysis, we cover this section on wavelet analysis with a brief discussion of two-dimensional wavelets. This section briefly covers wavelet analysis of an $m \times n$ image, $F_{m,n}$ where, in the univariate case, we assume that both dimensions are dyadic (i.e., powers of 2). Examples will be based on a square image, that is, of size $2^l \times 2^l$ for some integer $J_n$. The two-dimensional DWT maps the $m \times n$ image $F_{m,n}$ of wavelet coefficients. The univariate DWT is based on two types of wavelets: father wavelets measure smooth behavior and mother wavelets measure more detailed behavior. In the case of the two-dimensional DWT, there are four types of wavelets

$\Phi(x, y) = \varphi_0(x) \times \varphi_0(y)$ (captures smooth features of the image)
$\Psi^x(x, y) = \psi_0(x) \times \varphi_0(y)$ (captures vertical features of the image)
$\Psi^y(x, y) = \varphi_0(x) \times \psi_0(y)$ (captures horizontal features of the image)
$\Psi^{xy}(x, y) = \psi_0(x) \times \psi_0(y)$ (captures diagonal features of the image)

# Wavelets

The subscripts $h$ and $v$ indicate horizontal and vertical dimensions, respectively, and for example, $\varphi_j(y)$ is the father wavelet obtained by separately considering the one-dimensional $Y$ variable. The two-dimensional counterpart to Equation 1217 is given by

```
$g(x, y) = \sum_{k,m} s_{j,k,m}^s \Phi_{j,k,m}(x,y) + \sum_{j=1}^k d_{j,k,m}^v \Psi_{j,k,m}^v(x,y) + \sum_{j=1}^k d_{j,k,m}^h \Psi_{j,k,m}^h(x,y) + \sum_{j=1}^k d_{j,k,m}^d \Psi_{j,k,m}^d(x,y), \quad (12.38)$
```

where the first term in Equation 1238 gives a "smooth" approximation to $g(x,y)$-i.e., involves the higher values of $j$). The remaining summations measure the horizontal, vertical, and diagonal detail, respectively. The $s_{j,k,m},, d_{j,k,m}^v, d_{j,k,m}^h,$ and $d_{j,k,m}^d$ are called the smooth coefficients, vertical detail, horizontal detail, and diagonal detail coefficients, respectively. The two-dimensional DWT consists of the coefficients $s_{j,k,m}$, along with $d_{j,k,m}^v, d_{j,k,m}^h,$ and $d_{j,k,m}^d$ for $j=1,...$. Each of these coefficients is represented as a gray scale (or color) on a pixel in $2^j\times2^j$ image. In particular, for $J=3$ the-dimensional wavelet pyramid algorithm produces $n/2\times n/2$ coefficients of each level 1 detail ($d_{1,k,m}^v, d_{1,k,m}^h,$ and $d_{1,k,m}^d$). Also, there are $n/2\times n/2$ level 2 detail coefficients and $n/2^2\times n/2^2$ level 3 smooth coefficients of types $h, v, d$. Additionally, $n/2^3\times n/2^3$ level 3 smooth coefficients are retained. Figure 12.21 shows a schematic diagram of a two-dimensional DWT with $J=3$ where the original image contains $2^J\times2^J$ pixels where $f_0 \geq 3$.

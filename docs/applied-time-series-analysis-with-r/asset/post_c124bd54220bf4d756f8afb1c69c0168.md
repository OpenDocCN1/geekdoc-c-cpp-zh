*Caution:* We emphasize that two definitions are commonly used for the covariance

+ a. $\gamma_{ij}(k) = E[(X_i - \mu_i)(X_{i+k} - \mu_j)]$ (the definition given in Equation 10.2) which is used here, in Box, et al. (2008), and others

+ b. $\gamma_{ij}^{(k)} = E[(X_{i+k} - \mu_j)(X_i - \mu_j)]$, used by Shumway and Stoffer (2010), Tsay (2002), Blackwell and Davis (1991, 2002), among others.

Of course, the two definitions of cross-covariance lead to two definitions of cross-correlation. It is critical to note that these two are not equivalent, and we readers to always check for the definition of the cross-covariance before interpreting cross-covariance or cross-correlation results!

**Using R:** Base R function `ccf(xi,xj,type='covariance')` calculates $\gamma_{ij}^{(a)}(k)$ (where $xi$ and $xj$ denote the $i$th and $j$th time series, respectively). In order to compute $ccf$ as defined in Equation 10.2, you can use the call statement `ccf(xj,xi)`. In either case, using `type='correlation'` computes the associated cross-correlations.

Definition 10.3 extends the concept of white noise to the multivariate setting.

### Definition 10.3

The multivariate time series $\{\mathbf{a}_t; t \in T\}$ is said to be multivariate white noise if

+ 1. $E(\mathbf{a}_t) = \mathbf{0}$ where $\mathbf{0}$ is an $m$-component vector of $0$'s.
2. $\boldsymbol{\rho}(k) = \mathbf{0}, k \neq 0$, where in this case $\mathbf{0}$ denotes an $m \times m$ matrix of $0$'s.

It should be noted that Definition 10.3 imposes no restrictions on $\boldsymbol{\rho}(0)$. That is, $a_i$ and $a_j$ may be contemporaneously correlated, and it may be that $\rho_{ij}(0) \neq 0$ for at least one $i, j$ with $i \neq j$. We use the notation $\mathbf{a} \sim \text{MWN}(0, \mathbf{\Gamma})$ to specify that $\mathbf{a}$ is a multivariate white noise process with $\mathbf{\Gamma}(0) = \mathbf{\Gamma}$. At glance, it may seem contradictory for a multivariate white noise process to be contemporaneously correlated.

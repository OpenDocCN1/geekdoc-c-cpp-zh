Note that Equation 12.31 is the DWT version of Equation 12.21. Each of the discrete wavelet, $\psi_{j,k}$ in Equation 12.21 is associated with a “short wave” behavior. In the discussion following Equation 12.14 we noted that the Haar wavelet, $\psi_{j,k}$ is associated with behavior in the neighborhood of $t=2(k+0.5)$ with a duration of 2. Thus, larger values of $|d_{j,k}|$ are associated with time periods in which the data display some sort of “cyclic-type” behavior, with a duration of about 2. The $d_{j,k}$ are referred to as *detail coefficients*. Small values of $j$ are associated with more localized behavior, while the larger values of $j$ are associated with coarser detail behavior (i.e., longer-span behavior).

The partitioning in Equation 12.21 is referred to as a *multiresolution decomposition* (MRD). In the case $j_0=4$ (i.e., $n=16$) considered in Example 12.3 which follows, this decomposition becomes

```
$x_i = D_1(t) + D_2(t) + D_3(t) + D_4(t) + S_4(t)$
```

where the coefficients for $D_j(t), j=1, \dots, 4$ are obtained in the $j^{th}$ step of the pyramid algorithm. If the pyramid algorithm obtained in Step 3, then 16 coefficients $d_{1,k} k=1, \dots, 8; d_{2,k} k=1, \dots, 4; d_{3,k} k=1, 2$; and $s_{4,k} k=1$ have been calculated. Letting $S_j(t) = D_j(t) + S_j(t)$, it follows that $x_i$ can be written as $x_i = D_1(t) + D_2(t) + S_2(t)$, and the coefficients needed for the term $S_2(t)$ are $s_{2,k} k=1,2$ which were obtained in the second step of the pyramid algorithm.

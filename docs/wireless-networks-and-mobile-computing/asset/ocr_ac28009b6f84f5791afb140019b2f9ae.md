We now make the following observations:

**Observation 1.** *The application of the reduction rules 1 and 2 on the binary data during transmission ensures that the digit patterns $\overline{1}\overline{1}$, $1\overline{1}$ and $\overline{1}1$ do not occur in the transmitted data.*

**Observation 2.** *If the original data was an $n$-bit binary data frame, RBNS encoding can result in a frame of size $n+1$ RBNS digits.*

**Observation 3.** *The encoding and decoding processes need to scan from the least significant to the most significant digit position, and these can be conveniently overlapped with a pipelined serial transmission and reception of the digits.*

It is thus clear that the logic complexities for hardware conversion from binary to RBNS and vice versa are minimal. Thus, the increase in delay and energy consumption due to the encoding and decoding logic would be too small to considerably affect the energy savings offered by this scheme.

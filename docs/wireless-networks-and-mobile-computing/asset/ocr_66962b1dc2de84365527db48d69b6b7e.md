The essence of this approach is to partition the given channel assignment problem into a sequence of subproblems, each consisting of a specific subgraph with a uniform demand to be satisfied at each node. To satisfy a uniform demand from each node of the subgraph, we apply a greedy algorithm to first find a single frequency assignment to each node, and then multiple frequencies are assigned on a round-by-round basis (one frequency at each round) using the _increment_ in the starting frequency for the next consecutive round. The concept of _increment_ is such that if frequency φ(v) can be assigned at node v without any conflict with already assigned frequencies, the set of multiple frequencies φ(v)+k×increment for k = 1, 2, 3, ⋯, can also be assigned round by round at node v without conflict. Since s0 is the minimum frequency separation requirement between two calls in the same cell, the value of _increment_ will be at least s0. Thus, the multiple homogeneous demand can be satisfied easily using the single frequency assignment and the value of _increment_. The solution to the original problem will constitute a series of assignment phases. The starting frequency of a particular phase of assignment will be the frequency immediately next to the highest frequency assigned during the previous phase of assignment. When we move from one assignment phase, say phase i, to the next phase i + 1, we must ensure at the start that every node in the subgraph of phase i + 1 is assigned a frequency that does not cause any interference with the frequencies assigned at the last round to the nodes in phase i. It is sufficient to satisfy the last round frequencies assigned to the nodes in phase i while assigning frequencies at phase i + 1 because these are the only frequencies which may conflict with the frequencies of phase i + 1. This is because even the highest frequency assigned in the second last round in phase i and the least frequency in the first round of phase i + 1 are separated by at least i0 since the _increment_ is always greater than or equal to s0.


## 5.4.3 Minimum Slot Requirement for Cellular Networks

Let $D_7^{(2)}(G)$ be the sum of demands of all cells of a cellular Distance 2 clique, $G=(V,E)$, where the cardinality of $V$, $|V|\leq 7$. Then, $D_7^{(2)}(G)=\sum_{i\in G}w_i$, where $w_i$ is the demand from the cell $i$.

**Definition 10.** A *seven-node cellular Distance 2 clique* $G$ or its subgraph is called a *critical block* $CB_7$ which is composed of a maximum of seven cells, such that the sum of the demands of the cells in $CB_7$ is maximal over all possible cellular Distance 2 cliques in the network.

We denote the demand of a critical block by $D_7^{(2)*}$. Thus $D_7^{(2)*}=\max_{\forall G}D_7^{(2)}(G)$. Note that there may be multiple cellular Distance 2 cliques. We first consider the simpler case of homogeneous cell demand where all cells have the same demand.

# 5.4.4 Homogeneous Cell Demand

Let $w$ represent the homogeneous demand for all cells in the network. For $w=1$, the critical block demand $D_7^{(2)*}$ would be seven time slots. Referring to Figure 5.5(a), we see that due to structural symmetry, any Distance 2 clique can be chosen as the critical block. Without loss of generality, let the cellular Distance 2 clique $abcdefg$ be designated as the seven-node critical block, with node $g$ as the central node. Considering now the cellular Distance 2 clique $gbpqrdc$, centered at $c$, we note that

- Node $p$ is at Distance 3 from nodes $e$ and $f$ of $CB_7$. Hence $p$ can be assigned the same time slot as either of these two nodes.

- Node *q* is at Distance 3 from nodes *a*, *f* and *e*. Thus, *q*’s demand can be satisfied from any of these nodes.

- Nodes *a* and *f* are at Distance 3 from *r*.

A particular assignment may assign node *p* the same time slot as that of node *e*, while nodes *q* and *r* may be assigned the time slots of nodes *f* and *a*, respectively.

Thus, we find that the demand of the cellular Distance 2 clique *gbpqrdc* can be satisfied completely by the time slots assigned to the critical block. Figure 5.5(b) depicts a possible assignment scheme for the cellular graph of Figure 5.5(a). We now state the following results.

**Lemma 20.** For any given unsatisfied node *u* adjacent to one or more satisfied cellular Distance 2 cliques, it is always possible to find a satisfied node *v* at a Distance 3 from *u* such that the slot assigned to *v* is unused within Distance 2 of *u*.

**Proof.** By definition, a cellular Distance 2 clique is a seven-node subgraph of the cellular graph such that no node in the clique is more than Distance 2 from one another. Hence, considering a node *u* that is adjacent to some node *v* of the clique, it is easy to see that there must be at least two nodes in the cellular Distance 2 clique that are at a Distance 3 from *u*. Hence, *u* can be assigned the time slot corresponding to any of these Distance 3 nodes without violating the slot separation constraints.

Based on Lemma 20 we now demonstrate the minimum number of slots requirement of a cellular graph with homogeneous demand of one slot per cell in the form of the following theorem.

# **Theorem 20.** *The minimum slot requirement of a cellular graph with two-cell buffering having s₀ = s₁ = s₂ and a homogeneous demand of one time slot per cell is equal to the demand of the seven-node critical block of the graph.*

**Proof.** Let G* be the critical block of the network with node *g* as the central node. Let G be a cellular Distance 2 clique adjacent to G*, whose nodes have to be assigned time slots. As G is adjacent to G*, the four common nodes of G and G* have already assigned time slots. From Lemma 20, for the remaining nodes in G we observe that the symmetry of the cellular graph implies that for any node *i* in G, it is always possible to find at least one satisfied node *j* at Distance 3 from *i* such that the time slot assigned to *j* is unused within Distance 2 of node *i*. Hence, G can be satisfied using only the time slots from G*.

The above procedure can be repeated for all cellular Distance 2 cliques adjacent to G* to obtain a collision-free schedule using only seven time slots. By exploiting the symmetry of the cellular graph, this pattern can be repeated over the network by considering unsatisfied cellular Distance 2 cliques adjacent to each satisfied cellular Distance 2 clique.

+   **Step 1:** Assign slot numbers $(0, w - 1)$ to the central cell of the critical block.

+   **Step 2:** Assign slot numbers $(iw, (i + 1)w - 1) \bmod 7w$, $i \ge 1$ to the $i^{th}$ cell to the right of the central cell along a particular direction, say along the horizontal line as shown in Figure 5.5(b). That is, we assign the increasing order slot numbers $(0, w - 1), (w, 2w - 1), \ldots, (6w, 7w - 1)$ repeatedly to the cells to the right of the central cell along the horizontal direction.

+   **Step 3:** Assign slot numbers $(-iw, -(i - 1)w - 1) \bmod 7w$, $i \ge 1$ to the $i^{th}$ cell to the left of the central cell. That is, we assign the decreasing order slot values $(7w - 1, 6w), (6w - 1, 5w), \ldots, (w - 1, 0)$ repeatedly to the cells to the left of the central cell.

+   **Step 4:** For rows below the central cell, shift the $(0, w - 1)$ slot value 3 cells to the *left* and then repeat Steps 2 and 3 to obtain a slot assignment for each such row.

+   **Step 5:** For rows above the central cell, shift the $(0, w - 1)$ slot value 3 cells to the *right* and then repeat Steps 2 and 3 for each such row.

# 5.4.5  Heterogeneous Cell Demand

We now consider the general case of SAP, where cells have different demands, i.e., ∃ wᵢ, wⱼ, i ≠ j, such that wᵢ ≠ wⱼ. The seven-node critical block is insufficient to determine the optimal number of slots of the cellular graph, as demonstrated below.

**Example 24.** *Consider the cellular graph in Figure 5.7. The numbers in parentheses beside each cell denote the demand of the cell. The cellular Distance 2 clique abfjhde has a demand of 62 slots. The subgraphs bcgjief and abcfe have demands of 61 and 62 time slots, respectively. Thus, we see two candidate critical blocks in the network: subgraph abfjhde or subgraph abcfe. We arbitrarily choose abfjhde as a seven-node critical block. For the Distance 2 clique bcgjief adjacent to the critical block, cells g and j can have their demands satisfied from the slots assigned to the cells a and d. However, the demand of cell c (w_c = 12) is greater than the slots assigned to its two Distance 3 neighbors, d and h of the seven-node critical block. The demand sum of cells d and h, w_d + w_h = (4 + 6) < w_c = 12. Hence, it is necessary to assign slots in addition to those assigned to the critical block to satisfy the demand of cell c.

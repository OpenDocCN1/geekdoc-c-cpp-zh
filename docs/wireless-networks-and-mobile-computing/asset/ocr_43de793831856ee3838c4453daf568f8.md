# 8.5.3.5 **Effect of Silent Symbols on Energy Savings**

From Equation (8.46), we get the required peak transmitter power for a given BER value, which corresponds to the scenario where the transmitter is switched on for the whole of message transmission. However, in the CNS scheme, the transmitter will be put in *active* or *idle* state during the $N$ symbols, and hence the required SNR value for a given BER will be less than the value demanded by Equation (8.46). We keep the CNS symbol duration same as that of the original bit duration in the binary message. Assuming equal probability of occurrence of the symbols $A$, $B$, $C$ and $N$, 25% of the time the transmitter consumes negligible power (while transmitting the symbol $N$). The required CNS transmitter power will be three-fourths of the values as required by Equation (8.46). Noting that $10\log_{10}(3/4)\,\mathrm{dB}=-1.25\,\mathrm{dB}$ implies that the required SNR values for CNS will be reduced from the values given in Table 8.16 by 1.25 dB.

According to this association policy [1], users associating to an AP providing the maximum throughput may change to another AP with maximum RSSI depending on the available bandwidth. The drawback is that the change may also lead to an imbalance in the load distribution.



In [8], a numerical approach based on stochastic dynamic programming proposes to find the optimal association for a network having a small topology consisting of two APs only. Using the insights from the association rule for the two-AP problem, a near-optimal heuristic named RAT, has been proposed. An objective function is defined based on both rate and throughput and then an STA associates with that AP for which is objective function is minimized.



The AP selection and channel allocation are two fundamental access control problems. For users in the coverage area of one AP, this involves only channel allocation. However, for users that belong in the intersection of coverage areas of more than one AP, the selection of an appropriate AP to establish the connection is also important. Iordanis et al. [16] proposed a joint approach of AP selection and channel assignment with a view to satisfying a given user load vector with a minimum number of channels. The authors formulated this joint problem as a clique load balancing problem and using this as a guideline, a heuristic algorithm has been proposed.

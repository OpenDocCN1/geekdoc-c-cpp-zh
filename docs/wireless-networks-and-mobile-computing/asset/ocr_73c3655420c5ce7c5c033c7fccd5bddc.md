Such protocols with efficient collision resolution mechanisms would provide low end-to-end latency and low overhead for retransmission of the collided messages. Most existing broadcast and gossiping algorithms do not address these issues together.

In this section, we present deterministic broadcast and gossiping algorithms as proposed [13] for mobile ad hoc networks that have low broadcast latencies. The algorithms presented here assume collision detection capabilities at the mobile terminals.

The deterministic broadcast algorithm is based on breadth-first traversal of the nodes of the network. The algorithm allows *simultaneous transmission* by multiple nodes. Although this may result in collisions at the receiving nodes, the algorithm can detect and take corrective measures to eventually avoid collisions. The idea of breadth-first broadcast was first proposed in [7, 15] where the two different types of rounds of transmission, *control round* and *message round*, made the protocol somewhat complex. However, in [13], only one type of round of transmission has been used. The algorithm presented in citesinhA08 works for scenarios where network topologies change frequently due to high mobility of the nodes and takes $O(n \log n)$ time in the worst case. Both the average case and worst case performances of this breadth-first algorithm are better than the *polylog broadcast* algorithm proposed in [3]. Simulation results show that the time complexity of this breadth-first algorithm on an average is upper bounded by $8D \log n$. The reported simulation results were obtained for networks having 100 nodes to 1024 nodes. It may be noted that the asymptotic lower bound for deterministic broadcast in networks where spontaneous transmission by the nodes is not allowed is on average $\Omega(D \log n)$ [5].

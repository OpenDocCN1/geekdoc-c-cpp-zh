The task of assigning frequency channels to the cells satisfying the interference constraints and using as little bandwidth as possible is known as the *channel assignment problem* (CAP). In its most general form, the CAP is equivalent to the generalized graph coloring which is a well known NP-complete problem [10, 11, 12]. # 1.1.6 Ad Hoc Networks

Based on the application scenario, the common ways to design wireless networks are: i) infrastructure-based networks, ii) infrastructure-less networks and iii) hybrid networks which uses a mix of both infrastructure and infrastructure-less network components. The cellular mobile network discussed above is an example of an infrastructure-based network where base stations are the existing infrastructures in the network. Absence of such base stations in a network leads to an infrastructure-less network of which the ad hoc network is an example. In such an ad hoc network, the component telephone devices themselves create a communicating network of their own, without the support of any base station. Such ad hoc networks may be static (where the component units may not change their geographical positions) or dynamic (the component telephone units may be mobile, changing their geographical positions with time). An ad hoc network of mobile telephone devices or handsets is commonly known as a *mobile ad hoc network (MANET)*. Nodes in an ad hoc network usually communicate through a single channel by means of broadcasting or multicasting of data over the network.

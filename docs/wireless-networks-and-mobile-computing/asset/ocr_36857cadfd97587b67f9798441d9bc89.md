We next consider the assignment of the whole network with the frequencies explicitly in the interval [origin,origin + increment), satisfying all the frequency separation criteria. The way the value of _increment_ will be computed, the frequencies in the interval [origin,origin + increment) will definitely cover the assignment of all the highest demand nodes, but may or may not be able to cover the other nodes of the entire network. Let _V_ be the set of nodes covered with frequencies in [origin,origin + increment) in this phase of assignment and \(\gamma_{max}\) be their highest demand, and also let \(V'\) be the set of nodes left out and \(\gamma'_{max}\) be their highest demand. Let \(\gamma_{max} - \gamma'_{max} = w\). We then repeat the assignment of the nodes in _V_ for _w_ rounds, avoiding any interference, so as to satisfy _w_ demands of each of the nodes in _V_, and reducing their highest demand to \(\gamma'_{max}\). For those nodes in _V_ whose actual demand is less than the value of _w_, the assignment will be repeated for rounds equal to their respective actual demands. This will ensure that the highest demand node in \(V'\) will now move to the set of highest demand nodes of the entire network. As a result, in the next phase of assignment, this node must be covered by the algorithm. We will now consider the residual demand vector after reducing _w_ or the actual demand corresponding to the nodes in _V_ and start the second phase of assignment by setting the value of _origin_ equal to the frequency immediately next to the highest frequency channel used in this first phase of assignment. The entire process will be repeated until the residual demand vector becomes a vector of 0. We now present the assignment algorithm for a particular phase of assignment.

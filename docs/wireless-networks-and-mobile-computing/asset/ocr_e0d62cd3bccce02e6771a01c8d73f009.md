# 1.3.1.1  Call Setup

In order to set up a call from a mobile terminal (MT) to another MT, two distinct activities are involved: i) location registration and ii) call delivery. These two activities are known as location management functions. Each mobile terminal is required to update its location information periodically or at least when it changes its location. For this purpose, usually a number of cells are grouped together to form a location area (LA) and a mobile switching center (MSC) controls several such LAs. Whenever an MT moves from one LA to another LA, the MT initiates a request to its current MSC for updating the location databases. On the other hand, call delivery involves querying the location databases to find the current location of the called MT. We discuss these two activities in detail in Chapter 2.

# 1.3.1.2  Handoff Management

When an MT changes its position from one cell to another cell, the strength of the signal received by it from the base station (BS) of the old cell becomes less than that from the base station of the new cell. Accordingly, the control of this MT is to be handed over from its old BS to the new BS. A smooth handoff of control of the MT from the old BS to the new one without hampering the ongoing communication with this MT, if any, is known as handoff management. In other words, handoff or handover management enables the network to maintain a user’s connection when the user (MT) continues to move and changes its access point from one BS to another BS. There are three distinct stages for handoff: i) handoff initiation, ii) new connection generation and iii) data flow control.

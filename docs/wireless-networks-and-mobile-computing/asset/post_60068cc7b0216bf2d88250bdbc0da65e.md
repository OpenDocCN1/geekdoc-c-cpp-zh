# 8.5.2.1   Representation of Encoded Numbers

From Section 8.5.1, we see that the optimal encoding scheme corresponds to \(k = 2\). Therefore, starting from the least significant bit (lsb) position of the binary data frame, every bit pair (00, 01, 10 or 11) can be assumed to represent a CNS symbol. We shall refer to the four CNS symbols as *A*, *B*, *C* and *N* and the mapping of the bit pairs to the symbols as *bit pair to symbol mapping* or *BpS Mapping*. *N* stands for *null* to indicate communication through silent time periods or null signal on the channel. As an example, for a particular scenario, the bit pairs 00, 01, 10 and 11 may be mapped to the symbols *N*, *A*, *B* and *C*, respectively. Starting from the lsb of the binary data frame to be transmitted, the system would then read every successive bit pair to determine whether the corresponding transmitted CNS symbol is to be *A*, *B*, *C* or *N* and do the reverse mapping at the receiver to reconstruct the original binary string.

For the CNSTransmit procedure, data_frame refers to the binary string of length frame_len that needs to be transmitted. The transmitter first determines the BpS mapping of symbols, using either Scheme 1 or Scheme 2 described above. For Scheme 2, the BpS mapping needs to be transmitted for every frame, while for Scheme 1, transmitting just once for an entire session is sufficient. Using this mapping, the transmitter then interprets every bit pair, starting from the lsb and transmits the corresponding symbol sym.

On the other side, the receiver first receives the BpS mapping of the symbols from the transmitter, once for the entire session for Scheme 1 or with every frame for Scheme 2.

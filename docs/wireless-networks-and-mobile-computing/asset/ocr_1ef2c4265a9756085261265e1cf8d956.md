The technique of clustering and routing requires changing the clusters and the cluster heads dynamically. After certain time, a cluster head may be depleted of its energy and will no longer have the maximum residual energy among all nodes in the cluster. Also, the network connectivity may be changed if the nodes are mobile, energy of a node is so much depleted that it is disconnected from the network, or new nodes may have joined since the last computation of the clusters (and corresponding cluster heads). These situations should invoke computing the clusters afresh, depending on the current status of the network, e.g., network connectivity, residual energy of nodes, and transmission range of nodes.

An alternative approach for energy-efficient message routing is the use of a connected dominating set (CDS) as the backbone network, instead of the cluster heads forming the backbone network in the earlier technique. In this approach, we first construct a network graph \( G(V, E) \) with \( V \) as the set of vertices and \( E \) as the set of edges from the network. Corresponding to each node in the network, there is a vertex in the set \( V \), and two vertices will have an undirected edge between them if the corresponding two nodes of the network are within the transmission range of each other. We assume, for simplicity, that the transmission range of each node is the same. A dominating set \( DS \) of the graph \( G(V, E) \) consists of a subset of \( V \) such that every vertex of the graph is in \( DS \) or adjacent (at Distance 1) from at least one vertex in \( DS \). However, the induced graph on this dominating set is not connected and accordingly the nodes corresponding to this dominating set cannot constitute a backbone network.

Based on the way the value of $increment_1$ is computed, the frequencies in the interval $[origin, origin + increment_1)$ will not only cover the assignment of all the $\beta_h$ nodes having $\alpha_h$ demand but also may cover some of the other nodes of the network. Let there exist at least one among the $\beta_{h-1}$ nodes of demand $\alpha_{h-1}$ which is left out. In such a case, the maximum demand in $V'$ will be $\alpha_{h-1}$. Hence the number of rounds in the first phase will be $\alpha_h-\alpha_{h-1}=y_1$ and the bandwidth requirement of this first phase will be $(2s_1+6s_2)+increment_1\times y_1$. In general, in the $i$-th phase, all the $\beta_h+\beta_{h-1}+\cdots+\beta_{h-(i-1)}$ nodes having demands $\alpha_h,\alpha_{h-1},\cdots,\alpha_{h-(i-1)}$ must be covered and accordingly $increment_i$, the increment in the $i$-th phase, will be computed and the number of rounds in this phase will be $\alpha_{h-(i-1)}-\alpha_{h-(i-2)}=y_i$. There can be at most $h$ phases and finally the bandwidth requirement becomes $(2s_1+6s_2)+\sum_{i=1}^{h}(increment_i\times y_i)$. We note that $s_0\le increment_i\le max(s_0,3s_1+6s_2)\ \forall i$. Hence, $(2s_1+6s_2)+s_0(\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_i)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+\sum_{i=1}^{h}(increment_i\times y_i)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+max(s_0,3s_1+6s_2)(\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_i)$. Note that $\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_i=\alpha_h-1$ and hence $(2s_1+6s_2)+s_0(\alpha_h-1)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+\sum_{i=1}^{h}(increment_i\times y_i)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+max(s_0,3s_1+6s_2)(\alpha_h-1)$. The worst case situation occurs when all nodes have equal demands ($h=1$) and the greedy algorithm produces the worst possible ordering for finding a single frequency assignment to each node of the entire network.

The lower bound on bandwidth requirement of the cellular network having homogeneous demands of $w$ channels per node can be found from the results reported in [29]. From Theorems 1 and 2 in [29], it can be verified [50] that the worst case ratio of the bandwidth required by the above greedy algorithm to the optimal bandwidth is always less than or equal to 5/3. However, where 1) $s_0 \geq (s_1 + 5s_2)$ with $s_2 \leq s_1 \leq 2s_2$, and 2) $s_0 \geq (2s_1 + 3s_2)$ with $s_1 \geq 2s_2$, this ratio becomes 10/7. The benchmark Problems 3, 4, 7, 8, 10 and 11 all fall under one of these two cases.

It should be noted that the above analysis is based on a conservative estimate of the increment (taking it always to be the maximum) and thus the above ratio is a very loose upper bound on the deviation of the required bandwidth from optimality. However, computing the average case approximation ratio appears to be very difficult and is left as an open problem.

## 3.7  Conclusion

We have also shown that this methodology can easily be applied to the cases of non-homogeneous demands.

We explained an elegant technique for solving the channel assignment problem (CAP) which is applicable even to a cellular network of non-hexagonal structure. The assignment is performed for a given bandwidth $B$ with a view to minimizing call blocking.

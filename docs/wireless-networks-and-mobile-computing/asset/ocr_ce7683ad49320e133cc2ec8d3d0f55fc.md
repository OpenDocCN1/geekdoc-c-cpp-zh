Therefore, any two frequencies assigned to two nodes of the subgraph must be separated by at least $s_2$. Suppose the frequency channel $y$ ($\in [0,p]$) is assigned to any node of the subgraph. Then to satisfy the interference criteria, no frequency channel within the open interval $(y-s_2,y)$ and $(y,y+s_2)$ can be assigned to any node of the subgraph (including the node itself, since $s_0 \ge s_2$). That is, there would be an unusable gap of $(y-s_2,y)$ before $y$, and another such gap of $(y,y+s_2)$ after $y$ on the frequency spectrum line where gap $(a,b)$ implies that the integers within the open interval $(a,b)$ cannot be assigned to any node of the subgraph. We refer to $(b-a)$ as the length of gap $(a,b)$. Obviously, for $y=0$ (or $p$), one of these gaps of unusable frequencies, e.g., $(y-s_2,y)$ or $(y,y+s_2)$ will be beyond the interval $[0,p]$.

In addition, the central node $d$ is Distance 1 apart from each of the other nodes of the subgraph. Suppose, during the assignment, a frequency channel $x$ ($\in [0,p]$) is assigned to the central node $d$. To satisfy the interference criteria, no frequency channel within the open intervals $(x-s_1,x)$ and $(x,x+s_1)$ can be assigned to any node of the subgraph including the central node itself, since $s_0 \ge s_1$. That is, there must be unusable gaps $(x-s_1,x)$ and $(x,x+s_1)$ before and after $x$, respectively. If $x=0$ (or $p$), one of these gaps of unusable frequencies, e.g., $(x-s_1,x)$ or $(x,x+s_1)$ falls beyond the interval $[0,p]$.

Since $s_1 \ge s_2$, we can have more usable frequency channels if the minimum and maximum frequencies are assigned to the central node $d$, rather than to any other node.

It is always possible to assign either the minimum frequency or the maximum frequency to the central node $d$, but depending on the relative values of $s_0$, $s_1$ and $s_2$, it may or may not be possible to assign both of these to the central node for keeping $p$ minimum.

Based on the way the value of $increment_1$ is computed, the frequencies in the interval $[origin, origin + increment_1)$ will not only cover the assignment of all the $\beta_h$ nodes having $\alpha_h$ demand but also may cover some of the other nodes of the network. Let $V$ be the set of nodes covered with frequencies in $[origin, origin+increment_1)$ and $V'$ be the set of nodes left out. Let there exist at least one among the $\beta_{h-1}$ nodes of demand $\alpha_{h-1}$ which is left out. In such a case, the maximum demand in $V'$ will be $\alpha_{h-1}$. Hence the number of rounds in the first phase will be $\alpha_h-\alpha_{h-1}=y_1$ and the bandwidth requirement of this first phase will be $(2s_1+6s_2)+increment_1\times y_1$. In general, in the $i$-th phase, all the $\beta_h+\beta_{h-1}+\cdots+\beta_{h-(i-1)}$ nodes having demands $\alpha_h,\alpha_{h-1},\cdots,\alpha_{h-(i-1)}$ must be covered and accordingly $increment_i$, the increment in the $i$-th phase, will be computed and the number of rounds in this phase will be $\alpha_{h-(i-1)}-\alpha_{h-(i-2)}=y_i$. There can be at most $h$ phases and finally the bandwidth requirement becomes $(2s_1+6s_2)+\sum_{i=1}^{h}(increment_i\times y_i)$. We note that $s_0\le increment_i\le max(s_0,3s_1+6s_2)\ \forall i$. Hence, $(2s_1+6s_2)+s_0(\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_i)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+\sum_{i=1}^{h}(increment_i\times y_i)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+max(s_0,3s_1+6s_2)(\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_i)$. Note that $\sum_{i=1}^{h}y_i=\alpha_h-1$ and hence $(2s_1+6s_2)+s_0(\alpha_h-1)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+\sum_{i=1}^{h}(increment_i\times y_i)\le (2s_1+6s_2)+max(s_0,3s_1+6s_2)(\alpha_h-1)$. The worst case situation occurs when all nodes have equal demands ($h=1$) and the greedy algorithm produces the worst possible ordering for finding a single frequency assignment to each node of the entire network.

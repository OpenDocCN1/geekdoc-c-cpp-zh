To estimate the effect of device characteristics on energy savings, we consider the following examples using two commercially available popular chips:

**Example 35.** *For the CC1100 chip supporting O-QPSK modulation, we have* \(I_{low}=1.9\,mA\) *and* \(I_{tran}=8.7\,mA\). *At 868/915 MHz, values of* \(I_{high}\) *are 30.3 mA, 19.7 mA, 16.6 mA and 14.0 mA for 10 dBm, 5 dBm, 0 dBm and −5 dBm output power respectively. Also,* \(t_{rise}=88.4\,\mu s\) *and assuming a data rate of 2.5 Kbps, we have* \(t_p=400\,\mu s\). *Thus,* \(t_{rise}/t_p\) *may be taken as 0.221. Hence, from Equation (8.35), we get* \(\gamma'=61.1\%, 60.34\%, 59.94\%\) *and* \(59.46\%\) *for 10 dBm, 5 dBm, 0 dBm and −5 dBm output power, respectively. Thus,* \(\gamma'\) *is only marginally reduced from the theoretical value of 62.5% as given by Observation 5. The results are summarized in Table 8.12.*

**Example 36.** *For the CC2420 chip,* \(I_{low}=0.426\,mA\), \(I_{tran}\approx I_{low}\) *and values of* \(I_{high}\) *are 17.4 mA, 14 mA, 11 mA, 9.9 mA and 8.5 mA at 0 dBm, −5 dBm, −10 dBm, −15 dBm and −25 dBm output power respectively.* \(t_{rise}=0.1\,\mu s\). *As in the case of CC1100, we assume that* \(t_p=400\,\mu s\). *Hence, from Equation (8.35), we get* \(\gamma'=62.19\%, 62.12\%, 62.02\%, 61.96\%\) *and* \(61.87\%\) *for 0 dBm, −5 dBm, −10 dBm, −15 dBm and −25 dBm output power, respectively. The results are summarized in Table 8.13.*

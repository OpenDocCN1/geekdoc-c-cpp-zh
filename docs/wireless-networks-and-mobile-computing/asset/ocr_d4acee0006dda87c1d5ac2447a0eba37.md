In general, a radio in *transmit* or *receive* state consumes the maximum power while in *active* and *idle* states, it consumes very little power. Typically, the radio consumes the least power in idle state but takes longer to switch to transmit or receive state from idle state than from the active state. Thus, given a biased statistics of the symbols after encoding the message, one might choose to transmit the stream of $m$ symbols through an appropriate digital modulation scheme in which the transmitter is kept silent during the occurrence of the dominant symbol. During these silence intervals, the transmitter can save significant energy. The family of phase and frequency modulations such as phase-shift keying (PSK), frequency-shift keying (FSK), $m$-ary PSK, $m$-ary FSK and their variants are superior to the amplitude-shift keying (ASK) and $m$-ary ASK techniques. When one chooses to employ $m$-ary PSK/FSK modulation along with silent intervals for the dominant symbol which corresponds to an ASK transmission, the instantaneous power levels of the $m-1$ non-silent symbols may have to be increased appropriately due to the higher SNR requirements of ASK for the same bit error rate (BER) value compared to $m$-ary PSK/FSK. Thus, for a successful energy-efficient communication, the difference between the energy saved due to silence and the additional energy needed for the increased instantaneous power for $m$-ary PSK/FSK needs to be positive.

Let $V_{cc}$ be the supply voltage and $I_{high}$ and $I_{low}$ denote currents drawn in the *transmit* (TX) and *idle* states, respectively. Let $I_0 = I_{high} - I_{low}$. We assume that the device moves from idle to TX state in time $t_{rise}$ and during this transient period draws a current $I_{tran}$. Also, let $t_p$ denote the duration of a symbol ($A$, $B$, $C$ or $N$) which is sufficiently larger than the switching time $t_{rise}$. Typically, for the CC1100 chip, the value of $I_{tran}$ is 8.7 mA. Similarly, when the device moves from the TX state to the idle state, it takes a finite time, say, $t_{fall}$. Usually $t_{fall}$ is very small. For example, for the CC1100 or CC2420 chips, the device moves from TX state to idle state very quickly drawing insignificant extra energy over that in the idle state. We therefore neglect the energy consumed by the device during this $t_{fall}$ time.

The energy expenditure for transmitting the equivalent radix 4 data frame using the CNS protocol can be represented as the sum of the following three terms:

+   1. A base energy value $E_{base}$ that is needed throughout the duration of the transmission, and is equal to the energy required when the device is in the *idle* state. $$E_{base}=\frac{n}{2}2^n t_p I_{low}V_{cc}$$

delay is important and should be very small – not perceptible by human audio recognition systems; otherwise it may give rise to unwanted repetition of a message by the sender, often overlapping with the reply message from the receiver. Also, delay jitter in sending voice signals (e.g., using voice-over IP (VoIP)) is equally annoying to a human audio recognition system if different parts of the same sentence arrive with different lengths of intermittent delay. But it may be possible to compromise with the reliability of communication of voice signals to some extent. For example, if one or two bits of a voice packet are received erroneously, the human ear can possibly tolerate that and if not, the person receiving the message can ask the sender to repeat the message once again. This may also apply to communicating video signals.

On the other hand, for text data communication, delay and delay jitter are relatively unimportant, but reliability is extremely important. Quick allocation of channels to a requesting user for a specific purpose needs to be done by the network. Also, usage of minimum bandwidth for effecting the desired communication over the network is a direct concern for the designers of the network system; the network must provide uninterrupted service to the maximum possible number of users with the available bandwidth resource.

Due considerations need to be given for developing robust communication protocols for maintaining the desired QoS under all dynamic network conditions as discussed above that may arise from mobility of the component nodes.

In this chapter, we discuss various communication protocols used in ad hoc networks (stationary or mobile) for different types of communication requirements, e.g., broadcast, gossiping and point-to-point communication among the nodes in the network. Broadcast is a process by which a source node transmits a message to be received by every node in the network. On the other hand, if every node has a different message to be sent to all other nodes in the network, the situation is called gossiping [1].

While discussing the protocols for all these communication requirements, we assume that the nodes in an ad hoc network all communicate over only one single channel. Under this assumption, it is likely that when one node is trying to send a message to its neighbors (nodes within hearing zone of the transmitting node), other nodes may also transmit through the common channel during the same time. This may lead to a _collision_ of two or more messages at the receiving end. Because of mobility and limited battery power of the nodes, it is important to have fast, energy-efficient and deterministic (as opposed to randomized) broadcast and gossiping protocols for ad hoc networks by resolving collisions efficiently. Such protocols with efficient collision resolution mechanisms would provide low end-to-end latency and low overhead for retransmission of the collided messages. We first discuss the broadcast problem, followed by descriptions of gossiping and point-to-point routing protocols.

A cordless microphone has no physical cable connecting it directly to the sound recording or amplifying equipment with which it is associated [3]. This is also alternatively known as a radio microphone or a wireless microphone. Its body contains a small, battery-powered radio transmitter which transmits the audio signal from the microphone by modulated radio waves to a nearby radio receiver unit, which recovers the audio from the received radio signal by appropriate demodulation technique. The rest of the audio equipment is connected to the receiver unit by a cable. Cordless microphones are widely used in the entertainment industry, television broadcasting, seminars and interviews to allow the audience to speak and also the interviewers or performers or entertainers to move about freely while using a microphone to amplify their voices.

There are many different standards, frequencies and transmission technologies used to replace the microphone’s cable connection and make it into a cordless or wireless device. Users may transmit, for example, radio waves over the UHF (300 MHz to 3 GHz) or VHF (30 MHz to 300 MHz) frequency bands and use FM, AM, or various digital modulation schemes. Some low cost models may, however, use infrared light. Infrared microphones require a direct line of sight communication between the microphone and the receiver, while the costlier radio frequency models do not have such constraints. Some models operate on a single fixed frequency, but the more advanced models operate on a user selectable frequency to avoid interference and allow the use of several microphones in nearby places at the same time.

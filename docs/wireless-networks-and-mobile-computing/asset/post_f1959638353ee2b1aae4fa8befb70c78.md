### 2.2 Call Setup in Mobile IP Networks

All the above discussions on call setup refer to the public land mobile network. The location registration and call delivery in a mobile IP network, however, works in a different manner *without the use of any location databases* as in a PLMN. The standards for communication in a mobile IP network have been developed by the IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force) [18]. In such a network, mobile terminals in the same subnetwork can send their packets directly, but the terminals belonging to different subnetworks must send their packets through IP nodes or routers that perform the switching functions.

![](img/6578f6fc95ac71d2c7109e594d9df12f_77_0.png)

Standards allow the packets to be sent by a mobile node (MN) without interruption to another destination MN in some other subnetwork even when the source MN is moving from one subnetwork to another. (Note that the term MN is used instead of MT in order to follow the mobile IP conventions.) When an MN resides in its own registered area, it accesses the Internet through a home agent (HA) which is a router in its own subnetwork (where it is originally registered). When the MN moves to a different subnetwork, it accesses the Internet through a foreign agent (FA) which is a router in its currently visiting subnetwork. The destination node which receives the packet from this MN is called the correspondent node (CN). The essence of the mobile IP network architecture is illustrated in Figure 2.10 [1]. As shown in the figure, the MN has a home agent in its own subnetwork (subnet A) through which it accesses the Internet. When the MN moves to a different subnetwork (subnet B in the figure), it will have a foreign agent in subnet B through which it will access the Internet. The communication between the MN and the correspondent node is now effected through the MN’s HA and the current FA.

![](img/6578f6fc95ac71d2c7109e594d9df12f_78_0.png)

When an MN moves from one subnet to another, it has to register its new IP address (in the newly visited subnet) with its home agent. Thus, in effect, there are two IP addresses associated with every MN: one is the home address for its identification and the other is for finding its current location. The latter address is known as its *care of address (CoA)*. The association between the current CoA and the MN’s home address is maintained by some mobility binding rule with some lifetime period for this binding. Any packet destined for this MN is routed using the current CoA. The lifetime period of the binding with the CoA is determined at the time of MN’s registration at its home agent and after this period, this registration is deleted. The MN must re-register with its HA before the expiry of this time period to ensure continuous service (e.g., without interrupting ongoing calls) using this CoA. Thus, the MN may need to send a registration request message even when it is in its home subnet, i.e., the registration message may be sent directly to its HA (when it is still in its own subnet) or through an FA it is currently visiting. ### 2.3 Handoff Management

In Chapter 1, we mentioned three distinct stages for handoff: i) handoff initiation, ii) new connection generation and iii) data flow control. Actually, handoff initiation depends on the received signal strength by the MT from the BS and vice versa. This leads to two types of handoff situations: intracell handoff and intercell handoff. Intracell handoff may be initiated when the user moves even in the same cell, but the signal strength falls below the threshold value due to changed network surroundings (e.g., obstacles or reflecting walls, etc.) for reliable communication to be maintained between the BS and the MT. In this case, after the handoff, the ongoing call to the MT will be continued over a newly allocated channel through which the signal strength received by the MT from the BS and vice versa will be more to sustain a reliable communication. The intercell handoff is initiated when the MT moves from one cell to an adjacent cell so that the signal received from the old BS is of smaller strength than that from another BS in the direction of its movement.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_S &= P_N\left[1-\left(1-e^{-e_{\text{th}}^2/2\sigma^2}\right)^3\right] \\
&\quad + P_A(1-X_1X_2)+P_B(1-X_1X_2) \\
&\quad + P_C(1-X_1X_2).
\end{aligned} \tag{8.44}
$$

Assuming \(P_A=P_B=P_C\) for equal likelihood of the symbols \(A\), \(B\) and \(C\), we get

$$
P_S=P_N\left[1-\left(1-e^{-e_{\text{th}}^2/2\sigma^2}\right)^3\right]+3P_A(1-X_1X_2).
$$

Assuming \(P_N=0.25\) and \(P_A+P_B+P_C=0.75\), we get the following expression for \(P_S\):

$$
P_S=0.25\left[1-\left(1-e^{-e_{\text{th}}^2/2\sigma^2}\right)^3\right]+0.75(1-X_1X_2). \tag{8.45}
$$

Thus, from Equations (8.38) and (8.45), using \(k=2\) for the CNS scheme, the bit error rate is given by

$$
BER=\frac{2}{3}\left[0.25\left[1-\left(1-e^{-e_{\text{th}}^2/2\sigma^2}\right)^3\right]+0.75(1-X_1X_2)\right]. \tag{8.46}
$$

### 8.5.3.5 Effect of Silent Symbols on Energy Savings

However, in the CNS scheme, the transmitter will be put in *active* or *idle* state during the \(N\) symbols, and hence the required SNR value for a given BER will be less than the value demanded by Equation (8.46). We keep the CNS symbol duration same as that of the original bit duration in the binary message. Assuming equal probability of occurrence of the symbols \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) and \(N\), 25% of the time the transmitter consumes negligible power (while transmitting the symbol \(N\)). The required CNS transmitter power will be three-fourths of the values as required by Equation (8.46). ### 8.5.3.6 Effect of Data Compression on Energy Savings

We now consider the effect of data compression on the overall transmitter energy. Let \(P_b\) and \(P_C\) be the transmitter power required for binary FSK and CNS respectively. Also, let \(T_b\), \(T_C\) be the transmission times for binary FSK and CNS, respectively. Since CNS needs half the time for transmission than binary FSK needs, \(T_C=T_b/2\). Noting that \(10^{1.5/10}=1.4125\), we have \(P_C/P_b=1.4125\). Let \(E_b\), \(E_C\) be the transmitter energy requirements for binary FSK and CNS respectively. Hence we have

$$
E_C=P_CT_C=\frac{1.4125}{2}\cdot P_bT_b=0.7063E_b. \tag{8.48}
$$

**Theorem 28.** *Assuming an AWGN channel, for equal probability of occurrence of the symbols \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) and \(N\), CNS requires approximately 70% of the total energy as compared to binary FSK, resulting in a saving of 30% transmitter energy.*

In addition, CNS has the advantage of reducing receiver energy consumption by a factor of two, as the receiver receives all the symbol values in half the time required to transmit the original binary message. For evaluation of energy savings generated by CNS for healthcare applications, we assume the applications involve the transmission of ECG signals, JPEG images of blood samples and ASCII text data representing physiological parameters like blood pressure, blood sugar level, and temperature [1, 23]. The performance of CNS noted below is based on simulation results [1] on 50 real-life ECG signal samples taken from the QT database [27] and the JPEG images of blood, tissue and bone samples [28].

**WSNs for agriculture:** In WSNs deployed for monitoring crop health, information collected through sensor networks in the fields include climate data such as temperature, relative humidity, solar radiation and soil composition. Network architectures typically consist of a network of *acquisition stations* and a *gateway* node. Data acquisition is done through low-cost acquisition stations equipped with wireless transceivers and deployed in a mesh topology [24, 26]. The acquisition stations collect data such as temperature, relative humidity, solar radiation and soil composition, and also detect pest infestation and other data. The usual practice is to collect one sample per minute. Communications are based on low-power radio frequency links provided by inexpensive transceivers. A single gateway node collects the data from the acquisition stations for processing [25]. These data are assumed to be transmitted as short ASCII text messages [25, 26, 24], usually a few bytes long at the most. In a typical field deployment with EbT-based transmissions, considering a mesh network deployment for a field of size 60m × 60m, radio range of 0 to 80m with multi-hop communication, data rate of 30 packets/min = 0.5 packets/s and duty cycle of 15% at 20mA with a battery of 7.2Ah capacity, a radio battery would last for only 100 days [25, 26, 24].

From the simulation results, it follows that \(P_N\) is around 0.4751 for ECG signals, 0.2884 for JPEG data from blood samples and 0.388 for text data, as shown in Table 8.19. From these computations, we find that for ECG signals at the same BER, the energy saving from using CNS for ECG signals as compared to binary FSK will be approximately 50%.

Similarly, the energy savings for transmission of JPEG image files of blood and tissue samples was 33% for CNS, when compared to binary FSK. For text data, the energy saving was 42.6%. All these results are summarized in Table 8.19.

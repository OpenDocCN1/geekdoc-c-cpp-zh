Consider a CAP $P = (X, W, C)$ and an admissible frequency assignment $\Phi$ for $P$. After some time, demands of some of the cells may be increased due to fluctuations that often arise in real-life scenarios and let $W' = (w'_i)$ be this changed demand vector where $w'_i > w_i$ for some or all $i$. Given $\Phi$, an initial admissible frequency assignment, the objective of the *perturbation-minimizing* frequency assignment [17] is to accommodate the additional requirements as specified in $W'$ with minimum changes in the existing assignment.

# 3.1.3 CAP and Generalized Graph Coloring

In its most general form, the channel assignment problem is equivalent to the generalized graph coloring which is a well-known NP-complete problem [3]. The minimum span channel assignment problem can be represented as a graph coloring problem defined on a CAP *graph*. Call $k$ of cell $i$ is represented as node $(ik)$ of a CAP graph and the nodes $(ik)$ and $(jl)$ are connected by an edge with weight $c_{ij}$, if $c_{ij} > 0$. Let us assume that the channels are assigned to the nodes of the CAP graph in a specific order and a node will be assigned the channel corresponding to the smallest integer that will satisfy the frequency separation constraints with all the previously assigned nodes. It can be shown that the ordering of the nodes has strong impact on the required channel bandwidth. Suppose there are $m$ nodes in the CAP graph. Therefore, the nodes can be ordered in $m!$ ways and hence for sufficiently large $m$, it is impractical to find the best ordering by an exhaustive search.

Likewise, an MT may also move from one area to another where the backbone network remains unaltered, but the service provider is changed causing corresponding changes in communication techniques, protocols and service level agreements (e.g., tariffs) between the user and the service provider. For example, different service providers may operate over different frequency bands for message communication, and use different channel access mechanisms. One may use frequency division multiple access (FDMA) to access the channel, while the other may use code division multiple access (CDMA). The issues of compatibility of the mobile devices and the associated communication protocols, and designing the appropriate location registration, call delivery, handoff management techniques for such interoperability of the mobile services need also to be addressed to provide roaming facilities across different service providers. All these issues related to internetworking and interoperability constitute an important area of future research and development for providing the effective global roaming facilities to the mobile terminals.

# 1.3.2 Bandwidth Management

Because of the extreme popularity of mobile telephone systems, the number of cellular mobile users is increasing day by day almost exponentially. With about 200 million listed mobile users in 1997, the number has gone up to more than 6.8 billion in early 2015 and continues to increase at a high rate. Moreover, the users of mobile telephone systems no longer confine their use to voice messages and texts; multimedia signals including audio, still image and video are also communicated through cellular mobile networks. Quite naturally, the bandwidth requirement of the communication channel increases for multimedia signals.

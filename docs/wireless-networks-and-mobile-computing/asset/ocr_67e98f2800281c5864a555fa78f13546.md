GPS, by far the most widely used location sensing technology, has an accuracy of 1 to 5 meters in outdoor environments for 95 to 99% of the time [3]. To determine a ±δ bound on range measurement errors, NLOS error is assumed to be the major contributor for errors in measured ranges. The error due to thermal noise (η_tw in Equation (4.1)) is generally much smaller than the NLOS error component and ignored in the subsequent discussions. In [6], experimental results show that for indoor locations where line of sight exists between the transmitter and the receiver, the zero mean Gaussian model for normalized distance error closely models the NLOS distance error. For the indoor obstructed line of sight (OLOS) case, while the Gaussian model is not suitable, a mixed distribution consisting of zero mean Gaussian and exponential distribution fits the distance error very well.

While it is not possible to know the extent of error introduced in the measured range due to NLOS propagation, it is possible to estimate the maximum NLOS error that can be tolerated so that a transmitted signal is correctly interpreted at the receiver.

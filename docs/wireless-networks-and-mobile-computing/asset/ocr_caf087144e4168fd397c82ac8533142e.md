For non-coherent BFSK, the BER is \( \rho = \frac{1}{2} e^{-\gamma/2} \), where \( \gamma \) is the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). We use different values of frame size in the range \( 8 \leq m \leq 1024 \) and the expressions for \( X_1 \) and \( X_2 \) to obtain SNR values for the TFNS scheme with a non-coherent receiver and the corresponding SNR values for non-coherent BFSK to meet the same \( FER \) values. The results are summarized in Table 8.22; on average, for a given \( FER/m \), the SNR with the TFNS is nearly 2.68 dB higher than that with binary non-coherent FSK.

In the TFNS scheme, the transmitter will be on only during the non-zero symbols (1 and 2). The transmitter will be switched off during the zero symbols in a TFNS-encoded message. Thus, the required average transmitter power for a given BER will be less than the value demanded by Equation (8.64). We know that \( P_0 = 0.44 \) and \( 10 \log_{10}(1 - 0.44) \) dB = -2.518 dB. Hence, the average transmitter power for TFNS will be reduced from the values given in Table 8.22 by 2.518 dB. It follows that the average transmitter power \( P_1 \) for TFNS communication will be greater than the corresponding average power \( P_b \) for non-coherent BFSK by (2.68 - 2.518) dB = 0.162 dB. It makes \( \frac{P_1}{P_b} = 1.038 \). Figure 8.25 plots the scaled peak transmitter power for TFNS and BFSK.

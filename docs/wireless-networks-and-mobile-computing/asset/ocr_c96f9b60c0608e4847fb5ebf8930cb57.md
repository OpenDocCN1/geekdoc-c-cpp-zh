Authors in [6, 41] demonstrated that the bandwidth of the channel plays an important role in the magnitude of errors introduced and that a minimum bandwidth of 100 MHz is required for effective location estimation.

Several authors have attempted to mitigate the effect of NLOS errors [6, 42, 43, 1, 45, 4, 46, 47, 48, 49]. The least squares (LS) algorithm [50, 51], uses the Davidon algorithm [52] to find a location estimate that would minimize the residual of the estimate. The residual weighting algorithm (Rwgh) was proposed in [42] as another way to mitigate the effects of errors in distance measurements caused by NLOS propagations. Authors in [50] compare different geolocation algorithms for indoor environments, assuming the channel characteristics proposed in [6]. Authors in [43] proposed two algorithms for identification and correction of NLOS errors, based on _a priori_ knowledge of the NLOS errors. An NLOS error mitigation algorithm based on maximum likelihood detection has been presented in [53]. Another approach to location estimation was presented in [1], where the authors attempted to distinguish between LOS and NLOS measurements by assuming a statistical distribution of thermal receiver noise and signal characteristics.

Authors in [54] considered the problem of position estimation in large area networks, particularly sensor networks. They based their algorithm on measurement of signal strengths or time difference of arrival of signals and then the use of statistical methods to characterize the separation distances between two nodes. APS [55] is another location estimation algorithm that does not use GPS. However, the use of a hop-by-hop propagation process, such as DV hop or DV distance to propagate location estimates makes it unsuitable for mobile and random graphs.

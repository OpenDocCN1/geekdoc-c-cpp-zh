# 1.1 Applications Involving Wireless Communication

# 1.1.1 Cordless Telephones

A cordless telephone is a replacement of the handset cord of a landline telephone receiver set by means of a radio link so that the handset can be physically detached from the remaining part of the receiver set (may be considered the _base station_ of the cordless telephone) [2]. This modified handset communicates with the base station which, in turn, is connected to a fixed telephone line. The communication range of the handset is usually very small, just sufficient for covering short distances within a room or building. Unlike a corded telephone, a cordless telephone needs supply of power from the mains for its base station to work. The cordless handset is powered by a rechargeable battery, which is charged when the handset is placed on its cradle.

In 1994, digital cordless telephones operating around 900 MHz frequency range were introduced. Use of digital signals enabled the phones to be more secure (decreased eavesdropping) than those using analog signals. In 1995, the digital spread spectrum (DSS) technique was introduced for cordless phones. This technology enabled the digital information between the receiver and the base station of the cordless telephone to be spread in pieces over several frequencies , thereby making it very difficult for anyone to eavesdrop on the cordless conversations.

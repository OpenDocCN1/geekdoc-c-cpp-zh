A variant of freeway mobility model is the *Manhattan mobility model* also proposed in [32], in which the movements follow a Manhattan-like map in the region \(R\) consisting of horizontal and vertical streets as in a city grid. Nodes can move along these streets in both directions. After reaching a point of intersection, a node chooses the direction of its next movement: along the same direction or along the left road or the right road. The velocity of a node will follow the same rules discussed above for the freeway mobility model.

## 2.4.5 Group-based Mobility Model

This model [33, 34] is used to mimic certain real-life situations like movements of a group of tourists in a city or a group of trekkers on a mountain. The idea is that the set of nodes, after being randomly deployed, are divided into clusters or groups with a leader for each such group. The group leaders follow one of the mobility models as discussed above (e.g., random way point mobility model) and the other group members follow the leader with velocities which are small perturbations of that of the leader. However, the model adheres to the provision that when two groups cross each other, any group member may leave the current group and join the other with a certain probability.

The transmitter uses FSK modulation with two frequencies, such as $f_c$ and $f_c + \Delta f$ corresponding to the symbols 1 and 2, respectively, and is switched to *idle* state during the 0’s, when it consumes very little power compared to the *transmit* or *receive* state. Effectively, this is a hybrid modulation scheme involving FSK and ASK. The receiver diagram as shown in Figure 8.10 for RBNSiZeComm can also be used for the TSS scheme based on non-coherent detection and the frequencies $f_c$ and $f_c + \Delta f$ will correspond to the symbols 1 and 2, respectively. Also, $q_A$ and $q_B$ will now be the binary outputs of the threshold detectors corresponding to the symbols 1 and 2, respectively. Thus, in this case, the desired output $\hat{s}$ of the receiver can be expressed as follows:

+   i) If $q = 0$, $\hat{s} = 0$, corresponding to $s_A = s_B = 0$.
+   
+   ii) If $q = 1$,

$$\hat{s} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } e_A > e_B,\ (\text{when } s_A = 1, s_B = 0) \\ 2 & \text{if } e_B > e_A,\ (\text{when } s_B = 1, s_A = 0). \end{cases}$$

We consider a message frame containing $n$ binary bits. Corresponding to each such frame, we consider the message frame after converting the binary to ternary form containing symbols 0, 1 and 2, and having a length of $m$ digits, where $n$ and $m$ are related as $m = \lceil n \log 2 / \log 3 \rceil = \lceil 0.6309n \rceil$.

For performing the energy saving analysis of the TSS scheme, again the conventional bit error rate (BER) will not be suitable. Instead, the frame error rate (FER) will be used as in RBNSiZeComm scheme. BER has the limitation that the propagation of errors over the higher significant digits due to an erroneously received ternary symbol cannot be captured appropriately since it considers the transmission of each bit independent of the other bits.

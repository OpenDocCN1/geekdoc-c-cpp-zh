On the other hand, the total bandwidth for signal communication in cellular mobile networks is very limited. Apart from the 100 MHz band from 860 MHz to 960 MHz used by the GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications) system, the 1992 World Administrative Radio Conference in Geneva allocated the 1885 to 2025 MHz and 2100 to 2200 MHz bands — a total of only 230 MHz — for terrestrial and satellite communications. Within these two bands, the satellite band covers 1980 to 2010 MHz and 2170 to 2200 MHz for a total of 60 MHz, while the remaining 170 MHz is for terrestrial mobile communication. The frequency gaps between 2025 to 2110 MHz and beyond 2200 MHz are reserved for other services such as remote sensing, cable TV relay service, space research, etc. In 1995, the World Radio Conference changed the above frequency allocation a bit. The satellite band allocation for Region 2 (the Americas and the Caribbean) was modified to 1990 to 2025 MHz and 2160 to 2200 MHz for a total of 75 MHz, leaving only the other 155 MHz for terrestrial services. This has made the situation for cellular mobile communication in the countries of this region more difficult. Even with some later minor modifications, the end result is that the total bandwidth available for terrestrial uses is very small compared to the huge ever-increasing demand due to a large number of mobile users along with the increasing demand for multimedia communication.

One possible way to overcome this problem of bandwidth crisis is by intelligent re-use of the frequencies at sufficiently distant cells of the cellular network so that these two frequencies do not interfere with each other. Over the past two decades, research results on this topic have appeared in the literature, and yet a number of problems need to be solved to develop an effective algorithm for channel allocation in cellular mobile networks. We will discuss these issues later in this text.

### 1.3.3 Energy Management

Energy is a current buzz word. However, there are two distinct groups of scientists and technologists who are interested in energy. One group focuses on the generation of energy from unconventional sources, and the other group looks for efficient utilization of energy in various real-life applications. Our concern in connection with cellular mobile networks is the same as that of the latter group.

In particular, due to the mobility of the terminals, the mobile devices are operated with batteries having limited energy. Also, often the devices are small and hence the batteries used have small capacities of total energy. This has motivated the researchers to develop energy-efficient communication techniques which may be easily adopted in cellular mobile networks. Energy efficiency has been studied over the past few years for minimizing energy consumption in different layers of the communication protocols, e.g., in the physical layer, MAC layer, network layer, and so on. In the physical layer, for example, one may think of i) designing circuits with minimum number of gates and other electronic components to reduce the total power consumption, ii) selecting appropriate modulation and demodulation techniques for designing low-power transceivers. In the MAC layer, we may try to avoid collisions, or at least keep the collisions as few as possible, because a collision requires re-transmission of the collided message leading to waste of power. We may also try to keep the channel in sleep mode to use less power than in the normal mode and tweak the sleep, listen and awake periods for optimal performance in terms of energy consumption. In the network layer, the messages may be routed through a path requiring minimum hops and hence minimum energy consumption.

We will devote Chapter 8 to energy-efficient communication in various low-power wireless networks.

### 1.3.4 Security

Security in communication is one of the very important aspects to be considered in most real-life applications involving message communication. In particular, the wireless networks are more vulnerable to attacks by intruders than the wired networks. This has given rise to security issues like user authentication, encryption and decryption of messages and jamming. However, often the algorithms designed for these purposes consume a lot of time and energy (through the use of large programs or complex hardware) which may not be suitable for online wireless communication. Research to find suitable techniques for online low-energy secure communication in cellular mobile networks is ongoing.

### 1.4.1 Initialization and Leader Election

The first two issues on the above list pertain only to ad hoc networks due to the absence of any infrastructure in the network as in the cellular mobile systems. In order to establish a communication between two nodes in the network, every node of the network must have a unique identifier or *Id* which should also be known to all other nodes in the network. This assignment of unique *Ids* to the nodes is known as *initialization*. Referring to Figure 1.4, there are seven nodes of the ad hoc network which are given unique *Ids* 1, 2, · · · , 7 by this initialization process. While assigning the unique *Ids*, due consideration must be given to the possibility that any node may drop out (be switched off or the battery may be out of power) any time, or go out of communication range from others; and at any time, any new node may join anywhere in the network. The topology of the network should be determined after every such arrival or departure of a node. In other words, the initialization algorithm must consider such dynamic behavior of the network.

The second issue of leader election as given above is also a specific issue of ad hoc networks in absence of an infrastructure, e.g., a base station in a cellular mobile network. It is often easy to control communication in a network for optimizing other network parameters, e.g., network congestion due to flooding of messages, total energy expenditure on the nodes, and average message communication time, if the network has a so-called *leader* that will take control of message routing with a view to optimizing those parameters.

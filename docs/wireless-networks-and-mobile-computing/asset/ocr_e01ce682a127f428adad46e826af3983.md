In order to bound the error in a computed location caused by ranging errors, we again consider three beacon nodes instead of two, similar to the case of ±ε error in the positional estimates of the beacon nodes. Figure 4.5 depicts how we use three beacon nodes to compute a bound on the estimates of the coordinates of the unknown node *u*, assuming an error of ±δ in the measured ranges. With respect to *B*₁ and *B*₂, we consider a strip of width 4δ placed normal to *B*₁*B*₂, and at a distance *d*₁ from *B*₁. Point *T* will always lie within this strip. Similarly, with respect to another pair of beacon nodes, say *B*₁ and *B*₃, we imagine another strip of width 4δ placed normal to *B*₁*B*₃, over which *T* must also lie. Hence, the actual position of *T* can always be found within a rhombus (shown by solid lines in Figure 4.5) of side \(\frac{4\delta}{\sin\theta}\), where θ is the angle between the lines *B*₁*B*₂ and *B*₁*B*₃. Thus, the side of this rhombus will be bounded to \(4\sqrt{2}\delta\) or less if 45 ≤ θ ≤ 90 degrees, and within 8δ if 30 ≤ θ < 45 degrees. We now have the following lemma:

> **Lemma 10.** *For an error of ±δ in the measured ranges with each of the beacon nodes, the location of an unknown node can be bounded inside a rhombus of side \(\frac{4\delta}{\sin\theta}\) using three beacon nodes (say B₁, B₂ and B₃), where, without loss of generality, θ is the angle between the lines B₁B₂ and B₁B₃.*

All errors were assumed to be positive, following Equation (4.1). An environment was considered with a mix of LOS and obstructed LOS (OLOS) signals. OLOS signals represent the scenario where no LOS communication is present between two nodes and hence all received communication between them is through NLOS propagation. The LOS range errors are drawn from a Gaussian distribution of mean \(\mu\) and standard deviation \(\sigma\), while the OLOS range errors are drawn from an exponential distribution with parameter \(\lambda\) [6]. The parameters \(\mu\), \(\sigma\) and \(\lambda\) characterize the channel characteristics and hence the magnitude of ranging errors. The performance of the above algorithm was evaluated with varying channel characteristics, percentage of LOS/OLOS signals and percentage of reference nodes in the system. For each set of parameters, the experiments were repeated for 100 randomly generated graphs and the areas of the stable regions of residence of all nodes in the system were measured. Figures 4.11 to 4.14 demonstrate the results of the simulation for random graphs of 100 nodes. For example, with \(\lambda = 5.3\), \(\mu = 0.0\), \(\sigma = 0.3\), 80% LOS signals and 25% of the nodes designated as RNs, a median area of 1.98 sq. units and a mean area of 7.008 sq. units were obtained. Figures 4.11(a) and 4.11(b) illustrate the variation of the median and mean areas of the stable regions of residence (in square units) respectively, against \(\lambda\). The median area was chosen to measure as it is less affected by outlier cases. The mean area, on the other hand, may be significantly affected by situations such as a non-reference node \(i\) having only one neighbor (say \(j\)), in which case \(R_i = R_{ij}\) and the area of \(R_{ij}\) can be very small or large, depending on the ranged distance between the two nodes. Figures 4.12(a) and 4.12(b) show the variations of median and mean areas of stable regions of residence against \(\sigma\). Figures 4.13(a) and 4.13(b) demonstrate that the median and mean areas of the stable regions of residence decrease exponentially with increases in percentages of LOS signals.

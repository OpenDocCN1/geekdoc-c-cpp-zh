As noted in Chapter 1, security is an important concern in all applications of wireless communication. There are different possible types of security threats in a wireless communication network, whether cellular, ad hoc, cognitive radio network, or wireless sensor network. Such threats threaten a network in multiple ways. An attacker may be a node of the network (called an intruder), or may be a node outside the network. For example, if we are concerned about message communication amongst the nodes of a MANET, an internal attacker may be a misbehaving or adversary node which is a member of the same network, while an external attacker may be a node which is not an integral part of the network.



Internal attacks may be of several types. For example, a node of the network may misbehave in such a way that it receives all messages coming from its neighbors, but never responds to those messages or never forwards them to their destinations. Thus, effectively, this attacker node behaves like an infinite sink for all messages.

Another kind of internal attack may be a reverse activity in the form of sending unnecessary or duplicate messages to other nodes of the network which would cause undue flooding of the network with useless messages, consuming a lot of communication bandwidth and node energy, thus disrupting or delaying the message communications. Some nodes may become non-operational if they are depleted of their stored battery energy due to undesirable flooding in the network.

# 8.1.2  MAC Layer-Centric Approach

In the MAC layer, we need improved techniques for avoiding collision in grabbing a common channel for communication, reducing collisions of messages and reducing the frequency of message retransmissions. Note that collision leads to retransmission of a collided message which, in turn, involves additional energy that is in a sense wasted. Thus, collision of messages should be avoided or minimized in the designs of energy-efficient communication schemes. Also, in the MAC layer, there is another possible way of conserving energy. Usually, a transmitter may operate in *transmit* mode in which some bits can be transmitted, or *idle* mode. The power required to keep the transmitter in the *idle* mode is usually negligible compared to that used in its *transmit* mode. Because of this difference in power requirements, an intelligent way of saving energy would be to keep the transmitter in *idle* mode when the communication channel is sensed to be idle (i.e., no message to be transmitted), rather than keeping it always in the *transmit* mode. The decision for keeping the transmitter in the *transmit* or *idle* mode is made by the MAC layer of the communication protocol. Thus, a judicious selection (tweaking) of the idle and transmit periods of the transmitter may result in substantial energy savings in the long run. For further study of optimizing data transmissions by reducing collisions and retransmissions, the reader is referred to [7, 15, 13, 8, 14, 20].

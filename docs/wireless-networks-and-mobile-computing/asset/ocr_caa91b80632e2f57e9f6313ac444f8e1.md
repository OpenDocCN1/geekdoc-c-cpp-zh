# 6.1.2.2 Maximum Utility-Based Association

Kumar et al. [2] address an optimal association policy between the $STAs$ and the $APs$ in their ranges in which finding an optimal association policy is considered the maximum utility-based formulation of the problem. The authors note that an $STA$ associates to an $AP$ with a physical bit rate and eventually such an association will yield an $STA$ throughput dependent on the specific $MAC$ scheduling used. They used two $MAC$ scheduling techniques, namely random polling and proportional fair access, to calculate the throughput of an individual association. As per random polling access, the set of $STAs$ associated to a common $AP$ achieves equal individual throughput. If there are $m_j$ $STAs$ $i_1,i_2,\ldots,i_{m_j}$ associated with AP $j$ and these are associated with rate $r_{i_pj}$ where $1 \leq p \leq m_j$, the aggregate uplink throughput obtained by all the $STAs$ served by AP $j$ is of the form:

```
$$\frac{m_j}{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{m_j}\frac{1}{r_{i_pj}}}.$$
```

The individual throughput obtained by the $STA$ from AP $j$ can be expressed as

```
$$t_j=\frac{1}{\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{m_j}\frac{1}{r_{i_pj}}}.$$
```

For fair allocation of user throughput, they used log utility to formulate the aggregate throughput of the network as given by

```
$$\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_j\ln(t_j).$$
```

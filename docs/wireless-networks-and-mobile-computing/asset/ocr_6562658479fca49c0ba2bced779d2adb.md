# 6.1.2.3  Max-Min Fair (MMF) Allocation

One well known association policy [2, 3] is known as the *max-min fair (MMF)* allocation. As stated earlier, the *STAs* are associated to the *APs* in their range by obtaining physical bit rates. A rate vector is *MMF* if there is no other vector for which the rate of some node, say *i*, is higher without the rate of some other node, say *j*, that has a smaller than or equal to rate than *i*, being made lower. *STAs* associated on the basis of *MMF* allocation of their individual data rates may obtain uniform or fair throughput allocation. However, such a policy may lead to a reduced network throughput [3]. Furthermore, instead of the obtained data rate, max-min fair allocation can be formulated with the obtained bandwidth. A system provides a fair service if all users have the same allocated bandwidths. Unfortunately, such a degree of fairness may cause significant reduction of the network throughput, since all users have the same bandwidth allocation as bottleneck users. As described in [3], a bandwidth allocation of a weighted system is called max-min fair if there is no way to increase the bandwidth of a user without decreasing the bandwidth of another user with the same or less normalized bandwidth.

When an *STA* associates to an *AP*, it imposes a load on that *AP*. According to this association policy, an *STA* checks the load incorporated to an *AP* before associating it to the *AP*.

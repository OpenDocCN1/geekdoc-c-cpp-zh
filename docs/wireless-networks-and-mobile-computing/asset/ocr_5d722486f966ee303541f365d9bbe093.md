If the paging delay is unconstrained, the message traffic arising due to polling can be reduced to some extent by sequentially searching the cells in decreasing order of probability that the MT is currently residing in that cell [8]. However, this requires some prior knowledge about the recent history of movements of the MT within that LA. Location updates and paging subject to delay constraints have been considered by various researchers [9, 10] and are essentially based on having an upper bound on the time required for paging. In [9], a distance-based location update mechanism has been assumed and the residing area of the called MT is partitioned into a number of smaller areas. When an incoming call for the MT arrives, all these subareas are polled sequentially. The number of polling areas is limited to some maximum value, say N, so that the total paging delay is limited to the time required for N polling operations. Depending on the mobility pattern of the MT (which may be decided by a mobility model), call arrival rate and maximum delay N, the optimal distance may be computed for distance-based location update. Thus, an optimal scheme for a constrained paging delay scheme with distance-based location update is complex to implement. However, as in [10], if a movement-based location update is used along with the maximum number N of polling operations to limit the paging delay, the implementation of the overall scheme for location update and paging becomes relatively simple.

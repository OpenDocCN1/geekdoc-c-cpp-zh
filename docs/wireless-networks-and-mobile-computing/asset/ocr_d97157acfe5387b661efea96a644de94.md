Apart from this energy constraint, such mobile devices, because of their small size for portability, usually have small computing resources in the form of memory, computational power and speed of computation. They are also usually constrained by low communication bandwidth.

Mobility of component nodes in a network also gives rise to additional problems in network connectivity, e.g., when a node moves out of the range of communication from all other nodes in the network, it is dropped from the network. On the other hand, new nodes are also usually allowed to join a network at any time and may request additional communication channels for sending their messages. Such an allocation of new channels to newly arrived nodes must avoid all types of channel interferences with the existing communicating nodes in the nearby region. The communication protocols of the network must take proper account of such a dynamic network topology arising from all these situations.

Further, when a mobile communicating node changes its geographical position, it is possible that the channels assigned to this node may interfere with the channels allocated to other communicating nodes in the vicinity of its new location. Accordingly, some new non-interfering channel needs to be assigned to this node very quickly so as not to disrupt communication which is already in progress.

Quality of Services (QoS) is an important consideration for developing the communication protocols in any network. The quality of service is usually expressed or demanded by the users in several different forms like delay (time required for communicating packets of message), delay jitter (i.e., variation in delay for communicating different packets of the same message), reliability (e.g., bit error rate (BER)), availability of channels, and the bandwidth of communication under different application environments.

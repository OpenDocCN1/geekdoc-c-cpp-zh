To determine a ±δ bound on range measurement errors, NLOS error is assumed to be the major contributor for errors in measured ranges. In [6], experimental results show that for indoor locations where line of sight exists between the transmitter and the receiver, the zero mean Gaussian model for normalized distance error closely models the NLOS distance error. For the indoor obstructed line of sight (OLOS) case, while the Gaussian model is not suitable, a mixed distribution consisting of zero mean Gaussian and exponential distribution fits the distance error very well.

# 4.3 Estimation of Location Error

Assume that the location of a node $u$ is obtained through triangulation, using its neighboring beacons whose locations are already known. The error in the estimated position of node $u$ can arise from:

- 1. Uncertainties in the locations of the beacon nodes used for triangulating the location of node $u$.
- 2. Errors in ranging; NLOS errors are dominant.

# 4.3.1 Error due to Ambiguity in Node Coordinates

We first consider the case when the positions of the beacon nodes are not known accurately. Let us consider two beacon nodes $B_1$ and $B_2$ as in Figure 4.2, with corresponding estimated ranges as $r_1$ and $r_2$, respectively. Let $T$ represent the computed position of node $u$. Let $S$ be the foot of the normal drawn from $T$ to the line joining $B_1$ and $B_2$, with $d_1$ and $d_2$ being the distances of $S$ from $B_1$ and $B_2$, respectively. Let $d = d_1 + d_2$. The line \(B'_1B'_2\) can make an angle of \(\alpha \leq \frac{2\epsilon}{d}\) with \(B_1B_2\).

We can convert these coordinate values to \((x, y)\) with respect to \(B_1\) and \(B_2\) as:

```
$$\begin{aligned} x &= x'\cos\alpha-y'\sin\alpha\pm\epsilon \\ y &= x'\sin\alpha+y'\cos\alpha\mp\epsilon \end{aligned} \tag{4.6}$$
```

Since, in most cases $\frac{2\epsilon}{d}$ would be a small quantity, we have from Equation (4.6),

```
$$(x,y)\approx(x'-y'\alpha\pm\epsilon,\ x'\alpha+y'\mp\epsilon).$$
```

The coordinates of $T$ with respect to rotated x-axis formed by the line joining $B_1$ and $B_2$ are then

```
$$\begin{aligned} x &= d_1-\sqrt{r_1^2-d_1^2}\cdot\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{d}\right)\pm\epsilon \\ y &= d_1\cdot\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{d}\right)+\sqrt{r_1^2-d_1^2}\mp\epsilon. \end{aligned}$$
```

Thus, the error introduced in the computed position of node $u$ due to a maximum ambiguity of $\pm\epsilon$ in the advertised locations of the beacon nodes is given by

```
$$\begin{aligned} |x-x'| &= \sqrt{r_1^2-d_1^2}\cdot\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{d}\right)+\epsilon \\ |y-y'| &= d_1\cdot\left(\frac{2\epsilon}{d}\right)+\epsilon \end{aligned}\quad\le 3\epsilon\tag{4.7}$$
```

From Equation (4.7) we conclude that if we start with an uncertainty of $\pm\epsilon$ in the positions of the individual beacon nodes, this uncertainty can contribute to a maximum error of $\pm3\epsilon$ in the evaluated position of node $u$ along the axis normal to the line joining $B_1B_2$. However, no such bound can be given for the error in the coordinate value along the line $B_1B_2$.

# ### 4.3.2 Contribution by Range Estimation Error

Next we consider the case when there is only a maximum error of ±δ in the individual range estimates (due to NLOS errors and Gaussian noise). Again, δ is typically assumed to be quite small compared to d, r₁ or r₂. Assume that the beacon node coordinates used are error-free. That is, we start with B₁ and B₂ as the actual coordinates of the beacon nodes but with a range error of δ, so that the measured ranges from B₁ and B₂ are now r′₁ and r′₂, respectively, where r′₁ = r₁ ± δ, and r′₂ = r₂ ± δ. Hence, the modified value for d₁ is given by

$$d'_1 = \frac{d_2 + r_1'^2 - r_2'^2}{2d}$$

$$= \frac{d_2 + r_1^2 - r_2^2 \pm 2\delta r_1 \pm 2\delta r_2}{2d} \qquad \text{(neglecting }O(\delta^2)\text{ terms).}$$

Note that the distance d between the two beacon nodes B₁ and B₂ is assumed to be constant in the above expression for d′₁. Hence, the error resulting in the computed position of u along the x-axis (i.e., the line joining B₁ and B₂) is

$$|d'_1-d_1| \leq \frac{2\delta(r_1+r_2)}{2d}$$

$$\leq \frac{2\delta\cdot 2d}{2d} \qquad \text{since } r_1\leq d \text{ and } r_2\leq d$$

$$=2\delta. \tag{4.8}$$

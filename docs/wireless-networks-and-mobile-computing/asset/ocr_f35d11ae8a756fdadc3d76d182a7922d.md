The best software performance of RC4 stated in the literature [2] provides an account of 7 to 15 cycles per byte of RC4 keystream, depending on the processor and the clock speed on which the cipher was tested. This rate is slow for typical wireless communication applications.

In contrast to the software implementation, a hardware implementation of the RC4 stream cipher may possibly be faster and find potential applications in encrypting and decrypting message streams in a wireless communication network. A number of attempts for efficient hardware implementation of RC4 have already been reported in the literature.

A 3-cycle-per-byte efficient implementation of RC4 on custom pipelined hardware was proposed by Kitsos et al. [11] in 2003. Later, a patent by Matthews [17] was disclosed and provided a similar 3-cycles-per-byte architecture using hardware pipelining. Five years later, Matthews disclosed another patent [18] that included a design for RC4 hardware based on pipeline architecture that could increase the efficiency of the cipher to obtain 1-byte-per-cycle in RC4 PRGA. In 2013, Sen Gupta et al. [1] introduced a hardware implementation of RC4 that also produced 1 byte per cycle as in the Mathews’ patent [18]. We describe below a hardware implementation of RC4 [1] which is based on loop unfolding and instruction rescheduling with a three-stage pipelining, resulting in less hardware cost and complexity than in [18]. Note that wireless communication devices are often constrained by the computing resources and optimization of resources and speed are always beneficial.

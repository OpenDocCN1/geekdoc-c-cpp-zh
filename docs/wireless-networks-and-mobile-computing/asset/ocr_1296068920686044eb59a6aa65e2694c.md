In this section, we compare the performance of CNS with existing techniques as discussed below.



We first compare the performance of CNS with those of CtS [9] and VarBaTaC [10] in terms of the transmission length duration. We assume ideal channel conditions for this comparison. Because both CtS and VarBaTaC utilize complete silence for information communication, their performance degradation in the presence of noise will be much more than that suffered by CNS.

The biggest drawback of the CtS scheme is that the transmission time is exponential in the number of bits to be sent. VarBaTaC uses a variable coding base and controls the communication time by suitably tuning the base value. Suppose we want to transmit a value \( M \), where \( 0 \leq M < 2^k \), \( k \geq 0 \). The representation of \( M \) in base \( r \) will have \( m = \lceil \frac{k}{\log r} \rceil \) digits. Each of these \( m \) digits is then transmitted using CtS with a pulse separating each digit. For \( m \) digits, the number of pulses transmitted would be \( m+1 \) and the total time taken in the worst case would be \( (m+1)+m\cdot(r-1) \). For example, the decimal value \( (245)_{10} \) in base 16 is \( F5 \). Assuming base 16 is used for transmission, the number of pulses required by the VarBaTaC scheme would be 3 (i.e., \( 3e_b \) units of energy expenditure) and the total duration of transmission would be \( 3+(15+5)=23 \) time slots. To compare VarBaTac with CNS, we see that \( (245)_{10} \) in binary is \( 11110101 \). The frequency of both the bit pairs \( 01 \) and \( 11 \) is 2. We arbitrarily choose \( 01 \) to be the \( N \) symbol and map \( 11 \) to the symbol \( A \). Thus the sequence to be transmitted in CNS is \( AANN \) and the total duration of transmission would be four time slots, with only \( 2e_b \) units of energy expenditure by the transmitter for the two \( A \) symbols.

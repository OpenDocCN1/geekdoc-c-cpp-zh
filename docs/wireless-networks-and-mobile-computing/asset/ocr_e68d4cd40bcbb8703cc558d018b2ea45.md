In the next iteration, we compute the smallest enclosing circle $C'_i$ of each $vor(p'_i)$ which is completely enclosed in $C_i$. This proves that if $\rho_{new}$ is the revised value of $\rho$ in the next iteration, $\rho_{new} \leq \rho_{old}$.

**Remark 21.** *The iteration terminates when the value of $\rho$ reaches a local minimum, or in other words, $\rho_{new} = \rho_{old}$ is attained.*

To get rid of the local minimum, we apply a refinement step during the iterative process. Note that if a point (base station) $p_i$ is on the boundary of $\Pi$, at least 50% of the area of $C_i$ lies outside $\Pi$, and hence this region need not be covered. This indicates the scope of further reduction in the area of $C_i$. Thus, if a point goes very close to the boundary of $\Pi$, we move it to the centroid of $\Pi$, which is computed as follows:

Let $\Pi$ be a $m$ vertex convex polygon, and $(x_j,y_j)$ denote the $j$-th vertex of $\Pi$, $j = 1, 2, \ldots, m$. The centroid of $\Pi$ is the point having the coordinates $\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_j,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}y_j\right)$. It can be shown that the centroid of a convex region is always inside that region.

Such a major perturbation brings the solution away from local minima, and it leads to a further reduction in $\rho$. We continue the iteration with this initial placement until it reaches more local minima.

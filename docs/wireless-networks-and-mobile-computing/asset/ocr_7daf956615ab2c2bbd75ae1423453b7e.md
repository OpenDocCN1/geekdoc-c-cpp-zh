In [42], Gamst et al. presented some lower bounds on bandwidth for CAP that took the additional constraints for adjacent channel interferences and co-site interferences into account. Improving these results, Tcha et al. [7] presented some results on the lower bound which, however, did not explicitly include the co-site constraints. In [20], the authors derived a lower bound which, in some cases, was tighter than those presented in [42] considering all the co-channel, adjacent channel, and co-site constraints. In [44], new lower bounds were generalizations of the bounds presented in [20, 42]. All these lower bounds are defined on a general network of arbitrary cell structure.

For the special case of hexagonal cellular networks, the problem of determining the lower bounds has been studied by several authors [29, 28]. In this special type of network, a *k*-band buffering exists in the network if the channels assigned to any two calls in two cells which are more than distance *k* apart do not interfere with each other. In other words, for a *k*-band buffering network we need to define *k* + 1 constants $s_0, s_1, s_2, \cdots, s_k$ to specify the constraints for avoiding channel interferences where $s_i$, $0 \leq i \leq k$ denotes the minimum gap between two channels assigned to two calls in two cells which are distance *i* apart. Clearly, $s_0 \geq s_1 \geq s_2 \geq \cdots \geq s_k$. Authors in [29, 28] studied the problem of determining the lower bounds for a two-band buffering situation in hexagonal cellular networks using only the three parameters $s_0$, $s_1$, and $s_2$, where $s_0$, $s_1$, and $s_2$ are the minimum frequency separations required to avoid interference for calls in the same cell, cells at distances 1 and 2, respectively. They derived expressions for the lower bounds on bandwidths for different relative values of $s_0$, $s_1$, and $s_2$ for both uniform and non-uniform demand situations.

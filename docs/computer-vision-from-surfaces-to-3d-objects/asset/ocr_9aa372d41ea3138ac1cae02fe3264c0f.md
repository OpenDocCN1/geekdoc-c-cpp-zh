Figure 1.3a shows a log-log polar plot of | real[ZI⋆ (r, θ)] | from one image in our database. The general shape of this cross-spectrum appears to closely follow a power law. Specifically, we found that ZI⋆ can be reasonably modeled by B(θ)/rα, where r is spatial frequency in polar coordinates, and B(θ) is a function that depends only on polar angle θ, with one curve for the real part and one for the imaginary part. We test this claim by dividing the Fourier plane into four 45° octants (vertical, forward diagonal, horizontal, and backward diagonal) and measuring the drop-off rate in each octant separately. For each octant, we average over the octant’s included orientations and fit the result to a power law. The resulting values of α (averaged over all 28 images) are listed in Table 1.1.

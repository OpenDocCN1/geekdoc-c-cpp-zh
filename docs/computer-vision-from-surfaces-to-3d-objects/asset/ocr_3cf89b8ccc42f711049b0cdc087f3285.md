# INTRODUCTION

At the early processing stages in visual cortex, information is laid out in the form of maps of the retinal image. However, contrary to intuition, uniform surfaces are not mapped by uniform distributions of neural activity. We can perceive the three-dimensional (3D) shape of a uniform surface, but stereoscopic neurons are activated only by the contours of the surface, not the uniform interior. We perceive a uniform color figure, but color-selective neurons respond about five times less to the interior of the figure than to its boundaries.

*Proof.* The proof can be found, for example, in Theorem 1.34 in Folland (1989). It follows from the fact that minimizers must satisfy the equality in the uncertainty principle:

$|\langle Y_3u,u\rangle|=2\| (Y_1-a_1)u\|\,\|(Y_2-a_2)u\|$

so that also the Schwartz inequality (Equation [7.15]) has to be an equality:

$|\operatorname{Im}\langle Y_2u,Y_1u\rangle|=\|Y_2u\|\,\|Y_1u\|$ (7.16)

If the scalar product is equal to the product of the norms, then the vectors $Y_2u$ and $Y_1u$ have to be parallel:

$Y_1u=i\lambda Y_2u$

A direct verification proves that the Gabor filter $\Psi_0$ defined in Equation (7.1) satisfies this equation. As we said before, all the other filters were obtained from this via rotations and translations, so that they are coherent states in the sense of Perelomov (1986). Lee (1996) has shown these states are in agreement with the experimental data of the receptive profile (Figure 7.6).

Let us note that these minimizers are the coherent states of the reducible representation of the group. In Barbieri et al. (2010), we used the irreducible representation of the operators $Y_j$, so that we searched minimizers in the set of functions with fixed frequency in the Fourier domain, and we obtained the classical pinwheels structure, observed experimentally by Bosking et al. (1997).

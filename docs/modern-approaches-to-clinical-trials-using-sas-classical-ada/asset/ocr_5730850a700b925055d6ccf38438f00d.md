# 9.4.2 Covariate-Adaptive Randomization by Minimization

When there are a large number of prognostic factors in a trial, it becomes difficult to achieve overall balance across all of the covariates. Taves [6] and Pocock and Simon [7] proposed the minimization method (also known as dynamic allocation, covariate-adaptive, or adjusted randomization) for sequential treatment assignment. During the randomization process, the allocation probability of each subject is determined by the current overall balance among the groups.

In detail, let $l$ denote the total number of prognostic factors, among which continuous factors need to be categorized into meaningful ordinal levels. In a two-arm trial, let $n_{i1}$ be the number of subjects that are assigned to arm 1 and $n_{i2}$ assigned to arm 2, then the difference for $i$ is given by

$g_i = |n_{i1} - n_{i2}|, i=1, \dots$.

The total imbalance is measured by a weighted sum over the marginal imbalance for each factor $G = \sum_{i=1}^l w_i g_i$, where $w_i$ is a weight characterizing the importance of the $i$th factor. Based on previous allocations of patients, each new patient is more likely to be assigned to the treatment arm that would lead to the least overall imbalance G.

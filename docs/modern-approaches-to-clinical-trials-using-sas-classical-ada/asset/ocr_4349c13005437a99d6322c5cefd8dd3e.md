# ## 11.4.1 Introduction

It has been well recognized and documented that increased spending in pharmaceutical and biotech research does not reflect an increase of the success rate of clinical development. Woodcock [26] outlined some of the reasons for the declining success rate of pharmaceutical development namely (i) high bar for proving drug benefits due to diminished margin for improvement, (ii) increased costs and complexity potentially decreasing the ability to bring many candidates forward into the clinic, (iii) genetics and related disciplines are not yet fully developed, (iv) chronic diseases are harder for the study sponsor to choose easy targets for investigation and investment. The United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) hosted a “Critical Path Initiative” to identify key scientific challenges underlying the medical product pipeline and then released a *Critical Path Opportunities List* in 2006, especially learning from prior experience and ongoing innovative clinical trials, and accumulated data. The innovation implies the use of adaptive design methods and the potential use of a Bayesian approach. Chow et al. [27] define “Adaptive design as a design that allows adaptations to trial and/or statistical procedures of the trial after its initiation without undermining the validity and integrity of the trial”. The PRMA Working Group paper by Gallo et al. [28] refer to “An adaptive design as a clinical trial that uses accumulating data to decide on how to modify aspects of the study as it continues, without undermining the validity and integrity of the trial.” In the past decade, the application of adaptive design methods in clinical trials has become very popular due to its flexibility and efficiency. This efficiency is at times gained at a cost of increased complexity, logistical challenges especially for adequate drug supply, and other operational issues. In most cases, intensive planning must be undertaken to carefully define go/no-go criteria, which is likely to delay the start of the trial. The sponsor should have the adequately trained resources to implement and execute adaptive designs. All eventualities should be considered including the risk and benefits in case the trial is terminated at the interim analysis.

# 11.5 Summary

Clinical researchers face increasing challenges and are presented with unique opportunities in the new era of personalized/precision medicine to meet the needs of patients, payers, and the ever increasing costs of health economics. In this chapter, we gave an overview of population enrichment designs that are growing in the statistical literature, with a focus on designs of predictive enrichment strategies as as to increase the power and efficiency to detect an effective therapy with regard to a predictive biomarker in a clinical trial. We categorized the existing designs into classical designs, frequentist adaptive designs, and Bayesian adaptive designs. We also discussed the efficiency of a biomarker enrichment design compared to a traditional all-comers population, sensitivity/specificity of the assay, and the relative treatment effect of the biomarker positive group versus the biomarker negative group.

Our focus was on designs that involved one or multiple predictive biomarkers that correlate with clinical efficacy endpoints of interest and the experimental treatment. The case of biomarkers that relate to safety outcomes is an important research area but not covered in this chapter. In addition, prognostic biomarkers, pharmacometric biomarkers, and surrogate biomarkers were out of the scope of this chapter.

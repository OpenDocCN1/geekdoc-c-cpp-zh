# ## 6.2.7 Sample Size Considerations

# ### 6.2.7.1 Statistical Hypothesis for Determining PoC

Whenever sample size calculation is needed for a given study, the first point is to understand the hypothesis to be tested. In the process of designing a phase II dose ranging program, the initial statistical hypothesis is typically a proof of concept hypothesis. In the case when the study is designed with only two treatment groups – a placebo group is compared to the MTD of study product - the hypothesis can be expressed in a straight-forward fashion by comparing the response from the test treatment against the response from the placebo. If the comparison is based on means, then the hypothesis can be written as

```
$H_0: \mu_T = \mu_P$ VS. $H_1: \mu_T > \mu_P$
```

where $\mu_T$ is the test treatment group mean, and $\mu_P$ is the placebo treatment group mean. In other words, if the mean responses from the test treatment group are statistically significantly different from the mean responses from the placebo group, the concept is considered proven.

However, in a dose-ranging trial design including more than two treatment groups, the PoC hypothesis is not necessarily straightforward. This is because it might not be the most efficient to just test the general hypothesis that at least one group is different from the others, but rather use a contrast as the primary objective. A typical dose-ranging design consists of the highest dose of the test product (could be the MTD) on the upper end, and it consists of the placebo representing the zero dose group. Ideally, such a design includes three or four different doses between these two groups. Under this scenario, two possible contrast options could be considered in specifying the PoC hypothesis. One option is to use the contrast comparing two doses, keeping the hypothesis the same as in the two-group setting – only test the highest dose (may MTD) against a placebo, as expressed in the hypothesis above. Another option is to use a contrast that includes more than two treatment groups in order to perform the PoC hypothesis test.

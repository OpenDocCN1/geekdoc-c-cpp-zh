```
$A(z_n) = 1-\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\sqrt{k^2-z_n^2}\right)z_n\cdot\le z_n<k.\ z_n\ge k$
```

In the equation above, $ho^*$ is the cut-off for the Stage 1 futility stop and the critical value $k$ for the Stage 1 test statistic $z_n$ is selected (given $ho^*$) so that condition (3.4) holds. The function $A(Z_n)$ dictates how much conditional type I error rate to allow at the end of the study given observed interim results $Z_n=z_n$.

When the design includes an unblinded SSR in addition to early stopping (efficacy/or utility), the critical value $c$ for the final testing and new sample size per group for second stage $n_2=M-n$ are selected so that $CP_0(n_2, z_n)=A(z_n)$ and $CP_\delta(n_2, z_n)=1-\beta$.

It is important to note that the rule lets users decide how to select $n_2$ (e.g. how high the conditional power should be or whether to use empirical vs hypothesized effect size). Once the rule is set, the type I error rate is guaranteed at level $\alpha$. This method can also be viewed as a special case of the p-value combination principle.

Another method in the same category is by Müller and Schäfer [24]. The Müller and Schäfer method is a lot more general than the method by Prochan and Hunsberger and can be extended to multistage trials with adaptations other than sample size (e.g. hypotheses/endpoint changes). The latter allows an unplanned interim analysis to be inserted into a sequence of pre-planned interim analyses in a group-sequential trial. Even though such extensions are beyond the scope of this chapter, Müller and Schäfer principle is worth mentioning even in case of a simple 2-stage procedure for SSR, it highlights a fundamental role of the conditional error function shown by the Prochan and Hunsberger method. That is, after the Stage 1 data have been observed, the conditional error probability (or conditional rejection probabilities, CRP, in their terminology) given $Z_n=z_n$ is all the information from Stage 1 that needs to be carried into Stage 2 of the study. As long as the Stage 2 in the new design has conditional type I error equal to the CRP calculated from Stage 1, the overall type I error will be preserved.

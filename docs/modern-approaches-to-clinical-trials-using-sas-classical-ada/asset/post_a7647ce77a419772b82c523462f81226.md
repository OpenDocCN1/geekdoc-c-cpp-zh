## Example Code 11.6 Decision Macro Call

```sas
libname perm "directory of sample data";
%macro Decision(outcome, gene, par, alpha, seed);
%global seed;
%let sseed = &seed;

data outcome;
set &outcome;
run;

data gene;
set &gene;
run;

data par;
set &par;
run;

proc freq data=outcome;
table response*treatment/chisq;
out out=chisqtest quisq;
run;

data null;
set chisqtest("chis_value",P_PCHI);
run;
/*If the chi-square test is significant based on prescribed*
/*alpha, then print result and abort*/
/*Otherwise, need to move on to CVSD stage*/
%if &chisq_value <&alpha %then %do
ntitle "Chi-square Test SIGNIFICANT"
proc print data=chisqtest;
run;
%abort;
%end
%mend Decision;

%Decision(perm.adsl, perm.adgene, perm.par, 0.04,12345);
```

The output (not shown) has a chi-square p-value of 0.7877. Because the test is not significant at the specified α level, the signature component of the design will be implemented to select a potentially promising biomarker subgroup, also known as the sensitive subset.

## 11.4.2.4 Adaptive Seamless Phase/III Design

A seamless design combines objectives traditionally addressed in separate trials into a single trial. This type of design eliminates the time that would have occurred between the trials and may provide additional efficiencies in terms of the total number of patients or follow-up. An adaptive seamless design is the combination of trials and analysis where the final analysis uses data from before and after the adaptation. Though this design seems very promising, the statistical methodology for such designs must take into consideration potential biases and account for multiplicity, multiple looks at the data (interim analysis). In previous years several seamless design methods have been proposed in the literature. One of the commonly used methods is the adaptive p-value combination method and its variations [38-42]. Using these methods, mid-trial design modifications are possible without compromising the family-wise type I rate. P-based combination methods have been restricted to a single endpoint. Another approach that has been proposed [43] uses the multivariate normal method which is a modification to the group sequential computation. Stallard and Toder extended their work to incorporate a change of endpoint. Royston and Toder later at [44] proposed an approach along the same lines but include survival outcomes. These approaches make some assumptions about the joint distributions of test statistics in the interim and the final stage, and between subgroup populations and, hence can get complicated when used for the population selection. In the FDA guidance on adaptive designs, seamless phase/III designs are considered as a type of design "not well-understood" due to both statistical and operational issues. In addition, seamless designs exploratory studies with "adequate and well controlled studies." As a result, the FDA requires that the sponsor conduct extensive simulations and communicate with the health authority as early as possible if such a design is proposed.

# 5.3 Bayesian Model Averaging Continual Reassessment Method

In the CRM, the prespecified skeleton $(p_1,...,pJ)$ is usually arbitrary and subjective. As a result, the performance of the CRM may be compromised by model misspecifications, which is a common drawback of parametric models. To enhance the robustness of the CRM, we can construct multiple CRM models in parallel, such that each model is equipped with a different skeleton representing uncertainty in the parametric modeling structure [3]. If there is no preference over different modeling structures, then we assign a discrete uniform prior probability to each CRM model. Through the Bayesian model averaging (BMA) procedure [4, 5], we let the data determine the weight of each model based on the posterior model probability. Automatically, the BMA-CRM assigns a larger weight to a model with a better fit, which ensures the robustness of the estimated toxicity probabilities even when CRM models are misspecified.

To be more specific, let $M_k$ denote the CRM model that is associated with the $k$th skeleton $(p_{k1},...,pkJ})$ for $k=1,...,K$. Under the working model $M_k$, the toxicity probability at dose level $j$ is defined as

$\Pi_{kj}(\alpha_k)=exp(\alpha_k), \quad j=1,...,J$$

where $\alpha_k$ is the unknown parameter that is associated at a certain stage of the trial, $y_j$ patients have experienced the DLT among the $n_j$ patients treated at dose level $j$. Based on the observed data $D$, the likelihood function over model $M_k$ is

$$L[\alpha_k|D, M_k] \equiv P(\alpha_k|M_k) \alpha \prod_{j=1}^J {(\frac{\exp(\alpha_k)}{p_{kj}})^{y_j} (1-p_{kj}/\exp(\alpha_k))^{n_j-y_j}}$

In the Bayesian hierarchical modeling framework, we specify $P(M_k)$ to be the prior probability that model $M_k$ is the true model. If there is no preference *a priori* for any single CRM model, we simply take a discrete uniform distribution for the prior model probability; that is, $P(M_k)=1/K$; otherwise, prior information can be incorporated by assigning a higher probability to a more preferred model.

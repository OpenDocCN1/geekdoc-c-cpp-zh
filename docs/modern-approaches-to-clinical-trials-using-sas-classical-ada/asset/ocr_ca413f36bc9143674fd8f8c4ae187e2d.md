Given the requirements of demonstrating long-term drug safety, it is critical for the new compound to offer doses low enough so that in the phase III studies where patients are exposed to long-term treatment, there is no safety concern, and patients can still experience the treatment benefit of efficacy. Hence, it is desirable that in the PoC study, some doses of this new compound are explored to help detect a minimum effective dose (MinED). For these reasons, it is of interest to study a wide range of doses (so that the high dose is close to MTD to help with PoC, and the low dose is low enough to establish MinED).

In this example, the project team decides to study 5 parallel dose groups covering a wide range. In addition to these 5 dose groups, a placebo control and an active control are also used to confirm the efficacy and explore drug safety. In some of the early phase II clinical studies, there is no active control group. Active controls are more likely to be used in phase III development. The reason to choose an active control in this case study is, if the results fail to distinguish between a placebo and the tested doses, it will be unclear as to whether the test drug is truly ineffective, or happens to be a very high placebo response (i.e. placebo effect). Under this situation, the comparison between an active control and the placebo helps the project team to differentiate the two conditions given above if the active control is clearly superior to a placebo, then it can be concluded that the test drug is not efficacious. On the other hand, if the data cannot differentiate between the active control and the placebo, then this study is not informative because it is still unknown as to whether the test drug is beneficial or not. Hence, in this study, the active control is used for reference purposes only. When a treatment is already available on the market for the same indication and targeted population, it would be unethical to consider a placebo arm. In such circumstances, the choice of the low dose is important to demonstrate the dose response together with other pragmatic considerations to ensure patients' care.

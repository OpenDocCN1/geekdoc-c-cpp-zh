## Example 3: mTOR Inhibitor for HER2-Positive Trastuzumab-Resistant Advanced Breast Carcinoma

It has been postulated that drugs that inhibit the mTOR pathway may help advanced progressive HER2-positive breast cancer patients with resistance to trastuzumab. Everolimus, as an mTOR inhibitor, has been shown to potentiate the effect of trastuzumab. Dose response of combining everolimus and trastuzumab was established in a previous phase II study with an estimated 34% clinical complete response [15]. A confirming randomized, placebo-controlled trial was designed to test the hypothesis that this combination therapy is indeed efficacious compared to trastuzumab alone [16]. The primary endpoint was locally assessed progression-free survival (PFS), defined as time from randomization to first radiologically documented tumor progression or death according to Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumors (RECIST, v1.0). It could be assumed that the median PFS for the placebo group was 6 months, while for the treatment group was 8.25 months, about 37.5% improvement. If the trial was designed to have a total follow-up time of 2 years and an accrual time of 1 year, under the assumption of exponential survival curve and specification of log-rank test and equal allocation, the total sample size under a fixed-design can be approximated to $N=508$ achieving a power of 90%. The type I error was set at 0.025. Example Code 2.3 Sample Size for mTOR Inhibitor Trial calculates this sample size. The results of this everolimus combination therapy trial are displayed in Results of Trastuzumab-Resistant Breast Cancer Trial for Everolimus on PFS.

## Example 2.3 Sample Size for mTOR Inhibitor Trial

```
proc power;
twoamplesample test=logrank
groupedmesurvimes=(6 8.25)
accrualtime=12
totaltime=24
power=0.9
alpha=0.025 sides=1
total=.;
```

## Example 4: Non-Insulin Therapy for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

In this example, we present some event and sample size calculations for fixed-sample, non-inferiority trials for time-to-event endpoints. This example is motivated by the recent US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) guidance on assessing cardiovascular (CV) risk for therapies used to treat type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [17]. For non-insulin T2DM therapies, the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), which includes CV death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, and non-fatal stroke events, can be assessed in two Stages, as suggested in the guidance document. Here we capitalize “Stage” to distinguish the usage in the T2DM example from the interim analyses or “stages” that are used elsewhere in the chapter. The first Stage is a pre-market evaluation of the novel compound to placebo testing the Stage 1 hypotheses using all accumulated pre-market trial data:

```
$H_{10}: \frac{\hat{\lambda}_T}{\hat{\lambda}_C} \geq 1.8 \text{ versus } H_{11}: \frac{\hat{\lambda}_T}{\hat{\lambda}_C} < 1.8$ (2.2)
```

where $\hat{\lambda}_T$ and $\hat{\lambda}_C$ are the hazard rates of MACE for the novel treatment and control arms, respectively. Assuming the new drug application (NDA) is otherwise acceptable, if the CV criterion is, the sponsor obtains full marketing approval for the new drug. In the second Stage, the sponsor must evaluate the post-marketing criteria testing Stage 2 hypotheses combining new post-market trial data and pre-market data:

```
$H_{20}: \frac{\hat{\lambda}_T}{\hat{\lambda}_C} \geq 1.3 \text{ versus } H_{21}: \frac{\hat{\lambda}_T}{\hat{\lambda}_C} < 1.3$ (2.3)
```

This approach strikes a balance between providing evidence on cardiovascular safety to reassure patients and excessive delay of novel therapies reaching the marketplace. Each Stage is tested at a separate $\alpha=0.05$, though if the 1.3 criterion is assessed during Stage 1, multiplicity adjustment is required for the $\alpha$ in Stage 2 [18].

proc power does not currently support non-inferiority calculations for time-to-event endpoints.

## Example 2.4 Number of Events for Diabetes Mellitus

```
proc seqdesign;
  stage=2;
  alpha=0.05;
  power=0.9;
  noninferiority;
run;
```

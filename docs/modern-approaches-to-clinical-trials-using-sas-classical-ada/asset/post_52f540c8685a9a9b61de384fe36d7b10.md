As described above, this analysis approach is to strike a balance between providing evidence on CV safety to reassure patients and excessive delay of novel therapies reaching the marketplace. An important point for any analysis strategy, one that utilizes sequential methodologies or not, is to maintain confidentiality of the CV results at the end of Stage 1 to minimize bias for the ongoing or additional trials needed to assess cardiovascular safety at the $\alpha$ margin. This includes the public, because knowledge of the point estimates and confidence intervals may affect the enrollment of current or future trials, but also includes sponsor personnel, since trial integrity may be compromised. It is recommended that blinded sponsor personnel be independent of the study team, and limited to those individuals needed to initiate activities. Additional details on the confidentiality of interim results of CV trials for T2DM are available in [27, 28].

## 2.5 Special Issues

So far, we have demonstrated some statistical implementation of group sequential methods to clinical trials with different primary objectives. The validity of the trial that uses the sequential testing procedures relies heavily on strict adherence to the pre-specified stopping rules. In practice, deviations from the rules during the trial are common, and as a result, may compromise the integrity and interpretability of the final results. The rigidity of adhering to the rule presents challenges when deviations are ethically necessary and justified. Recent research on clinical trial monitoring introduces the use of conditional power and predictive power within the Bayesian paradigm. As discussed earlier, due to possible inflation of type I error, binding utility extends no flexibility in continuing the trial when utility boundary is crossed, while non-binding utility offers flexibility but may increase sample size. Conditional power [29] and predictive power [30] become additional monitoring tools for sequential designs that only reject the null hypothesis. Conditional power at the $i$th stage is defined as the total probability of rejecting the null hypothesis at any future stages given the observed statistic at stage $j$. A small conditional power ($\ll 0.05$) signals a small probability of declaring a successful trial given the current observed evidences. The trial can be stopped to accept the null hypothesis. Predictive power is the posterior probability that the test statistic at the final stage would exceed the rejection value given the observed data and a prior distribution of the hypothetical reference.

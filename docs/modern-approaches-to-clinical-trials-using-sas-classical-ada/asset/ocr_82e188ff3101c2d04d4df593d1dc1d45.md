# 10.3.3 Doubly Adaptive Biased Coin Design

The Doubly Adaptive Biased Coin Design (DBCD) is a class of RAR procedures that can target any allocation proportion $ho$ provided that some mild regularity conditions are met [47,48]. The key ingredient of DBCD is the allocation function $g(x, \rho)$, defined on $[0,1] \times [0,1]$. Hu and Zhang [48] proposed the following allocation function:

```
$g(x, \rho) = \begin{cases} \frac{\rho^\gamma}{x^\gamma}} & 0 < x < 1, \ \frac{(1-\ho)^\gamma}{(1-x)^\gamma}}{1-x} & x=0,1. \end{cases} \quad (10.8)$
```

The user-defined parameter $\gamma \geq 0$ controls the degree of randomness ($\gamma=0$ is most random and $\gamma \to \infty$ is almost a deterministic procedure). The role of $\gamma$ will become clear momentarily.

Suppose that the target allocation has been chosen as $\rho = \rho(p_E, p_C)$. After $m$ patients, the target allocation is estimated as $\hat{p}_m = \rho(\hat{p}_E, \hat{p}_C)$. The DBCD assigns the $\langle m+1 \rangle$st patient to $E$ with probability

```
$\phi_{m+1} E = g(\rac{N_E(m)}{m}, \hat{p}_m)$
```

where $g(x, \rho)$ is as in (10.8). If $\gamma=0$, the DBCD is equivalent to $\phi_{m+1} E = \hat{p}_E$, the SMLE model.

Hu and Zhang in [48] proved that under the condition $\rho = \rho(p_E, p_C)$ is continuous in its arguments and is continuously differentiable in some small neighborhood of the true value of $(p_E, p_C)$, both the sample treatment proportions and the estimators are strongly consistent and asymptotically normal. In particular, $N_E(m)/m$ has an asymptotically normal distribution with mean $\rho$ and asymptotic variance

```
$\sigma_{\gamma}^2 = \frac{\rho(1-\ho)}{1+2\gamma} + \frac{2(1+\gamma)}{(1+2\gamma)^2} ((\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial p_E})^2 \frac{p_E p_E}{\rho} + (\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial p_C})^2 \frac{p_C p_C}{1-\ho})$
```

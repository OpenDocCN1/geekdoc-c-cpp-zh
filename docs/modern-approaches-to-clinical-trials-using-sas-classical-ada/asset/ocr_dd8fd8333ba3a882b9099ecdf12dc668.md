This is to ascertain some initial data to estimate $p_E$ and $p_C$. We make an assumption that each patient's outcome is available immediately after randomization. While this is quite a restrictive assumption in practice time must pass before a patient's response is observable, it serves as a useful starting point for developing RAR therapy [14]. Consider $m \ge 2m_0$ patients have been treated and the $(m+1)$st patient is ready to be randomized in the trial. The treatment assignment for the $(m+1)$st patient will depend on the data accrued far. For $j=1, \dots, m$, let $\hat{c}_j = 1$ (or $0$) if the $j$th patient's outcome is success (or failure). Note let $Y_j = 1$ and $p_E = Pr(Y_j=1|\hat{c}_j=1)$, $p_C=Pr(Y_j=0)$ for $j=1, \dots, m$. Given $\Delta_m = (\hat{c}_1, \dots, \hat{c}_m)$ and $Y_m = (Y_1, \dots, Y_m)$, the treatment assignment for the $(m+1)$st patient will have to be determined.

Hu and Rosenberger in [14] define a RAR procedure as one whose randomization probability to treatment $E$ for the $(m+1)$st patient is determined as

$\phi_{m+1} = E=Pr(\\phi_{m+1}=1|\Delta_m, Y_m), m \ge 2m_0)$. (10.5)

Therefore, the randomization probability to treatment $E$ is random because $\phi_{m+1}C=1-\phi_{m+1}E$. Note that the quantity in (10.5) depends on $\Delta_m$ and $Y_m$ which are random variables. Based on $\Delta_m$ and $Y_m$, let $N_E(m)=\sum_{j=1}^m\hat{c}_j$ and $N_C(m)=\sum_{j=1}^m(1-\hat{c}_j)$ denote the number of patients assigned to $E$ and $C$, let $S_E(m)=\sum_{j=1}^mY_j$ and $S_C(m)=\sum_{j=1}^m(1-\hat{c}_j)Y_j$ denote the number of successes on $E$ and $C$. The success probabilities $p_E$ and $p_C$ are estimated as follows:

$\hat{p}_{Em}=\frac{S_E(m)}{N_E(m)}$ and $\hat{p}_{Cm}=\frac{S_C(m)}{N_C(m)}$. (10.6)

It should be noticed that $\hat{p}_{Em}$ and $\hat{p}_{Cm}$ in (10.6) are not usual estimators of binomial proportions with RAR treatment assignments $\hat{c}_1, \dots, \hat{c}_m$ are correlated and the denominators $N_E(m)$ and $N_C(m)$ are random variables. Martingale theory is used to establish asymptotic properties of the estimators in this case [14].

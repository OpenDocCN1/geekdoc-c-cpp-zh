As shown in the simulation study in 4.7 SAS MACRO BSMED, a fixed $a_0$ provides us additional flexibility in controlling the type I error. In addition, our informative prior specification only allows us to borrow historical meta-experimental data for the models for the historical and current data. Thus, the historical meta-experimental data has the most influence on $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$. For this reason, [6] and [10] call the power priors given in (4.10) "partial borrowing power priors."

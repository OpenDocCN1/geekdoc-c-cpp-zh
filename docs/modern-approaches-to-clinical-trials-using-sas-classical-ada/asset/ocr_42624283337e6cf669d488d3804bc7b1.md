# 10.2 Optimal Allocation

Consider a binary outcome clinical trial to compare an experimental treatment, $E$, versus a control treatment, $C$. Let $p_E$ denote the probability of success on $E$ ($q_E=1-p_E$) and $p_C$ denote the probability of success on $C$ ($q_C=1-p_C$). Suppose the trial has a sample size $n$ which is fixed and predetermined. Let $ho$ denote the allocation proportion for the experimental treatment where $0 < ho < 1$. This means roughly $n_E=nho$ patients are assigned to $E$ and $n_C=n(1-ho)$ patients are assigned to $C$. An experimental design problem is to find an optimal allocation $ho^*$ as a solution to some formal optimization problem. In a two-arm comparative trial, a common objective is to have sufficient power to test the study hypothesis. Consider testing the hypothesis $H_0: p_E=p_C$ versus $H_1: p_E 
eq p_C$ using the Wald test

```
$Z_W = \frac{\hat{p}_E-\hat{p}_C}{\sqrt{\frac{\hat{q}_E+\hat{q}_C}{n}}}$$
```

where $\hat{p}_E, \hat{q}_E, \hat{p}_C, \hat{q}_C$ are consistent estimators of the corresponding parameters. Under $H_1, Z_W$ has asymptotically normal distribution with mean $d=(p_E-p_C)\left{\frac{\hat{q}_E+\hat{q}_C}{n}\right\}^{-1/2}$ and unit variance. The allocation maximizes for a fixed sample size $n$ and fixed $p_E, q_E, p_C, q_C$ is one that minimizes the value of $\left{\frac{q_E}{n\rho} + \frac{q_C}{n(1-\rho)}\right\}$ for $\rho\in(0,1)$. This optimal solution is known as the Neyman allocation and it is given by

```
$$\rho_N = \frac{\sqrt{q_E}}{\sqrt{q_E}+\sqrt{q_C}} \\quad (10.1)$
```

The Neyman allocation assigns a greater proportion of patients to a more variable treatment group. Unfortunately, when $p_E+p_C>1$, the Neyman allocation is skewed in favor of the less successful treatment, which is inappropriate from an individual ethics perspective.

# ### 11.4.2.1 Adaptive Signature Design

The classical designs discussed earlier involve only one biomarker and it
is assumed that this biomarker positive set can be determined a priori.
The adaptive signature design [29] is a design proposed to select the
subgroup using a large number of potential biomarkers. This design is\appropriate when both the potential biomarkers and the cut off are
unknown; however, there is some evidence that the targeted therapy
may work in some of the shortlisted biomarkers. The method was
proposed initially using a combination of genetic markers and, hence, is
called the adaptive “gene” signature design. Recent developments
in Genomic technologies (especially Pharmacogenogenetics) have the
pot of identifying subgroups of patients who are likely to benefit
from a certain targeted therapy. The design and implementation of
studies with a large number of gene signatures are challenging. In
design there are three elements: (a) trial proposed to detect the overall
treatment effect at the end of the trial; (b) identification of the subgroupnof patients who are likely to benefit from the targeted therapy at the first
stage of the trial; (c) statistical hypothesis test to detect the treatment
difference based on the subgroup of patients who are randomized in the
latter half of the trial. These elements are pre-specified prospectively.
Appropriate tests are conducted for multiplicity in order to
control for type I error rate. The initial null hypothesis to test the benefit
of the targeted therapy against control is conducted in the overall
population. This test is conducted at a slightly lower significance level
than the overall alpha of 0.05 (example: 0.04 or 0.03). If the initial null
hypothesis is rejected at the lower significance level, then the targeted
therapy is declared superior to the control treatment for the overall
population. The hypothesis testing and analysis is complete at this
stage. If the first hypothesis is not rejected, then the signature
component of the design is used to select a potentially promising
biomarker subgroup.

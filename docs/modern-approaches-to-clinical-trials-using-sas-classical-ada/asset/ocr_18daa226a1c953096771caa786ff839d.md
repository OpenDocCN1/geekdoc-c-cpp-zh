For example, Jenkins et al. [48] proposed an adaptive seamless phase II/III design with a subpopulation selection that can be more flexible. It allows the trial to select the appropriate subgroup at the interim analysis or continue in all patients with both the subgroup and the full population. With this method, a combination of test statistics for the final endpoint each stage is used for hypothesis testing. The decision to extend to the second stage is based on an intermediate or surrogate endpoint correlated to the final endpoint. This method is presented in the context of oncology with the progression free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) as interim and final endpoints, though it can be extended to other indications. In this design, having a co-primary endpoint recognizes the uncertainty at the interim look on the best possible endpoint and population. The additional flexibility is in the fact that the testing can be still done on the overall population, subgroup population, or the co-primary endpoint at the final analysis and also stopping the trial at the interim analysis for futility. This mitigates the risk of missing a promising subgroup early in the trial which could be detrimental both from an ethical and commercial perspective. The proposed design uses PFS as the intermediate endpoint and overall survival as has been accepted as a confirmatory endpoint in many cancer types. The design can be viewed as a special case or extension of the existing seamless design methods which combines the population and endpoints of both stages in support of the licensing claims. There is a null and alternative hypothesis associated with each of the populations (Op and Sp).

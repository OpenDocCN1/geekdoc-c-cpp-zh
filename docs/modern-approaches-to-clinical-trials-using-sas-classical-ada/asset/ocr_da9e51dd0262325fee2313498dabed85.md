# 6.2.8 Example of the Design Stage

Rofecoxib® is a medication developed by Merck & Co. Inc. (Whitehouse Station, NJ, USA) for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and osteoarthritis (OA). In late 2004, Merck withdrew Rofecoxib® from the market because of concerns with excessive cardiovascular events. After this withdrawal, the drug development environment for any new anti-inflammatory agents became and more challenging. For every new product developed to treat RA or OA, both drug safety and efficacy were followed very closely to ensure that the efficacy of this new product can be well established, while there is no signal of any safety concerns. Hence, in the clinical development plan, after the PoC and early dose-dosing studies to demonstrate drug efficacy, there will be large scale, long-term phase III studies to follow for efficacy, cardiovascular safety, and gastrointestinal (GI) safety.

A true case study of developing a drug to treat osteoarthritis (OA) is introduced here as an example [16]. This is a four-week where OA patients first go through a screening visit. Then, after a wash-out of the current OA medication, patients are randomized between the treatment arms. Baseline measurements (demographic, medical history, background medications, efficacy, and other data) are collected prior to the first dose of the study drug. Thereafter, over the four-week treatment period, efficacy and safety measurements are continuously collected at each stage. Note that at the early stage of clinical development, the maximum length of the study is constrained by the length of animal studies completed in the non-clinical experiments. In this case study, the animal toxicity studies are followed up to 4 weeks, and hence, the current clinical study is designed with a four-week study period.

In order to offer the best opportunity for the new compound to show efficacy at the early phase II stage, the test dose to be used in the PoC should be as high as possible. In a typical development program for drugs treating OA, both the efficacy dose response and the safety dose-response relationships are assumed to be monotonic.

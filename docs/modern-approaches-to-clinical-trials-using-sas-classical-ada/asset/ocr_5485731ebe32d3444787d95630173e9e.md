Prior to implementation of a phase I clinical trial, extensive simulation studies should be conducted to examine the operating characteristics of the design, so that the design parameters such as the skeletons and the prior distributions can be properly calibrated. A good design should have robust and acceptable behaviors under various toxicity scenarios. To investigate the practical performance of the CRM and BMA-CRM, we consider six dose levels with monotonically increasing toxicity. The target toxicity probability is $\phi=30\%$. We use a non-informative prior distribution for $\alpha_i$ $\sim N(0,\sigma_i^2)$ and $\sigma_i^2=2$. The first cohort is treated at the lowest dose level, and the maximum sample size is 30. As expected, the BMA-CRM generates more robust dose-finding in comparison to the four individual CRMs using a single skeleton.

# 4.8 Summary

In this chapter, we presented a general Bayesian methodology and a detailed development of a SAS macro, BSMED, for meta-experimental design for survival data. Throughout this chapter, we only consider the exponential regression model, which is a potential limitation of the SAS macro BSMED. However, the exponential model is attractive in the sense that the individual patient-level survival data is not required. Another major motivation for using the exponential model in this chapter is that the historical data itself were often only available in aggregate form. Thus, the exponential model was most natural in this setting, even if we had individual patient-level data for the trials themselves.

We note that the assumed meta-regression survival model in the SAS macro BSMED is a log-linear fixed-effects model. There are several advantages of the fixed effects model over the random effects model. First, the fixed effects model is easier and more convenient to implement in PROC MIX. Second, the fixed effects model do not require the specification of the data values for the variability of the random effects, Third, the fixed effects model is more flexible to accommodate different annualized event rates of historical meta-survival data across previous trials. In addition, as shown in [11, 12], the fixed effects model and the random effects model essentially yield a similar sample size. If individual patient-level survival data are available, then the Bayesian methodology discussed in this chapter can be easily extended to other survival regression models discussed in [17].

As a future project, we will extend the SAS macro BSMED to allow different survival models, such as the Weibull regression model and other survival models discussed in [17] for the setting of the meta-design with individual patient level data.

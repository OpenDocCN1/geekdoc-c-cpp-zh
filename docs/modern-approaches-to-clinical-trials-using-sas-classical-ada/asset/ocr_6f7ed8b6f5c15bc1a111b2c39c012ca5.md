# 9.5.1 Play-the-Winner Rule

From an ethical standpoint, clinical trials should utilize the evidence accumulated from the data to assign more patients to the superior arm, so that more patients will benefit from the better treatment. Response or outcome-based adaptive randomization aims to achieve this goal by skewing the number of patients to a potentially better treatment group.

The play-the-winner rule attempts to allocate more patients to the potentially better treatment: staying with the winner until a failure outcome occurs and then switching to the alternative treatment [10]. This rule is often applied to the dichotomous cases, where the outcome is either a success or failure. In a study comparing two treatments A and B, Figure 9.2 Play-the-Winner Rule with Dichotomous Outcomes with Treatments A and B: the first patient is randomized to either treatment A or B with an equal probability. If the previous patient’s response is a success, the treatment for that patient retains for the next patient; while in the case of a failure, the next patient will receive the other treatment. Success is a solid circle and failure is an open circle.

Such alternating process continues until the total sample size is exhausted. The ratio of the numbers of subjects assigned to treatments A and B tends to $(1-p_B) / (1-p_A)$ as the number of patients in the trial goes to infinity, where $p_A$ and $p_B$ denote the response rates of treatments A and B, respectively.

hence, there is no MCP adjustment. For a simple trend contrast with low to discrete doses, the treatment groups are simply ordered from high. The placebo response is assumed to be 0, the lowest dose (2.5%) of MTD is assumed to have 20% response, the next lowest (5% of MTD) is assumed to have 40% response, the medium dose (12.5%) of MTD is assumed to have 60% response, the second highest dose (25% of MTD) is assumed to have 100% response. Then the coefficients -5, -3, -1, 1, 3, and 5 are assigned to these treatment groups [16]. Here, the primary hypothesis to be tested is:

```
$H_0: -5\mu_0-3\mu_1-\mu_2+3\mu_3+5\mu_4 \le 0$.
$H_1: -5\mu_0-3\mu_1-\mu_2+3\mu_3+5\mu_4 > 0$,
```

where $\mu_0$ denotes the mean of placebo group, $\mu_1$ denotes the mean of the 5% dose group, $\mu_2$ denotes the mean of the 12.5% dose group, $\mu_3$ denotes the mean of the 25% dose group, and $\mu_4$ denotes the mean of the 75% dose group.

In the case of a dose-response study with $k$ dose groups and a placebo, the sample size estimates based on a contrast can be derived as follows [21]. Let $\mu_i$ be the population mean for group $i, i = 0, \dots, k$, where $\mu_0$ is the mean from the placebo group, and let $c_i$ be the corresponding coefficients in the contrast. The null hypothesis of no treatment effect (i.e., weight sum of treatment effect across all doses) can be written as:

```
$H_0: L(\mu)=\sum_{i=0}^k c_i\mu_i \le 0$ vs $H_1: L(\mu)=\sum_{i=0}^k c_i\mu_i > 0$, with $\sum_{i=0}^k c_i^2 = 0$.
```

Under the alternative hypothesis and the condition of homogeneous variances, the sample size per group (i.e., equal sample size in each group) can be obtained as

```
$n = \frac{(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\eta})^2\sigma^2}{\sum_{i=0}^k c_i^2}$
```

where $z_{1-\alpha}$ and $z_{1-\eta}$ are the corresponding quantile points of a standard normal distribution.

```
proc print noobs label;
    var alpha power delta pi Ds;
run;
%mend events;
%events(2, 0.05, 0.2, 0.8);
```

The parameters to be specified in the %events are:

+   1. delta: ratio of the hazard of the experimental arm over that in thencontrol arm

+   2. alpha: significance level (one-sided type I error rate)

+   3. beta: false-negative rate (type II error rate)

+   4. pi: fraction of the population where treatment effect delta is limited; if
treatment effect applies to the entire study population, pi=1.

For example, if the hazard ratio is 0.5, the treatment effect is limited to
80% of the overall population, the number of observed events
required for a 80% power study (at a one-sided significance level of
0.05) is 80.4261, which would round up to 81 events (Example Code
11.5 Number of Events for Biomarker-Adaptive Threshold Design).

Biomarker development and validation is usually very expensive and time
consuming. Often times a reliable biomarker is not available during
t late phase of the clinical trial, namely pivotal trials. If a treatment
effect is not detected in the overall population but an increased
response is observed in the subgroup, an additional pivotal trial is
required for regulatory approval; thus, further increasing the cost and
time in the developmental process. This scenario is illustrated in the
following example. Ravaud et al. [35] presented a randomized study in
renal cell patients who express either EGFR/ErbB2. The
experimental arm was Lapatinib, an EGFR/ErbB2 tyrosine kinase
inhibitor. The overall survival rate showed no statistical difference
p(p=029 with median 43.1 weeks in standard of care versus 46.9 weeks
in Lapatinib). Among the subgroup of patients with over expression of
EGFR in their tumors, the overall survival rate was statistically significant
p=0.02 with median 37.9 weeks in standard of care versus 46 weeks in
Lapatinib). Since these findings were a result of the secondary subgroup

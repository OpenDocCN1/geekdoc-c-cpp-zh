# 8.5 Summary

The Bayesian model is a flexible tool that can be used to better understand the dose response. In some cases, it may converge where a traditional maximum likelihood model may not converge [2]. Moreover, there may be examples where the maximum likelihood model converges but estimates for the parameters such as ED50 be nonsensical. In such situations, a Bayesian analysis may yield robust estimates of the model parameters. Like all Bayesian analyses, the results are dependent on the assumed priors, careful consideration should be given to the center and spread of the prior distribution. For clinical trials, this means that the prior range should make biological and/or clinical sense. In small studies or studies with few doses, frequentist estimation could be explored in these cases to see if the convergence properties of analysis may yield robust estimates [2]. A Bayesian model could be improved. As stated in the ICH E4, dose-response information to support drug registration, the agency is open to Bayesian techniques of analysis and it reiterates the importance of Bayesian dose-response development because prior clinical and pre-clinical studies may be included in the estimation of the effect of the most recent trial, though reconciliation may be required if there are variable effects from trial to trial. Being able to change the prior information also provides an opportunity to assess the robustness of the conclusions that may be drawn for the current study.

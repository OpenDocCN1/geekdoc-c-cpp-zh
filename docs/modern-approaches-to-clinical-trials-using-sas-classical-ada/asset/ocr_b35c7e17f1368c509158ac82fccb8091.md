# 10.7.1 Redesigning a Real Clinical Trial

In this section we give an example of a published clinical trial where an optimal RAR design could be useful. Connor and co-authors in [66] reported the results of a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of the efficacy and safety of zidovudine (AZT) in reducing the risk of maternal-infant transmission of the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) type 1. In this, 477 pregnant women were enrolled and randomized equally between AZT [nc= 239] and a placebo [nc=238]. In the study, 409 women gave birth to 415 live-born infants. The estimated proportions of uninfected infants at 18 months were 0.917 for the AZT group and 0.745 for the placebo group; that, the AZT treatment reduced the risk of maternal-infant HIV transmission by approximately two thirds compared to placebo. An optimal RAR design would assign the AZT treatment more often, thereby achieving a more effective trial design.

We consider a redesign of this trial by simulation. Four competing randomization procedures will be compared:

+   1. Completely Randomized Design (CRD): Every patient is randomized between the treatment groups with a probability 1/2. (While such design would not be used in practice, it is included here to enable a comparison of variability with two RAR designs.)

+   2. Permuted Block Design (PBD): Randomization is applied in blocks of size 4 such that treatment numbers are equalized after every four patients.

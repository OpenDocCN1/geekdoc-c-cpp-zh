#  11.4.3.1 Bayesian Adaptive Randomization (BAR) Enrichment Design

An important feature of an adaptive clinical trial is to continually update the design by using the accumulated information from interim data, which fits naturally into the Bayesian paradigm. The Bayesian approach was actually developed specifically to deal with new data as come in by updating prior beliefs to provide direct probability statements about the treatment effect. Moreover, in contrast to frequentist adaptive designs that require the type I error rate to be controlled, Bayesian inference is not affected by the number or timing of the interim analyses [55], allowing multiple analyses of the accumulating study data that update the probabilities under investigation. As a result, Bayesian designs are appealing to adaptive enrichment designs where adaptations in enrollment, randomization ratio, and subgroup selection are carried out based on emerging data, particularly in exploratory studies to screen biomarkers.

In contrast to the equal (or fixed ratio) randomization approach in a frequentist design, this type of Bayesian design [56-57] implements an adaptive randomization method that allows the evaluation of treatments and biomarkers simultaneously, while providing more patients with potentially more effective treatments according to the patients’ marker profiles. The Bayesian design is usually based on a hierarchical model for the endpoint of interests. The model parameters are also random variables modeling the random effects regarding the association of treatments and biomarkers with the outcomes of interest. With the use of hierarchical models, information can be borrowed across different subgroups as a means to increase efficiency.

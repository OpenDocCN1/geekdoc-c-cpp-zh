+   - Total number of failures in the trial: $F(m) = F_E(n) + F_C(n)$, where $F_k(n)$ is the number of failures on treatment $k=E,C$;

+   - Estimated treatment difference $\hat{\Delta}(n) = \hat{ho}_E - \hat{ho}_C$;

+   - Empirical (average) power of the Wald test (with 2-sided $\alpha=0.05$).

Supplemental program ch10.4.2.sas was used to simulate the operating characteristics of the five designs I–V for a scenario when $p_E=0.7$ and $p_C=0.4$, based on 10,000 simulations. The trial sample size was set $n=120$ which corresponds to $\sim92\%$ power of the Wald test for the equal allocation design. The target allocation is 0.5 for designs I and II and it is 0.57 for designs III, IV, and V.

Figure 10.3 shows simulated distributions of the allocation proportion. For design I (CRD), the distribution is centered around 0.5 but variability is nontrivial (the range for the allocation proportion is 0.33 to 0.68). For design II (PBD), the treatment sample sizes are always perfectly balanced; therefore, the allocation is constant at 0.5. The RAR designs III (SMLE), IV (DBCD with $\gamma=2$) and V (ERADE) have approximately normal distributions that are centered around target allocation 0.57. Consistent with theory, ERADE has the lowest variability and SMLE has the highest variability among the three RAR designs.

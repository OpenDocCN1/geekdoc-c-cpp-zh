# 5.4 Fractional Continual Reassessment Method

Most of the dose-finding methods, including the aforementioned CRM and BMA-CRM, require the DLT outcomes to be ascertainable shortly after treatment. In other words, the toxicity outcomes of all the previously treated patients must be available in order to determine the next dose assignment. However, it may take a long period of time to measure the DLT outcomes after treatment. In a more common scenario, if the patient accrual is fast and the enrollment, missing toxicity data arise as the outcomes of the patients under treatment may still be unavailable when that information is needed. For example, in radiotherapy trials, DLTs occur long after the treatment is finished. In a phase I trial with pancreatic cancer, the goal was to determine the MTD of oxaliplatin with combination with gemcitabine and the concurrent radiotherapy therapy [7]. The accrual rate was one patient every two weeks, whereas, took on average nine weeks to assess the toxicity outcome of each patient after the treatment was initiated. Consequently, at the moment of dose assignment for a newly arrived patient, the patients under treatment might not have completed the full assessment period and, thus, the non-toxicity outcomes could be considered missing or censored at that time.

To accommodate late-onset toxicity, we can model the underlying times to toxicity through the well-known Kaplan-Meier estimator [8,9]. An alternative approach to handling censored data is to redistribute the mass of each censored observation to the right [10,11]. Typically, there is a specified toxicity evaluation window to determine the DLT outcome. Within the evaluation window [0, $	au$], if a patient experiences the DLT, we take $y=1$; if a patient still has not experienced the DLT after time $	au$, we take $y=0$. Due to continual decision making on dose assignment for each new cohort, patients currently under treatment may not have experienced the DLT when a new cohort of patients is ready for treatment. Under such circumstances, the toxicity event can be viewed as censored data, and we adopt the redistribution to the right approach by splitting the mass of 1 between the censoring point and beyond $	au$ (anywhere that is larger than $	au$).

# 10.6.1 More Than Two Treatments and Non-Binary Outcomes

Many RCTs involve more than two treatment arms. For instance, in a phase II dose ranging study, the treatment arms may be a placebo and/or an active control and several doses of an investigational drug. The study objectives may include estimation of various treatment contrasts and testing hypotheses of equality of treatment effects. Frequently, the optimal allocation ratio is unequal across treatment arms [53]. As noted in [33], “...the adaptive randomization light brightest in complicated multi-arm settings,” and indeed, optimal RAR designs are expected to provide more substantial gains over equal randomization in trials with more than two treatment arms (e.g. by placing more patients at the “most informative” dose levels). A general framework for finding optimal allocation designs to minimize a weighted sum of treatment sample sizes subject to constraints on power in a multi-arm setting was proposed by Timofeyev and co-authors in the context of a binary outcome trial [17]. Subsequently, their approach was extended to multi-arm, multi-objective clinical trials with normal and time-to-event outcomes [18,19,54,55]. For trials where the primary objectives involve estimation of multiple treatment contrasts, one can use the Da-optimal allocation [56]. For implementing optimal allocation in a multi-arm trial, one can use the DBCD procedure [48] or an immigrated urn model [26]. Both the DBCD and the immigrated urn model are RAR designs that yield consistent and asymptotically normal estimators and sample allocation proportions under widely satisfied conditions.

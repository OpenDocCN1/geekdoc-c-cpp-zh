# 5.2 Continual Reassessment Method

To incorporate the monotonic toxicity order, a dose-toxicity curve or a parametric model is often assumed. In contrast to algorithm-based designs, such as the 3+3 design, where dose escalation or deescalation is solely based on the data collected at the current dose level, model-based designs can pool all the information across different doses for decision making. Among a variety of model-based dose-finding methods, the continual reassessment method (CRM) is a very popular approach which is often cast in the Bayesian framework [1]. The CRM may take a single-parameter power model, a two-parameter logistic regression model, or a probit model. Different parametric models represent different patterns of the dose-toxicity curve, while the underlying true curve is typically unknown.

With a focus on the one-parameter power model [2], $p_1 < ... < p_j$ denote the specified toxicity probabilities of a set of doses for the drug under consideration, which is often known as the skeleton of the CRM. $\alpha$ denote the target toxicity probability specified by the investigator. We model the toxicity probability at dose level $j$, $j=1, ..., j$, in the form of

$P(\text{toxicity at dose level } j) = \pi_j(\\alpha) = p_j^{\exp(\alpha)}$

$\alpha$ is an unknown parameter. Suppose that at a certain stage of the trial, we observe the data that $y_j$ patients have experienced the DLT among the $n_j$ patients treated at dose level $j$, $j=1, ..., j$. If we let $D$ denote the observed data, the binomial likelihood function is given by

$L(\\alpha|D) \propto \prod [p_j^{\exp(\alpha))^{y_j}(1-p_j^{\exp(\alpha))})^{n_j-y_j}$

In the Bayesian paradigm, we often take a normal prior distribution for $\alpha$, i.e. $\alpha \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$, with a prior mean of zero and a prior variance $\sigma^2$. Using Bayes' theorem, we can estimate the toxicity probability at dose level $j$ by the corresponding posterior,

$\tilde{\pi}_j = \int p_j^{\exp(\\alpha)} \frac{L(\\alpha|D)f(\alphaalpha)}{\int L(\\alpha|D)f(\alphaalpha)} d\alpha$.

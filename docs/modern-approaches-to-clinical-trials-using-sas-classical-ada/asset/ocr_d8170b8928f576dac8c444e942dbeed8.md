# 3.5 Summary and Conclusions

Several different methods for blinded and unblinded sample size estimations were presented in this chapter for normally distributed data. Blinded methods reviewed included the one-sample variance method and the adjusted one-sample variance method. Unblinded SSR methods included Fisher’s combination test, the inverse normal method, CHW procedure, the promising zone design and conditional type I error preserving procedures (Proscan and Hansberger, Müller and Schäfer). With the exception of the promising zone and the Müller and Schäfer procedure, all the other unblinded methods can be viewed as a special form of a combination test.

These procedures can be extended to the case of a binary endpoint based on the asymptotic normal approximation of binomial distribution in situations where such approximation is appropriate. In cases where exact binomial distribution has to be used, the problem of SSR becomes more complicated and was not investigated within this chapter.

Except for the promising zone and the Proscan and Hansberger methods, unblinded SSR approaches reviewed were presented under the assumption that sample size adjustment is the only decision to be made at the interim analysis. i.e. to focus on the concept of SSR under each method). In practice, other decisions such as early stop for utility or efficacy accompany SSR. For more real-life situations, the approaches presented in this section remain generally applicable, but the type I and type II error rate calculation needs to be adjusted to reflect opportunities for early stopping.

When a design includes the opportunity for an early efficacy stop, the need to preserve the overall type I error rate is well accepted. Early stopping due to utility is more subtle. It is common knowledge that stopping for utility can,, reduce the false positive rate, making it possible to use a higher significance level at the final analysis while maintaining the overall type I error rate at the desired level [38].

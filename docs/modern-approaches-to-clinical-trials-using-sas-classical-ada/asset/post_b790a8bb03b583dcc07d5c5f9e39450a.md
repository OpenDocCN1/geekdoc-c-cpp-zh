
## 4.3 Meta-Regression Survival Models

### 4.3.1 Model for Future Survival Data

In this section, we discuss the meta-regression survival model under the general survival meta-experimental design setting discussed in 4.2 Meta Design Setting. Let $y_{jk}$ denote the subject-level time-to-event (failure time) and $v_{jk}$ denote the right censoring indicator such that $v_{jk}=1$ if $y_{jk}$ is a failure time and $v_{jk}=0$ if $y_{jk}$ is right censored, the subject in the $j$-th arm and the $k$-th trial for $i=1, \dots, n_{jk}, j=1,2, \dots, K$. We write $\hat{y}_{jk}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n_{jk}} y_{ijk}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n_{jk}} v_{ijk}}$ which denotes the total subject year duration and the total number of subjects, respectively, for $j=1,2, \dots, K$. We let $x_{jk}=0$ (control) or 1 for $j=2$ (experimental). Then, we have the observed aggregate meta-survival data denoted by $D_K=\{y_{jk}, v_{jk}, x_{jk}\}, j=1,2, \dots, K$.

We further assume that, individual-level failure time follows an exponential distribution with mean $1/\lambda_{jk}$, $Y_{jk}\sim Exp(\lambda_{jk})$, where $\lambda_{jk}>0$ is the hazard rate. The density function of $Y_{jk}|v_{jk}$ is given by

$f(y_{jk}|v_{jk}|\lambda_{jk})=\lambda_{jk}e^{-\lambda_{jk}y_{jk}}$. (4.2)

The log-linear model for $\lambda_{jk}$ assumes

$\log(\lambda_{jk})=\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k$. (4.3)

where $x_{jk}=0$ for $j=1$ (control) or 1 for $j=2$ (experimental) for $k=1, \dots, K$. To ensure model identifiability in (4.2), we impose the constraint $\sum_{k=1}^K\theta_k=0$. For simplicity, we take $\theta_K=-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\theta_k$. $\theta_k$ captures the differences among the trials. Note that under the exponential model, the design parameter $\gamma_1$ is the log hazard ratio of the treatment in the experimental arm. Let $\theta=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_K\}$ and $\gamma=(\gamma_0,\gamma_1)$. Then, the likelihood function based on the meta-survival data $D_K$ is given by

$L(\gamma,\theta|D_K)=\prod_{k=1}^K\prod_{j=1}^2\exp\{y_{jk}(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k)\}$,

$\exp\{-(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k)y_{jk}\}$. (4.4)

In (4.4), we see that the likelihood function does not depend on the sample size $n_{jk}$. It implies that under the exponential model, the likelihood function based on the individual level meta-experimental survival data reduces to the likelihood function based on the treatment level meta-experimental survival data.

### 4.3.2 Model for Historical Survival Data

In this section, we consider meta-regression survival models for the different types of the historical data discussed in 4.2.3 Input Historical Data. Suppose $y_{jk}$ is the total subject year duration and $v_{jk}$ is the censoring indicator such that $v_{jk}=1$ if $y_{jk}$ is a failure time and $v_{jk}=0$ if $y_{jk}$ is censored for the $k^{th}$ trial for $j=1, \dots, K_0$. In addition, $x_{jk}$ represents only one arm (control or experimental) if it is a scalar and both arms if it is a vector, as discussed in Section 4.2.3. Suppose that the trial-level aggregate data $D_k=\{y_{jk}, v_{jk}, x_{jk}\}, k=1, \dots, K_0\}$ are available from the $K_0$ historical studies. Assume that the individual failure time follows an exponential distribution, $Exp(\lambda_{jk})$, where the hazard rate $\lambda_{jk} > 0$. Then the model assumes

$log(\lambda_{jk}) = \gamma_0 + \gamma_1(j-1) + \theta_k, \quad (4.5)$

for $x_{0k} \leq j-1 \leq x_{2k}, k=1, \dots, K_0$. Suppose that the historical data are available only for the control arm. This is the first type of historical data discussed in Section 4.2.3 and it is easy to see that $x_{0k}=0$ for this case. Then the models for the historical and current data share only one common parameter, namely, $\gamma_0$. However, if the historical data are available only for the experimental arm, which is the second type of historical data, then $x_{0k}=1$ and the models for the historical and current data share one composite parameter $\gamma_0+\gamma_1$. Now, we consider the third type of data, in which the historical data are available for both arms. In this case, the models for the historical and current data share two common parameters $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$. The likelihood function for the data is given

$L(\gamma,\theta|D_K)=\prod_{k=1}^K\prod_{j=x_{0k}}^{x_{2k}}\exp\{y_{jk}(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k)\}$,

$\exp\{-(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k)y_{jk}\}$. (4.6)

## 4.4 Bayesian Experimental Design

In this section, we present a general methodology for Bayesian-experimental design. We first define the hypotheses for non-inferiority, which can be formulated as:

$H_0: e^{\gamma_1} \ge \zeta$ versus $H_1: e^{\gamma_1} < \zeta$ (4.7)

where $e^{\gamma_1}$ is the hazard ratio and $\zeta$ is the non-inferiority margin. Let $[\gamma, \theta]$ denote the collection of parameters. Following the literature, let $\pi^{(S)}[\gamma, \theta]$ and $\pi^{(F)}[\gamma, \theta]$ denote the sampling and fitting prior, respectively. The sampling prior play a role to capture a certain portion of the parameter space. Using the data $D_K$, the posterior distribution of $[\gamma, \theta]$ is given

$\pi[\gamma, \theta|D_K] \propto \prod_{k=1}^K\prod_{j=1}^2\exp\{y_{jk}(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k)\}\exp\{-(\gamma_0+\gamma_1x_{jk}+\theta_k)y_{jk}\}$

$\times \pi^{(F)}[\gamma, \theta]$. (4.8)

To complete the Bayesian-experimental design, we need to specify two proper sampling priors, $\pi_0^{(s)}[\gamma, \theta]$ and $\pi_1^{(s)}[\gamma, \theta]$, which are defined on the subsets of the parameter spaces induced by hypotheses $H_0$ and $H_1$. We define the key quantity as

$\beta_{sl}^{(n)} = E_{sl}[1\{P(e^{\gamma_1} < \delta D_K, \pi^{(f)}) \geq \eta_0\}], \quad (4.9)$

where the indicator function $1_{\mathbb{A}}$ is 1 if $\mathbb{A}$ is true and 0 otherwise, $0 < \eta_0 < 1$ is a prespecified Bayesian credible level, the posterior probability $P(e^{\gamma_1} < \delta D_K, \pi^{(f)})$ is computed with respect to the posterior distribution of $\gamma_1$ given the data $D_K$ under the fitting prior $\pi^{(f)}[\gamma, \theta]$, and the expectation $E_{sl}$ is taken with respect to the predictive marginal distribution of $D_K$ under the sampling prior $\pi_l^{(s)}[\gamma, \theta]$ for $l=0,1$.

This model is also called the benefit function, which is used to validate the results from the validation sub-sample. the benefit function used with this design is the minimum odds ratio [29] for the treatment marker interaction with a pre-specified number of biomarkers and the significance level. The risk difference is another benefit function that has been used which is the difference between the targeted therapy and the control in the probability of response as a function of baseline variables [20, 30-31]. The prediction is done for each subject in this sample and a predicted score is calculated to classify the subset as sensitive or non-sensitive. The subgroup is selected using a pre-specified cut-off for this predicted score. The second hypothesis test is conducted in this subgroup to see the benefit of the targeted therapy against the control. This test is conducted at a much lower significance (example: 0.01 or 0.02).

Generally, as the proportion of the sensitive patients increases, so does the difference in the objective response rates of the experimental arm and the control arm. Therefore, for prevalence above a certain threshold, the power of the overall effect test will dominate the power of the sensitive subset. The adaptive signature design preserves the ability to detect the overall effect in this case. However, its advantage over the traditional design (testing for overall effect with broad eligibility) is reduced. The formulas that can assist the investigators in assessing the power relation between the sensitive subset and the overall test are presented in [25].

The power macro is developed for calculating power based on formula (1.1) in [25]. When calculating the power of the overall test, the following parameters need to be specified:

+   1. pe: the expected probability of response for a patient receiving the experimental treatment

+   2. pc: the expected probability of response for a patient receiving the control treatment

+   3. alpha: one-sided significance level

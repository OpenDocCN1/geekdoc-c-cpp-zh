To facilitate the design, the maximum number of interim looks at at the data is pre-specified. At the interim monitoring phase, the interim looks or interim analyses can be conducted at precisely equally spaced intervals or when outcomes of equal number of subjects are ascertained. During the course of a trial, many factors may influence the frequency and timing of interim analyses. If the frequency or timing of the analyses is to be substantially altered from the planned schedule then the designs described above will become inappropriate. There are two distinct approaches to monitoring: continuous and discrete. As an abstract concept, continuous monitoring refers to the test statistic on a continuous scale as a function of the continuous information fraction. In the context of clinical trial, continuous monitoring means monitoring after observing the outcome of every single subject. In practice, this is seldom performed. Discrete monitoring, however, refers to monitoring after observing the outcomes of a group of subjects. This type of sequential monitoring is the focus of this chapter.

During the actual conduct of the group sequential trial, one may want to allow for flexibility in the analysis, while maintaining the integrity of the trial as bias may be introduced during interim examinations of accrued data. The (DSMB) data and safety monitoring board should not be understated. For instance, it may be necessary to set the dates of the interim monitoring looks so as to accommodate the schedule of the DSMB. Typically, the DSMB plans to meet after equal increments of calendar time, which do not necessarily coincide with the information fractions specified at the design stage.

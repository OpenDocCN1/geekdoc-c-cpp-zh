Optimal RAR designs have firm theoretical basis, and they can frequently be more efficient and more ethical than conventional equal allocation randomization designs [14,17-19]. Under widely satisfied conditions, optimal RAR designs have similar asymptotic proportions and maximum likelihood estimators) to the fixed randomization designs [35]. A recent review of proper statistical inferential procedures following optimal RAR designs can be found in [36]. It should be acknowledged that many optimal RAR designs are relatively new methods that have not yet demonstrated their potential in practice. To facilitate implementation of these designs, validated information systems (to perform interactive data entry and dynamic update of treatment randomization probabilities) must be developed. In addition, input from the health authorities (FDA, EMA) will be essential for successful implementation of optimal RAR designs in clinical trials.

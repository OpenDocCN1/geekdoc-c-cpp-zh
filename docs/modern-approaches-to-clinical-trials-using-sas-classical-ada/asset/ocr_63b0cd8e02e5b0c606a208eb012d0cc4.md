Beyond identifying the candidate set of models, at least two other questions need to be addressed when designing a dose-fining study: (i) How many, and which doses to utilize and, (ii) how many subjects to allocate to the different doses.

Regarding (i) we suggest using 4-7 active dose levels as a rule of thumb and consider at least a dose range [7]. Equal spacing on the scale is commonly used for the dose strengths. More formally one can also consider optimal design theory to calculate doses and allocations to these doses. The essential idea is to use information on the anticipated dose-response curve and then to calculate doses and allocation to the covariance matrix of the parameter estimates is minimized in a specified sense. When MCP-Mod is being utilized in a given study, a candidate set of dose-response models has then already decided upon and this information can be used to calculate, for example, a model-averaged optimal design for calculation of the model parameters (D-optimal design) or estimation of a target dose (c-optimal design). Due to practical constraints in terms of production of the doses and logistics, it might not be possible to use the calculated optimal design directly. But one can also restrict the search for an adequate design to those that are feasible both in terms of logistics and production. Several authors discuss optimal design theory in the context of dose-fining studies [8,9]. Atkinson et al. provide a general background on optimal designs and discuss how to implement them in SAS [10].

# ## 2.2 Examples of Classical Fixed-Sample Designs

Before we develop the theories of group sequential procedures in the next section, we will describe four examples of clinical trials that were planned using the classical fixed-sample design. However, these trials can be modified into new designs that use the group sequential methods. # Example 1: Pravastatin Therapy in Children with Familial Hypercholesterolemia

Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) is a genetic disorder characterized by high cholesterol levels in the blood, specifically very high levels of low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C). LDL-C is an undesirable type of cholesterol and therefore FH increases the risk of early cardiovascular disease. Accumulation of LDL-C in FH leads to deterioration of the vascular morphology and gives rise to increased intima-media thickness (IMT) of the carotid arteries. As a result, myocardial ischemia and coronary stenosis have been documented in young adults with this disorder. A clinical trial was designed to answer questions regarding the long-term (2 years) efficacy and safety of a cholesterol-lowering medication, pravastatin [13].

In this clinical trial, children aged between 8 and 18 years with FH were recruited from a medical center. The intervention of interest was pravastatin 20mg taken daily for children younger than 14 years or 40mg daily for those who were 14 years or older. This trial was placebo-controlled and double-blinded with children randomized to either pravastatin treatment or placebo. These children followed regularly over a period of 2 years. The primary outcome was defined as a change from baseline mean carotid IMT, comparing pravastatin and placebo groups after 2 years follow-up. The alternative hypothesis was that the pravastatin group has a greater reduction in carotid IMT from baseline than the placebo group.

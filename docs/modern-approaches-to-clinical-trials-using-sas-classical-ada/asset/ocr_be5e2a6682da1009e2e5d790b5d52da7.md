# 10.7.2 Implementing DBCD

# 10.7.2.1 Three Cohort Design

Here we describe two examples that can be modified by the reader in order to implement RAR designs in practice. Sample results are provided in the supplemental materials.

Supplemental program `ch10.7.2.sas` illustrates a practical implementation of the DBCD [$\gamma=2$] for a three cohort design. In this example, 60 patients are randomized to either $E$ or $C$ using PBD with block size 4, with responses generated from a Bernoulli distribution with a probability of 0.4 or 0.2, respectively. The second cohort (20 patients) is randomized to either $E$ or $C$ given the data from Cohort 1. Responses are generated from a Bernoulli distribution with probability of 0.4 or 0.2 for $E$ or $C$, respectively. The last cohort is randomized to $E$ or $C$ using the DBCD [$\gamma=2$] given data from Cohorts 1 and 2.

# 10.7.2.2 Two Cohort Sequential Design

Supplemental program `ch10.7.2.sas` also illustrates two cohort sequential design using DBCD [$\gamma=2$]. A total of 60 patients will be randomized into the first cohort (20 patients) is randomized to $E$ or $C$ using PBD with block size 4, with responses generated from a Bernoulli distribution with a probability of 0.4 or 0.2, respectively. After this cohort is complete, further allocation is sequentially: every patient is randomized to $E$ or $C$ using DBCD [$\gamma=2$], given the data from all previous patients and assuming responses are instantaneous. Patient responses are generated from a Bernoulli distribution with probability of 0.4 or 0.2 for $E$ or $C$, respectively.

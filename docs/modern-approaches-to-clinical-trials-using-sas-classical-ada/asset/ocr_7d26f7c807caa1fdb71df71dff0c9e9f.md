Third, due to the structure of independent increments for $S(t_j)$'s, it is possible to perform the actual computations that lead to these group sequential stopping boundaries and their crossing probabilities by the technique or recursive integration [3].

We have just developed the general distribution theory which can be applicable to any data generated from any arbitrary probability model in which a single parameter $	heta$ characterizes the relationship under investigation. In the following sections, we will consider two-arm randomized clinical trials with normal, binomial, or time-to-event endpoints as examples to illustrate that different models provide us with a test statistic whose same as that of the stochastic process statistic $S(t_j)$. In addition, when testing for superiority of one treatment over the other, it is customary to assume that $	heta_0 = 0$, but when testing for non-inferiority of one treatment to other, $	heta_0$ is often referred to as the non-inferiority margin, usually taking on a negative value.

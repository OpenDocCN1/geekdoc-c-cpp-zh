# 10.3.4 Efficient Randomized-Adaptive Design

Hu and co-authors in [50] considered the following question: Can a RAR procedure be found that preserves allocation randomness, and can target any pre-specified allocation and has minimal variability? They proposed a class of efficient randomized-adaptive designs (ERADE) for which the $(m+1)$ patient is randomized to $E$ with probability

```
$$\phi_{m+1} | E = \left\{ \begin{array}{cc} \hat{\phi}_m, & N_E(m) | m > \hat{\phi}_m \ \hat{\phi}_m, & N_E(m) | m = \hat{\phi}_m \ 1-\hat{\phi}_m, & N_E(m) | m < \hat{\phi}_m \end{array} \right.$
```

where $0 \leq \zeta \leq 1$ is a user-defined parameter that controls the degree of randomness ($\zeta=0$ is most deterministic and most random). Hu and co-authors recommended setting $\zeta$ between 0.4 and 0.7 [50]. ERADE is an extension of the biased-coin design [5] which is used to target equal allocation.

The theoretical properties of ERADE are similar to those of DBCD: both sample proportions and estimators are strongly consistent and asymptotically normal. However, a fundamental difference between the two designs is that the allocation function for ERADE is discrete, whereas for DBCD it is continuous in $ho$. This means that ERADE is less variable than DBCD. In fact, ERADE is equivalent to DBCD with $\gamma \to \infty$ and it is asymptotically best RAR procedure [51]. The sample proportion $N_E(m) / m$ for ERADE is asymptotically normal with mean $\rho$ and asymptotic variance $\left(\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial E}\right)^2 \frac{\rho q_E}{\rho\|+\left(\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial C}\right)^2 \frac{\rho q_C}{1-\rho}$, which is the lower bound on the variance of a RAR procedure targeting allocation $\rho$.

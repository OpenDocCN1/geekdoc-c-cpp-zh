However, because the nature of a dose-ranging trial is that it includes multiple treatment groups, this may cause some level of confusion – both to statisticians and nonstatisticians. However, if the decision is to only use a single degree of freedom contrast test as the primary comparison, then the situation can be simplified to lot. In the case of one contrast, the two major challenges would be what assumptions of dose-response relationship that the team is willing to make, and sample size allocation – either a balanced approach or an imbalanced approach. After these decisions are made, the statistician would then follow up and calculate the sample size for the study.

Suppose a contrast with one degree of freedom is selected, and the sample size allocation is determined, the typical sample size considerations, such as choice of treatment difference, choice of alpha and beta can then be specified. With this information, sample size can be calculated using an existing formula. An example of a real study design in a case of one test product developed for treatment of osteoarthritis is included in this manuscript to demonstrate how these methods can be applied in a real-world situation.

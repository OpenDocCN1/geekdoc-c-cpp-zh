# 10.1.3 A Classification of Response-Adaptive Randomization Designs

# 10.1.3.1 RAR Urn Models

The FDA draft guidance on adaptive designs classifies RAR methods as "adaptive study designs whose properties are less well understood" [20]. Indeed, there is a limited number of trials which successfully implemented RAR and, to our knowledge, none of the RAR trials have been run in a confirmatory (phase III) setting. However, as the knowledge on statistical properties, merits, and limitations of RAR is increasing, it is likely that more trials using RAR will be performed in the future [2,23]. In this subsection we briefly discuss three major types of RAR designs: 1) RAR urn models, 2) Bayesian adaptive randomization, and 3) optimal RAR procedures. In terms of maturity levels, the first two can be viewed as "established methods" with some examples of real-life applications, whereas the third type includes procedures whose potential is yet to be demonstrated in practice.

Urn models provide a useful probabilistic mechanism for implementing RAR in clinical trials [24]. The most famous RAR urn model is the randomized play-the-winner rule (RPWR), developed in 1978 by Wei and Durham [12]. The method assumes a two-arm comparative trial with binary outcomes, where a patient's treatment assignment is determined by randomly drawing a ball from the urn, and the urn composition is sequentially updated based on the outcomes from previous patients in the trial such that balls representing more successful treatment are chosen more frequently. The RPWR is a heuristic appeal of assigning fewer patients to the less successful therapy—the limiting allocation proportion of study patients assigned to a given treatment is inversely proportional to the failure rate of that treatment. However, such an allocation is not optimal in terms of any statistical criterion (in particular, statistical power with this approach may be lower than with 1:1 allocation). The RPWR was used to design a pediatric trial of extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), which compared the ECMO therapy versus the conventional therapy [13].

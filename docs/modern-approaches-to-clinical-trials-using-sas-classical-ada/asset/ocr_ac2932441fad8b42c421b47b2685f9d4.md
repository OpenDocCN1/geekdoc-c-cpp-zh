# 6.2.1 Dose-Response Relationship

In most cases, phase II features a learning period when the patient population may be first exposed to the test therapy for evidence of clinical benefit and risk; it is also a period to explore and recommend alternative doses to be tested in the later phase (e.g., phase III) confirmatory clinical trials. In the development of a first-in-class new chemical entity, the first phase Ia study is referred to as a proof-of-concept (PoC) study followed by phase Ib study called dose-ranging studies. A PoC study is often faster and cheaper because it only requires a well-tolerated dose of test therapy plus a control group (e.g., placebo), as appropriate. A dose-ranging study, needs multiple doses of the test therapy to characterize the dose-response relationship. Therefore, a classical phase II development program usually consists of a small-scale PoC trial followed by moderately sized dose-ranging studies.

Dose ranging studies are too often designed with a very small number of doses [5]. A dose ranging trial typically includes a placebo (representing zero dose), and several doses of the test product. One common design is a four-group study with placebo, low dose, medium dose, and high dose groups of the drug candidate under development. When designing a dose ranging study, it is important to keep the following questions in mind to achieve the study objective:

+   1. What dose-range should be considered in the study?
2. Was the concept proven?
3. How do you space the test dosages from the placebo to the maximum tolerated dose (MTD)?
4. How many treatment arms or dosage levels should be included?
5. What are the total sample size and allocation schedule?

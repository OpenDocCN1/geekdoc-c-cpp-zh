# 6.2.6 Number of Doses and Control Groups

As discussed in the previous sections, a wide dose range is critical in phase II dose ranging studies. Once the MTD is estimated from phase I and a wide dose range is considered, the natural questions to think about how many doses should be included in the study, whether a placebo control group is sufficient, or whether an active control group is also needed.

A single dose plus placebo arm design may be able to demonstrate the proof-of-concept, but cannot, by essence, characterize the dose-response relationship. Any attempt to interpolate a dose-response relationship between the placebo and the test dose would require very strong assumptions, which oftentimes are not realistic. Therefore, a dose-ranging study typically needs several test dose levels. One very commonly used design is a four-group study with a placebo, low dose, medium dose and high dose group of the candidate under development. Some authors suggest that more doses could help; in the first-dose-ranging study, adding more doses to the study design [19]. However, the number of doses to be used might be limited by practical and logistical considerations (e.g., available formulations of test doses, dosing frequencies, convenience for outpatients to use on their own or blinding complexity, etc.). Given many treatment groups already included in a study, it may not be practical to add many test doses into the same study.

In an early phase II dose-ranging trial design, typically the total sample size is limited depending on budget, availability of patients, and difficulty in recruitment. Under this circumstance, more treatment groups in a design imply provides a less precise estimate of treatment effect. Hence, blinding dose groups to a study design does not necessarily make the design more likely to deliver more informative results. A well-thought-out design strategy is needed before the dose groups can be determined.

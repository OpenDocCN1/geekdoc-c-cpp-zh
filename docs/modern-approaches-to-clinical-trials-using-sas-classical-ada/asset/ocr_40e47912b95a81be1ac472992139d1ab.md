# 6.5 Summary

There are a lot of challenges in designing the first dose-ranging clinical trial. The two main problems are: (1) there is not much information about the efficacy of the candidate product available and; (2) too many answers are expected for this trial to deliver. Project team members tend to anticipate that such a study will establish the PoC, identify the MinDED, characterize the dose-response relationship, and recommend doses for phase III designs. Fundamental statistical training points out that the best design is one that answers only one question. Hence, the nature of a dose-ranging trial makes it a very difficult design in order to address so many scientific questions, and so little information to begin with.

In fact, there should be a logical sequence of thinking in addressing these questions. The first step is to consider how wide a dose range should be. This is also the most critical step. In order to approach this question, information about the drug candidate under development (pre-clinical and clinical phase I), other marketed drugs in a similar class, the indication that this study is designed for, and the target product profile, can provide a better understanding of the potential dose range that needs to be explored. In practice, it is also important to have a good understanding of dosing frequency (from phase I results), formulation (from CMC), and other relevant issues.

Once the dose range is determined, the next step would be to consider the number of treatment groups – will there be need of an active control? How many doses should be included in this design? What dose spacing should be considered? Basically speaking, a trial with 4 to 5 test doses, plus a placebo control will deliver a good understanding of where the test medication is most active, if the dose range is wide enough, and if the dose spacing is reasonable. Six or more test doses (in addition to a placebo) may not necessarily deliver better results from a practical and logistical point of view (e.g., formulation, blinding, or others).

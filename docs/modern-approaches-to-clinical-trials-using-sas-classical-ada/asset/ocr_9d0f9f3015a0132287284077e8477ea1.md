If these estimators are maximum likelihood estimators, they are also asymptotically normal and efficient. The estimates produced by most SAS procedures are maximum likelihood estimators and they are efficient. Scharfstein, Tsiatis, and Robins have shown that, under the above conditions, if we are interested in testing the following general hypothesis,

$H_0: \theta = \theta_0,$

the joint distribution of the Wald statistics

$Z(t_j) = \frac{\hat{	heta}(t_j)-\theta_0}{\sqrt{Var[\hat{	heta}(t_j)]}}, \ j=1, \dots$$

computed sequentially at information fractions $t_1, t_2, \dots, t_j$, is asymptotically multivariate normal with

$E[Z(t_j)] = \eta\sqrt{t_j}, Var[Z(t_j)] = 1$

and for $t_{j1} < t_{j2},$

$Cov[Z(t_{j1}), Z(t_{j2})] = \sqrt{\frac{t_{j1}}{t_{j2}}}$

where $\eta = (\theta-\theta_0)\sqrt{t_j}$ is known as the drift parameter [19]. In this parameterization, the expectation of the Wald statistic is simply proportional to the square root of the information fraction.

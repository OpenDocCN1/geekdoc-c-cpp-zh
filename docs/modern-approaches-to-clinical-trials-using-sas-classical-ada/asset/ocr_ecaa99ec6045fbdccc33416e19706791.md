# 10.6.2 Delayed Response

One assumption for developing the theory of RAR is that each patient’s outcome is instantaneous. In practice, patient outcomes are measured at certain time points after the treatment has been administered. If the recruitment period is long enough and the outcomes are observed relatively quickly after randomization, RAR can still be applied successfully. This was justified theoretically in [57,58]. Simulation studies show that when approximately 60% or more of the patient outcome data are observed during the recruitment phase and used for purposes of adaptation, the large-sample properties of RAR designs are unaffected by delayed response [18,59]. However, if a delay in observing response is significant, convergence to the target allocation can be slowed down, which can diminish the advantages of RAR.

The BAR enrichment design can be naturally extended to multiple treatments and multiple markers. For a patient with a baseline characteristic following into marker group $j$, assuming the posterior estimate of effect size in treatment $i$ is $\delta_{ij}$, the randomization ratio to treatment $i$ can be expressed in the form of $\lambda_i / \sum \lambda_j$, where $\lambda$ is the tuning parameter. Note that when $\lambda=0$, it corresponds to equal randomization. When it becomes the "play-the-winner" design, in which the next patient is assigned to the current winner treatment based on the available data, and no randomization is involved. The larger the $\lambda$ is, the more imbalance the randomization will have.

The BAR enrichment design has been implemented successfully in several high-profile clinical trials in oncology. One example is the Biomarker-integrated approaches of targeted therapy of the lung cancer elimination (BATTLE) trial [56]. The BATTLE project consists of one umbrella trial and four parallel II studies with biomarker-based targeted therapies previously treated with chemotherapy but subsequently failed. The main objective of study was to estimate and test the disease control rate at 8 weeks for each treatment given the patients' biomarker profiles. The Bayesian probit model was used to characterize the disease control rate for each treatment by a marker subgroup with adaptive randomization. Another notable example is the I-SPY 2 (investigation of serial studies to predict your therapeutic response with imaging and molecular analysis) 2 phase II in the neoadjuvant setting for women with locally advanced breast cancer. It neoadjuvantly designed to test investigative agents in combination with standard chemotherapies in a curious population. By employing an innovative BAR design, the study aims to test new treatments in half the time, at a fraction of the cost and with significantly fewer participants [58]. Regimens that show a high Bayesian predictive probability of being more effective than the standard therapy will graduate from the trial with their corresponding biomarker signature(s). Regiments will be dropped if they show a low probability of improved efficacy with any biomarker signature. New drugs will enter as those that have undergone testing are graduated or dropped.

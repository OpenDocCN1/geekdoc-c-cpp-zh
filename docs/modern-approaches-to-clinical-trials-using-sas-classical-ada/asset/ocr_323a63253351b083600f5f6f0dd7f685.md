The cross-validation approach can considerably enhance the
performance of the adaptive signature design [29] because it permits
t maximization of information contributing to the development of the
diagnostic signature, particularly useful in the high-dimensional data
setting where the sample size or signal to noise ratio is limited. Cross-
validation also maximizes the size of the sensitive subset used to
test (validate) the signature. Molinar et al. [37] have shown that cross-
validation can provide a similar level of validity while being more efficient
than the split-sample approach. One drawback is the fact that the
selected gene set may not be stable when portions of the cases are
omitted. Freidlin et al. [6] argued that it does not mean that the
classification is unstable or that the classifier developed on the full data
set will not predict accurately for independent data. In high-dimensional
genetic profiling, good genomic signatures are generally not unique
predictive biomarkers. However, they are usually correlated. As a result,
successfully capturing the predictive genetic information is more
important.

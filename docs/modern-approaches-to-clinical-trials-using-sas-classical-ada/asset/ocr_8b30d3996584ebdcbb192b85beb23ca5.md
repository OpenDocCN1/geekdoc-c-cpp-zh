# 8.1 Introduction and Background

Phase II is an exciting and challenging phase where efficacy is initially tested for a new drug. This may include efficacy assessments for a single dose or multiple doses, possibly in separate studies (traditional phase Ia and IIb studies) or in a single study, such as a seamless phase Ia or IIb study.

When testing multiple doses, one of the key strategic decisions is how wide of a dosing range will be tested and what doses should be tested across that interval. Though many dose-ranging studies are powered for pairwise comparisons, the strength of dose-response modeling is in the ability to better understand the effect across a wide dose range or higher dose ratio helps adequately characterize the dose-response curve and enable better evaluation of doses not tested in a phase II study.

For example, assume that the first study is designed with 3 doses and a placebo where the doses are selected in the following manner: two near the maximal dose effect () and around the dose that produces 50% of the effect (ED₅₀); however, it does not incorporate any doses near the minimally effective dose. The second study includes a placebo and two doses with near the minimally effective dose and one close to the ED. These studies may be combined in the Bayesian framework to adequately characterize the dose response. The first study data could be incorporated as an informative prior when analyzing the second study. This will help to characterize the response of the compound adequately through borrowing information. This requires the assumption of exchangeability for this analysis, i.e. the data from the first and the second trial are exchangeable. While ranging studies are not often designed with multiple doses near a minimally or maximally effective dose, the results of phase II studies may appear to indicate that more than one dose achieved a minimal or maximum effect. The Bayesian dose-response design enables the seamless combination of study results.

Though this design seems very promising, the statistical methodology for such designs must take into consideration potential biases and account for multiplicity, multiple looks at the data (interim analysis), and the combination of data from independent stages. In previous years several adaptive seamless design methods have been proposed in the literature.

One of the the commonly used methods is the adaptive p-value combination method and its variations [38-42]. Using these methods, mid-trial design modifications including population selection are possible without compromising the family-wise type I error rate. The p-based combination methods for seamless designs have been restricted to a single endpoint. Another approach that has been proposed [43] uses the multivariate normal method which is a modification to the group sequential computation. Stallard and Toder extended their work to incorporate a change of endpoint. Royston and Toder later at [44] proposed an approach along the same lines but include related survival outcomes in the design. These approaches make some assumptions about the joint distributions of test statistics in the interim and the final stage, and between subgroup populations and, hence can get complicated when used for the population selection.

In the FDA guidance on adaptive designs, seamless phase II/III designs are considered as a type of design "not well-understood" due to both statistical and operational issues. In addition, seamless designs exploratory studies with "adequate and well controlled studies." As a result, the FDA requires that the sponsor conduct extensive simulations and communicate with the health authority as early as possible if such a design is proposed.

# 11.4.2.5 Flexible Adaptive Seamless (Enrichment) Design

In this design, the trial is carried out in two stages: In Stage I, all the subjects are recruited into the trial. On basis of interim data, it is decided to either continue the study in all subjects or only the biomarker-based subgroup population. Based on the results from the interim analysis, the Stage II test would conducted either on the full population, only the subgroup population or both the full and subgroup a co-primary population.

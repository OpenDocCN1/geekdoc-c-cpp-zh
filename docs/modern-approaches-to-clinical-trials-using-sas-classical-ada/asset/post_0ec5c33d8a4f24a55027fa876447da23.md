## 7.6 Conclusions

Current dose-finding practices often are not satisfactory: Many dose-finding studies are not properly designed (too few doses, dose-ranges too narrow, sample sizes not large enough, etc.) and the inferences drawn from them are not adequately accurate, leading to major consequences in drug development that are undesirable (e.g., high failure rate in phase III, need for label changes after approval, etc.).

Here, we have described a unified strategy for analyzing dose-finding studies, including the selection of one or more doses to take into further development. The proposed methodology combines the advantages of the multiple comparison and modeling approaches, consisting of a multistage procedure. Dose-response is tested in the first stage, using multiple comparison methods to identify statistically significant contrasts corresponding to a set of candidate models. If a dose-response signal is established in the first stage, the best model is used for dose-response and target-dose estimation. Different model-based dose selection methods, incorporating both statistical significance and clinical relevance, were presented and evaluated via the analysis of real and simulated data.

The MCP part of the proposed method is seen to maintain the FWER at its nominal level, has power comparable to the standard trend tests under monotone dose-response settings, and is better under nonmonotone scenarios, providing the set of candidate models is broad enough. The clear advantage of this new approach, in comparison to traditional multiple comparison dose-finding methods, is its added flexibility in searching for and identifying an adequate dose for future drug development through the use of modeling techniques. Several variants of the proposed methods are possible and have been investigated in the literature.

Compared to traditional ANOVA-based pairwise multiple comparisons, MCP-Mod has the advantage of enabling the use of more doses in the design, without requiring a much larger number of patients. In an ANOVA-type approach, only the information from the respective dose is used to estimate the dose-response, which means that the required sample size depends strongly on the doses studied, when a fixed MCP precision is required at each dose. By using modeling techniques MCP-Mod allows us to interpolate information across dose-levels, and the total sample size will not depend strongly on the number of doses studied. The possibility of using more doses will typically result in information-rich dose-finding designs and a better basis for decision making at end of phase II.

MCP-Mod can be applied to a broad class of parametric models beyond the standard setup of a normally distributed endpoint in a standard ANOVA model. For example, the response variable might be a count, binary, or time-to-event variable instead of being distributed. For the generalized MCP-Mod all that is needed is an estimate of the response at the doses, as well as the associated covariance matrix. In SAS, different methods can be used to produce these estimates. proc glmix is probably the most general procedure and might thus be recommended for most situations. Another advantage is that it produces the adequate multiplicity p-values. The Mod step of the procedure can then be performed using the proc code provided, which is quite flexible, and does not depend on the underlying distribution of the endpoint. Of course, other procedures, such as proc nlin or proc mixed could be used for the Mod step. In addition, the final analysis has usually to be adjusted for relevant covariates (e.g. region, age, etc.), patients' measurements are often recorded over time (necessitating the use of longitudinal models), and patients might receive more than one treatment (as in cross-over or incomplete block designs). In this chapter we have illustrated with two real data sets, how to use the MCP-Mod approach in practice. Furthermore, we have provided relevant SAS code to enable the interested reader to implement and perform the MCP-Mod approach in many situations of practical relevance. An alternative possibility to implement MCP is via the DoseFinding R package [11], which contains extensive functionality for both the design and the analysis of dose-finding studies. The commercial software package ADDPLAN-DF contains extensive tools for designing dose-finding studies using MCP-Mod [12]. In addition, Cytel has developed PROC MCPMOD as an add-on to SAS that focuses on the analysis of dose-finding studies using MCP-Mod.

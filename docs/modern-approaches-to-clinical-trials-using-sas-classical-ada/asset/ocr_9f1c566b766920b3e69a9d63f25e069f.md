# 10.3.5 Theoretical Comparison of Various RAR Designs

Hu and Rosenberger in [34] derived a relationship between optimality, variability and power that can be used to facilitate a comparison among various RAR designs. They considered a second-order Taylor series expansion of a non-centrality parameter of the chi-square test following RAR, and showed that three important factors have an impact on power of the test are the allocation target for a fixed design, the speed of convergence to the target, and the variability of the sample allocation proportion. For RAR procedures with the same allocation target, only variability of the sample proportions can be compared. A procedure with small variability is desirable. Hu and co-authors in [51] defined an asymptotically best RAR procedure as one that the smallest possible asymptotic variance of $N_E(m)/m$. A version of Theorem 1 of [51] by binary outcome trials can be formulated as follows:

**Theorem 1** Consider a two-arm binary outcome trial using a RAR procedure with target allocation $ho=ho(p_E, p_C)$. Suppose the following regular conditions hold:

+   1. $p_E, p_C\in(0,1)$

+   2. $N_E(m)/n\rightarrow\rho\in(0,1)$ almost surely

+   3. $\sqrt{N_E(m)/n-\rho}\rightarrow N(0,\sigma^2)$ in distribution, where $\sigma^2>0$.

Then we have $\sigma^2\ge B(p_E,p_C)=\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial p_E}\right)^2\frac{\rho p_E p_C}{\rho\|+\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial p_C}\right)^2\frac{\rho^2 p_C}{1-\rho}}$. $B(p_E,p_C)$ is called the lower bound for the variance of RAR procedures with allocation target $horho A RAR procedure that attains the lower bound is called an asymptotically best RAR procedure.

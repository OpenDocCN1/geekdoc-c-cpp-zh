# 4.1 Introduction

A typical drug development program may consist of multiple clinical studies with different study objectives, endpoints, and possibly different patient populations. There is rich literature for single trial Bayesian sample size determination [1-8]. Sutton et al. proposed a hybrid frequentist-Bayesian approach for sample size determination for a future randomized clinical trial (RCT) using the results of meta-analyses reported in the literature and suggested that the power can be highly dependent on the statistical model used for meta-analysis, and even very large studies may have little impact on a meta-analysis when there is considerable between study heterogeneity [9]. This raises a critical issue regarding how to appropriately account for between-study heterogeneity in a statistical model for meta-analysis. Recently, a Bayesian meta-analysis approach using survival models was developed for planning a phase II/III diabetes drug development program [10-12]. Their approach focuses on sample size determination and power at a drug program level by taking into account between-study heterogeneity within a meta-analysis framework. The authors considered the fitting and sampling priors of Wang & Gelfand for designing a Bayesian meta-analysis clinical trial with a focus on controlling the type error and power [1]. Their approach allows the incorporation of historical meta-survival data via the power priors of Ibrahim and Chen [13].

Built upon work [10-12], the goal of this chapter is the general and novel implementation of their proposed methodology into a user-friendly SAS macro, called BSMED, for Bayesian Survival Experimental Design using historical meta-survival data. In the development of the SAS macro, we extend the model and methodology of [10-12] for incorporating historical data. We note that in [10-12], it is assumed that the historical data are available only for the control arm from previous studies. As elaborated in Sections 4.2, 4.3, and 4.5 and illustrated in Section 4.7, the SAS macro BSMED allows for the incorporation of various types of historical meta-survival data, including those for the control arm only, those for the experimental arm only, those historical meta-survival data for both the control and experimental arms, or mixed meta-survival data of these three types from previous studies.

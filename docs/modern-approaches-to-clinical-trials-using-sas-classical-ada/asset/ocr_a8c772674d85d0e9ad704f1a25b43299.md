# 2.3.1 General Framework

In this section, we define the test statistic to be monitored and specify its distributional properties. This distributional theory is presented first in terms of a general framework which is then applied to studies with normal, binomial, and time-to-failure outcomes.

In the simplest situation, if we consider a two-arm randomized clinical trial comparing a test treatment with a control treatment, we can denote the treatment difference by a parameter $	heta$, which is also sometimes known as the effect metric. Depending on the stochastic model one chooses for the endpoint of interest, the choice of the parameter $	heta$ will also be different. For example, if a normal endpoint is considered, then $	heta$ represents a difference of means. For a binomial endpoint, $	heta$ will be a difference of proportions, a ratio of proportions, an odds ratio, or a log odds ratio. For a survival endpoint, $	heta$ may represent a difference of medias, a difference of survival rates for a given time point, a hazard ratio, or a log hazard ratio. In general, we can also consider $	heta$ as the coefficient of the treatment effect in a regression model, as such linear regression, logistic regression or Cox proportional hazard models. Further, we assume a design with interim monitoring up to the end of each stage. Therefore, we are collecting sequentially $l_1, l_2, ..., l_j$ units of statistical information about $	heta$. Sometimes, $l_j$ is also called the Fisher information at the $j$th stage. Since $l_j$ is the maximum information that we can obtain from the planned trial, we can also denote the information fraction by $t_j = l_j / l_j$. For normal or binomial endpoint, $l_j$ is proportional to $n_j$ and, similarly, $t_j = n_j / n_j$. However, for survival endpoint, $l_j$ is approximately proportional to $d_j$, the total number of events observed by $H_0: 	heta = 0$ monitoring stage. It is apparent that $H_0: 	heta = 0$ . This information fraction is therefore necessarily bounded by $t_j \in [0, 1]$ during the course of the trial.

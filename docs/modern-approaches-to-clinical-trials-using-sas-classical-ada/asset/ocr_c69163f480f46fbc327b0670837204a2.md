treatment assignment of the $(n+1)$ patient is chosen to minimize the loss $\mathcal{L}_{n+1}$ with a high probability. Atkinson [8] claimed that the allocation depends on

```
$$R_{n+1} = X_{n+1}^T(X^T X)^{-1}X^T Z.$
```

For example, the probability that the $(n+1)$ patient is assigned to the treatment group can be specified as

```
$$Pr(Z_{n+1}=1|R_{n+1}) = \frac{(1-n^{-1}R_{n+1})}{(1-n^{-1}R_{n+1})+(1+n^{-1}R_{n+1})\gamma}}$$
```

where $\gamma \ge 0$ is a prescribed tuning parameter to control the degree of randomness [9]. Obviously, if $\gamma = 0$, the procedure reduces to equal randomization; it becomes a deterministic allocation rule. Typically, $\gamma = 5$ is recommended to achieve a balance between the selection bias and the loss.

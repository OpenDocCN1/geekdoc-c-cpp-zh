A small value of $\sigma_{\gamma}^2$ is desirable from the standpoint of statistical power. Clearly, $\sigma_{\gamma}^2$ is decreasing in $\gamma$. When $\gamma=0$, $\sigma_{\gamma}^2$ is largest. When $\gamma\to\infty$, $\sigma_{\gamma}^2$ is smallest, but the procedure is almost deterministic. Rosenberger and Hu in [35] recommended using $\gamma=2$ as a tradeoff between power and randomness.

Let us describe the DBCD procedure with $\gamma=2$ targeting the RSIHR allocation (10.2). After $m$ patients, the target is estimated as $\hat{ho}_m=\sqrt{\hat{ho}_{Em}}/(\sqrt{\hat{ho}_{Em}}+\sqrt{\hat{ho}_{Cm}})$. Then the $(m+1)$st patient is randomized to $E$ with probability

```
$$\phi_{m+1}\\ E = \frac{\hat{ho}_m^2}{N_E(m)/m} + \frac{(1-\hat{ho}_m)^2}{N_C(m)/m}$
```

#### 7.1 Introduction
A well-known problem in pharmaceutical industry is the selection of appropriate doses for phase III. Historically, dose-finding studies were designed and analyzed based on multiple comparisons. However, the exact dose-response relationship is often unknown during phase II.

The MCP-Mod approach has recently been qualified as an efficient methodology for dealing with model uncertainty in design and analysis of phase II. At the design stage, a suitable set of models is identified, which also impacts decisions on sample sizes and allocations. At the analysis stage, dose-response is tested using trend tests on the specified models. Once a dose-response signal is established, the best model from the set is then used for target estimation.

However, the validity of its conclusions will highly depend on the appropriate choice of model. In the remainder of this chapter, we describe the core methodology of MCP-Mod in more detail, including an extension to general models, illustrate its application with examples, and provide the underlying SAS code.

#### 7.2 Methodology

##### 7.2.1 Original MCP-Mod Approach
In this section we first provide a short, technical description of the original MCP-Mod approach [2] for a single, normally distributed endpoint. Afterwards, we describe a recently developed variant of MCP-Mod that extends the methodology to dose-finding studies for general parametric models (e.g. for binary, count, or time-to-event endpoints and including certain longitudinal approaches), [4].

The MCP-Mod procedure is implemented in five steps; see also Figure 7.1 High-Level Overview of the Original MCP-Mod Procedure:

- At the design stage, identify several candidate dose-response shapes, which are likely to represent the true dose-response.
- Derive optimum contrast coefficients, such that the marginal power to detect a specific dose-response shape associated with the respective candidate model is maximized.
- At the analysis stage, evaluate the significance of the individual shapes in terms of a multiple contrast test based on the previously optimal coefficients, to assess whether a dose-response signal can be established. This constitutes the MCP part of MCP-Mod.
- Select a candidate model using model selection criteria such as AIC or BIC or use model averaging.
- Use the selected model(s) to produce inferences on adequate doses, employing a model-based approach. This constitutes the Mod part of MCP-Mod.

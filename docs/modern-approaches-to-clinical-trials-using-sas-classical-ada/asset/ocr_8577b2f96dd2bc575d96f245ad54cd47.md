# 10.6.4 Time Trends

In some clinical trials there may be systematic trends in unknown prognostic factors over time. For instance, the earliest enrolled patients may have a worse prognosis than the patients enrolled later due to changing aspects in the disease, standards of care, and other factors. One of the major criticisms of RAR is that it can lead to biased estimates and an inflated probability of type I error in the presence of time trends [35]. Several approaches the authors of [61,62] suggested applying RAR in blocks of patients but change adaptively between blocks. The subsequent data analysis through regression modeling should also adjust for the blocking factor. Simon and Simon in [63] developed randomization tests to analyze data following RAR trials. Their approach ensures control of the type I error rate in the presence of time trends. Hu and Roseberger in [64] proposed applying weighted likelihood techniques to reduce bias and mean squared error introduced by time trends. Finally, if covariates that cause heterogeneity are known in advance, one can consider using covariate-adjusted, response-adaptive randomization designs [14,65].

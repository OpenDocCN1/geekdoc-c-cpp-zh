The “Student Residual” column contains z-scores for residuals (called Studentized residuals) that provide a measure of the magnitude of the difference. Generally, a resid- >2 or <2 can be seen as statistically significant and may need further investigation. Because the Studentized residual for subject 10 is –2.404, this indicates that the predict- for this subject’s JOBSCORE was substantially smaller than expected. The columns labeled “–2–1 0 1 2” are a crude graph of the Studentized residuals. The “****” for subject 10 provides a visual indication that the prediction is substantially smaller than expected.

The Cook’s D gives an indication of the “influence” of a particular data point. A value close to 0 indicates no influence; the higher the value, the greater the influence. Note that subject 7 had a larger influence on the estimates of the regression coefficients than most other subjects.

Based on this analysis, you would examine why subject 7 had such a high Cook’s D score and why the prediction for subject 10 was so much less than expected. Sometimes, an examination of the data up miscoded values might indicate something different about some subjects that affect whether they should be included in the analysis.

The use of two difficult equality constraints, rather than three, tends to provide faster convergence. The difference in computation time could not be measured accurately, because each required only about 0.06 s of computation time as read from the clock on the Pentium/120. In each case convergence to $x^T=[0.322871\ 0.009224\ 0.046017\ 0.618172\ 0.003717\ 0.576715\ 2.977863]$ was obtained. These are the same results as reported by Carnahan et al. [5]

As is shown in Figure 1.6, similar convergence characteristics are obtained with the initial starting point of $x_i=0.1$ for $i=1,\ldots,n$. It is interesting to note that once the norm is below 1, the convergence rate is very fast. This suggests that another means could be used to get to the vicinity of the solution, and then a switch could be made to the Newton’s method. One such means is to use the direct search optimization suggested by Luus and Jaakola [22] to establish a good starting point to be used for Newton’s method. This optimization procedure is outlined in Chapter 2, where we examine steady-state optimization.

+   12. In the land of Puzzlevania, Aaron, Bob, and Charlie had an argument over one of them was the greatest puzzle-solver of all time. To end the argument once and for all, Bob agreed on a deal to the death. Aaron was bit better and hit his target with a probability of 1/3. Charlie was an expert marksman and never missed. A hit means a kill and the person drops out of the duel.

To compensate for the inequities in their marksmanship skills, the three decided that they would fire in turns, starting with Aaron, followed by Bob, and then by Charlie. The cycle would repeat until there was one standing. That man would be remembered for all as the Greatest Puzzle-Solver Of All Time.

An obvious and reasonable strategy is for each man to shoot at the most accurate shooter still, on the grounds that this is the deadliest and has the best chance of hitting.

Write a program to simulate the duel using this strategy. Your program should use random numbers and the probabilities given in the problem to determine whether a shooter hits his target. You will want to create multiple subroutines and functions to complete the problem. Once you can simulate a duel, add a loop to your program that simulates 10000 duels. Count the number of times each contestant wins and print the probability of winning for each contestant (e.g., for Aaron might output "Aaron 3595/10000 duels or 35.95%").

An alternate strategy is for Aaron to intentionally miss on his first shot. Modify the program to accommodate this new strategy and output the probability of winning for each contestant. What strategy is better for Aaron, to intentionally miss on the first shot or to try and hit the best shooter?

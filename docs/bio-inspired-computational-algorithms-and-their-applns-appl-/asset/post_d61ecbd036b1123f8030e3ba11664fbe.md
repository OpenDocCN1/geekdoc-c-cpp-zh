### ABSTRACT

Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems, especially those designed for edge AI scenarios, are limited to a specific set of speakers and platforms. The ubiquity of ASR brings an urgent need to recognize speech from a wide range of speakers and platforms. However, obtaining data from all platforms remains a significant challenge, primarily due to privacy, network, and hardware constraints. In this paper, we introduce a novel Self-Augmented Multi-Platform speech benchmark (SAMPLE) which benchmarks by (1) introducing two evaluation tasks at the platform and speaker levels, (2) leveraging the existence of user-generated content (UGC) on social media where audience comments on speech recordings from various platforms, and using self-augmented data to improve speech recognition robustness. To achieve this, we adopt Whisper and use a two-stage prompting approach to generate realistic text data. Using this generated data, we explore and examine various self-supervised training techniques to augment the training dataset and reduce recognition error rate.

Index Terms: Speech recognition, edge AI, multi-platform ASR, self-augmentation, self-supervised learning

### 1. INTRODUCTION

In recent years, the popularity of ASR applications has increased significantly in a wide variety of platforms, ranging from mobile devices to automobiles [1, 2], requiring a robust speech recognition in noisy acoustic environments. However, the primary challenge lies in inherent variability across diverse speakers and platforms. Aside from variations among distinct speakers' dialects, accents, and physical vocal tract shape, extreme variability may occur due to platform differences. This variability profoundly impacts the performance of speech recognition systems in real-world edge AI applications [3, 4], making it imperative to enhance the generalizability of ASR systems across diverse scenarios.

When these ASR systems are deployed to platforms such as Amazon Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Siri, they encounter various types of background noise, specialized accents, and speech patterns. In addition, some platforms employ beamforming or front-end speech enhancement, resulting in variations in audio quality, bandwidth, and acoustic characteristics [5, 6]. One straightforward solution is to use labeled audio data from every target platform to train and adapt the model. Yet, data collection from multiple platforms, either through data sharing or direct recording, is often prohibited by legal restrictions around user data privacy or demanding substantial efforts and costs [7, 8]. Therefore, it is desirable to reduce reliance on real recorded data from each platform.

To address this challenge, different data augmentation approaches have been proposed for robust speech recognition, including adding noise [9, 10], changing speaking rates [11], or manipulating pitch [12]. Recent progress in deep generative models also opens new possibilities for self-supervised speech enhancement and augmentation. For instance, text-to-speech (TTS) systems can be used to generate data from diverse speakers. Nevertheless, these methods often fall short in recreating unique variations across different recording conditions, such as those encountered with different microphone configurations.

In this paper, we present Self-Augmented Multi-Platform speech benchmark (SAMPLE), a benchmark framework designed to evaluate and improve ASR robustness in multi-platform scenarios. SAMPLE aims to answer the following two questions: (1) What is the degree of ASR performance variation across platforms and speakers? and (2) Can we augment data to improve ASR model robustness without using additional real recorded data? To answer the first question, we construct a text correction task to evaluate ASR performance across different platforms and speakers, and observe a performance gap in the error rates. To answer the second question, we propose a self-augmented framework for improving ASR, which leverages Whisper [13] as a two-stage data generation engine, and unpacks it as a speech data augmenter for ASR. The augmented data is then used to establish a unified training strategy for existing deep learning models.

Our contributions in this work are summarized as follows:

- 1. We introduce a new evaluation framework for ASR systems in multi-platform scenarios.
- 2. We investigate the impact of self-augmented data on ASR robustness.
- 3. We provide an open-source (available at https://github.com/usc-sail/SAMPLE) implementation of the entire framework, including data generation and model training to promote reproducible research.

### 2. RELATED WORK

### 2.1. Data Augmentation for ASR

Audio data augmentation methods like SpecAugment [10], noise injection [9], and speed perturbation [11] are widely adopted in ASR system training. Recent works have also explored asking pre-trained language models to generate text data to fine-tune ASR models (e.g., [14] and [15]). They propose using TTS models or pre-trained language models in textual data augmentation to enhance ASR. In contrast, our work focuses on using self-augmented data to improve ASR in multi-platform edge AI settings.

### 2.2. ASR Benchmarking

Benchmarking ASR systems across diverse conditions is crucial for understanding their capabilities and limitations. Existing benchmarks [16, 17] focus on robustness in noisy environments or various speaking styles, but not on multi-platform scenarios. Our SAMPLE framework specifically targets multi-platform ASR and introduces self-augmentation as a solution for robust recognition across different devices and acoustic environments.

### 5.1 Using GA in the allocation of electric power protective devices

This subsection is based on the published work Automatic Allocation of Electric Power Distribution Protective Devices (Burian et al. 2010).

The measurement of how well the electric power distribution system can provide a secure and adequate supply of power to satisfy the customer’s requirements is called “reliability”. Regarding electric power distribution systems, the electric utilities companies are responsible for the most reliable service as possible, reflecting the most advanced state of technology with reasonable cost to the end product that is the electric power³. Most utilities record outage information such as the number of outages, elapsed time, and the number of customers interrupted. These data and statistics may be reported for each circuit or operating division, for comparison purposes, using the standard performance indices.

The performance indices provide historical datum which can be used to determine increasing or decreasing trends and to measure whether system improvement plans have yielded expected results.

The quality model we consider in this subsection uses the following indices, based on the sustained outage data: the SAIDI and SAIFI indexes, explained as follows:

- 1. SAIDI (System Average Interruption Duration Index): defined by the rate of average interruption duration per customer served per year. This index is commonly referred to as minutes of interruption per customer.  
  SAIDI = Sum of Customer Interruption Durations / Total Number of Customers Served (29)
- 2. SAIFI (System Average Interruption Frequency Index): defined by the rate of average number of times that a customer’s service is interrupted during a reporting period per customer served in a given period (usually one year). A customer interruption is defined as one sustained interruption to one customer:  
  SAIFI = Total Number of Customer Interruptions / Total Number of Customers Served (30)

It is easy to see that what is desired is a circuit with minimal SAIDI and SAIFI with the smaller cost in protective installed devices. The resulting circuit with these characteristics will be the optimized circuit.

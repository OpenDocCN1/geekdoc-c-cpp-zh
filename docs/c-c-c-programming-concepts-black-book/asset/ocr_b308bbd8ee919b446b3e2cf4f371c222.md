To describe this technique, we need to have two variables i and j, where i represents the number of steps required to sort the array and j represents the number of sub-steps required in each step to place the ith element at the appropriate position. We initialize the values of i as 1 and j as i - 1. In addition, at the beginning of each iteration, we store the ith value of the list in a variable, named Temp.

Following is the stepwise execution of the insertion sort technique:

+   1. In this step, the initial values of i and j are 1 and 0. The second element is compared with the first element at this step. As the second element 20 is smaller than the first element 30, they interchange their positions, as follows:

Iteration 1: i = 1

Temp = A[i]

| 30 | 20 | 90 | 60 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j] == true; Swapped the position of A[j] and A[i] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 30 | 90 | 60 | 100 | 10 | 20 is inserted at the first position |

+   2. In this step, the initial values of i and j are 2 and 1. Therefore, at the beginning of this step, the third element 90 is compared with the second element 30. As 90 is greater than 30, no swapping will take place and the element comparison process will stop at that instant, as follows:

Iteration 1: i = 2

Temp = A[i]

| 20 | 30 | 90 | 60 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j] == false; |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 30 | 90 | 60 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j-1] == false; |
| 20 | 30 | 90 | 60 | 100 | 10 | As 90 is greater than 30 and 20, its position is not changed |

+   3. In this step, the initial values of i and j are 3 and 2. Therefore, at first, the fourth element 60 is compared with the third element 90. As the outcome of the comparison is true, these two elements interchange their positions. At this point, the position of 60 is not decided, as there may be some elements at the front of the list whose values are greater than 60. Then, the comparison takes place between 60 and the second element 30. This comparison process returns false. After that, the comparison takes place between 60 and 20. As this also returns false, 60 is placed at the third position in the list. The steps in this iteration are shown as follows:

Iteration 1: i = 3

Temp = A[i]

| 30 | 20 | 90 | 60 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j] == true; Swapped the position of A[j] and A[i] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 30 | ? | 90 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j-1] == false; 90 is placed at the fourth position |
| 20 | 30 | ? | 90 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j-2] == false; |
| 20 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 100 | 10 | 60 is placed at the third position |

+   4. In this step, the values of i and j are set as 4 and 3. As this fourth element is greater than all its previous elements, its position remains the same, as follows:

Iteration 1: i = 4

Temp = A[i]

| 20 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j] == false; |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j-1] == false; |
| 20 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j-2] == false; |
| 20 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 100 | 10 | Temp < A[j-3] == false; |
| 20 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 100 | 10 | As 100 is greater than 90, 60, 30, and 20, its position is not changed |

# ## METABOLIC HETEROGENEITY

Proliferating cells require abundant and diverse nutrients to support bioenergetic demands, growth and while maintaining cell functions or migratory properties. The fuels for these cells come from glucose, glycolysis, and lipolysis. Increased glucose uptake that occurs in hypoxia despite the presence of oxygen is referred to the “Warburg effect” or aerobic glycolysis. Indeed, glucose uptake in high tumors can cause extracellular levels of glucose reduced and elevated. Beyond aerobic glycolysis, anaerobic metabolism requires a wide range of nutrients. The pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) is essential for generation of adenine dinucleotide (NADPH) and ribose sugars which are needed for nucleotide synthesis, ATP production, and elimination of oxidative stress. Lipid and cholesterol are also used by cells to support tumors, which can be either taken up in the cells from free fatty acids or produced by fatty acid synthesis and de novo cholesterol synthesis.

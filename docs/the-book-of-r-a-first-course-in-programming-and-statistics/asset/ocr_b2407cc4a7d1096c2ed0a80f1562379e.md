Here, $y_i$ and $\epsilon_i$ refer to the response observation and random error term for the $i$th individual. The quantity $eta$ is a $(p+1) 	imes 1$ column matrix of the regression coefficients, and the observed predictor for all individuals and explanatory variables are stored in an $n 	imes (p+1)$ matrix $X$, called the design matrix:

```
\beta = \begin{bmatrix} \beta_0 \ \beta_1 \ \vdots \ \beta_p \end{bmatrix} \text{ and } X = \begin{bmatrix} x_{1,1} & x_{p,1} \ x_{1,2} & x_{p,2} \ \vdots & \vdots \ x_{1,n} & x_{p,n} \end{bmatrix}
```

The minimization of (21.2) providing the estimated regression coefficients is then with the following calculation:

```
\hat{eta} = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{eta}_0 \ \hat{eta}_1 \ \vdots \ \hat{eta}_p \end{bmatrix} = (X^T \cdot X)^{-1} \cdot X^T \cdot Y \quad (21.3)
```

It's important to note the following:

+   - The symbol represents matrix multiplication, the superscript $^	op$ represents the transpose, and $^{-1}$ represents the inverse when applied to matrices (as Section 3.3).
- Extending the size of $eta$ and $X$ (note the leading column of 1s in $X$) to create structures of size $p+1$ (as opposed to just the number of predictors $p$) for the estimation of the overall intercept $eta_0$.
- As well (21.3), the design matrix plays a crucial role in the estimation of other quantities, such as the standard errors of the coefficients.

# 26.3.2 Isosurfaces

One of the problems with plotting the individual spheres of points at each 3D evaluation is revealed by Figure 26-8—it's difficult to see spheres "inside" the 3D prism. This same issue complicates the general case of visualizing a continuous trivariate function.

To remedy this, you could instead produce an isosurface, which can be thought of as a kind of trivariate analog of a contour plot.

With an isosurface, you select a certain level of the values $w = f(x,y,z)$ and join up all the entries of $w$ at that level inside the 3D space to form a shape or "blob". These blobs show where the trivariate function takes on the chosen value. If you then plot these blobs at various levels, you will see a 3D version of the contour plots shown in Section 25.4.1, showing which levels hold the highest densities of observations.

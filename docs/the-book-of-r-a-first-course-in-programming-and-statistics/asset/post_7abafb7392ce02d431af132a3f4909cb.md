To properly understand the for loop, first look at the order of the arguments supplied to the calls to outer. Having vex first and second implies that each column of 200 in the resulting matrices corresponds to a span from -1 to 1 in v, which refers to moving from one end of one of the lines of points to the other, also along the width of the strip. What does change the color from red to white to blue in the plotted lines of points in the early stages of the loop, as it progresses, working its way around the strip, the specific span of that palette is incrementally shifted until it goes from blue to white as you rotate around the last few plotted lines of points. ## Torus

Another shape often expressed in 3D space is a ring torus (plural tori). This is the classic topological "shape with one hole" and resembles, for lack of a better word, a doughnut. The mathematical properties of tori are quite useful in many fields.

Parameterization of a torus may be achieved with these equations:

```
$x = F(\theta_2, \alpha) \cos\theta_1, \quad x = F(\theta_2, \alpha) \sin\theta_1, \quad z = \alpha \sin\theta_2 \quad (26.5)$

```
$F(\theta_2, \alpha, \beta) = \beta + \alpha \cos\theta_2$
```

with $0 \le \theta_1 < 2\pi$ and the same for $\theta_2$ (assuming angles measured in radians). The fixed values $\alpha$ and $\beta$ control the radius of the "tube" (in other words, the relative thickness of the doughnut) and the overall size of the torus in terms of distance from the hole to the middle of the tube.

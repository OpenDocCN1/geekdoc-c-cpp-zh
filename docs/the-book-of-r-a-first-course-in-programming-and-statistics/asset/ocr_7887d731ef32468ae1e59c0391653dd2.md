To calculate cumulative results, you use ppois.

| ppois(q=2,lambda=3.22)
| [1] 0.375754
| 1-ppois(q=5,lambda=3.22)
| [1] 0.107075 |

These lines find that the probability you observe at most two imperfections, Pr(X ≤ 2), is about 0.38, and the probability observe strictly more than five blemishes, Pr(X ≥ 6), is roughly 0.11.

A visualization of the cumulative mass function is given on the right of Figure 16-3, with the following:


# The qpois Function

The q-function for the Poisson distribution, qpois, provides the inverse of ppois, in the same way as qbinom in Section 16.1.2 provides the inverse of pbinom.

# The rpois Function

To produce random variates, you use rpois; you supply the number of variates you want as n and supply the all-important parameter as lambda. You can imagine

```
R rpois(n=15,lambda=3.22)
[1] 0 2 9 1 3 1 9 3 4 3 2 2 6 3 5
```

as selecting 1-foot-square metal sheets from the production line at random and counting the number of blemishes on each. Note again that this is random generation; your specific results are likely to vary.

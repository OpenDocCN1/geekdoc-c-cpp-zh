+   * The F-distribution is used to model ratios of two chi-squared random variables and is useful in, for example, regression problems (see Chapter 20). Its functions are df, pf, qf, and rf, as it involves two chi-squared values, it's dependent on the specification of a pair of freedom values supplied as the arguments df1 and df2.

+   * The gamma distribution is a generalization of the exponential and chi-squared distributions. Its functions are dgamma, pgamma, gamma, and it's dependent on "shape" and "scale" parameters provided as the arguments shape and scale, respectively.

+   * The beta distribution is often used in Bayesian modeling, and it has implemented functions dbeta, peta, gbeta, and rbeta. It's defined by two "shape" parameters a and $eta$, supplied as shape and shape2.

In particular, you'll encounter the chi-squared and F-distributions over the next couple of chapters.

In all of the common probability distributions you've examined over the past couple of sections, I have emphasized the need to perform "one-minus" operations to find probabilities or quantities with respect to an upper- right-tailed area. This is because of the cumulative nature of p- and q-functions—by definition, it's the lower tail that is dealt with. However, most p- and q-functions in R include an optional argument, lower, which defaults to FALSE. Therefore, an alternative is to set lower,TRUE in any relevant function call, in which case R will expect or return upper-tail areas specifically.

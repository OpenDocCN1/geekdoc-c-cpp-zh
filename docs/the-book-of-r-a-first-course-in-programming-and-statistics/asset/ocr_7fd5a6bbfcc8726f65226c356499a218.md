Now, the second female's height falls well inside the prediction interval, but the first female's height is significantly higher than the upper limit. it is important to realize that, technically, nothing has gone wrong in terms of the model fitting and interpretation—it is still possible that an observation can fall outside a prediction interval, even a 99 percent interval, though it's perhaps improbable. There could be any number of reasons for this occurring. First, the model could be inadequate. For example, you might be excluding important predictors in the fitted model and therefore have less predictive power. Second, although the prediction is within the range of the observed data, it has occurred at one extreme end of the range, where it's less reliable than that the observation itself may be tainted in some way—perhaps the individual recorded her handspan incorrectly, in which case her invalid observation should be removed prior to model fitting. It's with this critical eye that a statistician will appreciate data and models; this is a skill I will emphasize further as this chapter unfolds.

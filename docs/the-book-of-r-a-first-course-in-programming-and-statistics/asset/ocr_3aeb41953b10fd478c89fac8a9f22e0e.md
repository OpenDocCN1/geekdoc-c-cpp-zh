In view of this, The Book of R can be used purely as a programming text to learn the language or as an introductory statistics methods book with accompanying instruction in R Though it is not intended to represent an exhaustive discovery of the language, the aim is to provide readers with a comfortable learning tool that eliminates the fear of forbidding when voiced to me who have considered R from scratch. The fact remains that there are usually many different ways to go about any task—something that holds true for so-called high-level computer languages. What this text presents my own way of learning and programming in R, which I approach as a computer scientist and more as an applied analyst.

In part, I aim to provide a precursor and supplement to the work in The Art of R Programming: A Tour of Statistical Software Design, the other R text published by No Start Press (2011), written by Professor Norman Matloff University of California. In his detailed and well-received book, Professor Matloff comes at R from a computer science angle, that is, treating it as a programming language in its own right. As such, The Art of R Programming provides some of the best descriptions of R's computational features I've yet to come across (for example, running external code such as C from R programs, handling and manipulating R's memory allocations, and formal debugging strategies). Worthy, however, is the fact that some previous experience and knowledge of programming in general goes a long way to appreciating some of these more advanced features. It is my hope that my text will not only provide this experience but do so in R itself at a comfortable pace, with statistical analyses as the supplementary motivation.

This text, which serves as a "traveler's guide" as we backpack our way through R country, was born out of a three-day introductory workshop I began at the University of Otago in New Zealand. The emphasis is on active use of the software, with each chapter containing a number of code examples and practice exercises to encourage interaction. For readers not part of a workshop, just fire up your computer, grab a drink and a comfy chair, and start with Chapter 1.

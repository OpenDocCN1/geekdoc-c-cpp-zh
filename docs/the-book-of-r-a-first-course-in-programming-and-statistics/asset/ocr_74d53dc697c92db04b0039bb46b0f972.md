The $\epsilon$ term represents random error. In other words, you assume that any raw value of the response is owed to a linear change in a given value of $X$, plus some random, $residual$ variation or normally distributed noise.

# Parameters

The value denoted $eta_0$ is called the *intercept*, and that of $eta_1$ is called the *slope*. Together, they are also referred to as the *regression coefficients* and are interpreted as follows:

+   - The Intercept, $eta_0$, is interpreted as the expected value of the response variable the predictor is zero.

+   - Generally, the slope, $eta_1$, is the focus of interest. This is interpreted as the change in the mean response for each one-unit increase in the predictor. When the slope is positive, the regression line increases from left to right (when the slope is negative, the line decreases from left to right (the mean response is lower when the predictor is higher)). This implies that the predictor has no effect on the value of the response. The more extreme the value of $eta_1$ (that is, away from zero), the steeper the increasing or decreasing line.

# 20.2.2 Estimating the Intercept and Slope Parameters

The goal is to use your data to estimate the regression parameters, yielding the estimates $\hat{eta}_0$ and $\hat{eta}_1$; this is referred to as *fitting* the linear model. In this case, the data contains $n$ pairs of observations for each individual. The fitted model of interest concerns the mean response value, denoted $\hat{y}$, for a specific value of the predictor, $x$, and is written as follows:

```
$\hat{y} = \hat{eta}_0 + \hat{eta}_1x$
```

Sometimes, alternative notation such as $E[Y]$ or $E[Y|X=x]$ is used on the left side of (20.2) to emphasize that the model means (that is, the *expected value*) of the response. For compactness, simply use $\hat{y}$, as shown above.

Let your $n$ observed data pairs be denoted $x_i$ and $y_i$ for the predictor and response variables; $i = 1, \dots, n$. Then, the parameter estimates for the simple linear regression function

```
$\hat{eta}_1 = \rho_{XY} \frac{s_Y}{s_X} \quad \hat{eta}_0 = \hat{y} - \hat{eta}_1\bar{x}$
```

where

+   - $ar{x}$ and $ar{y}$ are the sample means of the $x_is$ and $y_is$.
- $s_x$ and $s_y$ are the sample standard deviations of the $x_is$ and $y_is$.
- $ho_{XY}$ is the estimate of correlation between $X$ and $Y$ based on the data (see Section 13.2.5).

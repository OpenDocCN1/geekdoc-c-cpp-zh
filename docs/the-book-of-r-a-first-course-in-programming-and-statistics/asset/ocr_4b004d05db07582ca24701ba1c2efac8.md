The answer, simply put, is that the predictors in a model affect
each other. Regarding the estimated coefficients of present predictors
easily change value as you control for different variables. As the num-er of predictors increases, these relationships become more and
complex, so both the order and direction of the selection algorithm
have the potential to lead you on different paths through the selection-
cess and arrive at final destinations, which is exactly what has-
pened.

As a perfect example of this, consider the main effect of pt in the nuclear
data. In forward selection, pt was added because it offered the "most signif-
cant" improvement to the model cost*date+cap. In backward selection, pt
was removed early, since it offered the least reduction in goodness-of-it
from the model cost*date+t1+t2+cap+pr+ne+cum. What this means
for the latter model, the contribution that pt might make in terms of
predicting the outcome is already explained by the other predictor
terms. In the smaller model, effect had not yet been explained, and so
pt was an attractive addition.

All this serves to highlight the fickle nature of some selection algo-ith, in systemic of the way they were implemented. It's important
to acknowledge that a final fit will probably vary between approaches
and that you should view these selection methods as helpful guidelines
for finding the most parsimonious model and not providing a universal,
definitive solution.

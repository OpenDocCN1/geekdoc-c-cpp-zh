# 22.2.3 Backward Selection

After forward selection, understanding backward selection (or elimination) isn't much of a stretch. As you might have guessed, where forward selection starts from a reduced model and works its up to a final model by adding terms, backward selection starts with your fullest model and systematically drops terms. The R functions for this process are drop1 F-tests and update.
The choice of forward versus backward selection is usually made on a case-by-case basis. If fullest model isn't known or difficult to define and fit, then forward selection is typically preferred. On the other hand, if you have a natural and easily fitted model, then backward selection can be more convenient to implement. Sometimes, researchers perform both to see whether the final model they arrive at is different (a perfectly occurrence).

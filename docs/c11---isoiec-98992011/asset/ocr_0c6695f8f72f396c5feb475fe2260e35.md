The E conversion specifier produces a number with E instead of introducing the exponent. The exponent always contains at least two digits, and only as many digits as necessary to represent the exponent. If the value is zero, the exponent is zero.

A double argument representing an infinity or NaN is converted in the style of an f or F conversion specifier.

A double argument representing a floating-point number is converted in the style f or e (or in style F or E in the case of a G conversion specifier), depending on the converted value and the precision. P let the precision no zero, 6 if the precision is omitted, or 1 if the precision is zero. Then, a conversion with style E would have an exponent of X:

+   - if P > X ≥ −4, the conversion is with style f (or F)

+   - P − (X + 1).

+   - otherwise, the conversion is with style e (or E) and precision P − 1.

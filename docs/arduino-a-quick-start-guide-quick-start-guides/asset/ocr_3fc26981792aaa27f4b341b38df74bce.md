This process depends on a certain temperature, and the wrong temperature is one of the most common soldering problems. If the temperature is too low, your solder joints might become fragile, and you also might have to touch the parts for too long, so you can damage them. An extremely high temperature can damage your parts right away. Experts can debate for hours and days about “the right temperature,” but 600 to 650 F (315 to 350 C) is a good compromise.

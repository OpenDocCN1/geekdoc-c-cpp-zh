The time-based preventive maintenance (it is important again to stress the condition-based preventive maintenance is also called preventive maintenance) is represented by the sub-net depicted by places W, RSI, UI, UPR, and RT; timed transitions TBPR, I, and PR, immediate transition TA; the arcs and transition attributes. A token in place W represents the time between preventive maintenance has not elapsed. When it does, denoted by the transition TBPR firing, whose mean time between preventive maintenance (mthm) is assigned. Such a firing removes a token from place W and stores a token in place AIR, which denotes the system is waiting for a maintenance team to become available to start the system inspection. It is worth mentioning that TA is an immediate transition, so as soon as a token is available in place RT, it does enable TA and fires it. Its firing removes a token from place AIR and stores a token in place UI under inspection. The mean time required to perform the system inspection (*mit*) is assigned to transition *I*; its firing removes a token from place UI and stores one token in place UPR, under preventive maintenance. Transition *I*'s priority is one since the preventive maintenance starts, the computer stops operating; the working out stops; thus disabling F2. The mean preventive maintenance (*pmnt*) is assigned to transition *PR* (preventive maintenance). When PR fires, denoting the preventive maintenance was performed, a token is removed from place UPR and one token is stored in place W (waiting for another preventive maintenance cycle), and one token in place *RT*, which represents the release of the maintenance team.

As already mentioned, the time to failure with an increasing failure rate is dictated by the sub-net composed of places *A1* and *A2*, the timed transitions *F1* and *F2*, and immediate transitions *C1* and *C2*, their respective attributes, and for k > 1. Tokens in place *A1* and *A2* represent the aging phenomenon. The steady-state availability is calculated $A = \frac{MT}{MT+MT}$. The unavailability is obtained $UA = 1-A$. The downtime in the period $T$ is calculated $DT = UA \times T$.

**Example 22.10.1.** Assume the system described in Figure 22.33, whose attributes are depicted in Table 22.10 and the number of repair teams is 1. Consider the time to corrective maintenance is exponentially distributed with mean $mttr=20h$. Also assume the inspection time is exponentially distributed with mean $0.5h$. The time to perform preventive maintenance is also exponentially distributed with mean $mtbpm$. The system time to failure is distributed according to Erlang distribution with parameters $k=5$ and $\beta=2.5\times10^{-3}h^{-1}$, which leads to $htft=400h$. Hence, the time to failure is $mtbf=2000h$.

The system availability and yearly downtime is not applied as $A=0.990099$ (in $UA=0.009901$) and $DT=86.7327$, respectively. Figure 22.34 presents the system availability considering preventive maintenance $mtbpm\in(450h,510h)$. The optimal mean time between preventive maintenance is $mtbpm=498h$. Considering $mtbpm=498h$, the system availability and yearly downtime($T=8760h$) are $A=0.994782$ (in $UA=0.005218$) and $DT=45.7063$, respectively. Hence, the yearly time difference when comparing a system maintenance with without preventive maintenance is $41.0264h$.

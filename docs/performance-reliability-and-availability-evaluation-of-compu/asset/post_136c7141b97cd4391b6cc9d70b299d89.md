$\hat{R}(t_1) = \hat{R}(\Delta t_0) \times (1 - \frac{r_1}{n_1})$, $\hat{R}(t_1) = (1 - \frac{r_1}{n_1})$ since $\hat{R}(\Delta t_0) = 1$. Beside, as $n_1 = n$, then $\hat{R}(t_1) = (1 - \frac{r_1}{n})$. Likewise, $\hat{R}(t_2) = \hat{R}(\Delta t_2) \hat{R}(t_1)$. Hence, $\hat{R}(t_2) = (1 - \frac{r_1}{n_1}) \times (1 - \frac{r_2}{n_2})$, where $n_2 = n_1 - c_1 - r_1$. Generalizing, we obtain $\hat{R}(t_i) = \prod_{j=1}^{i} (1 - \frac{r_j}{n_j}) \quad (25.2.2)$ where $n_j = n_{j-1} - c_{j-1} - r_{j-1}$. As the data is not complete, the formulas for computing MTTF and standard deviation are not valid because the respective values would be too pessimistic. Interpolation may be applied to estimate $\hat{R}(t_j)$ and $\hat{F}(t_i)$ at $t_j$, for $t_{i-1} < t_j < t_i$, $i \in \{1,2,...,n\}$.

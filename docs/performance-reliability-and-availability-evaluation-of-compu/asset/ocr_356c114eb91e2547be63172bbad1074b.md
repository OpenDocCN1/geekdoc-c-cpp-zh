As the expected value of $W$ ($MTTF$) = $E(W) = 1/ar{\lambda}$, then the confidence interval for the mean is estimated by

$\frac{2nar{T}}{\chi_{2n,1-(\alpha/2)}^2} < MTTF < \frac{2nar{T}}{\chi_{2n,\alpha/2}^2}$ \quad (25.3.45)

Consider the experiment when $k$ out of $n$ devices fail, that is, a **right-censoring-type-2 data** the respective confidence intervals can be estimated by the above equations, but $nTar{T}$ should be changed by

$nar{T} = \sum_{i=1}^k T_i + (n-k)T_k$,

so

$\frac{\chi_{2n,\alpha/2}^2}{2(\sum_{i=1}^k T_i + (n-k)T_k)} < \lambda < \frac{\chi_{2n,1-(\alpha/2)}^2}{2(\sum_{i=1}^k T_i + (n-k)T_k)}$ \quad (25.3.46)

and

$\frac{\sum_{i=1}^k T_i + (n-k)T_k}{\chi_{2n,1-(\alpha/2)}^2} < MTTF < \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k T_i + (n-k)T_k}{\chi_{2n,\alpha/2}^2}$ \quad (25.3.47)

where $T_k$ is the time of failure of the $k$ device. $1-\alpha$ is the degree of confidence. These expressions may also be adopted to provide a reasonable estimate for **right-censoring-type-1 data**. In such a case, $nTar{T}$ is changed by

$nar{T} = \sum_{i=1}^k T_i + (n-k)T_k$,

where $T_j$ is the closest, smaller or equal, value to $T_k$, where $T_k$ is the duration of the experiment.

$\frac{\chi_{2n,\alpha/2}^2}{2(\sum_{i=1}^j T_i + (n-j)T_k)} < \lambda < \frac{\chi_{2n,1-(\alpha/2)}^2}{2(\sum_{i=1}^j T_i + (n-j)T_k)}$ \quad (25.3.48)

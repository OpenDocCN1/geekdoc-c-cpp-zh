# Composing Parallel and Series Structures

Series and parallel structures can be combined to form more complex system configurations. In practice, a system may be decomposed into subsystems, and each subsystem may also be decomposed into smaller subsystems or components. Decomposition is very convenient when defining the structure, reliability, and availability functions of systems.

As one example, consider a computational system composed of a computer (HW), an operating system (OS), and three application ($A_1$, $A_2$ and $A_3$) programs (Figure 18.14,a). The application program $A_2$ is composed of two modules named $A_{21}$ and $A_{22}$, respectively. The computer is composed of a mother board (MB), a video controller (VC), a network controller (NC), a disk controller $DC$, and a power-source subsystem (PSS), which is composed of two power sources ($S_1$ and $S_2$) configured in hot-standby (Figure 18.14,b).

The power-source subsystem is configured in hot-standby; thus it is operational as long as one of the power sources correctly works.

> $\Psi(S_{pss}) = S_1 \vee S_2$,

where $S_{pss} = (S_1, S_2)$. Figure 18.15.a shows the RBD describing the power-source subsystem.

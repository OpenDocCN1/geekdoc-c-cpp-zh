It is worth stressing again; our goal is to maximize the probability term by finding the optimal $	heta_1$ and $	heta_2$. Hence, we seek the "$argmax$ of this term concerning $	heta_1$ and $	heta_2$. Therefore

$$\hat{	heta}_1 = argmax_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta)$

and

$$\hat{	heta}_2 = argmax_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta)$

Hence, find $	heta_1$ and $	heta_2$ by finding the roots of the derivatives of $\prod_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta)$ with respect to each component of $\Theta$, in this case, $	heta_1$ and $	heta_2$.

$$\frac{\partial \prod_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta)}{\partial \theta_1} = 0$

and

$$\frac{\partial \prod_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta)}{\partial \theta_2} = 0$

However, obtaining a derivative to calculate the parameter value, $	heta_j$, that maximizes $\prod_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta)$ is often not an easy task. It usually is easier to obtained the derivative of

$$\ln(\prod_{i=1}^n P(t_i \mid \Theta))$

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \ln(P(t_i \mid \Theta))$

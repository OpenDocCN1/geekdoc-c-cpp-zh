These benchmarks gathered data at three levels: (1) basic processor operations, (2) individual peripheral devices, and (3) system system configurations [27, 237].

The method adopted by Auerbach was to implement a "routine" to estimate the specific computer system "speed" (unit of code by unit of time). In this early attempt, no stipulations were made that the benchmark should be executed in computer systems of specific features. This leads to biased implementations by vendors to check out their new products according the benchmark problem at the expense of essential features [237].

In 1969, Buchholz developed a synthetic program for measuring computer system performance. His approach was towards implementing a program that initiates the real application. The sample program introduced was a file maintenance program written in PL1. Such work was a research for synthetic workload [61].

In 1976, a study group of the US government and industry was formed and implemented set benchmarks that contained the required attributes for reproducible results for supporting product selections. However, although the benchmark library had been used successfully, there was still not enough information to support such a general platform.

Some of the benchmarks mentioned in this section are orders that have been adopted for performance evaluation a long time. These old benchmarks are the seminal work on benchmarking computers. Other benchmarks described in this section are important due to their wide acceptance or standards for particular communities or groups. The term benchmark suite denotes a set of benchmarks with a specific set of rules that govern the conditions and methods, such as the test environment, input data, outputs, and evaluation metrics. A benchmark family is a set of benchmarks [317]. Whenever the context is clear, we adopt the term benchmark for all of them, and when it is required, we apply these specific terms.

Given that different application domains have different execution characteristics, a wide variety of benchmarks has been developed to characterize these domains. Furthermore, different types of benchmarks must satisfy the needs of many types of users. Designers of new computer systems, for example, often need benchmarks during the early stages of the project, it in the initial stages of a project it is common to use simulators, emulators, and prototypes to estimate performance, benchmarks to exercise the specific parts of interest of the new project instead of being a general benchmark that exploits many characteristics of the system since such a system may not yet be fully implemented.

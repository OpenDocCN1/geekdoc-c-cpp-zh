OREDA is a project constituted of oil and gas corporations, that other objectives, aims to produce and maintain a reliability database to sustain reliability information interchange among members. This project started in 1981 as an initiative of the Norwegian Petroleum Directorate (Petroleum Safety Authority). Non-members may access the reliability data by purchasing OREDA handbook licenses [320, 321, 406].

Reliability prediction methods have a long and distinguished history of application inside and outside the telecommunications business. The Bellcore/Telcordia standards-SR-332 - support reliability models for components applications. SR-332 methodology is similar to the MIL-HDBK-217. SR-332 employs a series of models for several classes of electronic, electrical and electromechanical parts to prevent infant mortality and steady-state failure rates, considering environmental circumstances, quality levels, electrical stress factors, and other parameters affected. It is worth noting that Bellcore (a spin-off of AT&T Bell Labs) became Telcordia Technologies. Later on, Ericsson acquired Telcordia [425]. SR-332 Issue 4 provides hardware reliability prediction process deployed from prominent industries [411].

FARADIP reliability data is represented as ranges. Overall, the lower values in these ranges are employed when assessing reliability design. In other words, these estimates might reasonably represent the device failure rates after some experience and presumed reliability growth. Failure mode percentages are also considered as impact factors. This database includes microelectronics, discrete elements, passive instruments and analyzers, electromechanical, power, mechanical, pneumatics, hydraulics, communication, alarms, and fire devices. The data supplied are for a typical ground-fixed environment$^1$. Variation to other environments and quality arrangements is dealt with employing factors [401, 402].

> $1$Typical ground-fixed environments are those moderately controlled settings as devices in maine racks, boxes, and shelves, with satisfactory cooling and unheated buildings and communications facilities. The term cooling air airflow over devices at or below the devices' temperature to draw heat away and cool down or stabilize the temperature to that of the air [402].

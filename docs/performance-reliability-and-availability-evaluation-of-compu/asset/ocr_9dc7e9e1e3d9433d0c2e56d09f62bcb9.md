As depicted in Section 13.3.2, SPN is a formalism well suited to represent modulated workload traffic. Such a model may be adopted to generate authentic and synthetic workloads and is suited for evaluation via numerical analysis and simulation. For example, the Markov chain specified in Example 24.3.6 is well represented by the SPN shown in Figure 24.36.

**Figure 24.36** Bursty Workload SPN - MMP.

The net is composed of two places, $P = \{p_0, p_1\}$, four transitions, $T = \{t_0, t_b, t_i, t_m\}$, and their respective arcs shown in Figure 24.36. The initial marking is defined as $M_0^0 = (1, 0)$. Exponentially distributed time variables are assigned to these transitions with rates $\alpha, eta, \lambda,$ and $\mu$, respectively. The transfer rate of type 1 $(tr = 1)$ is assigned to transition 2 $(t_m)$. When using this model to generate a real synthetic workload, when $t_i$ fires, a set of bytes is generated to be transferred and when $t_m$ fires, a set of bytes is generated to be transferred according to the distribution $Exp(\varrho_1)$.

At the initial marking, $t_m$ and $t_b$ are the only transitions enabled. When $\mu \gg eta$, $t_m$ is much more likely to fire than $t_b$. Firing $t_b$, the SPN reaches the initial marking $M_1^0 = (0, 1)$. $M_1$ enables $t_i$ and $t_0$. When $\lambda \gg \alpha$, firing $t_i$ the SPN reaches marking $M_1^M_1$ again. When $t_u$ fires, the SPN reaches marking $M_0^0 = (1, 0)$.

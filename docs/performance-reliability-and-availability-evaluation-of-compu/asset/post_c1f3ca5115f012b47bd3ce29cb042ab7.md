Assume $n$ similar devices have been placed under a test. If after a testing period of $\Delta t, n_i$ devices survived, the reliability may be estimated by

$$\hat{R}(t) = \frac{n_i}{n}.$$

Therefore, the unreliability may also be estimated by

$$\hat{UR}(t) = \hat{F}_R(t) = 1 - \hat{F}_R(t) = 1 - \frac{n - n_i}{n}.$$

where $D = n - n_i$ is the number of failures (defects - D) in the test period. Therefore

$$D = \hat{UR}(t) \times n.$$

If $n = 10^6$ (one million), the defects per million is estimated by

$$DPM = \hat{UR}(t) \times 10^6.$$

Consider a hazard rate of an entire population of products over time $(\lambda(t))$, where products will fail in early life ("infant mortality"), others will wait until out ("end of life"), and others will fail during their useful period ("normal life"). Infant mortality failures are usually caused by design, material, and manufacturing problems, wear-out failures are related to fatigue or exhaustion [377]. Regular failures are considered to be random.

Infant mortality is commonly represented by decreasing hazard rate (see Figure 16.6.a), wear-out failures are typically represented by increasing hazard rate (Figure 16.6.c), and regular life failures are usually depicted by constant hazard rate (see Figure 16.6.b). The overlapping of these three hazard rate functions forms the so-called bathtub curve (Figure 16.6.d).

The mean time to fail (MTTF) is defined by

```
$MTTF = \int_{0}^{\infty} t f_R(t) dt. \quad (16.2.10)$
```

As

```
$f_R(t) = \frac{dR(t)}{dt} = \frac{dR(t)}{R(t)},$
```

thus, let $u = t$ and $dv = \frac{dR(t)}{dt} \times t dt$; then $du = dt$ and $dv = R(t)$. Then, integrate by parts$^1$

```
$MTTF = -\int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{dR(t)}{dt} \times t dt = - \left[t R(t)\right]_{0}^{\infty} - \int_{0}^{\infty} R(t) dt\right] = - \left[0 - \int_{0}^{\infty} R(t) dt\right].$
```

The simplest definition of availability is expressed as the ratio of the expected system uptime to the expected system up and downtimes:

$A = \frac{E(Uptime)}{E(Uptime) + E(Downtime)} \quad (16.2.17)$

Consider the started operating at time $t = t'$ and fails at $t = t''$; thus $\Delta t = t'' - t' = Uptime$ (see Figure 16.9). Therefore, the system availability may also be expressed by

$A = \frac{MTTF}{MTTF + MTR}$

where $MTR$ is the mean time to restore, which in turn is defined by $MTR = MNRT + MTTR$. Therefore

$A = \frac{MTTF}{MTTF + MNRT + MTTR}$

where $MTTR$ is the mean time to repair, and $MNRT$ is the mean non-repair time, that is the portion of the $MTR$ that is not related to the actual repair of the system [401].

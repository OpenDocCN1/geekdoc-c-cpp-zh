# Whetstone

Whetstone is a benchmark for evaluating computer performance focused on float-point instructions. Whetstone was based on work by Bryan Wichman from the National Physical Laboratory and implemented by H.J. Curnow of the HM Treasury Technical Support Unit. The first Whetstone benchmark was written in Algo 60 in 1972. The Fortran codes were published in 1973. The first results were published for IBM and ICL mainframes in 1973. The instruction rate was calculated in KiloWhetstone Instructions Per Second or KWIPS. Later, Millions or MWIPS¹ was used. In 1980, facilities were added to time and produce speed ratings in MIPS (GMIPS and TIPS²), and MFLOPS (GFLOPS and TFLOPS). Afterward, the benchmark was also converted to Fortran 77, and capable reliable non-official versions were implemented in Basic, Visual Basic, C, C++, Java, and Python programming languages [87, 243, 460, 426].

## Dhrystone

The Dhrystone benchmark was implemented by Reinhold P. Wecker and published in 1984 using a subset of the ADA program language (the Pascal subset of ADA) [459]. Later on, it was translated to PASCAL, Fortran, and C. Among all these implementations, the C implementation is the most popular [317]. Dhrystone consists of 12 procedures included in one measurement with 94 states; during one loop (one Dhrystone), 101 tests (103 in the C version) are executed dynamically on integer instruction performance focusing on integer instruction performance since, in that, many computers used integer instruction, and the Wetstone benchmark already focused on floating-point instruction. The basis for the Dhrystone benchmark was a literature survey on the distribution of source language features, taking into account non-numeric, system-type programming. As a result, Dhrystone has a lower nesting depth. For each function executed, the number of instructions performed is usually low.

Moreover, most of the execution time is spent hanging strings, characters, and comparing them. At that time, many computers use integer instruction. Nowadays, however, Dhrystone may not be very useful if it is so short that it fits in-chip caches.

While there are competing uses for the crop residues, much burning of residues continues. Crop residues are low quality organic resources in regards to nutrient supply as indicated by low N concentrations (Table 3.3). Incorporation compared with removing soybean crop residue was found in the Guinea Savanna of Nigeria to have a fertilizer N value of about 15 kg/ha for maize that received no fertilizer N (Figure 3.2). However, crop residues left in the field and not consumed by termites and ruminants contributes to soil organic matter which regulates numerous soil properties and processes. It has been most common to incorporate plant residues before planting the next crop but there is potential advantage in avoiding tillage and leaving the crop residues on the soil surface.

Conservation agriculture (CA) integrates reduced or no tillage, ground cover by plants and plant residues, and crop rotation. Pittelkow et al. (2015) did a globally comprehensive analysis of 610 studies with 5463 comparisons of CA with some other management system. In considering the tillage component alone, mean yields were 11.9% less with no pre-plant tillage compared with the conventional tillage practice but the reduction was less in drier compared with humid production situations.

Methods to obtain the $4\times1$ matrix $K$ through optimal control techniques are investigated later in the book.

# 2.7.5 Pendub and Acrobat

Figure 2.14 shows a photograph of the Pendub as it appeared in the robotics laboratory at the University of Illinois in the 1990s [29, 330] and a sketch indicating its component parts. It is similar to Sutton’s CartPole [341], which is another example that is currently popular on openai.com. The control objective is similar to CartPole: starting from any initial condition, swinging the Pendub up to a desired equilibrium, without excessive energy.

The value of this example is explained in the introduction of [330], where they compare to CartPole, and a variation of Furita [137].

The balancing problem for the Pendub may be solved by linearizing the equations of motion around an operating point and designing a linear feedback controller, very similar to the classical cart-pole problem ... One interesting distinction of the Pendub over the classical cart-pole system and Furita’s system is the continuum of balancing positions. This feature of the Pendub is pedagogically useful in several ways, to show how the Taylor series linearization is operating point dependent and for teaching controller switching and gain scheduling. Students can also understand physically how the linearized system becomes uncontrollable at $q_1=0, \pmpm$.

# Acknowledgments

Let’s begin three decades back. In the mid-1990s I (figuratively) won the lottery: a Fulbright fellowship that took me and my family, including my young daughters, Sydney and Sophie, to Bangalore, India. Those nine months at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc) with Vivek Borkar were the start of fruitful collaborations and a long-lasting friendship. Vivek’s presence is felt on nearly every page of the second half of this book.

I was also fortunate to have interactions with Ben Roy while he was completing his dissertation research at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). His work with John Tsitsikis is an absolute de-force-de. Many aspects of the book draw from early RL research. My current research is to have similar long-lasting impact.

Prashant Mehta once said to me, "I know how you do it! You surround yourself with amazing people! Amazing is right, and he is right at the top. This book is a product of collaborations with Vivek, Prashant, and many others, including Ana Buís, Chen Duffy, Peter Glyn, Ioannis Kontooyannis, Eric Moulton, and many old friends at the United Technologies Research Center (UTRC); PhD advisor Peter Cairns was my first and one of my all-time best colleagues, who enthusiastically supported my first investigations into Markov theory. This set the stage for collaborations with Richard Tweed during my postdoctoral stay at the Australian National University. All amazing people, everyone will agree.

Young stars who influenced my research Shuang Chen, who recently defended his dissertation in the math department and is lead author of [88] on ODE methods. Many thanks to graduate student Fan Lu for comments on drafts and numerical experiments.

Prabir Barooah helped to draw me to the University of Florida, from my former home at the University of Illinois. I have benefited from our interactions, and with my students including Naren Roman and Austin Hoffman.

Max Raginsky helped me navigate the literature outside of my usual orbit. In particular, his advice along Polyak’s research [136] helped me to understand the connections from the USSR in the early days of RL and SA. Max’s research is an inspiration; while references to his work are scattered throughout the book, much of this material is suited for a more advanced monograph.

Much of Chapters 2 and 3 is based on the state space control course created at the Decision and Control Laboratory at the University of Illinois. Many thanks to Bill Perkins, Terry Basar, and Max Raginsky for allowing me to borrow material from the course manuscript [29], and laboratory manager Daniel Block for leading the design of innovative control experiments.

In 2018, I was fortunate to spend several months at the National Energy Laboratory (NREL), where I conducted research at the Autonomous Energy Systems laboratory. One of these outcomes was research on stochastic approximation, leading to the articles [40, 41, 85–87, 93]. The book would not be the same without collaborations at NREL Andrew Bernstein, Marcello Colombino, Emiliano Dall’Anese, and my former graduate student Yue Chen.

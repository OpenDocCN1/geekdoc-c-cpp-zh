# Introduction

To define reinforcement learning (RL), it is first necessary to define automatic control. Examples in your everyday life may include the cruise control in your car; the thermostat in your air conditioning, refrigerator, and water heater; and the decision-making rules in a modern clothes dryer. There are sensors that gather data, a computer to take the data to understand the state of the "world" (is the car traveling at the right speed? Are the towels still damp?), and based on these measurements an algorithm by the computer spits out commands to adjust weather needs to be: throttle, fan speed, heating coil current, etc. More exciting examples include space rockets, artificial organs, and microscopic robots to perform surgery.

The dream of RL is automatic control that is truly automatic; without any knowledge of physics or biology an RL algorithm tunes itself to become a super controller: the smoothest ride into space, and the most expert misecursion

The dream is surely beyond reach in most applications, but recent success stories have inspired industry, scientists, and a new generation of students.

DeepMind's AlphaGo set the world on fire following the defeat of Go champion Fan Hui in 2015, and Lee Sedol following the year (a story told in the 2017 film [185]). So after the astonishing sequel AlphaZero, which learns to play and Go by "self play" without any help from experts [150, 310, 322].¹

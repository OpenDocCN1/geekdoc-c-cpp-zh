# 10.2 Actor-Critic Methods

Glynn's research in the 1980s [142, 143] introduced likelihood ratio methods for stochastic descent without bias, based in part on [313] (i.e., the sensitivity surveyed in Section 6.8). Over one decade later, this was extended and applied to obtain the first unbiased stochastic gradient descent approach for reinforcement learning [238, 239] (see also [31, 321]), followed soon after with new insights in [344]. This was the start of the late nineties actor-critic revolution.

Two time-scale approximation and variance variance was still evolving in the late 1990s. Konda's work with Borkar [189, 190] and then Tsitsiklis [188, 193] helped to shed light on this topic, and [188, 192] introduced a major advancement in actor-critic theory and application: the computable features property (10.47) was introduced in this work, based on the $L_2$ theory surveyed over [353–356].

The introduction of the advantage function as a method to accelerate actor-critic algorithms was proposed in [172], following [23]. Proposition 9.2 of [172] was later applied to obtain the trust region policy optimization (TRPO) algorithm of which, which spawned many other approaches.

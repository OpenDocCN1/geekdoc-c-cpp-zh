**Figure 2.15** Description of the Pendubot: $\ell_1$ is the length of the first link, and $\ell_{c1},\ell_{c2}$ are the distances to center of mass of the respective links. The variables $q_1,q_2$ are joint angles of the respective links, and the input is the torque applied to the lower joint.

The Pendubot consists of two rigid aluminum links: Link 1 is directly coupled to the shaft of a DC motor mounted to the end of a table. Link 1 also includes the bearing housing for the second joint. Two optical encoders provide position measurements: One is attached to the elbow joint, and the other to the motor. That no motor is directly connected to link 2 - this makes virtual control of the system, as shown in the photograph, extremely difficult!

The system dynamics can be derived using the so-called Euler-Lagrange equations found in robotics textbooks [332]:

```
$d_{11}\dot{q}_1 + d_{12}\dot{q}_2 + h_1 + \phi_1 = 	au$,$
$d_{21}\dot{q}_1 + d_{22}\dot{q}_2 + h_2 + \phi_2 = 0$
```

The variables can be defined from Figure 2.15. Consequently, this model may be written in state form, $\dot{x} = f(x,u)$, where $x = (q_1,q_2,\dot{q}_1,\dot{q}_2)^T$, and $f$ is defined from the preceding equations.

This model admits variety equilibrium: For example, when $x^e = (-\pi/2,0,0,0)$ is an equilibrium, shown on the right-hand side of Figure 2.14. Three other possibilities are shown in Figure 2.14b, with $	au^e \neq 0$ and $x^e \neq 0$.

A equilibrium is obtained in the upright position, with $	au^e = 0$ and $x^e = (+\pi/2,0,0,0)$. It is clear from the drawing on the left-hand side of Figure 2.14 that the upright equilibrium is strongly unstable in the sense that when $	au = 0$, it is unlikely that the physical system will rest at all. Nevertheless, the velocity vector vanishes, $f(x^e,0) = 0$, so by definition the upright position is an equilibrium when $	au = 0$.

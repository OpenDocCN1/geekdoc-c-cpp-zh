With $x = (z, v)^	op$, we arrive at the two-dimensional state model,

```
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}x_1 &= x_2, \ \frac{d}{dt}x_2 &= -\frac{\kappa}{m} - g\sin(\theta(x_1)), \end{aligned}$ (2.56)$
```

where $	heta(x_1)$ is the road grade at $z = x_1$,

An examination of the potential energy $U$ tells us from which states we can reach the goal without control (setting $u=0$ in (2.56)). The potential energy is proportional to elevation and can be computed by integrating the negative of force, $-F(z)$. For the control-free model, we have $-F(z) = mg\sin(\theta(z))$, and hence

```
$U(z) = U(0) + mg\int_{0}^{z} \sin(\theta(z))\ dz.$ (2.57)$
```

The version of this model adopted in [338, 10] has these numerical values:

$\kappa=1, z=2.5, \theta(z) = \pi+3z$.

In this case $U(z) = U(0) + mg\sin(3z)/3$. Figure 2.11 shows the potential energy as a function of $z$ on the interval $[z^{min}, z^{max}]$. It has a maximum at $z^{goal}$, which implies that it is necessary to apply external force to reach the goal of any initial condition $z(0) < z^{goal}$ and $v(0) \le 0$.

**Is the goal reachable?** We examine again potential energy. Consider the force as a function of $z$ with $u(k)=1$ for all $k$. We obtain $-F(z)=mg\sin(\theta(z))-\kappa$, and the resulting potential energy is the integral, denoted $\dot{U}(z)=U(z)-\kappa$ and shown in Figure 2.11. We now have $U(z^{min}) > U(z^{goal})$, so from $z(0)=z^{min}$ we will reach the goal with this open-loop control law.

Consider the initial position $z(0)=-0.6$, for which $\dot{U}(z^0)$ is identified with a dashed line, and let $z'$ denote the other value satisfying $z' > z^0$ and $\dot{U}(z')=U(z^0)$. If $u(k)=1$ for all $k$, then initial condition $z(0)=-0.6$ and $v(0)=0$, the car will initially move to the right, and stall at time $t_1$, where $z(t_1)=z^1$. It will then reverse direction until it hits at location $z^0$, and this process will repeat.

# Where Is the Control Problem?

Let's consider the specific problem of stock portfolio management. The goal is to create a computer program that makes decisions second-by-second on what stocks to buy or sell. The goal is to “maximize profit,” but there is also the notion of risk, which is not easily defined without tools from probability and statistics.

Perhaps more significant is that this control problem is not of the centralized variety. Consider how Figure 2.1 is interpreted for stock trading. The process is the global economy and everything that goes along with it! The two blocks state feedback and observe the results of individual decision makers (the “agents”) who forecast future prices (and other events), and employ optimization strategies for online decision making. Trajectory generation will be local to each agent; this might represent purchase orders for new computers, new staff, or a new office closer to Wall Street.

In summary: trading is a game rather than a classical control problem, but this should not stop us. As an individual (or designing software for others), we can treat “process” as the actions of all other players as a larger process. Reinforcement learning is an appealing approach to control design because the learning (or training) does not require a detailed model (though significant data are required for training).

This is a great example of the value of both measurements and actuation in control solution – while the your measurements, the more money you can expect to earn in an optimal control – there is no better example to illustrate this. The book Flash Boys contains a popular treatment of the role of actuation – in particular, the cost of delay in the feedback loop [223] (see also [30]). It is claimed that millions of dollars can be made by reducing response delay by millisecond!

# State Feedback?

How we interpret $u(k) = \phi(x(k))$ The input $u(k)$ is easy to understand, given the preceding description of the stock portfolio management problem.

What is the $x(k)$? I don’t know, and I would not trust anyone who claims to have an answer! It is traditional to view prices as a stochastic process that evolves according to the actions of millions of citizens and hundreds of corporations. There is modeling based on martingales and changes of measure, so that theory from mathematical finance may provide intuition into how to construct a state process. A “quick reaction” might be this: $x(k) = x^0(k)$, the value of all stock prices at time $k$. Without any knowledge of finance, my gut tells me this would be a huge mistake. Here are examples of what many would add after reflection:

+   (i) History of prices. It is important to visit recent performance in terms of trends and volatility.

+   (ii) Forecasts of prices. You may have inside knowledge. You may realize that tweets from influential people provide insight on the decisions of others, which will then influence stock prices.

+   (iii) What is the objective? Once you have a formulation of reward and risk, make sure that these essential quantities are functions of your state process.

# 10.10 Some Ancient History

Many in the RL community have lost track of the ancient control theory for Markov chains pioneered by Mannl [235] and Borkar [60–62]. This work is easy to miss the language and notation is so different from what is used today in the RL literature. The authors begin with a family of transition matrices $T(\theta : \theta \in \mathbb{R}^d)$ and in later, the theory is extended to general state spaces and continuous time. One goal is to find the parameter $\theta^*$ that minimizes the average cost.

A reader might dismiss this as being from RL, since the family of models is assumed known. A closer look reveals that the observations are log-likelihoods, $L_{\theta} = log(T_{\theta}/T_{\theta^0})$, where $\theta^0$ is fixed but auxiliary (it plays no essential role in the theory). The setting is thus far more general than this. The very special case (10.37).

```
$L_{\theta}(z,z') = \log \frac{\phi^{\theta}(u'|x')}{\phi^{\theta^0}(u'|x')} \quad z, z' = (x',u') \in Z$.
```

This “ancient history” deserves closer inspection, as well as the substantial concurrent work in the USSR [136].

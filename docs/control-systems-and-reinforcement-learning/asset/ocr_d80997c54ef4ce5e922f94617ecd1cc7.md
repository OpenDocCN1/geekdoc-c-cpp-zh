The system identification is motivated by Bayes's rule. Suppose that $Z(k) = X^1(k)$ were indeed an MDP model with $V(k) = (U_1(k), U_4(k))$. Its controlled transition matrix is defined by the ratio of probabilities:

```
$P_v(z, z') = P\{Z(k+1) = z' | Z(k) = z, V(k) = v\} = \frac{P\{Z(k+1) = z', V(k) = v\}}{P\{Z(k) = z, V(k) = v\}}$
```

The cost identification step has similar motivation. For large $n_c$, we have the approximation

```
$c^{(m)}(z, v) \approx E[c(X(k)) | Z(k) = z, V(k) = v], \quad (7.53)$
```

so that the steady-state of $c^{(m)}(Z(k), V(k))$ approximates the steady-state average cost.
Shown on the right in Figure 7.4 is an illustration of a policy obtained using this approach.
The initial policy was chosen to be a perturbation of the “serve the longest queue” policy:

```
$$\phi^0 (v^1 | z) = \begin{cases} 0.85 & \text{if } z_1 \geq z_2 \ 0.15 & \text{else} \end{cases}$
```

with $\phi^0 (v^2 | z) = 1 - \phi^0 (v^1 | z)$ (provided $z_2 \geq 1$).

The final policy shown on the right in the figure is more similar to a threshold policy of the form “Serve buffer 4 when $X_1 \leq \varpi_1$ and $X_4 \geq 1$”, where $\varpi_1 \approx 10$. The resulting performance is close to that of the centralized optimal solution [255].

See the cover of this book for a clearer view of the policy, which shows the plot on the right in Figure 7 before it was converted to grayscale. The color indicates the probability that $U_1(k)$ is equal to one (which is minus the probability that $U_4(k)$ is equal to one, provided $X_1(k) + X_4(k) \geq 1$). The dark blue indicates a value of approximately 0.1, and dark red indicates approximately 0.9.

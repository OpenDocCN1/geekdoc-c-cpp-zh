Here is an approach that leads to a successful control solution in this example: The goal is to obtain a sequence of approximating MDP models with state process $X^1$ and input $U^1=(U_1(k), U_4(k))$. We simultaneously obtain an identical sequence of MDP models with state process $X^2$ and input $U^2(k)=(U_3(k), U_4(k)).

The procedure requires initialization: Choose a randomized policy $\phi^0=(\Phi_m^0, \Phi_b^0)$ so that the resulting Markov chain $X$ is positive. Then follow these steps for $m=0$ to $M$, with $M>1$ fixed, or by some stopping criterion:

+   (i) System identification: Simulate the Markov chain using policy $\phi^m$ to obtain the steady-state distribution

$$\omega_m^n(z,v,z') = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{1}\{X^1(k)=z, U^1(k)=v, X^1(k+1)=z'\} + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{1}\{X^2(k)=z, U^2(k)=v, X^2(k+1)=z'\}$

for $z=(z_1,z_2), z'=(z_1',z_2') \in \mathbb{Z}_2^2$, and $v \in \{v^0,v^1,v^2\} \overset{	ext{def}}{=} \{(0,0), (0,1), (0,0,1)\}$. Based on this, define a controlled transition matrix:

$$P_v^m(z,z') = \frac{1}{\gamma_m(z,v)}\omega_m^n(z,v,z'), \overset{	ext{def}}{=} \sum_{z'} \omega_m^n(z,v,z'). \quad (7.52)$

+   (ii) Cost identification: Include in your simulation the following estimate:

$$C^m(z,v) = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c(X(k))\mathbb{1}\{X^1(k)=z, U^1(k)=v\} + \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}c(X(k))\mathbb{1}\{X^2(k)=z, U^2(k)=v\}$

for each $z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^2$, and $v \in \{0,1\}^2$. Based on this, define a cost function:

$c^{(m)}(z,v) = \frac{1}{\gamma_m(z,v)}C^m(z,v).$

+   (iii) Policy update: Solve the AOE with controlled transition matrix $P^{(m)}$ and cost function $c^{(m)}$ to obtain a policy $\phi^{m+1}$.nDefine $\Phi^{m+1}$ to be a randomized policy approximating $\phi^{m+1}$, and go to step (i), with $m$ incremented to $m+1$.

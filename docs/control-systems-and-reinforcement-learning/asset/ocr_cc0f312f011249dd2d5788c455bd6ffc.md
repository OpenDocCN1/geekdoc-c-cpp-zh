To understand the practice of the optimization criterion (10.1) requires an entirely different control-theoretic "wardrobe," which is the topic of Section 10.5 and beyond. It explains why the first half of this chapter sets the stage for actor-critic methods. The actors are defined by a family of randomized policies $\{\phi : \phi \in \mathbb R^d\}$. The goal is to create an efficient algorithm that estimates the "best" policy within this family, where the notion of "best" can be defined in terms of discounted cost, total cost, or average cost.

We discover in 10.4.1 that most optimality criteria can be converted to average cost through design of the framework for simulation or experimentation. For this reason, the theoretical development of actor-critic methods focuses entirely on the average-cost criterion.

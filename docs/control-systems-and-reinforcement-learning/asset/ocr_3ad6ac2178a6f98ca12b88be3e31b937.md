One of the great “builders” at the intersection of RL and control theory is
Frank Lewis, who has led the creation of several collected volumes on this topic. I was surprised
when he thought of me a decade ago, leading to the contribution [165], and very
enthusiastic when he invited me to contribute to a new volume one decade later.

Until recently, I have regarded RL as a hobby, as motivation for simplified models for
complex systems (such as networks [254]), and as a vehicle to teach control theory. This
changed with the arrival of Aditya Devraj to the University of Florida, where he pursued
grade studies with me until he graduated from Stanford in the spring of 2020.
His curiosity and intellect were an inspiration in many ways, and in particular drove me to
learn more about the objective of RL over the past decade. The place of figures and many
of the theory in the second part of this book are taken from his dissertation [107]. He also
provided suggestions that improved many parts of the book.

I owe the Simons Institute great debt. During the spring of 2018, I was a long-term
visitor during our real-time decision making, which was doubly fortunate to
meet Jane and Aditya Devraj. We learned a great deal from fellow visitors,
Peter Bartlett (along with other locals). Our discussions then helped to motivate
the 2020 program on RL, which provided a massive crash on every aspect of the
subject, with a strong emphasis on the sort of bridge building I am attempting to pursue
this book. In the fall of 2020, I watched tutorials on recent actor-critic techniques just
before finishing Chapter 10 on this topic. The book benefited from the rl1theory winter
inar series, organized by Gergeely Neu, Chiara Pike-Burke, and Csaba Szesvesvari², which
was also inspired by the 2020 RL program at Simons.

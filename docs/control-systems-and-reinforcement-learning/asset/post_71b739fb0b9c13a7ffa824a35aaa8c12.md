$$\theta_{n+1} = \theta_n + \gamma_n[1-\theta_n]\Delta_n$$
$$\theta_{n+1} = \theta_n + \gamma_n\sqrt{1-\theta_n}\Delta_{n+1}$$

with $\gamma \in (0,1]$ and i.i.d. $N(0,1)$. In this, the multiplicative noise has far less memory than the example of Section 8.6.2. When this statement is true is made in [88], (where the example of (8.59) was first introduced.

(a) Verify that $\theta$ is a Markov chain whose margins are Gaussian for each deterministic $\theta_0$, and ergodic in the case for any $r \in \mathbb{R}$: $$\lim_{n\to\infty} P\{\theta_n \geq r\} = P\{\Delta_\infty \geq r}.$$ Consequently, the ODE approximation for this SA recursion is again linear, with $f(0) = -0$.

# 8.9 Notes

This chapter is distilled from many years of collaborations and discussions with Vivek Borkar, Ken Duffy, Ioannis Kontoyannis, and Eric Moulines, as well as recent collaborations with students and colleagues. Some of the material in this chapter is adapted from the first half of the book coauthored by recent graduate Adithya Devraj and colleague Ana Bušić.

For a full history of stochastic approximation, it is best to go to the classic texts [39, 65, 66, 24]. In brief, a scalar version of the stochastic approximation algorithm was introduced by Robbins and Monro in [31]. Blum in [58] extended the theory to vector-valued recursions. Convergence theory for SA appeared to be mature by the end of the 1990s, marked by the ambitious work of Benaim on the *dynamics of stochastic approximation algorithms* [37], which led to my favorite book on the subject [65]. As made clear in the second edition [66], the theory evolves and becomes stronger each year.

What follows is a bit more history on topics most relevant to RL.

### 8.9.1 SA and RL

One force behind the recent evolution of RL has the need for new theory to support RL algorithms, and one person has formed the strongest bridge between SA and RL is Bhatnagar [180, 296, 297, 379]. The fact that RL algorithms can be cast as instances of SA was observed in [169, 352]. Over the decade that followed, SA theory was a primary tool of the MIT RL school [192, 193, 353, 354, 356], which had tremendous impact on my own research. With increasing interest in RL, with particular attention on identification of sharp bounds on the rate of convergence.

### 8.9.2 Stability

The stability theory contained in Section 8.7.1 is taken from [69], which is expanded upon Borkar's monograph [65, 66]. The recent work [88] provides conditions justifying Assumption (PR4) for nonlinear SA recursions. It is shown that $E\|Z_n\|^4 < \infty$ holds for two significant assignments: The $ODE/\infty$ is stable in the sense of Lyapunov, and the underlying Markov chain satisfies a condition slightly stronger than geometric ergodicity.

Stability conditions for two time-scale SA can be found in [209], justifying the assumptions of Theorem 8.3.

Stability for SA is a similar corollary to the general stability theory for two time-scale SA, provided the assumptions hold. Fortunately, the stability of the Newton-Raphson method is almost universal, even when the function $f$ is not everywhere smooth. The example of (8.59) was first introduced in [8, 204].

The narrative is flipped in this book: rather than treating an ODE as simply an analytical tool, every algorithm in this book begins with an ideal ODE that is regarded as "step 1" in algorithm design. I believe this provides better insight into algorithm synthesis and analysis.

In particular, the recent thesis and book chapter [107, 110] (which built on a similar narrative) assume significant background in the theory of stochastic processes. In this book, we lift the veil: there is inherently *stochastic* over *stochastic* approximation, provided you are working with sinusoids or other deterministic probing instead of stochastic processes. The ODE methods used in Chapters 4 and 5 make no reference to probability theory.

Many newcomers to reinforcement learning may be dismayed to see that the theory and algorithms in this book are far from the dream portrayed in the popular media: reinforcement learning is often described as an "agent" interacting in a physical environment, and maturing as its experience. Unfortunately, today's technologies, the process of "learning from scratch while is unlikely to succeed very special cases such as online advertising.

The tone of this book is entirely different: we pose an optimal control problem, and show how to obtain an approximate solution by design of exploration strategies and tuning rules. This is not an eccentricity of the breath author but a disciplined and accepted approach to derive all of the standards to reinforcement learning. In particular, the Q-learning algorithm of Watkins and its extensions are designed to solve or approximate the “dynamic programming” equations introduced in the 1950s.

The field is young, and its future may look something like the dream you had in mind before you read this. I hope that in the future we will discover new paradigms for RL, perhaps drawing inspiration from intelligent beings rather than optimality equations from the past century. I am confident that the fundamental principles in this book will remain valuable without the shackles of the optimal control paradigm!

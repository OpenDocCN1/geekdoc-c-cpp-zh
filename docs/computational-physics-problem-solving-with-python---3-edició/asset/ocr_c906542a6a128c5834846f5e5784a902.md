Usually, textbooks approximate these equations for small oscillations, which diminish the nonlinear effects, and results in “slow” and “fast” modes that look much like regular harmonic motions. What is more interesting is the motion that results without any small-angle restrictions, particularly when the pendula have enough initial energy to go over the top (Figure 15.10). In Figure 15.11a, we see several phase-space plots for the lower pendulum with m₁ = 10m₂. When given enough initial kinetic energy to go over the top, the trajectories are seen to flow between two major attractors as energy is transferred back and forth between the pendula.

In Figure 15.11b is a bifurcation diagram for the double pendulum. This was created by sampling and plotting the instantaneous angular velocity θ̇₂ of the lower pendulum at 70 times as the pendulum passed through its equilibrium position.


## 9.7.5 An Algorithm for Region-Based Analysis

The following algorithm solves a forward data-flow-analysis problem on a reducible flow graph, according to some framework that satisfies the assumptions of Section 9.7.4. Recall that $f_{R,\mathrm{IN}[R']}$ and $f_{R,\mathrm{OUT}[B]}$ refer to transfer functions that transform data-flow values at the entry to region $R$ into the correct value at the entry of subregion $R'$ and the exit of the exit block $B$, respectively.

### Algorithm 9.53: Region-based analysis

**INPUT:** A data-flow framework with the properties outlined in Section 9.7.4 and a reducible flow graph $G$.

**OUTPUT:** Data-flow values $\mathrm{IN}[B]$ for each block $B$ of $G$.

**METHOD:**

- 1. Use Algorithm 9.52 to construct the bottom-up sequence of regions of $G$, say $R_1, R_2, \ldots, R_n$, where $R_n$ is the topmost region.
- 2. Perform the bottom-up analysis to compute the transfer functions summarizing the effect of executing a region. For each region $R_1, R_2, \ldots, R_n$, in the bottom-up order, do the following:
    - (a) If $R$ is a leaf region corresponding to block $B$, let $f_{R,\mathrm{IN}[B]} = I$, and $f_{R,\mathrm{OUT}[B]} = f_B$, the transfer function associated with block $B$.
    - (b) If $R$ is a body region, perform the computation of Fig. 9.50(a).
    - (c) If $R$ is a loop region, perform the computation of Fig. 9.50(b).
- 3. Perform the top-down pass to find the data-flow values at the beginning of each region.
    - (a) $\mathrm{IN}[R_n] = \mathrm{IN}[\mathrm{ENTRY}]$.
    - (b) For each region $R$ in $\{R_1, \ldots, R_{n-1}\}$, in the top-down order, compute $\mathrm{IN}[R] = f_{R',\mathrm{IN}[R]}(\mathrm{IN}[R'])$, where $R'$ is the immediate enclosing region of $R$.

Let us first look at the details of how the bottom-up analysis works. In line (1) of Fig. 9.50(a) we visit the subregions of a body region, in some topological order. Line (2) computes the transfer function representing all the possible paths from the header of $R$ to the header of $S$; then in lines (3) and (4) we compute the transfer functions representing all the paths from the header of $R$ to the exits of $R$ — that is, to the exits of all blocks that have successors outside $S$. Notice that all the predecessor blocks $B'$ in $R$ must be in regions that precede $S$ in the topological order constructed at line (1). Thus, $f_{R,\mathrm{OUT}[B']}$ will have been computed already, in line (4) of a previous iteration through the outer loop.

- To take the meet of transfer functions, take the union of the gen's and the intersection of the kill's.
- To compose transfer functions, take the union of both the gen's and the kill's. However, as an exception, an expression that is generated by the first function, not generated by the second, but killed by the second is not in the gen of the result.
- To take the closure of a transfer function, retain its gen and replace the kill by ∅.

The first five regions \(R_1, \ldots, R_5\) are blocks \(B_1, \ldots, B_5\), respectively. For \(1 \leq i \leq 5\), \(f_{R_i,\mathrm{IN}[B_i]}\) is the identity function, and \(f_{R_i,\mathrm{OUT}[B_i]}\) is the transfer function for block \(B_i\):

\[f_{B_i,\mathrm{OUT}[B_i]}(x) = (x - kill_{B_i}) \cup gen_{B_i}.\]

The rest of the transfer functions constructed in Step 2 of Algorithm 9.53 are summarized in Fig. 9.51. Region \(R_6\), consisting of regions \(R_2, R_3,\) and \(R_4\), represents the loop body and thus does not include the back edge \(B_4 \to B_2\). The order of processing these regions will be the only topological order: \(R_2, R_3, R_4\). First, \(R_2\) has no predecessors within \(R_6\); remember that the edge \(B_4 \to B_2\) goes outside \(R_6\). Thus, \(f_{R_6,\mathrm{IN}[B_2]}\) is the identity function,¹¹ and \(f_{R_6,\mathrm{OUT}[B_2]}\) is the transfer function for block \(B_2\) itself.

The header of region \(B_3\) has one predecessor within \(R_6\), namely \(R_2\). The transfer function to its entry is simply the transfer function to the exit of \(B_2\), \(f_{R_6,\mathrm{OUT}[B_2]}\), which has already been computed. We compose this function with the transfer function of \(B_3\) within its own region to compute the transfer function to the exit of \(B_3\).

Last, for the transfer function to the entry of \(R_4\), we must compute \(f_{R_6,\mathrm{OUT}[B_2]} \wedge f_{R_6,\mathrm{OUT}[B_3]}\) because both \(B_2\) and \(B_3\) are predecessors of \(B_4\), the header of \(R_4\). This transfer function is composed with the transfer function \(f_{R_4,\mathrm{OUT}[B_4]}\) to get the desired function \(f_{R_6,\mathrm{OUT}[B_4]}\). Notice, for example, that \(d_3\) is not killed in this transfer function, because the path \(B_2 \to B_4\) does not redefine variable \(a\).

Now, consider loop region \(R_7\). It contains only one subregion \(R_6\) which represents its loop body. Since there is only one back edge, \(B_4 \to B_2\), to the header of \(R_6\), the transfer function representing the effect of the loop body 0 or more times is just \(f^*_{R_6,\mathrm{OUT}[B_4]}\): the gen set is \(\{d_4, d_5, d_6\}\) and the kill set is \(\varnothing\). There are two exits out of region \(R_7\), blocks \(B_3\) and \(B_5\). Thus, this transfer function is composed with each of the transfer functions of \(R_6\) to get the corresponding transfer functions of \(R_7\). Notice, for instance, how \(d_6\) is in the gen set for \(f_{R_7,\mathrm{OUT}[B_3]}\) because of paths like \(B_2 \to B_4 \to B_2 \to B_3\), or even \(B_2 \to B_3 \to B_4 \to B_2 \to B_3\).

Finally, consider \(R_8\), the entire flow graph. Its subregions are \(R_1\), \(R_7\), and \(R_5\), which we shall consider in that topological order. As before, the transfer function \(f_{R_8,\mathrm{IN}[B_1]}\) is simply the identity function, and the transfer function \(f_{R_8,\mathrm{OUT}[B_1]}\) is just \(f_{R_1,\mathrm{OUT}[B_1]}\), which in turn is \(f_{B_1}\). The header of \(R_7\), which is \(B_2\), has only one predecessor, \(B_1\), so the transfer function to its entry is simply the transfer function out of \(B_1\) in region \(R_8\). We compose \(f_{R_8,\mathrm{OUT}[B_1]}\) with the transfer functions to the exits of \(B_3\) and \(B_4\) within \(R_7\) to obtain their corresponding transfer functions within \(R_8\). Lastly, we consider \(R_5\). Its header, \(B_5\), has two predecessors within \(R_8\), namely \(B_3\) and \(B_4\). Therefore, we compute \(f_{R_8,\mathrm{OUT}[B_3]} \wedge f_{R_8,\mathrm{OUT}[B_4]}\) to get \(f_{R_8,\mathrm{IN}[B_5]}\). Since the transfer function of block \(B_5\) is the identity function, \(f_{R_8,\mathrm{OUT}[B_5]} = f_{R_8,\mathrm{IN}[B_5]}\).

Step 3 computes the actual reaching definitions from the transfer functions. In step 3(a), \(\mathrm{IN}[R_8] = \varnothing\) since there are no reaching definitions at the beginning of the program.

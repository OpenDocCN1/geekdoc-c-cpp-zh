All of the functions of big data are from data processing. Big data analysis can identify the behavior features and patterns, provide an insight into the trends, and to help users make decisions. The prediction or optimization abilities of big data are enabled by training data sets or comparing historical results. Whereas, the functions of the DT rely on the simulation and evolution of the virtual models. Via ultrahigh-fidelity virtual models, the DT can simulate the entire operation process independently, according to the actual operating rules of the physical space. Compared with big data, the DT can visually run and verify the manufacturing process in the virtual space. However, although the implementation methods have their own characteristics, both of them share a majority of the same functions. For example, both of them can predict and diagnose the problems, as well as monitor, optimize, and improve the manufacturing process in real-time.

In addition, the same functions lead to similar effects. They both improve production efficiency, customer satisfaction, and precision of management, as well as reduce costs in their own way, thus promoting smart manufacturing. Moreover, they also extend the lifetime of products and equipment and reduce the development cycle.

In conclusion, although big data and DT differ from each other in some details, they are consistent in the overall direction of their background, functions, and effects. Therefore, they have a foundation for cooperating with each other.

## 9.4.1.2 Differences Between Big Data and Digital Twin

With respect to concept, the contents of big data are relatively simple, emphasizing the large scale and value hidden in the data. To extract value from the massive amount of data, big data uses advanced tools and algorithms that are different from traditional ones [46]. Whereas, the DT is composed of three components, of which data is an integral part. The DT is more concerned with the virtual–real dual reflection. Although both of them involve data, big data is more professional in terms of tools, algorithms, and platforms, and more efficient in data processing than the DT in this regard, as explained in Section 9.4.2.1. Therefore, big data can serve the DT.

In term of applications, both of them are applied in every stage of the product lifecycle from design to PHM. However, compared with the DT, there are barriers between the various phases of the lifecycle in the application of big data in manufacturing. As designers, manufacturers, service people, etc., may not be from the same company, the data from a certain phase sometimes may only be used in its own phase. Taking the interests and data sharing security into account, it does not fully realize the continuous flow of data in the product lifecycle. In contrast to it, the DT can collect, record, accumulate, and comprehensively process all the data from product design until retirement. It not only can be conducive to design, manufacturing, usage, and PHM of the product, but can also contribute to the next generation of the product. Therefore, the DT can make up for the deficiency of big data to break barriers in the product lifecycle.

In addition, big data and DT share some key technologies, such as IoT. However, big data focuses more on the technologies concerning data (e.g., cloud computing, data cleaning, data mining, machine learning, etc.), while the DT cares more about the technologies concerning cyber-physical fusion, for example, simulation, virtual reality, augmented reality, cyber-physical system (CPS), etc. The combination of their key technologies will be more effective for their application in the product lifecycle.

In conclusion, despite their different emphases, both of them have their own strengths. Moreover, their advantages are complementary to make up for their respective deficiencies. Therefore, they have a bonding point to cooperate with each other.

## 9.4.2 Comparison From Data Perspective

Data is the common content for both big data and DT. Their respective advantages in terms of data are analyzed as follows.

### 9.4.2.1 Advantages of Big Data Over Digital Twin

First of all, the large scale is inherent in the concept of big data. The volume of big data ranges from PB to EB, even to ZB. This abundant data means rich information and knowledge. In contrast, the DT does not indicate the specific quantity of data, as long as the data is not insufficient as an important part of the DT. However, by comparison, the amount of data that are collected, stored, managed, analyzed, and computed in big data, are certainly more than in the DT. Therefore, big data is more suitable for extracting knowledge from a larger volume of data.

In terms of multisource correlation, big data focuses on data attributes and highlights the relationships between the features. Big data is used to find correlations and knowledge through mining behavior features and patterns. The DT focuses on the consistency of the multisource data, which helps to simulate and deduce the manufacturing process. The inconsistency of the data will lead to conflicts. Therefore, big data is more suitable for greater varieties of data.

Besides, given that the massive data cannot be processed using regular data tools within a reasonable amount of time, big data processing has its own advanced tools, algorithms, and platforms. Whereas, the data processing methods of the DT are not specified. Therefore, the velocity of data processing in big data, which is more professional, is more efficient than in the DT.

In conclusion, big data is more professional and efficient than the DT in terms of volume, value, variety, and velocity of data, which are consistent with the characteristics of big data. This further deepens the role of big data in serving the DT.

### 9.4.2.2 Advantages of Digital Twin Over Big Data

As the DT is composed of three components, the data sources of the DT are different from those of big data. The data in big data are from physical entities, information systems, and the Internet, which are all generated by activities in the physical space. The data in the DT are not only from physical space, but also from virtual models. In addition, some data are derived from fusion operations, such as synthesis, statistics, association, clustering, evolution, regression, and generalization. In accordance with the different data sources, the data acquisition tools are also different. In addition to the tools they share for data from the physical space (e.g., sensors, RFID, etc.), the DT also needs model data interfaces to collect data from the virtual space, which are not required by big data in manufacturing. Therefore, the DT has more comprehensive data for use.

Furthermore, the data fusion of big data is the fusion of various objects data in a single phase of the product lifecycle, due to the barriers between different phases. Whereas, the data fusion of the DT involves entire elements, processes, and businesses. The DT can achieve data sharing and integration between different phases of the product lifecycle. Therefore, the DT extends the range of application of manufacturing data, and thus avoids duplication and waste.

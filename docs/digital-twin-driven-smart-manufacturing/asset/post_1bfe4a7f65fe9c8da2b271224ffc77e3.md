
With respect to how results are presented, big data prefers to use two-dimension and static tools, such as tables, charts, graphs, and file printing. Because of its virtual models, visualization in the DT is more visual, mostly three-dimension and dynamic, using images, videos, and virtual and augmented reality. Furthermore, because the object of big data is just data, the verification of the analysis of results must be through the physical execution process, or through simulation by a third party, which is relatively slow. Whereas, with its own virtual simulation function, the DT can complete the preverification of the results in the virtual space. Therefore, the DT is more advanced and convenient in terms of visualization and result verification.

In general, from the data perspective, big data is more powerful than the DT in respect to data, while the DT is more capable than big data in application. Therefore, it is an ideal option to develop the DT in combination with big data.

## 9.5 COMPLEMENTARITY BETWEEN BIG DATA AND DIGITAL TWIN

There are many visible and invisible problems in smart manufacturing, which can be reflected by the data. Big data brings more efficient, sharper insight and more intelligence to manufacturing. However, as manufacturing processes become more complex, it is difficult to quickly identify problems arising in the myriad of processes. In the general manufacturing process, designers and manufacturing staff are affiliated with different departments or systems, and work independently. The designers submit product concepts and design schemes to the manufacturing department. The manufacturing department then reflects on how to implement them. This can easily lead to product information loss. Once the design changes, it is difficult to achieve synchronous updates in the manufacturing process. Besides, due to processing and assembly errors and other factors, real manufacturing implementation and production planning may not be completely consistent, hence limiting the manufacturing effectiveness. It is also possible that the manufacturing process cannot meet all the design requirements. Moreover, common product maintenance methods are often passive rather than proactive and are often based on heuristic experience. Many faults can only be diagnosed in the situation where the product is used. Therefore, the questions become: how to feed back the effective information collected from the manufacturing process to the product design phase? How to realize the correlation and dynamic adjustment between manufacturing planning and implementation? How to promote fault prediction, diagnosis, and maintenance digitally? These are big challenges. The combination of big data analysis and the DT paves the way to solve the abovementioned challenges. The DT can integrate the different phases of the product lifecycle to break data barriers. Meanwhile, big data technologies can quickly process data and provide an insight into manufacturing to help make decisions. Moreover, embracing the concept of manufacturing as a service (MaaS), service-oriented smart manufacturing has received extensive attention. As an effective means, services can enable large-scale smart applications and manufacturing collaboration. Therefore, as shown in Fig. 9.4, the DT, big data, and services form a unity to promote smart manufacturing [9].

## 9.6 FUSION OF DIGITAL TWIN AND BIG DATA IN MANUFACTURING

### 9.6.1 Product Design Driven by Fusion of Digital Twin and Big Data

In the design phase, product innovation relies on the accurate interpretation of market preferences and customer demands, and the capacity to translate customer voices to product features and quality requirements. Big data enables designers to promote product innovations, and develop more customized products. After the product concept is confirmed based on big data, the function structure and components of the product can be designed in virtue of the DT. In the traditional design pattern, after completing the design, some key components must be first manufactured to allow the manufacturers to assess the design quality and feasibility. However, by taking advantage of the DT and big data, vivid simulation scenarios can be created to predict the finished quality of the designed product, and identify the design defects in the virtual space. The components are manufactured and assembled using virtual models simulating the actual production process. In this case, whether the designed parts can be manufactured, whether the parts can be assembled, whether the parts interfere with each other, and whether the design scheme meets the relevant specifications and functions requirements, can be quickly verified. If the design scheme cannot pass the simulation testing, the product must be redesigned in real-time. When the product is redesigned, the big data technologies will be used to identify problems to improve the design scheme. In the process of product design, the required tools and algorithms are used in the form of services. As a result, the DT in combination with big data shortens the design cycle, thus reducing the cost of time and money [9].

### 9.6.2 Production Driven by Fusion of Digital Twin and Big Data

In the production stage, the DT with big data can enable optimization of the production planning and real-time adjustment of the production process. Before the production begins, it is necessary to improve the production planning through confirming whether every requisite resource is available. First of all, hypernetwork-based manufacturing resource services of supply–demand matching and scheduling [47] are carried out by the services part in Fig. 9.4, to quickly find available resources. On this basis, all the processes and the resources for performing production tasks are integrated together for analysis and planning. Combining with big data analysis, the virtual space of the DT simulates and evaluates the different production strategies and planning until a satisfactory production planning is obtained. After acquiring the best production plan, it is delivered to the physical space to guide the actual production. If all the processes in the production conform to the plan, production can be carried out smoothly. Otherwise, faults and errors may occur from time to time. In this situation, real-time data is collected directly from the manufacturing resources to drive the virtual models to simulate the production process. The actual production results and plans are evaluated in the virtual space of the DT. If there are differences, big data analysis is used to find out the reasons and develop solutions, such as adjusting the equipment conditions or revising the plan. In the iterative interaction, the DT with big data ensures that the production can be fully implemented in accordance with the optimal plan. Besides, once the design changes, the production process can be easily updated in the virtual space of the DT, including updating the bill of materials, processes, and assigning new resources. After adjusting the production plans, the entire production process is simulated again to further optimize and maximize utilization of all resources. As a result, using the DT in combination with big data technologies, production planning and implementation can be understood and monitored better to achieve smart production [9].

### 9.6.3 PHM Driven by Fusion of Digital Twin and Big Data

In the daily operation of a product, through interaction between the physical product and the virtual model, problems can be identified quickly, which improves the efficiency of PHM. First, the virtual model in the virtual space is synchronized with the physical product through real-time data collected by sensors. The working status of the product and healthy conditions of the components are grasped in real-time. In addition, as complementary to the sensors data, the DT also integrates the historical maintenance data and the data derived from big data mining (e.g., the features and patterns of behaviors, operation rules, features of energy consumption, etc.). Through big data analysis of the above data, the DT can continually predict the health conditions and remaining life of the product.

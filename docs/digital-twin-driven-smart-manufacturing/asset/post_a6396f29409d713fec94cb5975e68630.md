## 7.5 SUMMARY

In this chapter, the methods of DT modeling and DT-driven PHM are proposed for complex equipment. The aim of the chapter is to give an insight into PHM driven by DT and provide a guideline for future works. The contributions of the chapter are summarized as follows. First, it extends the previous three-dimension definition of the DT to a five-dimension definition for complex equipment, which expresses the properties and characteristics of the DT in a more comprehensive and realistic aspect. Second, the DT-driven PHM method, including the framework, the three-stage procedure, and the coevolution mechanism are presented in detail, which takes advantages of the digital mirror models, physical–virtual interaction, and data fusion in the DT. Third, based on the above works, a case study introduces the DT-driven PHM method in the yaw system and the gearbox of the WT to deal with different fault types. It is verified that the proposed method can predict the fault cause and the maintenance procedures without accessing the WT first, and improve the prediction accuracy of fault cause benefiting from the cyber–physical fusion.

Considering the implementation costs and complexity of the DT, the proposed DT-driven PHM method will be useful for monitoring high-value and major equipment in a plant, and there must be sufficient data for DT modeling. To scale up the PHM application, challenges mainly include [27]:

- Constructing high-fidelity digital mirrors for complex equipment with different properties and behaviors.
- Processing large amount of DT data.
- Balancing costs and benefits of the DT.

Future works will focus on the following aspects:
- Increasing the accuracy of the constructed DT.
- Refining the operation mechanism for the DT-driven PHM.
- Further application of the proposed method to other types of complex equipment.

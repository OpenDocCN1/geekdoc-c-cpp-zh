## 10.5 DIGITAL TWIN SERVICE MANAGEMENT

The circulation, transaction, and sharing of DT services depend on efficient management. As shown in Fig. 10.4 [30], after DT service generation, the services are published in the service pool and management platform, where they are managed to be shared by various users. Management for these services covers three stages, that is, the beginning of life (BOL), middle of life (MOL), and end of life (EOL), which are described as follows [18].

The BOL covers the process from the various physical manufacturing resources and capabilities to the virtualized services. It contains the perception and connection of resources, transmission of perception data, data filtering and processing, resource virtualization, service modeling, and digital description. After more services are generated, service aggregation will emerge gradually. As a result all the services are gathered and integrated by various abstract correlations and relations (i.e., the functions, a variety of attributes, characteristics, and invocation histories) along with the continuous operation of the service platform. The MOL is the stage driven by demands from the service generation to be put in use. The operations are service searching and matching, service evaluation, service selection and service composition, service scheduling, and service transaction. The MOL consists of service execution and monitoring, fault tolerance, and logistics service for task implementation. Among these items, the logistics services [34] serve for the specific processes and results of some kinds of services. According to the different requirements of task implementation, the logistics could be the transportation of the results after service execution, as well as the transportation of the materials in the process of service execution. Finally, the services are released and disaggregated after execution in EOL [18].

### 10.6.1 Digital Twin Service Application in Product Design

In product design, it is the process of back-and-forth interactions among expected space, interpreted space, and physical space [36]. DT-driven design aims to turn the expected product in the designer’s mind into the digital representation in the interpreted space based on the existing physical products. To innovate a product, designers have to study plenty of data to acquire valuable knowledge [37]. However, the data concerning the product is one of the most important assets, and is not easy to access. Besides, the designers do not have the professional abilities to process massive data. Service is an answer to these problems. The designers just simply submit their needs to the services management platform. The service managers will match the data services that the designers need with the model services and algorithm services used to process the data. Through invoking and combining these services, the needed results will be returned

to the designers. As a result, the designers can acquire what they want in a “pay-as-you-go” manner [38]. Moreover, after the function structures and components of the product are designed, the design quality and feasibility need to be tested. In virtue of the DT, the designers can quickly and easily forecast product behaviors through verification of the virtual product without waiting until the product prototype is produced [39]. But the virtual verification needs the models of the manufacturing site (e.g., production line or shop-floor), which the designers do not have. After services encapsulation, the model services can be invoked and easily applied in product design, which makes product design more effective to reduce the inconsistencies between expected behaviors and designed behaviors, and greatly shorten design cycles and reduce costs.

### 10.6.2 Digital Twin Service Application in Production

In general, production is the entire process from the input of raw materials to the output of finished products, which is performed on the shop-floor. In the phase of production planning and production execution, the DT provides an effective method to draw up the plan and optimize the execution process [40]. First, a production task is submitted to the services management platform, and resource services of supply–demand matching and scheduling are carried out to find available resources. Then, based on the real-time status of the physical resources (e.g., machine tools, robot arms, materials), a production plan is drawn up. Digital twin shop-floor (DTS) can simulate the plan in virtual space and identify the potential conflicts even before the actual production [41]. However, building the DTS is a complex and specialized work, especially the models describing physical properties, rules, behaviors, etc. With the help of services, these models do not have to be created by manufacturers themselves. For physical equipment and pervasive rules, their models, which have been established by other manufacturers, can be bought to use in the form of services. Current manufacturers only need to create special models, which are private for themselves. Besides, during the operation of the shop-floor, some services, such as data processing and shop-floor management, would need to be invoked from the service system of the DTS.

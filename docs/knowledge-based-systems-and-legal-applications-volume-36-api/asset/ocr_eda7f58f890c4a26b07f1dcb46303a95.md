# 12.10. CONCLUSION

In this chapter we have described some of the features that we need
to be taken into account when choosing how to do the knowledge
representation for a legal KABS, and we have described a formalism pro-
duced for that purpose. The example formalism is to some extent jus-
tification for some of the points made in the early stages of the chapter,
and some extent their source. In conclusion we would like to make a
distinction between two aspects: expressiveness and what could be termed
engineering principles, the first concerning what can be built, and
second how it should be built. The first determines what can be
represented in a formalism: the formalism of the Toolkit is for example
inadequate in its treatment of time and modal knowledge. Given this
inadequacy, applications can still be built, but their functionality will
limited: thus in the policy application all the distinctly modal reasoning\ust be performed by the user, although he is supported in this by the
on-modal reasoning of the system. The system will tell him that Q
follows from P, but he must himself conclude the undesirability of P
from the undesirability of Q. The legal domain does make certain
dands on the expressiveness of a representation, and the nature of the
application to be built will depend on how well these demands are met.
this explains why a technically unambiguous system such as the Retire-
ment Pension Forecast Advisor [Spiegel-Sinclair 1988] can be
built using the very restricted representation provided by ADS expert syst-
them, while the highly ambitious TAXMAN III of McCarthy
requires his extensively elaborated language for legal discourse. In
terms the facilities offered by the Toolkit fall between the two, and does
the ambition of the applications supported.

In terms of engineering considerations, which concern how the
application is built rather than what is built, the legal domain also makes
dands. We suggested above that these suggest that the representation
be truly declarative and support an isomorphic representation of
source material. Our belief is that the Toolkit scores well with regard to
these points. But should be noted that these considerations do not
determine the range or styles of applications that can be built, but rather
the utility they will have in practice.

# KNOWLEDGE-BASED SYSTEMS
LE APPLICATIONS

The application of artificial intelligence techniques and the development of knowledge-based systems have now reached a broad range of domains — from medicine to electronic circuit design, aerospace navigation to chess-playing. One of the most promising and practical areas has become that of legal applications.

This book describes the largest project yet undertaken using knowledge-based techniques in the legal domain: the Alvey-DHSS Demonstrator project. An authoritative general introduction to representing legal knowledge sets the scene for sections describing several prototypes and successive experimental stages through which this project developed. A final section discusses many of the general lessons that can be drawn from the collaborators’ experience of their ‘KBS and Legal Applications’ project. These lessons cover issues of knowledge analysis, knowledge representation, the nature of systems interaction, co-operative working, and the social and organizational implications of legal knowledge-based systems.

This book will be of interest to all concerned with artificial intelligence and law. It will also be essential reading for students and professionals involved in building large-scale knowledge-bases for practical use, particularly for legal and governmental applications.

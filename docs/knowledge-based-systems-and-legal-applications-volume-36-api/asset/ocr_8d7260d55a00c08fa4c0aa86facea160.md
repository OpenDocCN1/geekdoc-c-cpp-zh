Now if we consider the kind of knowledge that an expert adjudicator might allow to decide claims for this benefit, we might see allowing claims of men aged 101 and women aged over 96. This would certainly pick out the correct group of people and would be the most convenient expression of the knowledge if the claim form gave the age of claimant. It does, however, 'compile' in both a certain amount of arithmetical expertise and knowledge of the current date, as well as the interaction 27(1) and 39(1). If the claim form contained not the age of the claimant but the date of birth, this would not be the most convenient expression of the knowledge, since a calculation would be required to get the age from the date of birth, and the expert would be likely instead to operationalize the knowledge as 'men born before 5/7/1888 and women born before 5/7/1893'. This conflates 27(1) and 39(1). The point here is when experts operationalize knowledge from a variety of sources the way which is of most to use them.

As far as performance and correctness either of which make any reference to the legislation, would be perfectly adequate. But consider now the question of maintenance of such a system. The initial expression, with its explicit reference to an age would become out of date within the year, and so reference need to be amended every 5 July. Suppose that 27(1) was amended, perhaps to introduce a pension age to both men and women: then the operationalization in terms of date of birth would no longer be acceptable. The point is not that changes need to be made: that is inevitable. The problem is that using the expertise alone as its explicit reference we would have no way of telling which parts of our representation need to be changed in order to respond to given changes either in legislation or in external circumstance. Similar problems arise with respect to validation of the knowledge base; expertise, by bringing together different knowledge elements to make the justification for particular items harder to establish. These points are discussed more fully in [Routen and Bench-Capon 1990]. Lastly, it should be necessary to explain and justify decisions in terms of the legislation, then this cannot be done from the operational knowledge alone.

# 1.12.2. Commentary

There are two main points to be made about the TAXMAN I system: the nature of the analysis that the system performs; and the significance McCarty attaches to the choice of primitive concepts, and the use of adjectives 'deep' and 'structured' in this context.

It is very straightforward to reconstruct the TAXMAN I system in logical terms. Template instances correspond to sets of assertions about an individual object together with an object-oriented indexing scheme, exactly as in the now familiar reconstruction of frames as 'bundles of assertions'. See for example [Hayes 1979] and [Charniak and McDermott 1985]. The reconstruction of frames as 'bundles of assertions' is criticized because it overlooks one of the fundamental motivations for frames, which is to deal with concepts - stereotypes or 'natural kinds' — that do not admit definition in terms of necessary and sufficient conditions. The reconstruction of TAXMAN I templates as 'bundles of assertions' is accurate. The attempt to deal with stereotypes and precisely defined concepts is what characterizes McCarty's later work on TAXMAN II.

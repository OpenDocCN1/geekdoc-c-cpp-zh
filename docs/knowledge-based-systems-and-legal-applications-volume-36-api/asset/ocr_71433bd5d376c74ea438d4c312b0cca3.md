The following are examples of regulations made under this power. Regulations made to clarify the notion of a day of incapacity for work are found in the Social Security (Unemployment, Sickness and Invalidation Benefit) Regulations 1983. Regulation 3(1) provides an example of a specific circumstance in which someone is deemed to be incapable of work:

+   person who is suffering from some specific disease or bodily or mental disability but who, by reason only of the fact that he has done some work while so suffering, is found to be incapable of work by reason thereof, is deemed to be so incapable if that work is

+   (i) work which is undertaken under medical supervision as part of his treatment while he is a patient in or of a hospital or similar institution; or

+   (ii) work which is not undertaken and which he has good cause for doing,

and from which, in either case, his earnings do not exceed £26.00 in the week in which that work is performed.

An example of a regulation prescribing circumstances disqualifying a person from sickness benefit

17(1)A person shall be disqualified from receiving sickness or invalid benefit for a period not exceeding 6 weeks as may be determined in accordance with sections 97-104 if

+   (a) he has become incapable of work through his own mis own, except that this disqualification shall not apply where the incapacity is due to venereal disease or, in the case of a woman who is not a wife, or being a wife, is separated from her husband, to pregnancy.

Thus we can see that the regulations go into quite specific detail, even naming particular ailments in some cases, and help clarify what is found in the primary legislation. Regulation 3(1) is meant to make explicit that a person should not be prevented from doing, and being paid for, therapeutic work which may save his recovery, and regulation 17(1) is meant to ensure that people do not profit by their own misconduct, whilst allowing free rein to the moral counsel of the adjudicator.

The other way in which the legislation may be clarified is by decisions in particular cases. When a decision is made, the party against whom the decision goes appeal. In the social security system of the UK the first appeal is to an appeals tribunal, and there is further right of appeal to a social security commissioner.

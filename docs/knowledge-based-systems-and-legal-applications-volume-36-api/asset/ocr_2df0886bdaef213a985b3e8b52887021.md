# 7.4. THE LOCAL OFFICE APPLICATION

By the midpoint of the project, a review of progress against a case objectives was carried out, it was apparent that certain of the supplementary benefit decisions involved judgement and case law in the same way as the mobility allowance decision. There were both practical and conceptual justifications which led us to merge the two areas into a local office application (aimed at providing decision support to adjudication officers in DHSS local offices), working on the assessment of Supplementary Benefit and its planned successor, income support. Practically, resources were scarce and had been unevenly distributed. Conceptually, our analysis during the first eighteen months of the project raised some concerns that the separation of three demonstrations, when considered from the perspective of the user requirement, was over-simplistic and essentially misguided. It became clear that it was dangerous to try to separate the treatment of hard and easy cases - not least until a case is finished to be sure what kind of case it is. Furthermore, the encoding of a large number of rules within a traditional knowledge based system did not obviously satisfy any existing need amongst DHSS staff - there was greater concern identifying which of the known rules would be applicable for a particular case, how those rules should be interpreted, what additional information was necessary before a decision could be safely arrived at. Lastly, it was questionable whether the training could really be separated from the decision making proved to be essential to the development of staff during their decision making activities, rather than in a separate training environment. The emphasis on training was therefore changed; rather than continuing to examine specialized computer-based training systems, the potential for the decision-support systems themselves to have a training rule to be investigated, was so, too, became an aspect of the local office application.

An initial analysis of requirements for the local office application focussed on trying to establish what support was required, based on what people found difficult and what the technology was capable of doing.

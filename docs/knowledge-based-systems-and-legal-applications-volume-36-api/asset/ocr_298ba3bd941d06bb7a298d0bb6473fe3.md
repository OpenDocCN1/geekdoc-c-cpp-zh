In any definition the distinction between the term being defined and the terms used to define it is important. In a first predicate calculus formalization such as D3 this information is lost. While this does not affect the deductive inferences that can be made on the basis of a definition, it is important for the way in which definitions are used. Thus, for example, we can sensibly say that a person becomes a widow when their husband dies, but not that their husband dies when they become a widow. These considerations are likely to be of importance when explaining arguments on definitions, and generally when reasoning about them. Thus, given the central role of definitions in law, it is highly desirable for this reason also that the knowledge representation formalism supports definitions in a comfortable and explicit manifest.

# 12.6. USEFULNESS OF FRAMES

In the above we discussed that frame representations as currently did not fit well with legal representation because of their unsatisfactory treatment of definitions. The less frames do have wide appeal, and this appeal is explicable in terms of what Ringland calls in [Ringland 1988] ‘bundling’ aspect. When people conceptualise the world they find natural to think in terms of objects with attributes, and of different classes of objects with characteristic and different attributes. This notion lies very deep in our conceptual framework, as is indicated by Somers, e.g. [1963] and Kiel, e.g. [1979]. Given that this is a natural way to effect this kind of division, it seems unwise to reject such systems because to of frame-like representations because of the failings below. What is needed is a different approach to frames which will avoid the above objections, whilst retaining the notion of distinct types of object with characteristic attributes. Turning into definitions, including those to be found in legislation, we find a very common form to be that says ‘X is a Y which is P’, such as ‘a bachelor is a man who is unmarried’. This kind of definition does, of course, fit well with a frame hierarchy, with the defined term being a subclass of the term used in the definition with indicated restriction on some of its attribute values. ```

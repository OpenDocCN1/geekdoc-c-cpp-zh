The Latent Damage System addresses the difficult issues relating to
time within which a claimant may begin proceedings in the law of
egligence if the loss or damage is discovered some time after its
occurrence (hence 'latent' damage). The Latent Damage Act 1986 was
passed to deal specifically with such questions, but it cannot be under-
stood without an extensive knowledge of tort, contract and product liabil-
y law within which it operates.

In operation and computational techniques employed, Capper and
usskind's system resembles a 'text animator' or computer-aided instruc-
(CAI) program more than it does an expert system that computesy inference over some explicit representation of knowledge. The comp-
tational details are not as important, however, as the nature of the
knowledge that the system contains.

Capper and Susskind's system is an example of a legal expert syst-
of a different kind from those considered so far. It is an expert syst-
them it incorporates Capper's expert analysis of the implications
of the Act and its provisions should be applied to the solution of
practical problems. It differs from the other expert systems in this section
in that it does not seek to represent what actually happens in legal prac-
t(Waterman and Peterson) and it does not try to simulate an expert's
advice-giving behaviour (Michalsen).

Capper and Susskind's system might have been classified with other
aches, especially the algorithmic representations of law described in
tnext section. These two incorporate an analysis of how a piece of
legislation can be applied to a problem P presumably an expert's analysis
if the system is to have much practical value. But I have put Capper and
usskind's system in this section because of the exceptional range of
additional legal knowledge it contains beyond a representation of what is
actually said in the Act.

# 4.4.1. Option Generation

If it is decided the problem requires amendment of legislation, the next step is to decide what can be done to cure the problem. Usually there will be several different ways of amending the legislation to reach a state where the problem no longer occurs. The retirement condition in the example mentioned when discussing issue verification above, could be expressed in a variety of ways; any work at all might be made a disqualification, or an hourly limit on work done could be proposed, or the pension could be reduced in accordance with earnings in a variety of ways, pound for pound, with a cut-off at certain earnings threshold, or by a certain proportion of earnings. The policy maker should at this point generate as many candidate solutions as his ingenuity will allow. It is important that policy makers are seen to have considered all possible options and not simply adopt the most immediate and obvious course of action. Even when particular options are infeasible or inconsistent with existing policy, it is important to consider why they should be rejected rather than remit them without consideration.

Couched in knowledge base terms, generating an option can be as choosing rules to delete, amend or add which will mean a description of the target group will provide necessary and sufficient conditions for possession of the desired feature. Often this will be impossible, and different options will approximate to this ideal to different degrees and in different ways. Using a logical model, the support given for option generation will comprise explanation of the mechanics of the problem, together with the means of determining the extent to which an option achieves the desired result.

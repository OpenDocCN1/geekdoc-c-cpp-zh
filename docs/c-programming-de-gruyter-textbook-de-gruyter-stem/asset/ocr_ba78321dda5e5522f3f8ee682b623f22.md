Here we need a criterion. Correlation coefficient is the general criterion of the linear degree of the line that is fitted, which can be used to judge the degree of the linear correlation of a group of data. It is defined as,

```
r = \frac{L_{xy}}{\sqrt{L_{xx}L_{yy}}} (6.7)
```

Here, the definition of $L_{xy}$ and $L_{xx}$ can be seen in formula (6.4), whereas $L_{yy}$ is defined as,

```
L_{yy} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (y_i - \bar{y})^2 (6.8)
```

The closer the absolute value of $r$ is to 1, the better the linear relation of the data. When the data has an accurate linear correlation, $r=1$. If the correlation coefficient is close to 0, it indicates that the linear degree of the data is bad or that the data has no linear relation at all. Therefore, after fitting, we usually need to compute the correlation coefficient to measure the linearity degree of the line.

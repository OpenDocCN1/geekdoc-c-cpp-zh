## 9.7 Ratio Estimation through Poisson Regression and GEE

Ratios are estimators of the form $\hat{R} = \frac{\tilde{Y}}{\tilde{Z}}$, where $\tilde{Y}$ and $\tilde{Z}$ are the sample means of two random variables Y and Z. Since both numerator and denominator are random variables, the asymptotic variance of the ratio estimator is usually estimated by the Delta method (or Taylor expansion). Other variance estimation, such as bootstrapping or jackknife, are also used in epidemiological studies, special cases of ratio estimators when $Y_i$ are zero-one responses, and the $Z$, measure the amount of time subjects are exposed to risk. It is well known that the coefficients of a properly coded indicator variable in a logistic regression model correspond to logarithms of odds. In this section, we make a similar observation with respect to the logarithm of the ratio of the risks (relative risk) or the ratio in the context of Poisson regression with logarithm link function. Unlike the case of the logistic regression model, the inverse of the Fisher information matrix of the Poisson model does not necessarily provide proper standard errors for the estimated logarithms of risks or ratios. However, appropriate variance estimates can be obtained by using the sandwich or empirical variance estimator estimated from the GEE methodology. Morel and Neerchal (2008) have shown that the expressions for the sandwich estimator for the GEE methodology correspond to the usual variance estimates obtained by applying a Taylor expansion. We will provide some examples using the GENMOD and GLIMMIX procedures.

Suppose $Y_1,Y_2,...,Y_n$ are distributed as independent Poisson random variables such that $E(y_j) = \mu_j = \exp\{\boldsymbol{\beta}^T x_j + \ln(z_j)\}, j=1,..,n$, where $x_j$ and $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ are $k$-dimensional vectors of covariates and unknown parameters, respectively, and evenln(z_j)$ is an offset variable. We are assuming the logarithmic link function, the canonical link for the Poisson distribution. The GEE estimator of $\boldsymbol{\beta}$, can be obtained by solving the estimating equations

$$\sum_{j=1}^{n} D_j^{-1}(y_j - \mu_j) = \mathbf{0}$$

where $D_j$, represents the $k$ dimensional column vector of first partial derivatives of $\mu_j$ with respect to $\boldsymbol{\beta}$, and $\text{Var}(y_j) = \mathbf{v}_j$. Under canonical link, the estimating equations can be written as

$$\sum_{j=1}^{n} x_j x_j^T \sum_{j=1}^{n} (y_j - \mu_j) = \mathbf{0}. \quad (9.10)$$

If the Poisson model holds equations (9.10) provide the maximum likelihood estimator of $\hat{\beta}$, with variance estimate of $\hat{\beta}$ being

$$\widehat{Var}(\hat{\beta}) = \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n} \mathbf{x}_j \mathbf{x}_j^T\right)^{-1} = \hat{I}_0^{-1}, \quad (9.11)$$

where $\hat{v}_j = \mu_j = \exp\{\mathbf{x}_j^T \hat{\beta} + \ln(z_j)\}$. Furthermore, when the Poisson model is not satisfied, an estimator of asymptotic variance of $\hat{\beta}$ can be obtained using the GEE methodology, which is given by

$$\widehat{Var}(\hat{\beta}) = \hat{I}_0^{-1} \hat{I}_i^{-1}, \quad (9.12)$$

where $\hat{I}_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (y_j - \mu_j)(y_j - \mu_j) \mathbf{x}_j^T$. Consider now the following Poisson model for $k$ independent groups

$$E(y_{ij}) = \exp\{\beta_i + \ln(z_{ij})\}, i=1, 2,...,k, j=1, ...,n_i. \quad (9.13)$$

It can be shown that the maximum likelihood estimator of $\beta_i$ is given by

$$\hat{\beta}_i = \ln\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} y_{ij}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} z_{ij}}\right) = \ln(\hat{R}_i), i=1, 2,...,k. \quad (9.14)$$

For the $i$-th group, it can be shown that equation (9.10) becomes

$$\exp(\hat{\beta}_i) \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} z_{ij}^{-1}\right) \left[\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (y_{ij}-z_{ij}) \exp(\hat{\beta}_i)\right] = 0,$$

where $\exp(\hat{\beta}_i) = R_i$. Therefore, the solution to this equation yields the explicit representation given above in equation (9.14). Similarly, the sandwich estimator in equation (9.12) provides the large sample size estimator of asymptotic variance of the logarithm of the means, namely

$$\widehat{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i) = \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (y_{ij}/\hat{R}_i z_{ij})^{-2}\right)^2, \quad i=1, 2,...,k. \quad (9.15)$$

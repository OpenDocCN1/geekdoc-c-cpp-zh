The parameter labeled as "Intercept" in the CONTREG procedure corresponds to $\ln(\mu)$ and the one labeled "_Alpha" to $\kappa$. When the ZINB distribution is fitted, "Inf_Intercept" corresponds by default to $logit(\mu)$. The results of the Pearson’s GOF test indicate that Negative-binomial model does not fit the data, since the Chi-square statistic turned out to be 23.22 with an associated P-value of 0.0016. On the other hand, the Zero-inflated Negative Binomial model seems to fit the data reasonably well. Under the ZINB distribution, the value of the Chi-square statistic turned out to 10.58 with an associated P-value of 0.1023. Recall that degrees of freedom are calculated as (Number of Categories minus one) (Number of Parameters in the Model). The reported Chi-square statistics under the NB and ZINB models by Cohen’s (1966) were 24.53 and 10.60, respectively. These data are also amenable to being modeled by Poisson and Zip, however do not fit the data. We will provide an illustrative example of ZIP in the next section.

Now, we discuss the parameter estimates of the ZINB model. Assuming that the $n=926$ inhabitants exclaimed to a “representative" sample of the inhabitants of the village, under the ZINB model, then the estimated proportion of non-infected inhabitants in the village is $\omega=0.4680(46.8\%)$, whereas the estimated proportion of infected inhabitants in the village is $(1-\hat{\omega}) = (1-0.4680) = 0.5320(53.2\%)$. Note that there is a proportion of zeros coming from the NB. The contribution of zeros by NB, representing the individuals who are falsely deemed to be de-free by the medical test, is:

$(1-\hat{\omega})\frac{1}{1+\hat{\kappa}} = (0.5320)\frac{1}{1+(0.8213)} \approx 0.1832$.

Note that $0.4680+0.1832=0.6512$, which is equal to, all the zeros observed in the data.

## 6.1 Introduction

Historically, the standard model for count data is the Poisson distribution. There are several characterizations of the Poisson distribution. Cameron and Trivedi (1998, pp. 4-8) provide four of those characterizations: the law of rare events, the Poisson counting process with waiting times between events, and the Poisson-stopped binomial. Since some of the assumptions behind these characterizations can be violated easily in practice, it is difficult to find real-life Poisson sets. Hilbert (2007, pp. 8-13) describes several possible violations of distributional assumptions. The violation of these assumptions may lead to the presence of overdispersion. Thus, overdispersion seems to be in the vast majority of Poisson data. In this chapter we will cover widely used likelihood models for overdispersion in count responses, namely, the Negative-binomial, the Zero-inflated Poisson (ZIP), the Zero-inflated Negative-binomial (ZINB), and a few Hurdle models.

As GLM, the variance function expresses the variance of a response as a function of the mean. namely, from the DIST= option in the MODEL statement, or from the user-supplied variance function (see the included Variance Functions in the GLIMMIX documentation). The matrix $R$ is a variance matrix specified by the user through the RANDOM statement. This matrix must not be confused with the R-side matrix previously described. If the conditional distribution of the data contains an additional scale parameter, it is either part of the variance functions or part of the $R$ matrix. This will not be the case in the models discussed here since they all have $\phi=1$. The method of Pseudo-likelihood estimation based on linearization to estimate the parameters of the GLMM in (9.6) was proposed by Wolfgang and O’Connell (1993) based on a first-order Taylor linearization of $\mu$ around some initial estimators $\hat{\beta}$ and $\hat{\gamma}$. This will be discussed in the next chapter with more details. Since marginal models do not have a random term $\gamma$, Wolfgang and O’Connell’s (1993) linearization boils down

$P = X\beta + \varepsilon^*$, (9.8)

where $P = \Delta^{-1}(\mu-g^{-1}(\eta)) + X\beta$, $\Delta = \frac{\partial g^{-1}(\eta)}{\partial \eta} = \hat{\Delta}$, \varepsilon^* \sim \text{Normal}(0, \Sigma)$ and

$$\text{Var}(P) = \Sigma^* = \hat{\Delta}^{-1}A^{1/2}RA^{1/2}\hat{\Delta}^{-1}$ corresponds to a mixed model. The reader can see this mixed with the G-side $Z\gamma$. As a result of Wolfgang and O’Connell’s (1993) terminology, the variance-covariance matrix $\Sigma^*$ represents the R-side of (9.8). As previously stated, the matrix $R$ in $\Sigma^*$ is specified by the user through the RANDOM statement. For marginal models, this specification is stated by RANDOM_RESIDUAL, and it is followed by the options SUBJECTS=effect, which identifies the structure of $G$ for G-side effects and the covariance structure of $R$ for R-side effects. Here we focus on R-side effects. The GLIMMIX procedure provides a wide variety of covariance structures. Many applications call for either diagonal (TYPE=VC) or unstructured covariance matrices. The TYPE=VC (variance components) option is the default for each random effect. It is recommended to model unstructured covariance matrices in terms of their Cholesky parameterization (TYPE=CHOL) rather than TYPE=UN (unspecified).

## 8.1 Introduction

In Chapter 7 we considered maximum likelihood estimation of the Dirichlet-multinomial (DM) distribution proposed by Mosimann (1962), and the Random-clumped Multinomial (RCM) distribution proposed by Morel and Nagaraj (1993) and Necheral and Morel (1998). Maximum likelihood estimation of the parameters of these models using the classical Fisher scoring method poses computational challenges. In the case of the DM distribution, the challenges are overcome by noting that the Fisher information matrix (FIM) can be computed using the Beta-binomial distribution, which is the univariate version of DM. On the other hand, the case of the RCM distribution the computation of the FIM can become a huge burden, then an approximation result can be used to obtain a two-stage procedure for computing the maximum likelihood estimates. An analogous approximation result is also available for the DM distribution. In this chapter, we provide a brief look at the computational details underlying the Two-stage procedure, which is not only computationally efficient but also provides appropriate standard errors. We provide a statement of this result merely to indicate and discuss the impact of the result. Using the approximate information matrix in the Fisher scoring method will result in high computational savings. While our experience shows, for most practical cases, that

## 8.2 A Two-stage Estimation Procedure

The main computational burden in obtaining the MLE is due to the need to compute FIM at every iteration. Our approximation to FIM, stated later in this chapter, addresses this issue and provides excellent accuracy. The idea of a Two-stage procedure is to use this approximation to the FIM in the Fisher scoring algorithm until convergence is achieved, and then perform an extra iteration with the exact FIM. The additional iteration with the exact FIM leads to better accuracy for the estimated errors. Throughout this section we will refer to the equations of Chapter 7 frequently. We will refer the reader specifically to section 7.3 to review the notations. As discussed in the previous chapter (see 7.3), the MLE of $\theta$, say $\hat{\theta} =(\hat{\pi}_1, \hat{\pi}_2, \dots, \hat{\pi}_k, \hat{\rho})$, can be found iteratively by the Fisher scoring method. This algorithm can be as

```
$$\hat{\theta}_{(s+1)} = \hat{\theta}_{(s)} + \left\{ n \mathbf{I}(\hat{\theta}_{(s)}) \right\}^{-1} \frac{\partial L(\hat{\theta}_{(s)})}{\partial \hat{\theta}_{(s)}}$$
```

$\hat{\theta}_{(s)}$ and $\hat{\theta}_{(s+1)}$ denote the s-th and the (s+1)-th iterates respectively, and $I(\hat{\theta}_{(s)})$ is the FIM for a single observation evaluated at the s-th iteration. The initial values for starting the iteration can be obtained by applying the method of methods.

Recall, once that the FIM is defined as

```
$$\mathbf{I}(\mathbf{t}) = E \frac{\partial^2 \log P(\mathbf{t})}{\partial \mathbf{t} \partial \mathbf{t}'} = \sum_{\mathbf{t}} \frac{\partial^2 \log P(\mathbf{t})}{\partial \mathbf{t} \partial \mathbf{t}'} \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{t}).$$
```

The FIM given in Equation (8.2) is a summation over the multinomial sample space, denoted by $\Omega$, of all possible values of the vector $\mathbf{t}$. As the cluster size m and the number of categories $(d+1)$ increase, the multinomial sample space $\Omega$ becomes larger rapidly, and hence the computation of (8.2) can be very slow. We will now state that the approximations to the Fisher information matrices of the overdispersed distributions DM and RM. This approximation to the FIM leads to essentially no loss of accuracy in the maximum likelihood estimates when the Fisher scoring method (8.1) is implemented. This suggests a Two-stage estimation procedure for maximum likelihood estimations of the parameters of the overdispersed multinomial models under discussion. For details, we refer to Neerchal and Morel (1998).

First, let us denote the FIM of the Dirichlet-multinomial distribution by $\mathbf{F}_{DM}(\mathbf{0})$. Individual elements of the FIM, a $(d+1) \times (d+1)$ symmetric matrix, will be denoted by $F_{DM}(\pi_i, \pi_j)$ for $i,j=1,2,...,d$; $F_{DM}(\pi_i, \rho)$, which is also equal to $F_{DM}(\rho, \pi_i)$ for symmetry, for $i=1,2,...,d$; and $F_{DM}(\rho, \rho)$. Similarly, the FIM of the Random-clamped Multinomial distribution is denoted by $F_{RCM}(\mathbf{0})$. Individual elements of the FIM of the Random-clamped Multinomial distribution are denoted by $F_{RCM}(\pi_i, \pi_j)$ for $i=1,2,...,d; i \neq j$; which is also equal to $F_{RCM}(\rho, \pi_i)$ due to symmetry, for $i=1,2,...,d$; and $F_{RCM}(\rho, \rho)$.

Under the Dirichlet-multinomial distribution, as the cluster size increases, as $m \to \infty$, we have that

```
$$\begin{aligned} F_{DM}(\pi_i, \pi_j) & \to c^2(\Psi'(\pi_i) + \Psi'(\pi_{d+1})) & i=1,2,...,d \\ F_{DM}(\pi_i, \pi_{i'}) & \to c^2\Psi'(\pi_{d+1}) & i=1,2,...,d, i \neq i' \\ F_{DM}(\pi_i, \rho) & \to c[\pi_{d+1}\Psi'(\pi_{d+1}) - \pi_i\Psi'(\pi_i)] & i=1,2,...,d & \frac{2}{\rho^4} \\ F_{DM}(\rho, \rho) & \to c\sum_{i=1}^{d+1} \Psi'(\pi_i) + \Psi'((\rho)) & \frac{4}{\rho^6} \end{aligned}$$ (8.3)
```

where $c = \rho^2(1-\rho^2)$, $\Psi'(x)$ is the trigamma function given by $\Psi'(x) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Psi(x)$, and $\Psi''(x) = \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\log(x)$ is the usual digamma function. Interestingly, the limiting FIM of the DM distribution connects to that of the Dirichlet distribution. As indicated in Chapter 7, the Dirichlet distribution is the conjugate prior of the Multinomial distribution. Note pointed out by Jordan and Kote (1969, Section 13.1) and as shown in Neerchal and Morel (1998, Equations (A.8) and (A.9)), the computation of the individual elements of the FIM of the DM distribution can be performed in terms of its marginal distributions, i.e., the Beta-binomial distribution. This observation provides significant relief in the computational effort needed computing the FIM of the DM distribution.

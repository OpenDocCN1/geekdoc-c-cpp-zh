the two judges. Subjects would usually receive similar grades from different judges; the grade vectors $X_{ij}$ would be dependent. $T_i$ does not satisfy one of the basic assumptions underlying the Multinomial distribution. Usually the raters' responses are positively correlated; therefore the vector $T_i$ exhibits overdispersion relative to the Multinomial distribution. The case of underdispersion occurs when the graders are negatively associated. Bartfay and Donner (2001) proposed the Dirichlet-multinomial distribution to model the probabilities of a pair of responses. In other words, they proposed to model $T_i$ as a Dirichlet-multinomial with parameters $(\pi,\rho)$. Cohen's kappa can be used for this model; it was shown that $\kappa \equiv \rho^2$. This is also true $(\kappa \equiv \rho^2)$ for three judges (m=3) and two grading scores (C=2). In this case, the estimates of Kappa for the Beta-binomial and the Random-clumped Binomial distributions are not necessarily the same. For $m \ge 4$ we do not know if $\kappa \equiv \rho^2$. For the case m=2, Morel (1992) showed that the probability functions of Dirichlet-multinomial (DM) and the Random-clumped Multinomial (RCM) distributions are the same. Thus, under either the DM or the RCM distribution, the probability function of T turns out to

```

```

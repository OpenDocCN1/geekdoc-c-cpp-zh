GEE is similar to Quasi-likelihood in the sense that neither approach is based on a likelihood function. While Quasi-likelihood adjusts the standard errors by a scale, GEE standard errors are obtained from the sandwich estimator of variance-covariance matrix. It will be instructive to the reader to obtain the Quasi-likelihood estimates for these data sets using codes similar to those given in Chapter 3, and likelihood-based models are substantially different from the GEE. ## 9.6 An Example on Seizure Counts for Epileptics

Thall and Vail (1990) analyzed data from a clinical trial to study the efficacy of an epileptic drug. Patients suffering from partial or complex seizures were randomized to receive either phenytoin or a placebo. There was a lead-in phase lasting eight weeks where patients were not exposed to treatment. The number of seizures during this eight-week period was recorded and used as baseline. Randomization was performed at the end of this period, which was followed by a treatment phase lasting eight weeks. Although each patient "crossed over" to the other, only the data from the treatment phase are analyzed. $Y_{ij}$ denote the number of seizures reported by subject $i$, $j=1, 2,...,5$ in time interval. Note that $j=1$ represents baseline and $j \geq 2$ represents the treatment phase. Then, the log-linear model with logarithm of time as an offset fits

$E(Y_{ij}) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 X_{i1} + \beta_2 X_{i2} + \beta_3 X_{i1} X_{i2} + \ln(t_{ij})$

where $X_{it} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if Treatment} \\ 2 & \text{if Baseline} \end{cases}$

The results of fitting a GEE model with mean and an exchangeable working correlation, be found in GENMOD documentation. The results of the GEE model with random effects and a compound variance-covariance matrix, be found in GLIMMIX documentation. Program 9.3 shows programming statements for first reading the data and creating the offset variable, and then fitting the previously described GEE using the GLIMMIX procedure.

# Preface

This book focuses on the modeling and analysis of data exhibiting overdispersion using a variety of SAS procedures. Overdispersion, also known as extra variation, arises in Binomial, Multinomial, and count types of data when variances are larger than those allowed by the Binomial, Multinomial, or Poisson models. This phenomenon is caused by clustering of the data and lack of independence. As pointed out by McCullagh and Nelder (1989), 'Overdispersion is not uncommon in practice. In fact, some would maintain that overdispersion is the norm in practice and nominal dispersion the exception.' The problem of overdispersion is encountered every day by practitioners from pharmaceutical departments, biostatistics and biometrics departments, universities, government agencies, health research organizations, and sampling survey organizations. The intent of this book is to provide the techniques necessary for the proper analysis and modeling of such problems. These techniques include Likelihood and Likelihood models, as well as Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE), Generalized Linear Overdispersion Models (GLOM), Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMM).

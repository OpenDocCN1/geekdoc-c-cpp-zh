$L(\	heta; t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \ln\{Pr(T_j = t_j)\}, \quad (7.12)$

where $t_i$ is a realization of $T_i$. In Section 2.6 of Chapter 2 and Section 4.8 of Chapter 4, the MLE of $\theta$, say $\hat{\theta}$, can be found iteratively as

```
$\hat{\theta}_{(S+1)} = \hat{\theta}_{(S)} + \left[I(\hat{\theta}_{(S)})\right]^{-1} u(\hat{\theta}_{(S)}), \quad (7.13)$
```

where $u(\hat{\theta}_{(S)})$ is the r-dimensional column vector of first derivatives of the log-likelihood function $u(\	heta) = \frac{\partial L(\	heta;t)}{\partial \theta}$ evaluated at $\theta=\hat{\theta}$, and $I(\hat{\theta}_{(S)})$ is the rxr Fisher information matrix $I = -E\left[\frac{\partial^2 L(\	heta;t)}{\partial \theta \partial \theta}, \frac{\partial^2 L(\	heta;t)}{\partial \theta \'}\right]$ evaluated at $\theta=\hat{\theta}_{(S)}$. As in previous chapters, $I(\hat{\theta}_{(S)})$ can be replaced by the Hessian $H(\hat{\theta}_{(S)})$.

# 7.4 An Example of a Generalized Logit Model

The example is taken from Stokes, Davis, and Koch (2000) and is used in the LOGISTIC and CATMOD to illustrate the fitting of a Generalized Logit Model.

The background of this data set is given in the SAS documentation. We are using this example from the SAS documentation, already familiar to many readers, as an introduction to Generalized Logit Model estimation. Discussion of overdispersion in this context will be introduced in subsequent sections. Over the course of one school year, third-graders were exposed to three different styles of mathematics: a self-paced computer-learning style, an approach, and a traditional class approach. The students were asked which they prefer, and their preferences, classified by the type of program they are in, a regular school day versus a regular day supplemented with an afternoon school program.

The Breslow and Day (1980) test for homogeneity of the odds-ratios (across strata) is 9.32 on degrees of freedom and can be found at the end of the output of the FREQ procedure. A saturated logistic regression model produces similar results. The value of the Likelihood Ratio test for homogeneity of the logarithm of the odds-ratio, provided by the Type 3 for the interaction, is 11.04; it was obtained using the programming statements given below:

```
*** Saturated Model ***
proc gcnemod data=oesophophageal_cancer;
class age_stratum alcohol;
model case/m = age_stratum alcohol age_stratum*alcohol/ dist=binomial
link=logit type3;
```

For further information on the CMH estimator of the odds-ratio and the Breslow and Day test of homogeneity, we refer the reader to Stokes et al. (2000), Fleiss et al. (2003) and the SAS documentation for the FREQ procedure.

# 2.9 A Poisson Example of Failures of Electronic Equipment

From the examples in the previous sections we move to a Poisson example, which is the next most commonly used GLM. We will illustrate the comparison of the estimates and interpret the diagnostic plots. Jorgensen (1961) and Lomer et al. (1971) analyze data consisting of a poisson of electronic cycles under operation (weeks) in which each cycle was divided into two operating regimes. The data are provided in Table 2.7. The response variable is y_j, j=1,2,...,9, representing the number of failures in the j-th cycle. The components of the vector x_j = (x_{j1}, x_{j2}) are the times spent in regimes one and two during the j-th cycle of operation.

As in From et al. (1971), we fit a Poisson model with λ_j = β_0 +x_{j1} + β_2x_{j2}. Note that this model does not have any interaction and the link function is the identity function. Since this is the canonical link, by default the GLIMMIX procedure uses the Newton-Raphson algorithm with the Hessian matrix. GLIMMIX allows the use of the Fisher scoring method up to a specific iteration with the option SCORING=number. If the algorithm converges within the number of iterations specified in the SCORING option, the covariance matrix of the parameter estimates will be the inverse of the Fisher Information Matrix and not the inverse of the Hessian. To replicate the results of From et al. (1971) we use the identity link and the Fisher Scoring Method.

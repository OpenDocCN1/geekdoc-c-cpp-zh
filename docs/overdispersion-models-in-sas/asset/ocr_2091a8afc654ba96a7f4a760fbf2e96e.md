# 9.10 Small Sample Bias Corrections for Sandwich Estimators

It is well-known that in small samples, the GEE estimator of the variance-covariance matrix of the parameter estimates leads to inflated Type I error rates. To cope with this problem, resampling methods such as the jackknife and the bootstrap have been suggested to estimate robust covariance matrix. As pointed out by Mancel and DeRouten (2001), these methods fail for binary responses and for small numbers of subjects (clusters). If $\hat{H}^{-1}\hat{G}\hat{H}^{-1}$ denotes the “sandwich” estimator using GEE with $\hat{H}^{-1}$ as the “model-based” covariance matrix, Mancel and DeRouten argue that the residuals used to estimate $\hat{H}$ tend to be too small. Thus, the “sandwich” covariance matrix would be expected to underestimate the covariance matrix of the parameters, and its bias towards underestimation when the bias of the residuals is large (i., when the number of clusters is small). Thus, to reduce the small sample bias by using a first-order Taylor series approximation on the residuals in the computation of $\hat{G}$, Fay and Graraud (2001) propose two sample corrections. The first of these corrections is similar to that of Mancel and DeRouten (2001) but has the advantage that it may be applied to more general frameworks than GEE, such as the Cox proportional hazard and logistic regression. The second correction by Fay and Graraud (2001) consists of using f (or t) distribution instead of Chi-square (or normal) distribution, with an adjustment on the degrees of freedom. Kaumarni and Karoll (2001) propose an adjustment to ensure the use of the sandwich estimates combined with the distribution quantities provides coverage with probability below the nominal value. Pan and Wall (2002) propose an estimator and allows for f-test (or t test) that takes into account the bias of the “sandwich” estimator and allows for testing more one parameter simultaneously. Morel (1989), inspired by similar work in the survey sampling literature, propose a small sample correction to the GEE estimator and implemented it with the generalized link function with nominal-type data. The correction not only reduces the small sample bias in the Type I error rates but also guarantees that the estimated variance-covariance matrix is positive. It can be applied for testing one or more parameters, but differs from other corrections in the adjustment to the residuals of $\hat{G}$. That is, the correction is additive and not multiplicative, with the added term varying as the number of clusters (primary sampling units) increases. Morel, Bokossa and Neerhaal (2003) consider this additive correction to the GLM and GLMM frameworks, which are applicable to univariate and multivariate-type responses. The GLIMMIX procedure provides a variety of multiplicative small sample bias corrections, which are de-facto found in their documentation under the heading “Residual-based Estimators.” The de-facto by Morel, Bokossa and Neerhaal (2003) correction, MBN for short, is under the heading “Design-adjusted MBN Estimator.”. THE SURVEYLOGISTIC procedure provides the option of adjusting the sandwich variance estimator with the Morel (1989) correction.

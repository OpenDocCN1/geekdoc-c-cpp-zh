## 7.10 A Forest Pollen Count Problem

Mosimann (1962) analyzed a problem where four arboreal types of forest pollen (pine, oak, and alder) were counted. This is the first example in the literature where the Dirichlet-multinomial distribution was applied to model overdispersed. The data, which can be found in Table 7.4, consisted of n = 73 sets, each representing the counts on the frequency of occurrence of the four fossil pollen grains made at a certain depth of a core. The pollen was counted until a total of $m_j = 100$ grains were reached. As in Mosimann's analysis, we will regard the data from different sets as independent. For the j-th set ($j = 1, 2, ..., 73$), $t_j = (t_{j1}, t_{j2}, t_{j3})$ is the observed vector of counts for the types "pine", "fir" and "oak", with the usual constraint $t_{j1} + t_{j2} + t_{j3} \le 100$. Assume that $t_j$ is a realization of $T_j$. We will find the MLE of $\theta = (\pi, r')$ when $T_j$ is modeled as an Dirichlet-multinomial($pi,r;m_j$) or as a Random-clumped Multinomial($pi,r;m_j$). Note that $\pi = (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3)$, where $\pi_1, \pi_2$ and $\pi_3$ represent the proportion of pollen grains of pine, fir and oak falling in the core during a particular time, respectively. Also note that $\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_3 < 1$, and that the proportion of pollen grains of alder is $\pi_4 = 1-(\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_3)$.

$\pi_1, \pi_2,$ and $\pi_3$, are respectively, the probability of pollen grains of fir, pine, and oak.

In this example the sample size is relatively large (n = 73) and the estimates of $\pi = (\pi_1, \pi_2, \pi_3)$ under both models are close to each other. The estimate of $\rho$ under the Dirichlet-multinomial turned out to be slightly larger. However, the difference between two sets of MLE seems to be in the estimated standard errors: the errors under the Random-clumped Multinomial are in this example smaller than those under the Dirichlet-multinomial model. This is an inherent property of the Random-clumped Multinomial distribution that holds for large $m_j$. This property will be discussed in the next chapter.

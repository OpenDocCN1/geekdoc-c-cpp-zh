# 8.2 A Two-stage Estimation Procedure

The main computational burden in obtaining the MLE is due to the need to compute FIM at every iteration. Our approximation to FIM, stated later in this chapter, addresses this issue and provides excellent accuracy. The idea of a Two-stage procedure is to use this approximation to the FIM in the Fisher scoring algorithm until convergence is achieved, and then perform an extra iteration with the exact FIM. The additional iteration with the exact FIM leads to better accuracy for the estimated errors. Throughout this section we will refer to the equations of Chapter 7 frequently. We will refer the reader specifically to section 7.3 to review the notations. As discussed in the previous chapter (see 7.3), the MLE of $\theta$, say $\hat{	heta} =(\hat{\pi}_1, \hat{\pi}_2, \dots, \hat{\pi}_k, \hat{ho})$, can be found iteratively by the Fisher scoring method. This algorithm can be as

```
$$\hat{	heta}_{(s+1)} = \hat{	heta}_{(s)} + \left\{ n \mathbf{I}(\hat{	heta}_{(s)}) \right\}^{-1} \frac{\partial L(\\hat{	heta}_{(s)})}{\partial \hat{	heta}_{(s)}}$
```

$$\hat{	heta}_{(s)}$ and $\hat{	heta}_{(s+1)}$ denote the s-th and the (s+1)-th iterates respectively, and $I(\\hat{	heta}_{(s)})$ is the FIM for a single observation evaluated at the s-th iteration. The initial values for starting the iteration can be obtained by applying the method of methods.

Recall, once that the FIM is defined as

```
$$\mathbf{I}(\mathbf{t}) = E \frac{\partial^2 \log P(\mathbf{t})}{\partial \mathbf{t} \partial \mathbf{t}'} = \sum_{\mathbf{t}} \frac{\partial^2 \log P(\mathbf{t})}{\partial \mathbf{t} \partial \mathbf{t}'} \mathbf{P}(\mathbf{t})$.
```

The FIM given in Equation (8.2) is a summation over the multinomial sample space, denoted by $\Omega$, of all possible values of the vector $\mathbf{t}$. As the cluster size m and the number of categories $(d+1)$ increase, the multinomial sample space $\Omega$ becomes larger rapidly, and hence the computation of (8.2) can be very slow.

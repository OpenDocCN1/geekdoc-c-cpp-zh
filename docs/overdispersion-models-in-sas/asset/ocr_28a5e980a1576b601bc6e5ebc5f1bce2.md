# 9.2 Marginal and Conditional Models

GEE can be applied to the so-called Marginal and Conditional Models. Marginal Models or Population-average Models are used to make inferences about population averages. On the other hand, Conditional Models are used when the focus is on subject-specific inference. The procedures MIXED and GLIMMIX distinguish between two different ways in which random effects affect a GLM, G-side and R-side. To distinguish these two types of random effects, consider a parallel study for evaluating the efficacy of a cream for hair growth where subjects were randomized to either apply the cream that supposedly grows hair, or to use a cream lotion placebo with nothing but inert ingredients. Subjects applied the lotions to their heads for a total of eight weeks, and a dermatologist observed hair growth on a weekly basis. Data are collected at weekly intervals to quantify the possible hair growth. Note that a string of measurements are obtained for each subject. We can account for the correlation of each subject by modeling it in different ways. One way is to model hierarchically, with subjects within the subject treated as random, i.e., normally distributed with zero mean and variance². This approach introduces a variance-covariance structure to the string of measurements from the subject. This specification is referred to as "G-side." The model is said to be conditional, perhaps as a reference to the fact that it is specified using a conditional argument. Inferences, previously stated, are subject-specific. On the other hand, the string of measurements associated with each subject can be treated as a multivariate random vector having certain correlation structure.

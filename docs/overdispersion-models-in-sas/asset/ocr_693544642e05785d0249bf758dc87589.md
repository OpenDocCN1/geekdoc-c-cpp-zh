$0 \le \omega \le 1$ and $f(y;alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_s)$ may be any discrete distribution defined over the domain $y=0, 1, \dots,$ with parameters $(\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_s)$. While comparing the presentation here with Cohen's original work, it is important to note a crucial change in notation. Note that from equation (6.9) the probability of $y=0$ is $\omega(1-\omega)f(0)$, whereas in Cohen's notation, the same probability is given by $(1-\omega)+\omega f(0)$. We follow the parameterization in Section 2.2, Chapter 8 of Johnson et al. (1992), which is consistent with the notations used in GENMOD and COUNTREG. In Cohen's paper, $\omega$ represented "the proportion of the infected or susceptible group in the population," which was associated with the probability function $f(.)$. Our parameterization permits us to interpret $\omega$ as the Zero-inflation Probability measure. This family of distributions allows one to model an excess of zeros and is readily applicable to both the Poisson and the Negative-binomial distributions. It is possible to take $\omega$ negative, provided that

```
$$\omega \ge \frac{-f(0)}{1-f(0)}$ \quad (6.10)$
```

In such a case the proportion of zeros associated with $f(y;alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_s)$ are reduced.

If $f(y;mu)$ corresponds to the probability function of a Poisson distribution with parameter $\mu$, it follows from (6.9) that $Y$ is a Zero-inflated Poisson distribution (ZIP) with parameters $(\mu, \omega)$, its probability function is

```
$Pr(Y=y) = \omega I(y=0)+(1-\omega)\frac{e^{-\mu y}}{\Gamma(y+1)}, y=0,1,\dots$ \quad (6.11)$
```

$I(.)$ is an indicator function. The reader should note that there are two sources of zeros in (6.10), arising from a zero-degeneration distribution with probability $\omega$, and the other one from a Poisson($\mu$) with probability $(1-\omega)$. Note that, $Pr(Y=0)=\omega+(1-\\omega)e^{-\mu}$, which is always greater than or equal to $e^{-\mu}$ for $\omega>0$. Similarly, for $\mu<0$, as in (6.10), with probability of observing zero under the ZIP model will be smaller than the probability of zero under the Poisson distribution. The mean and variance of the ZIP distribution can be shown to be

```
$E(Y) = (1-\omega)(\mu+\mu^2)$ \quad (6.12)$
```

```
$Var(Y) = (1-\omega)(\mu+\mu^2)$ \quad (6.13)$
```

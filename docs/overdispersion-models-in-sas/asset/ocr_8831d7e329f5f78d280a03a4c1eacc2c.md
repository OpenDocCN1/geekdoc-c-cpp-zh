Morel and Koehler (1995) proposed a methodology that allows for different levels of overdispersion (or underdispersion) in the components of multinomial responses. This approach can be viewed as a generalization of the single scale inflation based on the Pearson's Chi-square statistic. They introduce category-specific scaling factors by replacing (7.16)

```
$Var ((\mathbf{T}_j) = m_j \mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j) \mathbf{C}^T(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j), \quad (7.20)$
```

where $\mathbf{\Phi}$ is a diagonal matrix $\mathbf{\Phi} = \text{Diag}(\phi_1, \dots, \phi_k)$ and $\Delta(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j) = \mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j)\mathbf{C}^T(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j)$. The case when $\Phi$ is an unknown $d \times d$ covariance could also be considered. However, we will focus only on the case where $\mathbf{\Phi}$ is a diagonal matrix and $\mathbf{C}(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j)$ is the Cholesky decomposition of $\Delta(\boldsymbol{\pi}_j)$. This has a straightforward interpretation, as we will see here. In the context of quasi-likelihoods, variance structure (7.20) is a meaningful extension of the single scale inflation based on the Pearson's Chi-square statistic to multinomial responses. In particular, different degrees of overdispersion can be accommodated by different categories.

# 4.5 A Generalization of the Binomial Distribution

Altham (1978) proposed two generalizations of the Binomial distribution which the Bernoulli outcomes are identically distributed but not independent. The proposed generalizations led to a two-parameter distribution where m variables are assumed to have a symmetric joint distribution with no second or higher 'order' interactions. Different generalizations are obtained depending on whether the 'additive' or 'multiplicative' definition of 'interaction' for discrete variables is used. These two generalizations allow the variance to be greater or less than the variance under the Binomial distribution. In other words, Altham's generalizations allow for either overdispersion or undersdispersion. We will focus only on the 'additive' generalization. This additive model was published simultaneously by Kupper and Haseman (1978). We will skip the details of the derivation of the Binomial distribution with Additive Interaction.

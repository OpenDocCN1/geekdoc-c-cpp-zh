where $\Gamma(\cdot)$ is a gamma function and $C=(I-ho^2)/ho^2$, $ho$ the overdispersion parameter. Under the Beta distribution,

$$E_r(P) = \frac{a}{a+b} = \pi$$

and

$$V_r(P) = \frac{ab}{(a+b+1)(a+b)} = \rho^2\pi(1-\rho),$$

so the first two moments satisfy equations (4.1) and (4.3). If $P\sim \text{Binomial}(\pi;m)$ and $P\sim \text{Beta}\{C,C(1-\rho)\}$, then it can be the probability function of the unconditional distribution of T

$$Pr(T=t) = \frac{\binom{m}{t}}{t} \frac{\Gamma(t+C)}{\Gamma(m+C)} \frac{\Gamma\{m-t+C(1-\pi)\}}{\Gamma(Cp)\Gamma\{C(1-\pi)\}}, \quad t=0,1,...,m,\quad4.8$$

where, as before, $C=(I-\rho^2)/ho^2$. The probability function of equation (4.4) defines the Binomial distribution with parameters $\pi$, $\rho$ and $m$, and denoted as Beta-binomial($\pi,\rho;m$). The density function (4.7) is the conjugate prior of Binomial. This makes derivation of (4.8) relatively straightforward. Since the first two moments of the Beta distribution satisfy equations (4.1) and (4.3), the first three moments of the Beta-binomial are as equations (4.4) and (4.5), then $E(T)=m\pi$ and $Var(T)=m\pi(1-\pi)\{1+\rho^2(m-1)\}$. In Section 4.10 we provide a SAS program for generating data from the Beta-binomial distribution for any given configuration of $\pi$, $\rho$ and $m$.

# 4.3 The Random-clumped Binomial Distribution

The Random-clumped Binomial distribution results from an effort to model meaningfully the physical mechanism behind extra variation. It was informally introduced in Section 1.5 of Chapter 1. We provide a formal derivation here.

Let $Y_1, Y_2,...,Y_m$ be independent and identically distributed Bernoulli($\pi$) random variables. For each $i$, $i=1,...,m$, define $Y_i$ as $Y$ with probability $\rho$, or as $Y_i^0$ with probability $(1-\rho)$, $0<\rho<1$. $Y_i$ can be represented as

$$Y_i = Y I(U_i\le \rho) + Y_i^0 I(U_i > \rho), \quad i=1,2,...,m,\quad4.9$$

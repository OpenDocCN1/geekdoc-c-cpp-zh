Subjects would usually receive similar grades from different judges; the grade vectors $X_{ij}$ would be dependent. $T_i$ does not satisfy one of the basic assumptions underlying the Multinomial distribution. Usually the raters' responses are positively correlated; therefore the vector $T_i$ exhibits overdispersion relative to the Multinomial distribution. The case of underdispersion occurs when the graders are negatively associated. Bartfay and Donner (2001) proposed the Dirichlet-multinomial distribution to model the probabilities of a pair of responses. In other words, they proposed to model $T_i$ as a Dirichlet-multinomial with parameters $(\pi,\rho)$. Cohen's kappa can be used for this model; it was shown that $\kappa \equiv \rho^2$. This is also true $(\kappa \equiv \rho^2)$ for three judges (m=3) and two grading scores (C=2). In this case, the estimates of Kappa for the Beta-binomial and the Random-clumped Binomial distributions are not necessarily the same. For $m \ge 4$ we do not know if $\kappa \equiv \rho^2$. For the case m=2, Morel (1992) showed that the probability functions of Dirichlet-multinomial (DM) and the Random-clumped Multinomial (RCM) distributions are the same. Thus, under either the DM or the RCM distribution, the probability function of $T$ turns out to

$$\begin{aligned}\{\{\pi_i+\pi_i(1-\pi_i)\pi_i, & \text{if the i-th component of } t \text{ is 2} \\2(1-\pi_i)\pi_i, & \text{if the i-th and s-th components of } t \text{ are 1}\end{aligned} \quad (7.40)$$

Recall that under the parameterization $\rho^2$ represents the coefficient of agreement $\kappa$. We would like the reader to note that Landis and Koch (1977) have provided the following guidelines (which are shown in Table 7.8) for interpreting the value of Kappa. ## 7.8 Guidelines for Interpreting Kappa

| Kappa | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| < 0 | No agreement |
| 0 — 0.20 | Very low agreement |
| 0.21 — 0.40 | Low agreement |
| 0.41 — 0.60 | Moderate agreement |
| 0.61 — 0.80 | Full agreement |
| 0.81 — 1.00 | Almost perfect agreement |

According to these guidelines, any value of the Kappa coefficient greater than 0.60 is considered full agreement or almost perfect agreement. Since the point estimate of Kappa in Sissel's and Ebert's ratings was 0.38 with an approximate 95% confidence interval of about (0.26, 0.51), one might say, based on the above guidelines, the agreement between the two movies was between low and moderate.

## Appendix 7.1: Data Generation under Dirichlet-multinomial and Random-clumped Multinomial Distributions

In this appendix, we describe the generation of a random sample $\mathbf{t}_1, \mathbf{t}_2, \dots, \mathbf{t}_n$ under either the Dirichlet-multinomial or Random-clumped Multinomial distribution.

Under the Dirichlet-multomial model, a random sample $\mathbf{p}_1, \mathbf{p}_2, \dots, \mathbf{p}_n$ is first generated from a $(d+1)$ dimensional Dirichlet density function
$$f_D(\mathbf{p}; \alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_{d+1}) = \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 + \dots + \alpha_{d+1})}{\prod_{i=1}^{d+1} \Gamma(\alpha_i)} \prod_{i=1}^{d+1} p_i^{\alpha_i-1}$$
where $\alpha_i = c\pi, i=1,2,\dots,(d+1), c = \rho^2(1-\rho), 0 < \rho < 1,$ and $\Gamma(\cdot)$ denotes the gamma function. Then, given $\mathbf{p}_j, j=1,2,n, \mathbf{t}_j$ is generated from a Multinomial distribution with parameters $\mathbf{p}_j$ and $m$. The unconditional distribution of $T$ is Dirichlet-multinomial and has probability function as in equation (7.30). The RANDDIRICHLET function in the IML library was used to generate a random sample from a Dirichlet distribution.

The Dirichlet variates $\mathbf{p}_j$'s can be obtained from Beta variables (Brier, 1979, Theorem 4.1; Kotz, Balakrishnan, and Johnson, 2000, p. 448). In the following discussion, we have dropped the subscript '$j$' for convenience. Generate $X_i$ from a Beta distribution with parameter $\alpha_1 = c\pi_1$, and $\alpha_2 = c(1-\pi_1)$, then $p_1 = x_1$. Then generate $x_2$ from a Beta distribution with parameter $\alpha_1 = c\pi_1$, and $\alpha_2 = c(1-\pi_1-\pi_2)$, and compute $p_2$
$p_2 = x_2(1-p_1)$. The $d$-th component of each Dirichlet distribution is obtained as
$p_d = x_d \sum_{i=1}^{d} p_i, where $x_d$ is generated from a beta distribution with parameters $\alpha_i = c\pi_d$
and $\alpha_2 = c(1-\sum_{i=1}^{d} \pi_i)$. Recall that $p_{d+1} = 1-\sum_{i=1}^{d} p_i$, so there is no need to generate the $(d+1)$ component. The RANDDIRICHLET function in the IML model library is used to generate a random sample from a Dirichlet distribution.

Generation of data under the Random-clamped Multinomial model is much simpler and be found in Morel and Nagaraj (1993). We generate each $i=1,2,...,n$, using equation (7.32).

As discussed in this chapter, the two distributions are strictly different, they are both overdispersed relative Multinomial distribution and have the same mean and variances. In simulation and in Monte Carlo studies where the interest is in correlated multinomial responses with first moments in (7.28) and (7.29), the algorithm in equation (7.32) is simpler and faster than the algorithm for generating Dirichlet-multinomial responses.

It can be shown that the probability function of T in (4.10) is given by

$Pr(T=t) = \pi Pr(X_1=t) + (1-\pi)Pr(X_2=t), \ t=0,1,\dots$

where $X_1 \sim \text{Binomial}\{(1-\rho)\pi+\rho;m\}$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Binomial}\{(1-\rho)(1-\pi);m\}$. Note that this is a mixture of two Binomial distributions. As pointed out in the previous section, overdispersed binomial distributions might arise by placing the appropriate prior on the probability of success P of a Binomial distribution. The Beta-binomial distribution is one example where the prior placed on P is the Beta distribution. The distribution given in (4.11) was proposed by Morel and Nagaraj (1993) for modeling overdispersion where the unconditional distribution of the total number of successes T, is obtained from placing on P a two-point prior on the points $P_1 = (1-\rho)\pi$ and $P_2 = \rho\pi$, with probabilities $\pi$ and $(1-\pi)$, respectively. If T has the distribution given by equation (4.11), it will be denoted as
nT \sim \text{Random-clumped Binomial}(\pi, \rho;m\)$. In Section 4.10 we provide a SAS program for generating data from the Random-clumped Binomial distribution for any given configuration of $\pi, \rho$ and $m$.

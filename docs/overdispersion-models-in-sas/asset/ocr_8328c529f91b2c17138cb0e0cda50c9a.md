Since all the overdisp considered here reduce to the Binomial distribution under the null hypothesis, the likelihood ratio statistic can be computed by subtracting the maximum value of the likelihood under an overdispersed model from the maximum likelihood value under the Binomial distribution. While computing this test statistic poses no difficulty at all, properties of the likelihood ratio test cannot be on the boundary of the parameter space. Self and Liang (1987) have shown in this case (Case 5 in their paper), the asymptotic distribution of the likelihood ratio test is a 5:50 mixture of a point mass at zero and a Chi-square distribution with 1 degree of freedom. The likelihood ratio test statistic is 880.8–873.6–7.2 under the Beta-binomial, 7.0 under the Random-clumped Binomial, 3.2 under the ZIB, and 7.3 under the Generalized Binomial with Additive Interaction. Their appropriate P-values are 0.004, 0.004, 0.037, and 0.003, and strongly support the hypothesis of overdispersion under either model.

While the presence of overdispersion is easily accepted under any of the four models, observe all four overdispersion models compete violently for being chosen as the model to describe these data. In order to further examine the quality of fit of these distributions to these data, we consider this example by computing the traditional Pearson's Goodness-of-fit chi-square statistic

> $$\chi^2 = \sum_{i}\frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}$

In this example, the computation of the Pearson's Chi-of-fit GOF is straightforward since all the clusters are of the same size ($m=3$). In the next section, we will see a generalization of these tests to case where the $n=337$ nuclei (clusters). Each of them has a number of chromosome associations that might take one of the values $\{0, 1, 3\}$. The associated frequencies $O_s, s=0, 1, 3$ are found in Table 4.1.
The expected frequencies are $E_s=nPr(T=s)$, $s=0, 1, 3$, where $Pr(T=s)$ is based on the maximum likelihood estimates under any of the distributions previously discussed. The programming statements for comparing the Pearson's Goodness-of-fit Chi-square statistic are provided in Program 4.2. Recall the temporary SAS files "pred1" through "pred5" contain the observed and predicted frequencies for the five models under consideration.

## 7.1 Introduction

This chapter provides a gentle introduction to the analysis of multinomial count data exhibiting overdispersion. We begin with a brief discussion of Multinomial models and provide a basic introduction to two most commonly used link functions. A brief example gives the reader the flavor of some the issues arising in multinomial data which were not present in the binomial data. We then introduce the concept of multinomial overdispersion as well as the scale adjustment

![](img/fa69b648c17ad451eb7eb4ad8d92f5fb_213_0.png)

approach used in the Quasi-likelihood Estimation method for multinomial data. While most of the QLE literature focuses on a single scale factor for all categories, we illustrate a method of accommodating a different scale factor for each category. Analogous to Chapter 4, we give the two likelihood-based models, Dirichlet-multinomial and Random-clustered Multinomial distributions, for modeling binomial overdispersion. The latter is a multivariate generalization of the Random-clustered binomial distribution described in Chapter 4. A brief description of their construction and properties are followed by examples. In the case of binomial data, we use a Generalized Linear Overdispersion Model (GLOM) which accommodates link functions to both the vector of probabilities as well as the overdispersion parameters. The models described in this chapter may be implemented in a variety of ways. In some cases, we use the procedures and provide the codes we have developed. In addition to using PROC LOGISTIC, we also illustrate the use of the UNMIXED procedure to estimate some of the models described in this chapter.

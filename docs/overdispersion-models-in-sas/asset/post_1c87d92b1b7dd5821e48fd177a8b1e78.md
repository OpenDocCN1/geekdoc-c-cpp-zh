## 6.2 The Negative-binomial Distribution

The benchmark model to account for overdispersion on count responses is the Negative-binomial (NB) distribution. This distribution can be derived in several different ways and has been studied extensively over several centuries (Johnson, Kotz, and Kemp 1992, Section 3, Chapter 5). The most well-known interpretation of the NB is that it is the distribution of the number of failures before a pre-determined number of success occur in a sequence of Bernoulli trials. However, in the context of overdispersion, its ability to account for the lack of equidispersion (equality of the conditional mean and variance) in clustered data with count responses is of primary interest. We now present a derivation of the Negative-binomial distribution which more directly relates to overdispersion. We first assume that for a given cluster, the conditional distribution of the number of counts (on the rate parameter) is Poisson. Further, hierarchically, the rates vary according to a Gamma distribution. Then, it turns that the unconditional distribution of counts is Nb. Toward that end, $Y_j$, j=1,2,...,n be independently distributed random variables such that $Y_j \sim \text{Poisson}(u_j)$. The probability function of $Y_j$ is

$$\Pr(Y_j|u_j) = \frac{e^{-u_j} u_j^{Y_j}}{\Gamma(Y_j+1)} \quad (6.1)$$

where $\Gamma(\cdot)$ is the gamma function, for integer arguments $y+1$ becomes $y!$. Let us assume that the rate-parameters $u_j$, $j=1,2,...,n$, are independent and identically distributed as Gamma($\alpha$,$\beta$). That is, the density function of each $u_j$ is

$$f(u) = \frac{u^{\alpha-1}}{\Gamma(\alpha)\beta^\alpha} e^{-\frac{u}{\beta}}, u > 0 \quad (6.2)$$

where $\alpha$ is the shape parameter and $\beta$ the scale parameter.
The mean and the variance of the gamma distribution under the above parameterization are

$$E(U) = \alpha\beta$$

and

$$\text{Var}(U) = \alpha\beta^2.$$

It can be shown (Johnson, Kotz, and Kemp 1992, p. 204) that the unconditional distribution of $Y_j$ is given by

$$\Pr(Y_j=y) = \frac{\Gamma(y+\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(y+1)} \left(\frac{1}{1+\beta}\right)^\alpha \left(\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}\right)^y, y=0,1,... \quad (6.3)$$

which can be easily recognized as Negative-binomial($\alpha$,$\beta$) with probability of success $\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}$.

It follows that the unconditional mean and variance of $Y_j$ are given by

$$E(Y_j) = \alpha\beta \quad (6.4)$$

and

$$\text{Var}(Y_j) = \alpha\beta(1+\beta) \quad (6.5)$$

The Negative-binomial corresponds to a Poisson-gamma mixture model where the mixing distribution is the Gamma, the conjugate prior for the Poisson distribution. There is an analogy between this model and the Beta-binomial in the sense that the latter arises by a similar mechanism, by obtaining the unconditional distribution of Binomial when the probability of success varies according to Beta distribution. Under parameterization $\mu = \alpha$ and $\kappa = \frac{1}{\alpha}$, the unconditional probability function of $Y_j$ is given by

$$\Pr(Y_j = y) = \frac{\Gamma(y+\frac{1}{\kappa})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{\kappa})\Gamma(y+1)} \left(\frac{\kappa\mu}{1+\kappa\mu}\right)^y, y=0,1,... \quad (6.6)$$

This parameterization yields the following well-known representation of the first moments

$$E(Y_j) = \mu \quad (6.7)$$

and

$$\text{Var}(Y_j) = \mu + \kappa\mu^2 = \mu(1+\kappa\mu) \quad (6.8)$$

Cameron and Triv (1986) consider a general class of NB models with mean $\mu$ and variance function $\mu+\mu^p$, $-\infty < p < \infty$. The case $p=2$, provides the standard formulation of the NB model and corresponds to the common characterization of overdispersion relative to the Poisson variance function described in Section 1.3. The case $p=1$ provides the variance function of overdispersion for Poisson-like counts considered by Dean (1992). GENMOD, GLIMMIX, and CONTREG provide maximum likelihood estimators of $\mu$ and $\kappa$ for the case of $p=2$. CONTREG also provides the Negative-binomial model with linear variance function $\mu(1+\kappa\mu)$. The parameter $\kappa$ is known as the "dispersion parameter". Program 6 provides the programming statements for generating the probability functions of the Poisson and NB distributions with the same mean $\mu$, and associated variances $\mu$ and $\mu(1+\kappa\mu)$, respectively. In the example, the values of $\mu$ and $\kappa$ are 10 and 0.2. Thus, for both the variance is 10, and the variance of Negative-binomial is 30, which is three times the variance of the Poisson distribution. The plots of the two distributions are depicted in Output 6.1. A program for the generation of Negative-binomial is provided in Appendix 6.

Joe and Zhou (2005) proposed a Generalized Poisson distribution which provides an alternative to the NB model. The Generalized Poisson distribution has a scale parameter and reduces to the Poisson distribution when the scale parameter is zero. It has heavier tails than the NB distribution. For details and an example on Generalized Poisson distribution, we refer to the documentation of the GLIMMIX procedure.

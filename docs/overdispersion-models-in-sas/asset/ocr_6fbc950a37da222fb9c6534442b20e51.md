The Negative-binomial corresponds to a Poisson-gamma mixture model where the mixing distribution is the Gamma, the conjugate prior for the Poisson distribution. There is an analogy between this model and the Beta-binomial in the sense that the latter arises by a similar mechanism, by obtaining the unconditional distribution of a Binomial when the probability of success varies according to a Beta distribution.

Under the parameterization $\mu = \alphaeta$ and $\kappa = \frac{1}{\alpha}$, the unconditional probability function of $Y_j$ is given by

```
Pr(Y_j = y) = \frac{\Gamma(y+\frac{1}{\kappa})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{\kappa})\Gamma(y+1)} (\frac{\kappa\mu}{1+\kappa\mu})^y, y=0,1,... (6.6)
```

This parameterization yields the following well-known representation of the first two moments

```
E(Y_j) = \mu (6.7)
```

and

```
Var(Y_j) = \mu + \kappa\mu^2 = \mu(1+\kappa\mu) (6.8)
```

Cameron and Treiped (1986) consider a general class of NB models with mean as in (6.7) and variance function $\mu+\kappa\mu^p$, $-\infty < p < \infty$. The case $p=2$, which corresponds to a quadratic function on the mean $\mu$, provides the standard formulation (6.8) of the NB model and corresponds to the common characterization of overdispersion relative to the Poisson distribution variance function described in equation (1.6) of Section 1.3. The case $p=1$ provides the variance function of overdispersion for Poisson-like counts considered by Dean (1992). The GENMOD, GLIMMIX, and CONTREG provide maximum likelihood estimators of $\mu$ and $\kappa$ for the case of $p=2$. CONTREG also provides the Negative-binomial model with a linear variance function $\mu(1+\kappa)$. The parameter $\kappa$ is known as the "dispersion parameter". Program 6 provides the programming statements for generating the probability functions of the Poisson and NB distributions with the same mean $\mu$, and associated variances $\mu$ and $\mu(1+\kappa\mu)$, respectively. In the example, the values of $\mu$ and $\kappa$ are, 10 and 0.2. Thus, for both the variance is 10, and the variance of Negative-binomial is 30, which is three times the variance of the Poisson distribution. The plots of the two distributions are depicted in Output 6.1. A program for the generation of Negative-binomial numbers is provided in Appendix 6.

Joe and Zhou (2005) proposed a Generalized Poisson distribution which provides an alternative to the NB model. The Generalized Poisson distribution has a scale parameter and reduces to the Poisson distribution when the scale parameter is zero. It has heavier tails than the NB distribution. For details and an example on the Generalized Poisson distribution, we refer to the documentation of the GLIMMIX procedure.

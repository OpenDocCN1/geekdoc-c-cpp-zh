# 6.2 The Negative-binomial Distribution

The benchmark model to account for overdispersion on count responses is the Negative-binomial (NB) distribution. This distribution can be derived in several different ways and has been studied extensively over several centuries (Johnson, Kotz, and Kemp 1992, Section 3, Chapter 5). The most well-known interpretation of the NB is that it is the distribution of the number of failures before a pre-determined number of success occur in a sequence of Bernoulli trials. However, in the context of overdispersion, its ability to account for the lack of equidispersion (equality of the conditional mean and variance) in clustered data with count responses is of primary interest. We now present a derivation of the Negative-binomial distribution which more directly relates to overdispersion. We first assume that for a given cluster, the conditional distribution of the number of counts (on the rate parameter) is Poisson.

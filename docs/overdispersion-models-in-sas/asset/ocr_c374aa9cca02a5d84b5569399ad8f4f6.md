# 10.5 Beta-binomial and Random-clumped Binomial GLOMMs

In this section we present two GLOMMs, namely, the Beta-binomial and the Random-clumped Binomial with random effects. Recall the discussion on R-side and G-side models from Section 10.1. In a GLOMM, the overdispersion distributions are used in the R-side, while the random effects operate in the G-side. Intuitively, overdispersion models will try to capture the inherent lack of fit in the data relative to the Binomial (or Poisson in the case of counts data), while the random effects are used to capture the heterogeneity from subject to subject. Of course, in practice these two artifacts manifest as excess variance; therefore, the R-side and G-side effects end up competing with each other to grab parts of the excess variability observed in the data.

The data used for the illustrative example here correspond to a 2x2 cross-over design with sequences AB and BA. Within each period, subjects provide repeated correlated Bernoulli outcomes following either the BB or the RCB distribution. We first address the Beta-binomial GLOMM. Applications of this GLOMM might be found in baby care studies where mothers are asked to use a particular type of diaper on their babies for one whole week. For the next week, they switch over to a different diaper product. The outcome of interest is whether or not leakage was observed for each diaper use. For simplicity, we will assume no period effect.

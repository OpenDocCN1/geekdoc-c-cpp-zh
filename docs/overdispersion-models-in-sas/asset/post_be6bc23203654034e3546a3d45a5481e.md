| | CHOL | Specifies an unstructured variance-covariance matrix parameterized through its Cholesky root. This parameterization ensures the resulting variance-covariance matrix is at least positive semidefinite. If diagonal values are nonzero, it is positive definite. The GLIMMIX procedure constrains the diagonal elements of the Cholesky root to be positive. This guarantees a unique solution when the matrix is positive definite. In general, the Cholesky root parameterization is recommended over a completely unstructured covariance matrix (TYPE=UN). If A is a positive definite matrix, its Cholesky root is a lower triangular matrix C such that CCᵀ=A. The option CHOL(q) determines how many bands below the diagonal are modeled. Elements in the lower triangular portion of C in bands higher than "q" are set to zero. If you consider the resulting covariance matrix A=CC', then the order parameter has the effect of zeroing all off-diagonal elements that are at least "q" positions away from the diagonal. Bear in mind, the Cholesky decomposition is computationally slightly more involved. | | |

## 9.5 An Example with Binomial/Binary responses

In this section, we will illustrate the use of GEE for responses that are either Binomial or Binary (Bernoulli) outcomes, using the Teratology example about ossification of the phalanges discussed in Section 4.9. Recall that the purpose of the experiment was to investigate the synergistic effect of phenytoin (PHT) and trichlorophene (TCPO), and that the presence or absence of ossification at the phalanges was considered as a 2×2 factorial, with PHT and TCPO as the two factors.

The levels of PHT were 60 mg/kg and 0 mg/kg, and the levels of TCPO were 100 mg/kg and 0 mg/kg. The ossification data can be found in Table 4.6. We first obtain GEE estimates using an independent working correlation matrix. Program 9.2 provides the programming statements for fitting a GEE model to the Teratology data using a log link and an independent working correlation matrix. In the DATA step, data are rearranged so that we use the CLASS statement and specify a factorial model with interaction. The REPEATED statement requests the GEE estimates and passes on the litter identification to SAS. Excerpts of the results are provided in Output 9.2.

### Program 9.2: Fitting a GEE Model with Independent Correlation Matrix to the Ossification Data

```sas
/* Ossification Example from Morel and Neelheer (1997), Statistics in Medicine, 16, 284-2853 */
data ossification;
length tx $;
input n @@;
PHT = 1;
TCPO = 1;
do i=1 to n;
  input tx @@;
if tx = 'PHT' then PHT = 0;
if tx = 'TCPO' then TCPO = 0;
if tx = 'PHT+TCPO' then do
  PHT = 0;
  TCPO = 0;
end;
output;
end;
drop n;
datalines;
Control 18 8 9 9 7 9 0 5 5 3 8 9 10 5 8 5 1 6 0 5
8 8 9 10 5 4 7 9 10 6 6 1 9 8 9 6 7 7
Control 17 8 8 9 5 5 4 7 10 10 6 8 8 2 7 7
2 5 5 2 8 8 1 2 7 7
PHT 19 1 9 4 9 7 4 4 7 0 0 8 1 7 7 2 8 8
TCPO 16 0 5 7 10 4 4 8 14 2 8 0 6 9 3 2 9 7 1 9 10 8 6
PHT+TCPO 11 2 0 7 1 8 7 8 0 10 4 0 6 7 7 6
ods html;
title "***Ossification Data -- GEE using GENMOD with Independent Working Correlation Matrix***";
proc genmod data=ossification;
class litters tcpo pht / param=ref;
model m = tcpo*pht / dist=binomial link=logit;
repeated subject=litters / type=ind;
run;
ods close;
```

To fit a GEE model with an exchangeable correlation structure, the binomial-type of responses need be expanded into strings of zeros and ones. The programming statements below show this conversion of the responses and the implementation of the GENMOD procedure. Selected results are shown in the continuation of Output 9.2. Note that the beta estimates are not identical to obtained under the GEE with the independent working correlation matrix. The estimate of the exchangeability parameter turned out to be 0.295.

### Program 9.2: Continuation

```sas
data ossification_b;
```

## 7.12 Modeling the Coefficient of Agreement via the Dirichlet-multinomial and Random-clumped Multinomial Distributions

Cohen’s Kappa coefficient (Cohen, 1960) is a statistical measure of inter-rater agreement. It measures the agreement between two raters (or judges) who rate (or classify) n subjects (or items) into C mutually categories. Cohen’s Kappa is also used in the so-called calibration studies, where two rate n subjects regarding some variable of interest—one representing the “gold standard” and the other acting as the “novice.” The aim of these studies is to qualify the trainee by showing that the Kappa coefficient is “large enough” so that the trainee is in agreement with the trainer.

Let $\pi_{ij}$ represent the joint probability that a subject is classified into the i-th category by the first rater and the j-th category by the second rater, $i=1,2,...,C, j=1,2,...,C$. Then,

$\pi_0 = \sum \pi_{ij}$ represents the probability that the two graders agree on the classification of a subject.

Under the assumption that the two graders judge independently of each other, the probability of agreement by chance alone is $\pi_c = \sum_{i=1}^C \pi_{*i}$ where $\pi_{*i} = \sum_{j=1}^C \pi_{ij}$ and $\pi_{*j} = \sum_{i=1}^C \pi_{ij}$.

Kappa coefficient ($\kappa$), which compares the degree of observed agreement to the agreement one would expect purely out of chance, is defined as

$$\kappa = \frac{\pi_0 - \pi_c}{1 - \pi_c} (7.39)$$

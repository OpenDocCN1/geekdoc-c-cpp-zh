An important aspect of these models is the clear connections with GLMs. The likelihood models discussed in this chapter allow for link functions (logit, probit, etc) in the probability of success and the overdispersion parameter. In principle, two linear predictors are possible: the customary one with the mean of the model, and the one associated with the overdispersion parameter. The estimation methods employed here are highly similar to those used in GLMs. The maximum likelihood equations of these models are highly non-linear in the parameters; therefore, we will have to rely on iterative methods for computing the MLEs. Although the simplifications discussed in equations (2.23) and (2.24) of Chapter 2 for distributions in the exponential family with canonical links is not known to hold for the overdispersed models discussed in this chapter, both estimation methods, the Newton-Raphson and Fisher Scoring methods, can be implemented for the MLE of the parameters of the model.

# 4.2 The Beta-binomial distribution

Modeling overdispersion with binomial responses can be approached hierarchically by setting a prior on the probability of success of the Binomial, and then examining the variability in the count marginally. Let T represent the total of successes in a cluster of size m. It is assumed that T |P ~ Binomial(P,m). The probability of success P, 0 < P < 1, be distributed according to an arbitrary distribution with density f (P) (or probability function if the distribution of P is discrete) in the (0,1) interval such that

> E_f(P) = π (4.1)

and

> V_f(P) = σ² (4.2)

Since P² < P for any P between zero and one, it follows that E_f(P²) < E_f(P). Then σ² + π² < π, which implies σ² < π(1-π). Thus, there exists a real number ρ², 0 < ρ² < 1 such that σ² = ρ²π(1-π). Then, (4.2) can be written as

> V_f(P) = ρ²π(1-π). (4.3)

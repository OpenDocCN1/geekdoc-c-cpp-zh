Efron explained that a genuine random sampling was infeasible, the subjects may have been sampled in clumps. Efron states that

> "Clumped sampling is just one of possible causes of overdispersion, a habitual source of concern to users of the binomial and Poisson models."

We use the toxoplasmosis data in Table 3.2 to illustrate two methods for fitting Quasi-likelihood models. The first method, which consists of inflating the covariance matrix of the MLEs by a suitable constant, is available in both the GENMOD and GLIMMIX procedures. The second method linearizes the GLM and an estimation technique known as Pseudo-likelihood (PL). The PL method is available only in the GLIMMIX procedure. We will discuss the PL method in more detail later in Chapter 9.

Program 3.2 presents programming statements for fitting a Quasi-likelihood regression model by first obtaining the MLEs and inflating the covariance matrix by a moment estimator of the scale parameter $\phi$, as indicated in (3.6). We first compute the $z_i$, the standardized values of frankly. The statement "random_residual" produces the previously discussed Quasi-likelihood procedure. The results are provided in the Output 3.2. The estimated value of the scale parameter, the Pearson's Chi-square statistic, normalized by its degrees of freedom, is $\phi=1.94$. The results of this analysis indicates that the linear and cubic terms of the standardized rainfall are statistically significant (P<0.05). The option "s" in the model statement stands for "solution" and provides the parameter estimates.

# Chapter 3 Quasi-likelihood Functions 67

In a clinical study of osteoporotic patients at high risk for vertebral fractures, subjects were randomly assigned to either a group receiving (bisphosphonate) active therapy (n1 = 28) or to a placebo group (n2 = 25). The number of vertebral fractures (new or worsening) was measured during a period of time that lasted approximately 14 and 39 months. The data are presented in 3.6. This has has been discussed in Morel and Neerlich (2010).

Let Yj and Zj represent the number of vertebral fractures and the years of follow-up for the j-th subject in the i-th treatment group, j = 1, 2,...,n, i = 1. Conditioning on Zi, E(Yj) = μj/Zj,

Var(Yj) = μj/Zj², ϕ > 1, and link ln(μj/Zj) = α + τ, where α is an overall mean and τ

the effect of the i-th treatment group. (duration of follow-up) is an offset, Zi > 0. The link function can be written as ln(μj) = α + τ + ln(Zi). Including the offset variable in the model leads to estimation of rate of vertebral fractures normalized by the number of years of follow up. In this example we will use the GENMOD. The estimate of the scale parameter will be based on Pearson’s Chi-square statistic. The programming statements are provided in Program 3.5. There are three estimates. They provide the logarithm of the rates of the two groups and their difference, along with the associated ratios and rate ratios. The results are reported in Program 3.5. The estimate of the scale parameter turns out to be 1.7311, indicating the presence of dispersion.

# 3.5 A Carcinogenesis Pre-clinical Experiment

The aim of this example is to provide a simple application of an overdispersed Poisson model. Similar to Binomial models, overdispersed Poisson models are used to accommodate higher variability in the data permitted by the Poisson model. In a clinical experiment analyzed by Gail (1980) and by Lawless (1987), a total of 76 female rats were injected with a carcinogen for mammary cancer. Then, all animals were given Retinol Acetate to prevent mammary cancer for 60 days. After this phase, the 48 animals that remained tumor-free were randomly assigned to continue the Retinoid prophylaxis (n=23) or Control (n=25). Rats were then pelted for tumors twice weekly, and observations ended 182 days after the initial carcinogen injections. The main objective of analysis was to assess the difference in the development of tumors between the Retinoid and Control groups. The times of occurrences to mammary tumors as well as the number of tumors during the 182-day phase can be found in Gail et al. (1980). Table 3.4 contains only the number of tumors of the 48 rats.

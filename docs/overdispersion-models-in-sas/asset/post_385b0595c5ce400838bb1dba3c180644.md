The logarithm of the ratio R, as in equation (9.14), is 0.1998 with an estimated standard error of R = 0.0247, as in equation (9.15). If the $y_i$'s are zero-one responses (Bernoulli outcomes) and the $z_i$'s are all identical to one, the estimator in equation (9.14) reduces to the logarithm of a proportion (or logarithm of a risk), that is,

$$\hat{\beta}_i = \ln(\hat{P}_i), \quad (9.17)$$

where $\hat{P}_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} y_{ij}}{n_i}$, by equation (9.15), the estimated variance of (9.17) is

$$\hat{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i) = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (y_{ij}-\hat{P}_i)^2}{n_i\hat{P}_i^2} + \frac{(1-\hat{P}_i)}{n_i\hat{P}_i}, \quad i=1, 2, \dots, k. \quad (9.18)$$

Then, the estimated logarithm of the relative risk based on two independent groups

$$\hat{\beta}_i - \hat{\beta}_j = \ln\left(\frac{\hat{P}_i}{\hat{P}_j}\right), \quad (9.19)$$

The estimated variance of (9.19) reduces to the familiar expression

$$\hat{Var}(\hat{\beta}_i - \hat{\beta}_j) = Var(\ln(\frac{\hat{P}_i}{\hat{P}_j})) = \frac{(1-\hat{P}_i)}{n_i\hat{P}_i} + \frac{(1-\hat{P}_j)}{n_j\hat{P}_j}. \quad (9.20)$$

The case of a rate ratio, where the $y_{ij}$ are zero-one responses representing the presence or absence of a certain disease (or adverse event) and the $z_i$ are the time to the event, is derived easily from equations (9.14) and (9.15). To illustrate the estimation of Relative Risk and Risk Ratio, consider the following illustrative example from Morel and Neerchal (2008). Table 9.4 shows a hypothetical dataset from a clinical trial with two treatment groups coded as "1" for Active and "2" for Placebo, where the rate ratio is four. Subjects were randomized to each treatment group. Subjects were followed for 52 weeks, and the occurrence of a Adverse Event (AE) and the dropout at risk in weeks to the AE were recorded. Note that subjects 1, 2, 3, 11 and 12 dropouts and the occurrence of the AE was recorded as zero. On the other hand, subjects 4 and 13 through 17 completed the study without experiencing the AE under consideration. Their time to AE is censored and was recorded as simply as 52 weeks. The remaining subjects experienced the AE under consideration at some time during the study.

The programming statements for computing the previous results for the Relative Risk and the Rate Ratio are provided in Program 9.5. Output 9.5 depicts the main results obtained using the GENMOD procedure.

## Program 9.3: Relative Risk and Rate Ratio for Binary Responses

```
/*
Relative Risk and Rate Ratio
Hypothetical Data from a Clinical Trial with Rate Ratio = 4
Morel and Neerchal (2008)
*/
data adverse_events;
    input y treat time;
    offs = log(time);
    id = _n;
datalines:
    1 4.6
    1 10.0
    1 14.5
    1 52.0
    1 7.2
    1 16.3
    1 17.6
    1 23.8
    1 24.5
    1 29.5
    2 6.1
    2 32.5
    2 52.0
    2 52.0
    2 52.0
    2 52.0
    2 31.0
    2 32.0
    2 38.4

ods html;
title "Relative Risk Results";
proc genmod data=adverse_events;
class id treat;
model y = dist=poisson link=log;
repeated subject=id / type=ind;
lsmeans treat;
estimate "Active Vs Placebo" treat 1 -1;
run;

title "Rate Ratio Results";
proc genmod data=adverse_events;
class id treat;
model y = dist=poisson link=log offset=offs;
repeated subject=id / type=ind;
lsmeans treat;
estimate "Active Vs Placebo" treat 1 -1;
run;
ods close;
```

The logarithm of the Relative Risk as in interval (9,19) is 0.6931. Its estimated standard error, obtained from (9,20), is 0.5477. An approximate 95% confidence interval is (-0.3804, 1.7667). The estimated Relative Risk is exp [0.6931]=2.0 and an approximate 95% confidence interval is (0.638, 5.8513). If the aim of the analysis is to estimate the Rate Ratio not the Relative Risk, the logarithm of time to AE is an offset variable. In this case, the main results are as follows: the logarithm of the estimated incidence rates for the Active and Placebo groups are -3.5066 and -4.8929, respectively (9.14). Using equation (9,15), their associated standard errors obtained from the sandwich variance estimate are 0.3332 and 0.5266, respectively. The estimated rates for the Active and Placebo groups are 0.0300 and 0.0075, respectively. In the Placebo group, the expected occurrence of 52(0.0075)=0.39 AE per subject year. On the other hand, the rate is higher in the Active group, which is exp(0.0300)=1.03 AE per subject year. The estimated Rate Ratio is 4.00. An approximate 95% confidence interval is (1.1794,35.863). Note that the above approach is a computational trick which gives the correct relative risk and rate ratio with their appropriate standard errors, and therefore the other parts of the output may not be relevant. An alternative way of obtaining the results associated with the relative risk is the following:

title "Relative Risk Results";nproc genmod data=average_events descending;

estimate 'Active Vs Placebo' treat 1 -1;
run;

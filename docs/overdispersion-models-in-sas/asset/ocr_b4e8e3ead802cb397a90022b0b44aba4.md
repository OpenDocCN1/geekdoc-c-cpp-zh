In order to obtain $\hat{eta}$, the MLE of $\beta$, one needs to solve the estimating equations

```
$$\frac{\partial L(\eta;\phi;\mathbf{y})}{\partial \beta} = \mathbf{0}.$\quad (2.19)$
```

These equations are known as the maximum likelihood score equations. More often than not, the solution of (2.19) does not have an explicit form and is thus solved iteratively. Several numerical methods are available. The iterative procedure for the Newton-Raphson Method is given by

```
$$\hat{\beta}_{(S+1)} = \hat{\beta}_{(S)} + {\hat{H}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)})\}^{-1}\mathbf{u}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)}).\quad (2.20)$
```

and the widely used Fisher Scoring Method is implemented as follows

```
$$\hat{\beta}_{(S+1)} = \hat{\beta}_{(S)} + {\hat{I}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)})\}^{-1}\mathbf{u}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)}).\quad (2.21)$
```

where $\mathbf{u}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)})$ is the k-dimensional vector of first derivatives of the log-likelihood function (2.16) evaluated at $\hat{\beta}_{(S)}$, $\hat{H}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)})$, is the $k\times k$ Hessian matrix (2.17) evaluated at $\beta=\hat{\beta}_{(S)}$, and $\hat{I}(\hat{\beta}_{(S)})$ is the $k\times k$ Fisher information matrix (2.18) evaluated at $\beta=\hat{\beta}_{(S)}$. The subscript "$s$" refers to the step or iteration.

The preceding discussion applies to the computation of MLE in general. In the case of the exponential family, further simplifications due to the relationship between the derivatives of the log-likelihood function and the first moments given in equations (1.9) and (1.10). Nelder and Wedderburn (1972) and Wedderburn (1974) showed that the solution of the maximum likelihood equations (2.19) is equivalent to an iterative weighted least-squares procedure. ```

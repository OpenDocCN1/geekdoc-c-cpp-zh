## 4.9 A Teratology Example about Ossification on the Phalanges

Consider the ossification data of Table 4.6, which has been previously analyzed by Hartsfield et al. (1990), and Neerchel (1997). When we introduced the concept of overdispersion, come from a two-way design with 81 pregnant phenytoin (PHT) and trichloropropene oxide (TCCO). The purpose of the experiment was to investigate the synergistic effect of phenytoin and TCCO. The presence or absence of ossification on the phalanges is considered a measure of the teratogenic effect of the substances under study. In some ossification did not occur at all; in others the ossification was almost complete. The middle third phalanges were selected to provide about 20% chance of ossification in the control groups. In the experiment each pregnant mouse received dosages of water, a sham control group (Sham) three treated groups received daily, by gastric gavages, 60 mg/kg of phenytoin (PHT), 100 mg/kg of trichloropropene oxide (TCCO), or 60 mg/kg phenytoin and TCCO. For illustrative purposes, the controls were combined and referred to as combined controls. We present here the analysis of the middle third phalanges. The fetus then be seen as a 2 x 2 factor, with PHT and TCCO as the two factors. The levels of PHT are 60 mg/kg and 60 mg/kg, and the levels of TCCO are 100 mg/kg and 0 mg/kg. On day 18 of gestation, features were recovered, stained, and cleaned. Then, by visual inspection, the presence or absence of ossification was determined for the different joints of the right and left forceps. The data on the middle third phalanges are shown in Table 4.6. The example discussed in Section 1.1 corresponds to the 11 mice that were treated with both substances PHT and TCCO.

Let $\pi_{tp}$, represent the unconditional probability of omission on the left middle third phalanx of any fetus in the (t,p)-treatment group of the 2x2 factorial experiment, where

$t = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if TCPO is absent} \\ 1 & \text{if TCPO is present} \end{cases} \text{ and } p = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if PHT is absent} \\ 1 & \text{if PHT is present} \end{cases}$

For the $j$-litter on the $(t$

$T_{tp} \sim \text{Binomial}(\pi_{tp},m_{tp})$
$T_{tp} \sim \text{Beta-binomial}(\pi_{tp},\rho_{tp},m_{tp})$
$T_{tp} \sim \text{Random-clumped}(\pi_{tp},\rho_{tp},m_{tp})$ (43)

The case where $T_{tp}$ are believed to be binomially distributed ignores the litter effect and assumes litters are inside. To explain possible differences in $\pi_{tp}$ among the treatment groups, we will consider the following logit $\ln\left(\frac{\pi_{tp}}{1-\pi_{tp}}\right) = \beta_0+\beta_1 I(t=1)+\beta_2 I(p=1)+\beta_3 I(t=1,p=1)$, (34)

where $I(\cdot)$ represents an indicator function. The link function in (34) provides estimates of the teratogenic effects of the substances used in the experiment and possibly synergistic effect. It is common with teratological data to have varying degrees of intra-litter correlation. This problem has been properly addressed by Kupper et al. (1986) and Williams (1988). To account for possible different-litter effects, we will consider the following logit $\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{tp}}{1-\rho_{tp}}\right) = \alpha_0+\alpha_1 I(t=1)+\alpha_2 I(p=1)+\alpha_3 I(t=1,p=1)$, (35)

The first link function in (35) assumes a common intra-litter correlation, i.e., $\rho \equiv \frac{\exp(\alpha)}{1+\exp(\alpha)}$. The other link function expresses the logs of $\rho$'s as the main effects and its interaction (full model). Note that the overdispersed models defined in (33), (34) and (35) fall into the category of Overdispersion Models previously defined in (31) and (32). We will now consider maximum estimation under the three distributions in (31) and (32) with three functions $\ln\left(\frac{\pi_{tp}}{1-\pi_{tp}}\right) = \beta_0+\beta_1 I(t=1)+\beta_2 I(p=1)+\beta_3 I(t=1,p=1)$ and $\ln\left(\frac{\rho_{tp}}{1-\rho_{tp}}\right) = \alpha_0$ (36)

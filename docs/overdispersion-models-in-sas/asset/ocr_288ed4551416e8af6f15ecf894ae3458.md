The maximum likelihood results are reported in Table 4.2. The five models provide similar estimates of the probability $\pi$. In fact, after rounding up to three decimals, the estimates are essentially the same, 0.581. It is common to get similar estimates for the mean parameter under both Binomial and overdispersion models. In this example, the Beta-Binomial, Random-clumped Binomial, and Generalized Binomial provide basically identical results for estimates of $\hat{\pi}$ and $ho$ with corresponding standard errors. The variance estimates of $\hat{\pi}$ under these three models are approximately 1.7 times the estimate obtained by using the Binomial distribution. This factor turns out to be 1.11 under the ZIB model. The Binomial distribution fails to recognize the intrinsic clustering of the data. It is also interesting to note that the maximum value of the likelihood under are almost identical. Note that none of these distributions are nested in the other, and their mathematical formulas do not lend themselves to any specific comparison of this type. The estimates under the ZIB seem to be slightly different.

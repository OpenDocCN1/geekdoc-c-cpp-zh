As expected, the beta estimates under both methods are identical: $\hat{eta}_1 = 1.0641$. The OLS estimator of $\sigma^2$ is $\hat{\sigma}^2 = 371.91$, and the MLE $\hat{\sigma}^2 = 322.33$. Note that $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{15-2}{15}\hat{\sigma}^2$. Under the MLE method, the associated t-statistic for testing the significance of the slope is 2.02. This can be viewed as a realization of a t-distribution with 13 degrees of freedom, under the null hypothesis that the true slope is zero. Its P-value is 0.0641. This result is not significant at the 5% level. On the other hand, the MLE method gives a t-value 2.17 with an P-value of 0.0488. One might conclude this result is significant at the 5% level. This example illustrates how these estimates OLS and MLE, might produce different inferences in some circumstances—if the 5% level is strictly enforced. Here, the use of the MLE method would be useful if one had enough evidence to believe that the distribution of errors is Normal. The `plots=residuals` option in the PROCIMMIX statement produces the diagnostic plots depicted in Plot 2.1. In the upper plot, the Residual Predictor corresponds to $\hat{\epsilon}_i$, versus $y_i$. The plots indicate that the linear regression fit is adequate and assumption of normality is defensible. We may then proceed with the inference based on the MLE.

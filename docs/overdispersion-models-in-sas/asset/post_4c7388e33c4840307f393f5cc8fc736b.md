The results of the omnibus GOF test under the Beta-binomial and Random-clumped Binomial distributions are reported in Table 5.4. It ended with 10 intervals around the Random-clumped Binomial model and 9 intervals, and we combined the last two under the Random-clumped Binomial model so that Chi-square tests had the same degrees of freedom. The P-values of the GOF test under the Beta-binomial and Random-clumped Binomial distributions are 0.0014 and 0.2205. These tests are based on six degrees of freedom (number of intervals minus one, minus the number of parameters being estimated). The Random-clumped Binomial distribution fits the data well, while the Beta-binomial does not. The P-values were also estimated by bootstrapping the data parametrically with 20,000 replications. They turned out to be 0.0012 and 0.2124, those obtained by using the Chi-square distribution with six degrees of freedom. The QQ-plots 5.1 are for the residuals obtained observed minus expected frequencies. Sutradhar (2001) and Sutradhar et al. (2008) provided the theoretical properties of the GOF test given in the previous section. It is shown that the GOF test enjoys asymptotic properties similar to those of the usual Pearson's GOF. In particular, it is shown that the Chi-square distribution provides an adequate approximation to the null distribution. As will be elaborated in the next section, the appropriate degrees of freedom the approximating Chi-square distribution need some adjustment, especially when there are a large number of parameters to be estimated. A practical approach to this issue is to use adaptive bootstrapping to obtain the p-value. We will present the results of a simulation which will illustrate this approach and also demonstrate that the test statistic is quite powerful in distinguishing between the Beta-binomial and Random-clumped Binomial distributions.

The two simulations discussed here were first presented in Sutradhar et al. (2008). They are designed to investigate the properties of the omnibus GOF test discussed in the previous section. Two questions were addressed in their simulations, namely, the distributional aspect of the GOF test, and its characteristics as measured via the Type I error rates and power. We provide in this section some results with the latter. In the simulation, three values of $\pi = 0.1, 0.3, 0.5$ were considered along the three values of $\rho = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7$. These generating of the cluster sizes were considered, namely, the cluster size $m = 15$; randomly choosing the size of a cluster from a set $\{10, 15, 20\}$ with Prob(cluster size 10) = 0.40, Prob(cluster size 15) = 0.20, Prob(cluster size 20) = 0.40; and finally, generating the cluster sizes from a Poisson model where $m = Y+1$. For each combination of $\pi$ and $\rho$, and for each scheme of selecting the cluster, a total of 2000 data sets were generated; each data set of size $n=400$. In Table 5 we report the results associated with the Beta-binomial and Random-clumped Binomial distributions. The estimated Type error rates are given for $\alpha=0.1$ when the cluster sizes are randomly generated. We also estimated the power for testing $H_0$: Beta-binomial versus $H_1$: Random-clumped Binomial, and $H_2$: Random-clumped Binomial versus $H_1$: Beta-binomial. The degrees of freedom for the Pearson's Chi-square statistic was $R=3$, where $R$ was the number of non-overlapping intervals covering the $[0,1]$ interval. The values of $\rho$ were $\rho=0.1$.

## 5.4 Extension of the GOF When Covariates Are Present: The Hiroshima Chromosome Aberration Example

As pointed out in Section 4.8, in the presence of cluster-specific covariates, both the probability of success $\pi$ and the overdispersion parameter $\rho$ can potentially differ from cluster to cluster. To model this situation we suggested the use of two link functions, referred to GLM, for the probability of success and one for the overdispersion parameter. The link functions, as defined in equations (4.31) and (4.32),

$g(\pi_i) = x_i\beta = \sum_{j} x_{ij}\beta_j$ and $f(\rho_i) = z_i\alpha = \sum_{j} z_{ij}\alpha_j$.

The GOF test in equation (5.4) can be accommodated to account not only for varying cluster sizes, but also for varying probability of success and degrees of overdispersion.

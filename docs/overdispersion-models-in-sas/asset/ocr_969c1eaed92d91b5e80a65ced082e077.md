# 4.9 A Teratology Example about Ossification on the Phalanges

Consider the ossification data of Table 4.6, which has been previously analyzed by Hartsfield et al. (1990), and Neerchel (1997). When we introduced the concept of overdispersion, come from a two-way design with 81 pregnant phenytoin (PHT) and trichloropropene oxide (TCCO). The purpose of the experiment was to investigate the synergistic effect of phenytoin and TCCO. The presence or absence of ossification on the phalanges is considered a measure of the teratogenic effect of the substances under study. In some ossification did not occur at all; in others the ossification was almost complete. The middle third phalanges were selected to provide about 20% chance of ossification in the control groups. In the experiment each pregnant mouse received dosages of water, a sham control group (Sham) three treated groups received daily, by gastric gavages, 60 mg/kg of phenytoin (PHT), 100 mg/kg of trichloropropene oxide (TCCO), or 60 mg/kg phenytoin and TCCO. For illustrative purposes, the controls were combined and referred to as combined controls. We present here the analysis of the middle third phalanges. The fetus then be seen as a 2 x 2 factor, with PHT and TCCO as the two factors. The levels of PHT are 60 mg/kg and 60 mg/kg, and the levels of TCCO are 100 mg/kg and 0 mg/kg. On day 18 of gestation, fetures were recovered, stained, and cleaned. Then, by visual inspection, the presence or absence of ossification was determined for the different joints of the right and left forceps. The data on the middle third phalanges are shown in Table 4.6. The example discussed in Section 1.1 corresponds to the 11 mice that were treated with both substances PHT and TCCO.

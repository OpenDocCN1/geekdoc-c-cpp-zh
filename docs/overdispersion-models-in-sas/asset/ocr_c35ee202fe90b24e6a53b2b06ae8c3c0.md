# 10.4 Example Multinomial Model with Random Effects

In this section we will demonstrate the fitting of a Multinomial model with random effects using the shoulder pain study example of Section 9.9. In the study, there were 22 subjects in the active group (with abdominal suction) and 19 subjects in the control group (without abdominal suction). Recall that after laparoscopic surgery, patients were asked to rate their pain on a visual analog scale, morning and afternoon, for three days after the operation (for a total of six different times). The response was ordinal with a five category group, where the value “1” indicated “low pain,” and “5” indicated “high pain.” There were covariates, namely Gender, Age, and Time, with all time being subject-specific. Let $y_{ij}$ represent the response of the j-th subject in the i-th treatment group at the i-th time, $i=1, 2, j=1, 2, \dots,$ ($n_i=22, n_j=19, t=1, 2, \dots, 6$), with

```
$E(y_{ij}) = \frac{\pi_{1ij}}{\pi_{1ij} + \pi_{2ij} + \pi_{3ij} + \pi_{4ij}}$
```

Note that a subject with his/her repeated measures constitutes a cluster. We consider the following Logit GLMM as

$$\ln(\pi_{1ij} / (\pi_{2ij} + \pi_{3ij} + \pi_{4ij})) = \alpha_1 + \beta_1X_{ij} + \beta_2X_{2ij} + \beta_3X_{3ij} + \beta_4t + u_{ij}$$
$\$\ln(\pi_{2ij} / (\pi_{1ij} + \pi_{3ij} + \pi_{4ij})) = \alpha_2 + \beta_2X_{ij} + \beta_2X_{2ij} + \beta_3X_{3ij} + \beta_4t + u_{ij}$$
$\$\ln(\pi_{3ij} / (\pi_{1ij} + \pi_{2ij} + \pi_{4ij})) = \alpha_3 + \beta_1X_{ij} + \beta_2X_{2ij} + \beta_3X_{3ij} + \beta_4t + u_{ij}$$
$\$\ln(\pi_{4ij} / (\pi_{1ij} + \pi_{2ij} + \pi_{3ij})) = \alpha_4 + \beta_1X_{ij} + \beta_2X_{2ij} + \beta_3X_{3ij} + \beta_4t + u_{ij}$ (10.14)

where the $u_{ij}$ are independent and identically distributed $N(0, \sigma^2)$, $X_{ij}$ and $X_{12}$ are classification variables, respectively, representing the treatment group and the gender; $X_{13}$ represents the subject's age, and “t” is the linear value of Time. Conditionally on the random effect $u_{ij}$, the pain scores in the j-th treatment are considered independent. The random effect in equation (10.4) is the one known as “random intercepts.” It is possible to have a more general structure $Z_{ij}$, in place of $u_{ij}$. We refer to the chapter in Kuo and Kuo (2001) for a discussion on this and for example of a Generalized GLMM effect on consumer preference. We will fit the GLMM using the GLMIX procedure. The programming statements are shown in Program 10.3. We exemplify the use of GLMIX by considering the classical empirical covariance matrix of the fixed effect, and by additionally requesting the small sample bias correction of Morel, Bokossa, and Neeraj (2003). This approach is invoked with the PROC option “empirical=mbn”. The three models (GLMM, GLMM with empirical variance estimates, and GLMM with empirical variance and small sample bias variance correction) were all fit using the Maximum Likelihood method based on Adaptive Quadrature. The shoulder pain study data can be found in Program 9.6 of 9.9.

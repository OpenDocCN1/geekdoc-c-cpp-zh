It is important that the GENMOD procedure does not handle random effects of any type. Terms as G-side or R-side are not present in the theoretical discussion of the GENMOD procedure ("Details: GENMOD Procedure" of the SAS Documentation). Readers may recall that the GENMOD procedure is essentially devoted to regression in the exponential family. It can also accommodate the Zero-inflated Poisson and the Zero-inflated Negative-binomial models, neither of which is a member of the exponential family. In GENMOD, repeated measurements can be analyzed in conjunction with the GEE methodology following the original guidelines in Liang and Zeger (1986), and Zeger and Liang (1986). The distinction, as we will see later, between GENMOD and GLIMMIX is more evident in the two procedures practically implement using the GEE methodology. The procedure relies on Quasi-likelihood and moment estimation, where the latter relies upon the Pseudo Likelihood estimation based on linearization. However from some minor variations, both procedures basically incurred in the computation of the "sandwich" estimator.

The GEE methodology is a two-step estimation process. To begin, with a type of Quasi-likelihood model is stated when one assumes correlation or variance structure on the repeated measurements. The parameters of this initial model (working model) are estimated. Once the working model is fit, the "robust," "empirical," or "sandwich" variance-covariance matrix is then estimated. To understand how the "sandwich" variance arises, consider for a moment the set-up of Generalized Linear Models discussed in Chapter 2. We know the beta estimates are estimated iteratively around $eta$ in equation (2.22), that is,

```
$$\hat{eta}_{(s+1)} = \hat{eta}_{(s)} + (\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{D})^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu})$
```

Then, the Taylor expansion of $\hat{eta}$ around $\beta$ yields

```
$$\hat{eta}-\beta \triangleq (\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{D})^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu})$
```

The variance-covariance matrix of $\hat{eta}$ from the Taylor's expansion, would be

```
$Var(\hat{eta}-\beta) \triangleq Var(\hat{eta}) \triangleq (\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{D})^{-1}\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu})(\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{D})^{-1}$.
```

If we define $\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{D}$ by $\mathbf{H}$, and $\mathbf{D}^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\mathbf{y}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^{\top}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\mathbf{D}$ by $\mathbf{G}$, the above variance-covariance matrix can be written as

```
$Var(\hat{eta}) \triangleq \mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{H}^{-1}$.
```

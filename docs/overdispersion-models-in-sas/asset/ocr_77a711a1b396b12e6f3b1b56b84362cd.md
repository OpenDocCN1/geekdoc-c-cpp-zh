# 3.6 An Osteopor Example of Patients at High Risk for Vertebral Fractures

We now turn to Quasi-likelihood estimation in Poisson models using offsets. An offset is technically defined as the linear predictor with a coefficient of one. Offsets are useful especially in Poisson models since they normalize the mean. A typical example of offset is "duration of exposure". Occurrences of counts of a certain event typically depend on duration of exposure: the longer the exposure, the more likely we observe larger counts. In this example we assume the form of overdispersion defined as $E(Y)=\mu$ and $Var(Y)=\mu\Phi, \Phi > 1$. Note that this variance structure can be written as

$Var(Y)=\mu(1+\kappa), \kappa>0$, which is one form of overdispersion for the Poisson model discussed in Dean (1992).

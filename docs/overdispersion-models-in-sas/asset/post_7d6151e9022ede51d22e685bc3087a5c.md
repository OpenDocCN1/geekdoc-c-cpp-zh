## 9.8 The GEE Methodology for Multinomial Responses

Consider a situation where n are clusters. As pointed out earlier chapters, these clusters can be, for example, households, where members of a household are the elemental units; pregnant rates, which the fetuses within a single rate are the elemental units; where members of a group are the elemental units; and so on. In a longitudinal study involving repeated measures, each individual constitutes a cluster, and the repeated measurements are the elemental units. Let $m_j$ represent the number of elemental units in the $j^{th}$ cluster, $j=1,...,m_j$. Let $y_j$ denote a $(d+1)$-dimensional column vectors, and $y_j^*$. The vector $y_j^*$ consists entirely of zeros except for position $s$, which will contain one of the $l$-th unit in the $j$-th cluster falls in the $s$-th category. Let $\pi_j^* = (\pi_{js1}^*, \dots, \pi_{jsd})$ denote the expected value of $y_j^*$. Then $0 < \pi_{js} < 1$ and $\sum_{s=1}^{d+1}\pi_{js} = 1$. Due to this linear dependency the last category of $y_j^*$ will be dropped. Let $y_j$ and $\pi_j$, represent the vectors $y_j^*$ and $\pi_j^*$ without the last category. Associated with each $y_j$ is a $k$-dimensional row vector of explanatory variables $\mathbf{x}_j$ such that the mean $\pi_j$ depends on a $r$-dimensional vector of unknown parameters $\theta$ and the vector of explanatory variables $\mathbf{x}_j$ through a smooth link function $g(\theta,\mathbf{x}_j)$ such that $\pi_j = g^{-1}(\theta,\mathbf{x}_j) = \begin{pmatrix} g^{-1}(\theta,\mathbf{x}_{j1}) \\ \vdots \\ g^{-1}(\theta,\mathbf{x}_{jd}) \end{pmatrix}$ (9.21).

Examples of link functions (9.21) were given in Section 7.3. The GEE methodology can now be implemented using the mean-structure above and a variance-covariance structure $\mathbf{V}_j$ for the observations $y_{ji}$, $l=1,2,...,m_j$, where $\mathbf{V}_j = \begin{pmatrix} y_{j1} \\ y_{j2} \\ \vdots \\ y_{j,m_j} \end{pmatrix}$ (9.22).

Let $\mathbf{D}(0,\mathbf{x}_j)=\mathbf{D}_j$ represent the $d \times r$ matrix of first partial derivatives of $\mathbf{g}(0,\mathbf{x}_j)$ with respect to $\theta_0$, and let $\mathbf{D}_j = (\mathbf{D}_{j,1}, \mathbf{D}_{j,2}, ..., \mathbf{D}_{j,m_j})^T$ represent the $(dm)_j \times r$ matrix of first partial derivatives associated with mean vector $\mu_j$. $$\sum_{j=1}^n \mathbf{D}_j^T \mathbf{V}_j^{-1} (\mathbf{y}_j - \mu_j) = \mathbf{0}. \quad (9.23)$$

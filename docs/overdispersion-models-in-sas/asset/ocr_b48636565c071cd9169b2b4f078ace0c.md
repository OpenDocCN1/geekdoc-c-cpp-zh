# 10.5 Beta-binomial and Random-clamped Binomial GLOMMs

In this section we present two GLOMMs, the Beta-binomial and the Random-clamped Binomial with random effects. Recall the discussion on R-side and G-side models from Section 10.1. In a GLOMM, the overdispersion distributions are used in the R-side, while the random effects operate in the G-side. Overdispersion models will try to capture the inherent lack of fit in the data relative to the Binomial (or Poisson in the case of counts data), while in practice these artifacts manifest as variance; the R-side and G-side effects each competing with other to grab part of the overvariance observed in the data.

The data used for the illustrative example here correspond to a 2x2 cross-design with sequences AB and BA. Within each period, subjects repeated Bernoulli outcomes following either the BB or the RCB distribution. We first address the Beta-binomial GLOMM. Applications of this GLOMM might be found in baby studies where mothers are asked to use a particular type of diaper on babies for one week. For the next week, they switch over to a different diaper period. The outcome of interest is whether or not leakage was observed for each diaper use. For simplicity, we assume no period effect.

Table 10.4 provides beta estimates, standard errors, and P-values for the model in equation (10.14) under a GLMM with no empirical option, a GLMM with the EMPIRICAL=CLASSIC option, and a GLMM with the EMPIRICAL=MBN option. The three models provide the same beta estimates. The variance estimates (and consequently their P-values) are different. The model with EMPIRICAL=CLASSIC option provides the same (except for the Time effect) standard errors are smaller than those under the regular GLMM. These variance estimates are likely to be smaller than they should be due to small sample bias embedded into the empirical EE variance. As a consequence of this bias, inflated false positive rates are expected to occur. Recall that there were 22 subjects in the age group and 19 in the control group. The model with the GLMM with the EMPIRICAL=MBN option reduces the bias in the estimated empirical variance estimator by slightly inflating the variance estimates. The amount of correction vanishes as the sample size increases, for large samples, the EMPIRICAL=CLASSIC and the EMPIRICAL=MBN variance estimates become indistinguishable. The Treatment and Time effects remain highly significant under the more stringent variance estimates obtained under the EMPIRICAL=MBN option. The age effect which is significant under the EMPIRICAL=CLASSIC option, but is only marginally significant under the EMPIRICAL=MBN option.

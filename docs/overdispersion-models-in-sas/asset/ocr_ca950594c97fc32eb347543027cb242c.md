Let $\mu_i(T)$ be the i-th central moment of a random variable $T$, $\mu_i(T) = E\{T-E(T)\}^i$.

Recall that the coefficients of skewness and kurtosis are defined as $\mu_3(T)/\{\mu_2(T)\}^{3/2}$ and $\mu_4(T)/\{\mu_3(T)\}^2$, respectively. Bokossa (1999) showed the coefficients of skewness and kurtosis for the Beta-binomial, and the Random-clumped distributions

```
Skewness(Beta-binomial) = \frac{(1-2\pi)[1+(2m-1)\rho^2]}{[m\pi(1-\pi)[1+(m-1)\rho^2]]^2}(1+\rho^2) (4.23)
```

```
Skewness(Random-clumped) = \frac{(1-2\pi)[3+(m-1)\rho^2+m(m-2)\rho^3]}{[m\pi(1-\pi)[1+(m-1)\rho^2]]^2}(1+\rho^2) (4.24)
```

```
Kurtosis(Beta-binomial) = \frac{1}{m\pi(1-\pi)[1+(m-1)\rho^2]]^2}(1+\rho^2)\{1+2(3-m)\rho^2 \\n+3(m-2)\pi(1-\\pi)\rho^3+6m^2\rho^4+3m(m-6)\pi(1-\\pi)\rho^5 \\n-18m^2\pi^2(1-\pi)\rho^4\} (4.25)
```

and

```
Kurtosis(Random-clumped) = \frac{1}{m\pi(1-\pi)[1+(m-1)\rho^2]]^2}\frac{1}{\pi(1-\\pi)}\{1+7(m-1)\rho^2+6m(m-1)(m-2)\rho^3 \\n-(m-1)(2m-3)\rho^4+3(m-2)+2(m-1)(m-6)\rho^2 \\n-8(m-1)m(m-3)\rho^3\-(m-1)(m-2)(m-3)\rho^4\} (4.26)
```

As expected, for the boundary case $\rho=0$, the coefficients of skewness (4.23) and (4.24) yield $(1-2\pi)/[m\pi(1-\\pi)]^2$, which is the coefficient of skewness of the Binomial distribution. For the other extreme case of $\rho=1$, where a Bernoulli zero-outcome is clamped m, the Beta-binomial and Random-clumped Binomial distribution have $(1-2\pi)$ as their coefficient of skewness. For $\pi=0.5$ the coefficients of skewness are always zero. Ifho<0.5$, the Beta-binomial is less skewed than the Random-clumped. For $0.5<\pi<1$, the skewness of the Random-clumped Binomial is less than that of the Beta-binomial. In general, for $\pi\neq0.5$, the Random-clumped Binomial distribution has longer tails, because it has, in absolute value, a greater coefficient of skewness. The coefficients of skewness for both distributions are almost the same when $m=4$. When the cluster size m increases, the difference between them becomes pronounced.

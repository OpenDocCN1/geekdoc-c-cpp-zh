# 7.2 The Multinomial Distribution

The Multinomial distribution is a generalization of the Binomial distribution. Recall that the Binomial distribution arises as the number of "successes" in m independent Bernoulli trials, with the same probability of success $\pi$ in each trial. We will write T $\sim(\pi;m)$ to denote the count T is distributed as a Multinomial distribution with m trials and the probability of success $\pi$.

The Multinomial distribution arises as a natural extension of the Binomial distribution when each independent trial has more than two mutually exclusive outcomes. Consider a case of m independent repetitions (or trials) of an experiment, each resulting in one of the (d+1) mutually exclusive events $E_1,E_2,...,E_{d+1}$. In each repetition of the experiment, the probability of occurrence of event $E_i$ is $\pi_i$, with $\sum\pi_i=1$. Let $\bf T}=(T_1,T_2,...,T_{d+1})$ denote the random vector of the number of occurrences of events $E_1,E_2,...,E_{d+1}$ out of m trials, with

$\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}T_i=m$. Let $\bf t}=(t_1,t_2,...,t_{d+1})$ represent a representation of $\bf T^*$. $\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}t_i=m$. Then, the

vector $\bf T$ is said to have a Multinomial distribution with parameters $(\pi';m)$, with $\pi'=(\pi_1,\pi_2,...,\pi_{d+1})$. The joint probability function of $\bf T^*$ is

```
$Pr(\f T^*=\bf t) = \frac{m!}{t_1!t_2!...t_{d+1}!} \prod_{i=1}^{d+1} \pi_i'^{t_i}$
```

The special case of $d=1$, with two mutually exclusive outcomes for each trial, corresponds to the Binomial distribution. We then have a 2-dimensional random vector $\bf T=(T,m-T)$ where $m-T$ is also distributed as Binomial$(1-\pi;m)$. Since m is known, the random variable $T$ does not provide additional information; therefore, one might reduce the dimensionality of $\bf T^*$ and conduct analyses, as we have shown in previous chapters, based entirely on $T$.

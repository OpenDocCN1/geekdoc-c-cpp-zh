We conclude this with some final remarks regarding the Hurdle models in relation to the other zero-inflated distributions. In the ZIP or ZINB, the focus is on modeling the extra zeros observed in the data relative to the underlying Poisson or NB models. This is achieved by mixing the underlying Poisson or NB model with a degenerate distribution at zero. The weight for the generator is adjusted in such that it picks up the "shortfall" of zeros in the ZIP or NB model. As noted earlier in the definition of ZIP, the weight can be negative to accommodate situations where there is a shortage of zeros in the data. Thus, zero-inflated models are naturally ample for interpreting the zeros contributed from two different sources, as explained in the Trematode Data example. A Hurdle model is obtained by combining a given probability of zero counts with either Poisson or NB truncated at zero. Therefore, the probability of zeros under the terminal may be either greater than or equal to, or less than the probability of zeros under the terminal Poisson of NB, as the case may be. This is depicted in the pairs of plots obtained in Output 6.5. The Hurdle model will accommodate a shortage of zeros as well as extra zeros without the hassle of negative weights. It can be shown that the ZIP or ZINB can be reparameterized as a Hurdle model with excess zeros written as ZIP (ZINB) with $\omega > 0$, whereas a Hurdle model with a shortage of zeros can only be written as ZIP (ZINB) if we allow negative weights. Because of the equivalency, the practitioner has the flexibility of using either the Hurdle model or the Zero-inflated model for any given application. It can be verified that the maximum values of likelihood under the two models are identical. In the case of data sets with less than expected, we would recommend Hurdle models, due to the convenience of not dealing with negative weights. As an illustration of this consider the previously discussed Mullally's (1986) example. The computed value of negative two times the log-likelihood function under a Hurdle Poisson and a ZINB models are identical and equal to 5470.5. Similarly, for the Hurdle-binomial and ZINB models the corresponding values are 5449.9 due to the equivalency of these two pairs of models. ## Appendix 6.1: Generation of Data under the Negative-binomial Distribution

To generate a random sample $Y_j, j=1,2,...,n. Y_j \sim \text{Negative-binomial}(\mu,\kappa)$ where the mean of the distribution is $\mu$ and the dispersion parameter $\kappa$ proceeds as follows:

- Define $\alpha$ and $\beta = \kappa\mu$ as $\alpha = \frac{1}{\kappa}$ and $\beta = \kappa\mu$.
- Generate a sample $U_j, j=1,2,...,n$ such that $U_j \sim \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \beta)$.
- Then, for each $j, j=1,2,...,n$, generate $Y_j \sim \text{Poisson}(U_j)$.

Recall the mean and variance of a Negative-binomial distribution are, respectively, $\mu$ and $\mu + \kappa\mu^2$.

| Generation of data under a Negative-binomial distribution with mean Mu and dispersion parameter Kappa |
| :--- |
| Mean of Y's is $\mu$ |
| Variance of Y's is $\mu + \kappa\mu^2$ |
| Recall $\alpha = 1/\kappa$ and $\beta = \kappa\mu$ in the procedures GENMOD, GLIMMIX and COUNTREG the dispersion parameter is Kappa |

```sas
data one;
  Mu = 30;
  Kappa = 0.1;
  alpha = 1/kappa;
  beta = kappa * mu;
  n = 2000;
  seed = 1979;
  Variance = mu * (1 + kappa);
  do i=1 to n;
    u = beta * rangam(seed, alpha);
    y = ranpois(seed, u);
    output;
  end;
run;

/* Get estimates of Mu and Kappa using GLIMMIX */
proc glimmix data=one;
  model y = / dist=negbin link=identity;
  ods select all; output output=parms;

title "True Mu and Kappa of Y's Negative-binomial(Mu, Kappa)";
proc print data=one noobs;
where id = 1;
var Mu Kappa;
run;

title "Estimated Mu and Kappa using GLIMMIX";
proc print data=parms noobs;
var Effect Estimate;
run;

title "True Mean and Variance of Y's Negative-binomial(Mu, Kappa)";
proc print data=one noobs;
where id = 1;
var Mean Variance;
run;

title "Estimated Mean and Variance";
proc means data=one n mean var maxdec=2;
var y;
run;
```

## Appendix 6.2: Generation of Data under the Poisson Hurdle and Negative-binomial Hurdle

The programming statements for generating random counts from a Poisson Hurdle and a Negative-binomial Hurdle model are provided below. The truncated data under either the Poisson or Negative-binomial model are generated using the rejection method. In the example, parameters for the Hurdle model were $\pi_i = 0.4$ and $\lambda = 10$, and for the Negative-binomial model, $\pi_i = 0.4, \lambda = 10$ and $\kappa = 0.1$.

```sas
/* Generation of data under a Poisson Hurdle Model with parameters Pi_1 and Mu,
under a Negative-binomial Hurdle Model with parameters Pi_1, Mu and Kappa */
ods html;
title "Poisson Hurdle Model with Pi_1=0.4 and Mu=0.10";
data Poisson_Hurdle_Data;
n = 8000;
pl = 0.4;
mu = 10;
seed = 1979;
/* Underlying True Mean and Variance */
p2 = exp(-mu);
plc = 1 - pl;
p2c = 1 - p2;
Phi = plc / p2c;
Mean = Phi*mu;
Var = Phi*mu*(mu+1)- Mean*Mean;
do j=1 to n
  u = uniform(seed);
  if u <= pl then do
    y = 0;
    output;
  end
  else do
    -- Crossing the hurdle;
    -- Get Truncated Poisson using Rejection Method;
    do until (y>0)
      y = ranpoi(seed,mu);
    end
    output;
  end;
end;
run;

title2 "Underlying True Mean and Variance";
proc print data=Poisson_Hurdle_Data noobs;
where j=1;
var Mean Var;
format _all 8.4;
run;

title2 "Estimated Mean and Variance";
proc means data=Poisson_Hurdle_Data n mean var maxdec=4;
var y;
run;

ods html close;
```

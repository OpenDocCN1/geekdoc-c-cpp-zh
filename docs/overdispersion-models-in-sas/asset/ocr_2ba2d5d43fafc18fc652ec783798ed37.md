Table 4.3 shows the observed and expected frequencies of the number of associations under the five distributions considered in this example. The Binomial distribution does not fit the data well, since $X^2 = 8.11$ on two degrees of freedom with an associated P-value of 0.017. A similar observation can be made about the Zero-inflated Binomial, where $X^2 = 4.71$ on one degree of freedom with an associated P-value of 0.030. Degrees of freedom are computed as (number of categories minus minus) (number of parameters estimated). In the case of the Binomial distribution, degrees of freedom are $(4-1)=2$. The overdispersed distributions have an additional parameter, $ho$. That makes their degrees of freedom $(4-1)-2=1$. The Binomial, the Random-clumped Binomial and the Generalized-Binomial with Additive Interaction fit the distribution of the number of associations fairly well, with Pvalues of the Chi-squared GOF tests being 0.384, 0.347 and 0.439, respectively. When there are multiple models, all are acceptable by a Goodness-of-fit criterion, and the models are not nested within each other, is difficult to choose a single model as the final recommendation. This can happen even in a regression modeling situation. Consequently, it is advisable to discuss the options with the subject-matter experts and communicate to them the meaning of each model and seek their input as which model is closer to the phenomenon they study.

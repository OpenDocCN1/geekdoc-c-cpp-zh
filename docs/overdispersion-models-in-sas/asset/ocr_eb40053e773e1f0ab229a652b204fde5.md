These functions $f(.)$, $g(.)$, and $h(.)$ are twice differentiable smooth functions. There are, that the reader might have noticed, three linear parameters, namely $\eta_{1} = \mathbf{x}\mathbf{eta}$, $\eta_{2} = \mathbf{v}\mathbf{\delta}$ and $\eta_{3} = \mathbf{z}\mathbf{\alpha}$. Maximum likelihood estimates can be obtained iteratively using appropriate numerical methods.

Currently, GENMOD, GLIMMIX, and COUNREG procedures support maximum likelihood estimation using the Negative-binomial distribution, with COUNREG allowing for linear and quadratic variance functions. GENMOD and COUNREG handle Zero-inflated Poisson (ZIP) and Zero-inflated Negative-binomial (ZINB) models. Both procedures permit the use of link functions, such as the log and profit, on the Zero-inflation Probability parameter. The ZIP and ZINB distributions can be fit using the NLMIXED procedure, which requires the user to write his/her own programming statements.

# 6.6 An Example of Urinary Tract Infections (UTI) in Men Infected with HIV

Van den Brooks (1995) proposed a score test for testing the Zero-inflation probability parameter $\omega$ in the case of the Zero-inflated Poisson distribution (6.10), which permits us to test whether the number of zeroes is too large to fit the Poisson distribution. Van den Brooks's test is illustrated using data from a study conducted in the Department of Internal Medicine at the Utrecht Hospital, the Netherlands, where $n=98$ human immunodeficiency virus-infected (HIV)-infected men were followed up for two years. Urinary cultures were obtained during the first visit and every six months when signs and symptoms of urinary tract infections (UTI) occurred, or when patients had fever of unknown origin. CD4+ cell counts were also measured. CD4+ count is a blood test to determine how well the immune system is working in people who have been diagnosed with immunodeficiency (HIV). CD4+ counts are reported as the number of cells in a milliliter of blood. A normal CD4+ count typically ranges from 500 to 1,500 cells per milliliter of blood. In general, a decreasing CD4+ count is an indication of the progression of HIV. This means that the immune system is getting weaker; as a result, the patient is more likely to get sick. For more details on this chapter we refer to Hoopelman et al. (1992).

Table 6.2, which was kindly provided by Prof. Jan den Brooks, Utrecht University, the Netherlands, contains, for each of the 98 subjects participating in the study, the number of times they had UTI (Number of Episodes), the Time to Follow Up (in months), and the immune status by CD4+ counts.

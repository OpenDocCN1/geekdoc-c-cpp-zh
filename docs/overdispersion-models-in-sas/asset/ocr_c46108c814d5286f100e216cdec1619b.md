To illustrate the use of logistic regression in case-control studies we consider the Ille-et-Vaine study of oesophacheal cancer. Ille-et-Vaine is a French department created from part of the province of Brittany during the French Revolution. Mortality from cancer concerning the oesophagus was known to be high in Brittany and Normandy. Tuyns et al. (1977) collected data in the department of Ille-et-Vaine where 200 males (cases) were diagnosed with oesophacheal cancer. A sample of 778 subjects (free of cancer) was drawn from electoral lists in each commune. Of those, 775 subjects provided sufficient information for analysis purposes. Breslow and Day (1980) used this example to illustrate different statistical methodologies for cancer research. We refer to the readers to Chapters 4 and 5 of their book. The data of the Ille-et-Vaine case-control study are shown in Table 2.6.

Our approach to overdispersion model specification is based on a variety of probability distributions capable of accounting for intra-cluster correlations, and may be referred to as a D-side specification. LMM are also known in the statistical literature as variance components models. They are often used to model repeated measures data, longitudinal data and clustered data. They may also be applicable to time series and spatial data. See McCulloch and Searle (2001) for historical developments of the mixed model. The reader is also referred to Littell et al. (2006) for more recent developments.

The next step in describing a GLMM is now clear. GLMM is obtained by including random effects in the linear predictor we subscript the random effects by "j" so that they act the same on all elemental units within the cluster. Note that $\gamma_j$ is still vector of appropriate dimension to accommodate possibly different random effects for the beta-parameters. The model equation for the Generalized Linear Mixed Model then becomes

```
\mu_{ji} = \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{y}_{ji})\= \mathbf{g}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{eta}, \mathbf{x}_{ji}, \gamma_j) \\left( \begin{array}{c} \mathbf{g}_j^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{ji} + \mathbf{z}_{ji}\gamma_j) \ \vdots \ \mathbf{g}_d^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{ji}\boldsymbol{eta} + \mathbf{z}_{ji}\gamma_j) \end{array} \right)
```

where $\mathbf{z}_{ji}$ are usually variables and $\gamma = (\gamma_1', \gamma_2', ..., \gamma_n')^T$ is assumed to be a zero-mean multivariate Normal random vector. We can re-write the model equations for the mean more succinctly as

```
\left[ \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{y}|\gamma) \right] = \mathbf{g}(\gamma) = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{eta} + \mathbf{Z}\gamma \\ \left[ \mathbf{Var}(\mathbf{y}|\gamma) = \mathbf{V}(\mu, \alpha) \right] = \mathbf{V}\left[ \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \mathbf{Z}\gamma \right] \right]
```

so that we can see the correspondence between LMM and GLMM. The unconditional mean can be obtained by integrating the random effects out. This can be symbolically represented as

```
\mu_{ji} = \int \mathbf{g}_j^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{ji}\boldsymbol{eta} + \mathbf{z}_{ji}\gamma_j) d\mathcal{F}(\gamma_j).
```

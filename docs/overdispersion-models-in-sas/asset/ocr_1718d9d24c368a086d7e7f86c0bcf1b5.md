# Preface

This book focuses on the modeling and analysis of data exhibiting overdispersion using a variety of SAS procedures. Overdispersion, also known as extra variation, arises in Binomial, Multinomial, and count types of data when variances are larger than those allowed by the Binomial, Multinomial, or Poisson models. This phenomenon is caused by clustering of the data and lack of independence. As pointed out by McCullagh and Nelder (1989),'Overdispersion is not uncommon in practice. In fact, some would maintain that overdispersion is the norm in practice and nominal dispersion the exception." The problem of overdispersion is encountered every day by practitioners from pharmaceutical departments, biostatistics and biometrics departments, universities, government agencies, health research organizations, and sampling survey organizations. The intent of this book is to provide the techniques necessary for the proper analysis and modeling of such problems. These techniques include Likelihood and Likelihood models, as well as Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE), Generalized Linear Overdispersion Models (GLOM), Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMM), and Generalized Linear Overdispersion Mixed Models (GLOMM).

Theory, methods, and implementation (in SAS) using illustrative examples will be integrated throughout the text. Thus, readers will be provided with a pathway from theoretical model to interpretation of the results in the context of a concrete application. Presentation of material the book is mainly example driven. Theoretical discussions are minimal; theoretical concepts are presented with real examples and data sets. We provide a clear pathway from the model to the formulation to the final interpretation of results.

The book is written for an audience at the level of an applications-oriented master's degree who has experience with the use of the SAS DATA step and familiarity with few basic PROCs. It will be accessible to those with a bachelor's degree and adequate work experience. This book might be used as a supplementary text for a graduate-level course of Generalized Linear Models (GLM), GEE, or GLMM; or as an applied introduction to GLM overdispersion models as illustrative examples. GENMOD, GLMIX, and NLMIXED are the procedures used most in this book. It will be helpful if readers familiarize themselves with the syntax of these procedures as they are reading the corresponding sections. In particular, knowing how to perform Binomial and Poisson regression in the context of clustered/correlated data and being familiar with the repeated and random statements in GENMOD and GLMIX will help. Since some examples in this book bring together biostatistics and survey methodology through the concept of overdispersion, readers should also be prepared to use the SURVEIGISTIC procedure in conjunction with their cluster statement.

The Random-clumped Multinomial, one of the distributions discussed in this book, originated from an insightful comment made by Distinguished Professor Wayne A. Fuller from Iowa State University in 1986. One of the authors of this book, Jorge G. Morel, was running a simulation as part of his dissertation work and needed a method to generate overdispersed multinomial data. The natural choice at that time was Fuller left left our desks with a 'Please see me – WAF,' he suggested an elegant and deft way of generating correlated multinomial responses in a manner that the first two of the generated data matched those of the Binomial. The proposed method introduces the clustering of responses within a cluster, providing a clear interpretation of the observed overdispersion.

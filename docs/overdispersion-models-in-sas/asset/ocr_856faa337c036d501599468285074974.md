Monte Carlo Error {SE(ρ)} = Maximum 5,000 Replications of $\frac{SE(ρ)}{SE(ρρ)}$ (8.9)

Here, SE() denotes the estimated standard error from either the approximate or the exact method, as appropriate. Algorithms for data generation under the DM and RCM models are given in Appendix 7.1, Chapter 7. The results of this Monte Carlo study are very good, as demonstrated in Tables 8.2 through 8.5. Tables 8.2 and 8.3 display the results for the estimates of the parameters of the models, and Tables 8.4 and 8.5 show the results for the estimates of the estimated standard errors. In all cases we have tried, the estimates obtained by this Two-stage procedure are indistinguishable from the exact maximum likelihood estimates. The relative errors in the corresponding standard error estimates are of the order $10^{-5}$. For all practical purposes the Two-stage procedure, which involves the computation of the FIM matrix only once (if a final update is called), gives nearly the same results as using the FIM in each iteration. The approximation results for the FIMs of the overdispersion models make a very interesting comparative study. While time-saving may not be a motivation for them to use it in a one-time analysis of a single data set, it is entirely worthwhile to consider their use in a simulation setup, or even in parametric bootstrapping for obtaining p-values, the FIM needs to be evaluated thousands of times. This is just one example of simulation studies we have performed and feel well confident about our recommendation.

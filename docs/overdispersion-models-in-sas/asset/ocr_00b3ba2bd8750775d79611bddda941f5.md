We conclude this with some final remarks regarding the Hurdle models in relation to the other zero-inflated distributions. In the ZIP or ZINB, the focus is on modeling the extra zeros observed in the data relative to the underlying Poisson or NB models, This is achieved by mixing the underlying Poisson or NB model with a degenerate distribution at zero. The weight for the generator is adjusted in such that it picks up the "shortfall" of zeros in the ZIP or NB model. As noted earlier in the definition of ZIP, the weight can be negative to accommodate situations where there is a shortage of zeros in the data. Thus, zero-inflated models are naturally ample for interpreting the zeros contributed from two different sources, as explained in the Trematode Data example. A Hurdle model is obtained by combining a given probability of zero counts with either Poisson or NB truncated at zero. Therefore, the probability of zeros under the terminal may be either greater than or equal to, or less than the probability of zeros under the terminal Poisson of NB, as the case may be. This is depicted in the pairs of plots obtained in Output 6.5. The Hurdle model will accommodate a shortage of zeros as well as extra zeros without the hassle of negative weights.

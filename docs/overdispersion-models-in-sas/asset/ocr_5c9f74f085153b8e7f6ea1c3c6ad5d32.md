If the $y_i$'s are zero-one responses (Bernoulli outcomes) and the $z_i$'s are all identical to one, the estimator in equation (9.14) reduces to the logarithm of a proportion (or logarithm of a risk), that is,

```
$$\hat{eta}_i = \ln(\hat{P}_i), \quad (9.17)$
```

where $\hat{P}_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} y_{ij}}{n_i}$, by equation (9.15), the estimated variance of (9.17) is

```
$\hat{Var}(\hat{eta}_i) = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (y_{ij}-\hat{P}_i)^2}{n_i\hat{P}_i^2} + \frac{(1-\\hat{P}_i)}{n_i\hat{P}_i}, \quad i=1, 2, \dots, k. \quad (9.18)$
```

Then, the estimated logarithm of the relative risk based on two independent groups

```
$$\hat{eta}_i - \hat{eta}_j = \ln\left(\frac{\hat{P}_i}{\hat{P}_j}\right), \quad (9.19)$
```

The estimated variance of (9.19) reduces to the familiar expression

```
$$\hat{Var}(\hat{eta}_i - \hat{eta}_j) = Var(\ln(\frac{\hat{P}_i}{\hat{P}_j})) = \frac{(1-\hat{P}_i)}{n_i\hat{P}_i} + \frac{(1-\hat{P}_j)}{n_j\hat{P}_j}. \quad (9.20)$
```

The case of a rate ratio, where the $y_{ij}$ are zero-one responses representing the presence or absence of a certain disease (or adverse event) and the $z_i$ are the time to the event, is derived easily from equations (9.14) and (9.15). To illustrate the estimation of Relative Risk and Risk Ratio, consider the following illustrative example from Morel and Neerchal (2008). Table 9.4 shows a hypothetical dataset from a clinical trial with two treatment groups coded as "1" for Active and "2" for Placebo, where the rate ratio is four. Subjects were randomized to each treatment group. Subjects were followed for 52 weeks, and the occurrence of a Adverse Event (AE) and the dropout at risk in weeks to the AE were recorded. Note that subjects 1, 2, 3, 11 and 12 dropouts and the occurrence of the AE was recorded as zero. On the other hand, subjects 4 and 13 through 17 completed the study without experiencing the AE under consideration. Their time to AE is censored and was recorded as simply as 52 weeks. The remaining subjects experienced the AE under consideration at some time during the study.

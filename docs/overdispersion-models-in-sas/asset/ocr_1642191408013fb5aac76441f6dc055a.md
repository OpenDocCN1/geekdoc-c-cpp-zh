# 1.6 The Exponential Family

The main tasks to be covered in this book, namely Quasi-likelihood Functions, Generalized Linear Models (GLM), Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE), and Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMM), are motivated by the generality of the many statistical methods applicable to all members of the exponential family. Therefore, we begin with a brief introduction to the exponential family of densities by outlining some of their properties. The exponential family is a class of density functions sharing certain characteristics and properties. Among the members of this family are the Normal, Bernoulli, Binomial, Multinomial, Poisson, Negative-binomial, Beta, and Gamma distributions. This family of distributions plays a very important role in the classical statistical inference techniques. Following the literature, we use the canonical parameter and canonical link set up for ourどうか, but all examples will be in the family notation, which may be or not be canonical.

A density function in this family can be written

```
$f(y) = \exp\left\frac{y\theta - b(\theta)}{\phi} + c(y, f(\phi))\right\},\quad (1.8)$
```

$b(.)$ and $c(.)$ are some specific functions. The parameter $\theta$ is a real-valued **location parameter** and is known as the **natural** (or canonical) parameter. The function $b(\theta)$ is referred to as the **cumulant** and the parameter $\phi$ as the **dispersion (or scale) parameter**. We assume that $b(\theta)$ is continuously twice differentiable.

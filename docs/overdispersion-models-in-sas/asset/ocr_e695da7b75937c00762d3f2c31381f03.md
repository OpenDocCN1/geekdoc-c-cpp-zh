# 9.11 Testing for Homogeneity

GLM for Multinomial models feature a separate regression equation for the probability of each category. Thus, a natural topic of interest is to examine the covariates affect the differences in the same way, or if specific covariates tend to impact a specific category. This is examined using the hypothesis of homogeneity. The following example is intended to illustrate the use of a Generalized Logistic Model for testing of homogeneity with Multinomial correlated outcomes. The data will be analyzed using the GEE methodology since the Multinomial outcomes exhibit overdispersion. For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to the case of two products and nominal responses, that is, the 2x3 contingency table. However, the methodology discussed in this Section can be extended to cover the case of RxC contingency tables. Let $\pi_{11}$, $\pi_{12}$ and $\pi_{13}$, respectively, the underlying true and unknown probabilities (rates) of Product 1 with observing three events that were treated as "Low," "Medium," and "High.". Similarly we define $\pi_{21}$, $\pi_{22}$ and $\pi_{23}$ as the corresponding probabilities for Product 2. The hypothesis of homogeneity can be stated as

> $H_0 : \pi_{11} = \pi_{21}$ and $\pi_{12} = \pi_{22}$ and $\pi_{13} = \pi_{23}$ $H_1 : \pi_{11} \neq \pi_{21}$ or $\pi_{12} \neq \pi_{22}$ or $\pi_{13} \neq \pi_{23}$ (9.30)

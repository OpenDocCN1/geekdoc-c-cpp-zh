# 2.8 Odds-ratios and Logistic Regression

One of the main advantages of logistic regression is the clear interpretation of its parameters in terms of odds-ratios, and the ability to implement it with a variety of studies that are different in nature. Consider a hypothetical example where subjects are cross-classified according to the presence or absence of a certain risk factor, and the presence or absence of a certain disease. The underlying true and unknown joint probabilities of exposure/disease can be represented in a 2x2 contingency table

| Exposure Factor | Yes | No | Total |
|---|---|---|--|
| Present | $\pi_{11}$ | $\pi_{12}$ | $\pi_{1+}$ |
| Absent | $\pi_{21}$ | $\pi_{22}$ | $\pi_{2+}$ |
| Total | $\pi_{+1}$ | $\pi_{+2}$ | 1 |

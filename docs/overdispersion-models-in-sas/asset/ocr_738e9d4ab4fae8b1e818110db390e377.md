# 5.2 Testing “No Overdispersion” against an Overdispersed Binomial Alternative

In this section we consider the tests for the null hypothesis of “No Overdispersion” under several specific Overdispersed Binomial Distributions. One such was tested by Tarone (1979) under the Beta-binomial model. Suppose that $t_1, t_2, \dots, t_n$ are realizations of $T_1, T_2, \dots, T_n$, where the $T_j$ are independent random variables such that each $j$, $j=1, 2, \dots, n$, $T_j \sim 	ext{Binomial}(\pi, ho; m_j)$. Under the Beta-binomial model, for $H_0: ho = 0$ versus the one-sided alternative $H_1: ho > 0$ is obtained as follows. If $\hat{\pi}$ is the maximum likelihood estimator of $\pi$ when $T_j$ are assumed to be distributed Binomial, then Tarone’s GOF one-sided statistic

```
$Z = \sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^n \frac{(t_j - \hat{m}_j)^2}{\hat{\pi}(1 - \hat{\pi}) - \sum_{j=1}^n m_j(m_j - 1)}}$ (5.1)
```

which under the null hypothesis is asymptotically distributed as a standard Normal distribution.

Tarone also drew a two-sided GOF test of the Binomial distribution against the Generalized Binomial with Additive Interaction element of Altham (1978). This model was simultaneously proposed by Kupper and Hasek (1978) and is termed “Correlated Binomial Model.” The GOF test is

```
$X^2 = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^n \frac{(t_j - \hat{m}_j)^2}{\hat{\pi}(1 - \hat{\pi})}}{\sum_{j=1}^n m_j(m_j - 1)}$ (5.2)
```

which under the null hypothesis is asymptotically distributed as a Chi-square distribution with one degree of freedom. Clearly, the statistic in equation (5,2) is the square of the statistic given in equation (5.1). Also note that these are not GOF statistics in the sense that they are not testing the adequacy of the distribution under the null hypothesis, but indicate the significance of the overdispersion parameter in the model under the alternative hypothesis.

Prentice (1986) extended the Beta-binomial distribution to allow for some degree of negative correlations among the binary variables within experimental units. In the context of the extended Beta-binomial model Prentice provided a two-sided Z-statistic by (5,1), allowing tests for either overdispersion or underdispersion. These tests are locally most powerful with specific attributes.

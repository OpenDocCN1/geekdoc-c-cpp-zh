Adjusting the partition algorithm. Median-of-three pivot selection suggests some adjust-ments to our partitioning scheme. Previously, we swapped the pivot with the last entry in the array prior to partitioning. But here the first, middle, and last entries are sorted, so we know that the last entry is least as large as the pivot. To get the pivot out of the way, we can swap it with the next-to-last entry, a[last - 1], as Figure 11-12 shows. Therefore, the partition algorithm can begin its search from the right at last - 2.

Also notice that the first entry is at least as small as the pivot, and it lies in the subarray S_f. We can then leave the last entry in place and have the partition algorithm begin its search from the left at index first + 1. Figure 11-12b shows the status of the array at this point, just prior to partitioning.

This scheme provides a side benefit that simplifies the loops for the two searches. The search for the entry that is greater than or equal to the pivot. That search will terminate, at worst, will stop at the pivot. The search from the right looks for an entry that is least than or equal to the pivot. That search will terminate, at worst, will stop at the first entry. Thus, the loops need not do anything special to prevent the searches from going beyond the ends of the array.

After the search loops end, we need to position the pivot between the subarrays S_f and S_l. We do this by swapping the entries at [indexFrom.f] and [last - 1].

> Note: The quick sort rearranges the entries in an array during the partitioning process. Each partition places one entry—the pivot—in its correct sorted position. The entries in each of the two subarrays are before and after the pivot will remain in their respective subarrays.

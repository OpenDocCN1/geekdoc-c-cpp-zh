# 2.6.1 The Fibonacci Sequence (Multiplying Rabbits)

Rabbits are very prolific breeders. If rabbits did not breed, their population would quickly get out of hand. Suppose we assume the following "facts," which were obtained in a recent survey of randomly selected:

+   * Rabbits never die.
* Rabbits reach sexual maturity exactly two months after birth; that, at the beginning of its third month of life.
* Rabbits are always born in male-female pairs. At the beginning of every month, each sexually male-female pair gives birth to exactly one male-female pair.

Suppose that started with a single newborn male-female pair. How many pairs would there be in month 6, counting the pairs that took place at the beginning of month 6? As it is a relatively small number, you can figure out the solution:

+   * Month 1: 1 pair, the original rabbits.
* Month 2: 1 pair still, because the rabbits are not yet sexually mature.
* Month 3: 2 pairs; the original pair has reached sexual maturity and has given birth to a second pair.
* Month 4: 3 pairs; the original pair has given birth again, but the pair born at the beginning of month 3 are not yet sexually mature.
* Month 5: 5 pairs; all rabbits alive in month 3 (2 pairs) are now sexually mature. Add their offspring to those alive in month 4 (3 pairs) to yield 5 pairs.
* Month 6: 8 pairs; 3 newborn pairs from the pairs alive in month 4 plus 5 pairs alive in month 5.

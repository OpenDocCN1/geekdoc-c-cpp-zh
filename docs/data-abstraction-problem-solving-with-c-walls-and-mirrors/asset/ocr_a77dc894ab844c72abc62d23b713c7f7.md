# APPENDIX E Mathematical Induction

Prove that

```
$rabbit(n) = (a^n - b^n)/\sqrt{5}$
```

where $a = (1 + \sqrt{5})/2$ and $b = (1 - \sqrt{5})/2 = 1 - a$.

**Basis.** rabbit(0) is undefined, begin at $n=1$. Some algebra shows that $rabbit(1) = (a^1 - b^1)/\sqrt{5} = 1$. However, $rabbit(2)$ is also a special case. That is, you cannot compute $rabbit(2)$ from using the recurrence relationship given here. Therefore, in this inductive proof we must include $n=2$.

When $n=2$, more algebra will show that $rabbit(2) = (a^2 - b^2)/\sqrt{5} = 1$. Thus, the formula is true when $n$ is either 1 or 2.

**Inductive Hypothesis.** Assume that the formula is true for all $n$ $1 \le n \le k$, where $k$ is at least 2.

**Inductive Conclusion.** Show that the formula is true for $n=k+1$. To do so, we can proceed as:

```
$rabbit(k+1) = rabbit(k) + rabbit(k-1)\n$= [(a^k - b^k) + (a^{k-1} - b^{k-1})]/\sqrt{5} \qquad$ (inductive hypothesis)\n$= [(a^{k-1}(a+1) - b^{k-1}(b+1)]/\sqrt{5} \qquad$ (factorization)\n$= [(a^{k-1}(a^2) - b^{k-1}(b^2)]/\sqrt{5} \qquad$ $(a+1=a^2; b+1=b^2)$
$= [a^{k+1} - b^{k+1}]/\sqrt{5}$
```

Because the formula holds for $n=k+1$, it holds for all $n>2$ by the principle of mathematical induction. **(End of proof).**

Note that the previous proof requires you to show that $a+1=a^2$ and $b+1=b^2$. Simple algebra will demonstrate the validity of these equalities, exactly how we did discover after the factorization step? Some experience with inductive proofs will give you the confidence to determine and verify the auxiliary relationships—such as $a+1=a^2$—that are necessary in a proof. Here, after we introduced the factors $(a+1)$ and $(b+1)$, we observed that these factors were equal to $a^2$ and $b^2$, respectively, we could finish the proof. Thus tried to show that $a+1=a^2$ and $b+1=b^2$; indeed, we were successful. Inductive proofs often require adventurous algebraic manipulations!

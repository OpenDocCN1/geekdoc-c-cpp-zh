After noting the value p of the pointer in this index record (you will need p in step 2 to remove the data record), you remove the index record from L (Figure 21-15b). Because L contains only one value (recall that a node must contain at least two values), and L's siblings cannot share a record, you merge L with one of the siblings and bring a record from the parent P (Figure 21-15c). Notice this is analogous to the merge step for a 2-3 tree. However, P now has only one value and two children, and its siblings cannot share a record and you must merge P with its sibling P', and bring a record down from P's parent, Q. Because P is an internal node, its children must be adopted by P' . (See Figure 21-15d).

After this merge, parent Q is left with only two children and one record. In this case, however, Q's sibling Q' can share a record and so you redistribute children and records among Q', and the parent S completes the remove. (See Figure 21-15e). If a removal propagates all the way to the root, leaving it with only one record and two children, you are finished because the definition of B-tree allows this situation. If a removal causes the root to have a single child and no records, you remove the root so that the tree's height decreases by 1, as in Figure 21-15f. The removal of the index record is complete, and you must remove the data record.

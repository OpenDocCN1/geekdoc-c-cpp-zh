THE INTERNET

**Background of the internet and the web**

**Understanding the world wide web**

The Net and Web

First of all, it is important to understand what you are dealing

with. The “net” and the “web” are NOT the same thing. So many

people nowadays use the terms as the one, which is not entirely

technically correct. In fact, put it this way, the internet has been

around for about 7 decades in various forms whereas the World

Wide Web is only around 4 decades in age. So, they could not

possibly be the same.

Internet Definition

The internet can be defined as a network of computers, a super

network of networks with computers connected to other

computers to link networks with networks. It is the hardware

connections. The internet has a history much longer than the

“World Wide Web” as we can see from the beginnings…

**History of the internet**

The reason

Believe it or not, the internet came into existence because of the

cold war and originally as a warfare communications master as

opposed to anything friendly. In the late 1950’s, 4th October

1957 to be more exact, the USSR (Soviet Union as it was back

then) launched “Sputnik”, the first earth orbiting artificial satellite

Space

Having successfully launched a satellite into space, the USSR

had the upper hand with communications. The speed of

communication and the fact it would be near impossible in those

days to destroy an opponent’s communication device from earth

when it is orbiting our planet at such heights, provided the

Soviet Union with greater communication power.

Cuban Crisis

Following the early 1960’s missile threat and USSR installations

so close to mainland USA, known as the Cuban Crisis, most

Americans prepared for nuclear war, the government certainly

planned for a thermo-nuclear war.

It was a very tense and threatening time in history. The USA

knew how advanced the USSR had become and this made the

threat very real. It was important for the US government to

develop a technology for warfare, such as communication

warfare more advanced than the eastern bloc had in their

arsenal.

Computer Network

The way forward would be a network of computers able to

communicate during warfare. Both sides possibly believed a

nuclear war could be won, or possibly they did not care if it

could be won but as long as the other side did not win!

What was needed was a network of super computers on an

information superhighway that would continue to operate during

such a violent and disruptive war. A network whereby if one

installation being ‘taken out’ of commission by a positive missile

hit, it would not disrupt the flow of information around the rest of

the network. Thus, the concept of a successful network of

computers was brought about in history.

Work began on a decentralized network, whereby it would have

distributed system of governance and would operate even after

most of the network is destroyed.

**Movement**

Anticipated Movement

We are constantly waiting for the leaning tower of Pisa to fall.

Why? Because of the anticipated movement we have within our

minds. We have all experienced an object tipping to the point of

topple.

Blurred outlines

An image with blurred outlines is perceived to be moving.

Multiple images

A process of movement whereby stages of are captured. The

start, middle and end.

Optical Movement

The eye is forced to move from one element to another by using

placement of objects, colours, strategic emphasis and curves.

Other forms of optical movement

The eye can move from one object to another, tricking the brain

into believing it is moving. Have you ever sat on a train and

thought the platform is moving? Optical illusions can send

incorrect signals to the brain.

Design can provide the eye with movement throughout a

publication.

Studying how the eye moves around objects, focussing on one

thing after another and the reasons as to why we see one object

before another can assist design strategy for movement around

the publication. A flow from one element to another.

**Balance**

Symmetrical (formal balance)

Using a vertical line will highlight a mirror image. It is pleasant to

the eye. Think about how beautiful people tend to have a more

symmetrical face than others. Our brains find an immediate

order and is content with looking at all other parts within the

order of appearance.

Asymmetrical (informal)

Although not mirrored, our brain can find comfort with other

parts performing the function of a level order. A balance can be

found in many other ways such as lots of small objects being

offset by one large object, as well as:

Colour balance … we tend to look for vibrant colours first and as

long as we have a balance across the display then our brains

find comfort.

Value … balance between light and dark.

Shape … across the whole display, offering the viewer a

balance of shapes on one side as opposed to the other.

Positioning … A sharp object in the foreground and less

distinctive objects in the background but balancing each against

each other.

Texture … using a balance of texture can provide movement of

the eye across two very different objects but providing.

Radial

All parts of a display radiate from the centre, providing balance

outward in all directions.

**Emphasis**

Attracting attention by use of focal point within a page or image:

Emphasis by Contrast

Larger subjects in the foreground become a focal point.

Colour, for example a solitary red poppy within a field of green

grass becomes an immediate focal point.

Lines, with various horizontal objects, one solitary vertical object

automatically grabs the eye.

Irregular shapes through the eye to any standard geometric

shape as the brain looks for order within chaos.

Emphasis by Isolation

An item on its own within white space will draw the eye.

Objects grouped together with individualised object positioned in

isolation to the side will become the natural focal point as our

brain questions why something has been isolated from the

community. Our natural social integration questioning comes

into play.

Emphasis by Location

Placing objects centrally will often be perceived as the focal

point.

Objects placed in between lines of perspective will typically be

observed first.

**Unity and Harmony**

The ability to tie all the parts of a design together as the one

piece of work (also known as the design’s Gestalt). A design,

first and foremost is a whole piece, followed by each section

therein. It is important to bring everything together, tie it all as

one, allowing the other disciplines of design to work with you.

Proximity

Bringing all objects within an area close together and providing

the viewer with a pattern. Remember the brain likes to achieve

some sort of order.

Repetition / Consistency

Each object having something in common, such as a colour,

shape, texture or pattern. As soon as we see a group of people

wearing the same hat but different clothing we continue identify

them as a grouping, a unification because of one common

article.

Continuation

Objects can be linked with a continuation of line, edge or

direction. Lines and colour can be used to separate but can also

be used for unity. A continuous line or colour can link objects

together, whereas broken lines or colours can separate.

*Learning Note—finding the ideal mix*

It is important to create an equilibrium between

creativeness and unity. Ensure web pages are not

designed haphazardly but include unity without lacking the

interest and avoiding boredom throughout. The use of

movement and equal status of the principles of design should

assist in this quest for design utopia for all your creations.

**Elements of design**

The principles of design are how we engage the elements.

Lines

Direction of lines can convey mood, for example, horizontal

lines are calming and vertical lines are more dynamically active.

Shapes

Shapes can be colour arrangement or lines enclosed to create

shapes. Curves are friendly and inviting.

Texture

Allows objects to have depth, to provide the viewer with a

surface quality impression and can be used as a background

image or to show emphasis to area sections.

Value

Relative lightness or darkness of an area. Increasing or

decreasing the value of a section can provide emphasis through

focal points as well as creating depth.

Colours

Warm colours such as red, orange and yellow tend to have a

forward advancing quality as opposed to cooler colours such as

blue and green that tend to recede.

Space

Sometimes referred to as ‘white space’ and is good to have.

Place information with adequate white space, everything seems

to have order and less messy. Space allows the eyes to have a

breather, we all need space at times to gather thoughts and to

continue forward.

continues to evolve with newer versions.

adhering to the protocol within web browsers of http, with web

pages having a computer file extension of either .htm or usually

a more appropriate .html allowing machines to associate the

computer code with a web page. The tags provide indicators as

to the content and how best to display within web browsers.

The main image file types for web design, acceptable by the

The acronym is for Joint Photographic Experts Group and is the

most popular file format for photo images and is known as one

of the 'Interchange Formats", being acceptable to most

applications. There are variable compression states ranging

from 0 to 12, with the lowest number providing the highest rate

of compression but the lowest quality and vice versa, this is

'lossy' compression. Lossy compression means evident loss of

quality the more compression is applied. The vast majority of

photographic images within web pages tend to be JPEG. It is

important to note html requires the file name to use the file

extension of .jpg and everything to be lowercase. Try not to use

spaces within file names and not to long either.

**.gif**

Graphics Interchange Format - is again one of the most popular

'Interchange Formats' (read by almost every software available

on the market today). It is very popular for images within web

pages, especially useful for logos or basic clip art / line art work,

although not as good for photos as the .jpg format, due to the

reduced colour range of only 256 colours and increased

compression rate. GIF's can be saved using a variety of palettes

to suit many differing web browsers. GIF supports transparent

pixels and animation.

coding. The purpose is to make notes related to the html for

anyone requiring additional information or reasons for such

coding. Ideal for reviewing and / or sharing with others. Use <!--

followed by the actual comment itself, ensuring to end with -->

As for CSS comments, commence with /* followed by the actual

comments and ensuring the end is with */

Image editors often have a facility to “Save for web” and allow

the user to reduce the file size and / or resolution.

Padding can be used within CSS to provide the essential “white

space” within web page design. HTML can also be used to

provide blank line breaks where required using <br>

RGB colours can be defined by hexadecimal e.g. #1111ee or

percentages e.g. rgb(33%, 55%, 12%) or indeed values

e.g. rgb(225,12,18)

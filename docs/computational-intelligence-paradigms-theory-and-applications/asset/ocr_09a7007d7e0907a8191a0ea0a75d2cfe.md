According to the standard mathematical notation for IF-THEN and AND

$A_1(x_1) \wedge A_2(x_2) \wedge \cdots A_m(x_m) \longrightarrow B_r(u)$ (6.15)

Where $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ are the several input variables, $r = 1, \ldots, R$ is the rule number, and $A_{ri}$ and $B_r$ are words from natural language.

Applying the union operator by writing the rule base with max/min operators as follows:

$\mu_R(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n, u) = \max\min\{\mu_{Pr}(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n), \mu_{Br}(u)\}$ (6.16)

where $\mu_{Pr}(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n)$ is the premise of the $r$th rule. This representation is the standard max/min representation of a rule base that is used for fuzzy controllers. Instead of the max/min representation a so called max-prod representation is also usual, where the algebraic product

$\mu_R(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n, u) = \max\min\mu_{Pr}(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n), \mu_{Br}(u)$ (6.17)

is used to build the relation between the premise and the conclusion.

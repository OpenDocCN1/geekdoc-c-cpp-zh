Combining rules into a rule base, the example from above may help when it is rewritten as

- 1. IF the type of dirt is greasy THEN the washing time is high, (OR)
- 2. IF the type of dirt is moderate greasy THEN the washing time is medium, (OR)
- 3. IF the type of dirt is not greasy THEN the washing time is low

which describes the union of three rules in a linguistic way. For the complete rule base $R$ one can combine the relations formed for each individual rule with a fuzzy union operator, which is the fuzzy OR.

According to the standard mathematical notation for IF-THEN and AND

$A_{r1}(x_1) \wedge A_{r2}(x_2) \wedge \cdots \wedge A_{rn}(x_n) \longrightarrow B_r(u)$  (6.15)

Where $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ are several input variables, $r = 1, \ldots, R$ is the rule number, and $A_{ri}$ and $B_r$ are words from natural language.

Applying the union operator by writing the rule base with max/min operators as follows:

$\mu_R(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n, u) = \max_r \min\{\mu_{P_r}(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n), \mu_{B_r}(u)\}$  (6.16)

where $\mu_{P_r}(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n)$ is the premise of the $r$th rule. This representation is the standard max/min representation of a rule base that is used for fuzzy controllers. Instead of the max/min representation, a so-called max-prod representation is also usual, where the algebraic product

$\mu_R(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n, u) = \max_r \{\mu_{P_r}(x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n)\cdot \mu_{B_r}(u)\}$  (6.17)

is used to build the relation between the premise and the conclusion.

## 6.12 Fuzzy Composition

Composition of fuzzy relations plays a crucial role in the study of approximate reasoning. Composition is defined as the operation of combining fuzzy relations in different product spaces with each other. This allows the fuzzy relations to operate with a given fact to produce an output that represents the decision in a fuzzy way, known as fuzzy reasoning, which is a special case of the more general operation called fuzzy composition.

Two relations of the form given in Equation (6.15)

$R: X \times Y \longrightarrow [0,1]$

$S: Y \times Z \longrightarrow [0,1]$

can be composed to one relation

$T: X \times Z \longrightarrow [0,1]$

using the max and min operators for union and intersection, one can express the composition operation $T = R\circ S$ by the corresponding membership functions. Based upon the mathematical properties, composition can be performed either by min-max composition or the max product composition.

**Min-Max Composition:** Let $\bar{R}_1 \subseteq X \times Y$ and $\bar{R}_2 \subseteq Y \times Z$ denote two fuzzy relations, the min-max composition on these relations is defined as

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2 = \max\{\min\{\mu_{\bar{R}_1}(x,y),\mu_{\bar{R}_2}(y,z)\}\}, \quad x\in X, y\in Y, z\in Z.$

**Max Product Composition:** Let $\bar{R}_1 \subseteq X \times Y$ and $\bar{R}_2 \subseteq Y \times Z$ denote two fuzzy relations, the max product composition on these relations is defined as

$\bar{R}_1 \bar{R}_2 = \max_y\{\mu_{\bar{R}_1}(x,y)\times\mu_{\bar{R}_2}(y,z)\}, \quad x\in X, y\in Y, z\in Z.$

Consider $\bar{R}_1 \subseteq X \times Y$ and $\bar{R}_2 \subseteq Y \times Z$ defined as:

$$\bar{R}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0.4 & 0.5 \\ 0.3 & 0.7 & 0.7 \\ 0.2 & 0.6 & 0.9 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\bar{R}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 & 0.4 \\ 0.9 & 0.4 \\ 0 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix}$$

The min-max composition can be computed as

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_1,z_1) = \max\{\min(1,0.1),\min(0.4,0.9),\min(0.5,0)\}$  
$\qquad = \max\{0.1,0.4,0\} = 0.4$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_1,z_2) = \max\{\min(1,0.4),\min(0.4,0.4),\min(0.5,0.8)\}$  
$\qquad = \max\{0.4,0.4,0.5\} = 0.5$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_2,z_1) = \max\{\min(0.3,0.1),\min(0.7,0.9),\min(0.7,0)\}$  
$\qquad = \max\{0.1,0.7,0\} = 0.7$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_2,z_2) = \max\{\min(0.3,0.4),\min(0.7,0.4),\min(0.7,0.8)\}$  
$\qquad = \max\{0.3,0.4,0.7\} = 0.7$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_3,z_1) = \max\{\min(0.2,0.1),\min(0.6,0.9),\min(0.9,0)\}$  
$\qquad = \max\{0.1,0.6,0\} = 0.6$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_3,z_2) = \max\{\min(0.2,0.4),\min(0.6,0.4),\min(0.9,0.8)\}$  
$\qquad = \max\{0.2,0.4,0.8\} = 0.8$

Therefore the result obtained by min-max composition is

$$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.4 & 0.5 \\ 0.7 & 0.7 \\ 0.6 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix}$$

The max product composition can be computed as follows:

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_1,z_1)=\max\{(1*0.1),(0.4*0.9),(0.5*0)\}$  
$\qquad =\max\{0.1,0.36,0\}=0.36$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_1,z_2)=\max\{(1*0.4),(0.4*0.4),(0.5*0.8)\}$  
$\qquad =\max\{0.4,0.16,0.4\}=0.4$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_2,z_1)=\max\{(0.3*0.1),(0.7*0.9),(0.7*0)\}$  
$\qquad =\max\{0.03,0.63,0\}=0.63$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_2,z_2)=\max\{(0.3*0.4),(0.7*0.4),(0.7*0.8)\}$  
$\qquad =\max\{0.12,0.28,0.56\}=0.56$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_3,z_1)=\max\{(0.2*0.1),(0.6*0.9),(0.9*0)\}$  
$\qquad =\max\{0.02,0.54,0\}=0.54$

$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2(x_3,z_2)=\max\{(0.2*0.4),(0.6*0.4),(0.9*0.8)\}$  
$\qquad =\max\{0.08,0.24,0.72\}=0.72$

Therefore the result obtained by max-product composition is

$$\bar{R}_1 \circ \bar{R}_2=\begin{bmatrix}0.36&0.4\\0.63&0.56\\0.54&0.72\end{bmatrix}$$

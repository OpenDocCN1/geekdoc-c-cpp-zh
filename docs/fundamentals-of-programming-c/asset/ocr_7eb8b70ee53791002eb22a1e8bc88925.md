Since 0.1 cannot be represented exactly within the constraints of the double-precision floating-point representation, the repeated addition of 0.1 leads to round errors that accumulate over time. Whereas 0.1+0.9 rounded off may, 0.1 added 10 times may be 1.000001 or 0.999999, neither of which is exactly 1.

Listing 9.15 (badfloatcheck.cpp) demonstrates that the == and != operators are questionable when comparing floating-point values. The better approach is to check if two floating-point values are close enough, which means they differ by a very small amount. When comparing two floating-point numbers x and y, we must determine if the absolute value of their difference is small; for example, |x-y| < 0.00001. The abs function was introduced in Section 8.2, and we can incorporate it into an function, as shown in Listing 9.16 (floatequals.cpp).

# 6.3 Abnormal Loop Termination

By default, a while statement executes until its condition becomes false. The executing program checks this condition only at the “top” of the loop. This means that if the Boolean expression that makes the condition becomes false before the program completes executing all the statements within the body of the loop, all the remaining statements in the body must complete before the loop can again check its condition. In other words, the while statement and of it cannot exit the loop somewhere in the middle of its body.

dinarily this behavior is not a problem. Usually the intention is to execute all statements within the body as an indivisible unit. Sometimes, however, it is desirable to immediately exit the body or recheck the condition from the middle of the loop instead. C++ provides the break and continue statements to give programmers more flexibility designing the control logic of loops.

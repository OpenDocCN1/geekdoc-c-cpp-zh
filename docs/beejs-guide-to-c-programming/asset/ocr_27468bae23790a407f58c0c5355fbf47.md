Here the C spec gets a little fury. It assures us that char is a single byte, i.e. sizeof(char) == 1.
But in C$3.6$ it goes out of its way to say:

A byte is composed of a composite sequence of bits, the number of which is implementation-defined.

What—what? Some of you might be used to the notation that a char is 8 bytes, right? I mean, what is it, right? And the answer is, "Almost certainty." But C is an old language, and machines back in the day, had we say, a more relaxed opinion over how many bits were a byte. And through the years, C has retained this flexibility.

But assuming your bytes in C are 8 bytes, they are virtually all in the world that you'll ever see, the range of a char is...

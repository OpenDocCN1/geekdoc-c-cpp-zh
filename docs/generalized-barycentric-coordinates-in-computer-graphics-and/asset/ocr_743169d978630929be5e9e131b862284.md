While existence of a thrust network within a masonry structure is sufficient to guarantee the structure's stability, the thrust line does not necessarily encode the true forces flowing through the structure: many possible thrust networks can exist within a masonry volume, and any one of these certifies the structure's stability. Consider for instance a four-legged stool: the stool stands with any one of its legs removed, and the true force flowing through the four legs cannot be determined by examining the geometry of the stool alone. In practice such static indeterminacy is commonplace.

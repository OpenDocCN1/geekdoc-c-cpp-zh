
where $n\mathcal{V}$ is the dimension of the space $\mathcal{V}_k(P)$. Our goal is to find a coefficient matrix $S$ such that

$$
\nabla_{P,k}\phi_i^{(k)} = \nabla\phi_i^{(k)} + \sum_{a=1}^{n_P} S_{ia}\zeta_a \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n\mathcal{V}.
$$

To explicitly compute $S$, we further define matrices $R$ of size $n\mathcal{V} \times n_P$, and $M$ of size $n_P \times n_P$ with the following forms:

$$
R_{ia} = \oint_{\partial P} (\zeta_a \cdot n) \phi_i^{(k)} ds - \int_P \phi_i^{(k)} \nabla \cdot \zeta_a dx - \int_P \zeta_a \cdot \nabla \phi_i^{(k)} dx,
$$

and

$$
M_{ab} = \oint_{\partial P} \zeta_a \cdot \zeta_b dx.
$$

Replacing $v$ and $p$ with $\phi_i^k$ and $\zeta_a$ in (12.9) yields the linear system of equations

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{n_P} S_{ia} M_{ab} = R_{ib} \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n\mathcal{V} \quad \text{and} \quad b = 1, \dots, n_P.
$$

Therefore, the coefficient matrix is obtained as $S = RM^{-1}$.

In order to better understand the gradient correction scheme, let us denote by $p_v$ the perturbation to $\nabla v$ that gives the corrected gradients, that is,

$$
\nabla_{P,k}v = \nabla v + p_v.
$$

Inserting the above in (12.9), we get for any $p \in [\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(P)]^d$,

$$
\int_P p_v \cdot p_v dx = \left[ -\int_P v \nabla \cdot p \, dx + \oint_{\partial P} v (p \cdot n) ds \right] - \int_P p \cdot \nabla v \, dx. \quad (12.10)
$$

Several observations can be made. First, the integral on the left-hand side of the above equation is exactly computed by the utilized quadrature (recall that we require the quadrature to be exact for polynomial functions up to order $2k-2$). Second, notice the right-hand side of the above expression represents the difference of the two quadrature approximations (the two quadrature approximations are the terms in the bracket and the last term, respectively) of $\int_P p \cdot \nabla v \, dx$. Therefore, (12.10) shows that the perturbations $p_v$ capture the difference between two approximations of $\nabla v$ when integrated against polynomials of order $k-1$. $p_v$ is, in fact, the “smallest” element in $[\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(P)]^d$ with respect to the $L^2$-norm that does so according to (12.7)–(12.8). Third, the smaller the error in the satisfaction of the discrete divergence theorem (i.e., right-hand side of (12.10)), the smaller the perturbation is to the gradient. This error depends on the accuracy of quadrature as well as the nature of the functions in the local space $\mathcal{V}_k(P)$. Last but not least, by setting \(\boldsymbol{p} = \nabla_{P,k}q - \nabla q = \boldsymbol{p}_q\) in (12.10), we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_P (\nabla_{P,k}q - \nabla q)^2 d\boldsymbol{x} &= -\int_P q \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{p}_q d\boldsymbol{x} + \oint_{\partial P} q (\boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \boldsymbol{n}) ds - \int_P \boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \nabla q d\boldsymbol{x} \\
&= - \int_P q \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{p}_q d\boldsymbol{x} + \oint_{\partial P} q (\boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \boldsymbol{n}) ds - \int_P \boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \nabla q d\boldsymbol{x} \\
&= 0,
\end{aligned}
$$

which implies that the corrected gradient coincides with the exact gradient when applied to k-th order polynomials, i.e.,

$$
\nabla_{P,k}q = \nabla q \quad \forall q \in \mathcal{P}_k(P). \qquad (12.11)
$$

This property of the correction scheme plays an important role in ensuring that the resulting discretizations are polynomially consistent and satisfy the patch test.

To aid in better understanding (12.11), we consider the case with linear space \(\mathcal{V}_1(P)\), where an explicit expression for the corrected gradient can be readily obtained. Let \(\boldsymbol{p} \in [\mathcal{P}_0(P)]^d\) and \(v \in \mathcal{V}_1(P)\). We arrive at the following expression for the corrected gradient:

$$
\nabla_{P,1}v = \nabla v + \frac{1}{|P|} \left( \oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds - \int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} \right),
$$

We can see from this expression that the perturbation is a constant field proportional to the error in satisfying the identity \(\int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} = \oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds\) by the volumetric and surface quadrature schemes. This implies that if \(v \in \mathcal{P}_1(P)\), then there is no correction to its gradient, i.e., \(\nabla_{P,1}v = \nabla v\). Additionally, in the 2D case, recall that the variation of \(v\) on \(\partial P\) is piecewise linear, and the boundary gradient is exact. In this case, \(\oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds = \oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds = \int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x}\), and subsequently

$$
\nabla_{P,1}v = \nabla v + \frac{1}{|P|} \left( \int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} - \oint_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} \right). \qquad (12.12)
$$

The perturbation is simply the difference between the volume average of \(\nabla v\) and its approximations through the quadrature. An immediate observation from (12.12) is that

$$
\int_P \nabla_{P,1} v dx = \int_P \nabla v dx.
$$

This shows that the corrected gradient \(\nabla_{P,1}v\) under the action of the quadrature behaves like \(\nabla v\) under exact integration.

Generally, it is expected from the definition of the correction that

$$
\int_P \psi \cdot \nabla_{P,k} v dx - \int_P \psi \cdot \nabla v dx = O(h_P^k) \|\nabla v\|_{L^2(P)^d}
$$

for \(v \in \mathcal{V}_k(P)\) when \(\psi\) is a sufficiently smooth vector field. Here, \(h_P\) is the diameter of element \(P\).

## 12.5.2 Gradient correction for vectorial problems

The gradient correction scheme defined in the preceding subsection for scalar problems can be readily extended to vectorial problems. For a vector field $\mathbf{v} = [v_x, v_y]^T$ (or $\mathbf{v} = [v_x, v_y, v_z]^T$ in 3D) $\in [\mathcal{V}_k(P)]^d$, the gradient correction scheme takes the form

$$
\nabla_{P,k} \otimes \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} (\nabla_{P,k} v_x)^T \ (\nabla_{P,k} v_y)^T \end{bmatrix} \text{ in 2D}, \quad \nabla_{P,k} \otimes \mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} (\nabla_{P,k} v_x)^T \ (\nabla_{P,k} v_y)^T \ (\nabla_{P,k} v_z)^T \end{bmatrix} \text{ in 3D}.
$$

Similar to the scalar case, the corrected gradient satisfies the discrete divergence theorem,

$$
\int_P \mathbf{p} : \nabla_{P,k} \otimes \mathbf{v} \, d\mathbf{x} = \int_{\partial P} (\mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{n}) \cdot \mathbf{v} \, ds - \int_P \mathbf{v} \cdot (\nabla \cdot \mathbf{p}) \, d\mathbf{x} \quad \forall \mathbf{p} \in [\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(P)]^{d \times d},
$$

and, moreover, for any sufficiently smooth second-order tensorial field $\boldsymbol{\psi}$, the element-level consistency error satisfies

$$
\int_P \boldsymbol{\psi} : \nabla_{P,k} \otimes \mathbf{v} \, d\mathbf{x} - \int_P \boldsymbol{\psi} : \nabla \mathbf{v} \, d\mathbf{x} = O(h_P^k) \|\nabla \mathbf{v}\|_{L^2(P)^{d \times d}}.
$$

In the computational implementation of 2D problems, let us assume the set of basis functions $\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_1^{(k)}, \dots, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{2n\mathcal{V}}^{(k)}\}$ of $[\mathcal{V}_k(P)]^2$ are of the form

$$
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{2i-1} = [\phi_i^{(k)}, 0]^T, \quad \boldsymbol{\phi}_{2i} = [0, \phi_i^{(k)}]^T
$$

for $i=1, \dots, n\mathcal{V}$, and where $n\mathcal{V}$ is the dimension of the space $\mathcal{V}_k(P)$. The correction scheme for vectorial problems in practice amounts to correcting each basis function as follows:

$$
\nabla_{P,k} \otimes \boldsymbol{\phi}_{2i-1}^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} (\nabla_{P,k} \phi_i^{(k)})^T \ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \nabla_{P,k} \otimes \boldsymbol{\phi}_{2i}^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \ (\nabla_{P,k} \phi_i^{(k)})^T \end{bmatrix}
$$

for $i=1, \dots, n\mathcal{V}$, and where $\nabla_{P,k} \boldsymbol{\phi}_i^{(k)}$ is the computed corrected gradient of the shape function according to the procedure outlined for the scalar problem.

Similarly, let us assume the set of basis functions for $\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_1^{(k)}, \dots, \boldsymbol{\phi}_{3n\mathcal{V}}^{(k)}\}$ of the 3D space $[\mathcal{V}_k(P)]^3$ are of the form

$$
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{3i-2}^{(k)} = [\phi_i^{(k)}, 0, 0]^T, \quad \boldsymbol{\phi}_{3i-1}^{(k)} = [0, \phi_i^{(k)}, 0]^T, \quad \text{and} \quad \boldsymbol{\phi}_{3i}^{(k)} = [0, 0, \phi_i^{(k)}]^T
$$

for $i=1, \dots, n\mathcal{V}$. The gradient correction scheme for 3D vectorial problems corrects each basis function as

$$
\nabla_{P,k} \otimes \boldsymbol{\phi}_{3i-2}^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} (\nabla_{P,k} \phi_i^{(k)})^T \ \mathbf{0} \ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \nabla_{P,k} \otimes \boldsymbol{\phi}_{3i-1}^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \ (\nabla_{P,k} \phi_i^{(k)})^T \ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}, \quad \nabla_{P,k} \otimes \boldsymbol{\phi}_{3i}^{(k)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{0} \ \mathbf{0} \ (\nabla_{P,k} \phi_i^{(k)})^T \end{bmatrix} \quad (i=1, \dots, n\mathcal{V}).
$$

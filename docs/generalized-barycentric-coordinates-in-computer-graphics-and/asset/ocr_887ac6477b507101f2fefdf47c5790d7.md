# 7.4.2 Shape interpolation

Planar shape interpolation is a classical problem in computer graphics. It is important for creating animation sequences and for exploring shape spaces. A naive and widely used interpolation approach is to linearly blend the input maps. If f^1(z) and f^2(z) are conformal, then f^t(z) = (1-t)f^1(z) + t f^2(z), for t ∈ [0, 1] is holomorphic but is not, in general, conformal. However, generating conformal maps based on the logarithm of the derivative (see Section 7.4.1) also leads to a natural way for interpolating two or more conformal maps. The idea is to linearly blend the log derivatives rather than the maps themselves, and then to reconstruct the map (which is conformal by construction). For brevity, let us consider the most common case where we want to blend a given conformal map f with the identity map. Since the logarithm of the derivative of the identity map is 0 we have l'(z) = t l(z), where l(z) is the log derivative of f(z). Blending l linearly leads to natural visually pleasing results, since rotations (the imaginary part of l) are blended linearly.

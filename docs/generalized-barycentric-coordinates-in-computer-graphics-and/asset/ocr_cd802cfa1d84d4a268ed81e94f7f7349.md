where $n\mathcal{V}$ is the dimension of the space $\mathcal{V}_k(P)$. Our goal is to find a coefficient matrix $S$ such that

$$
\nabla_{P,k}\phi_i^{(k)} = \nabla\phi_i^{(k)} + \sum_{a=1}^{n_P} S_{ia}\zeta_a \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n\mathcal{V}.
$$

To explicitly compute $S$, we further define matrices $R$ of size $n\mathcal{V} \times n_P$, and $M$ of size $n_P \times n_P$ with the following forms:

$$
R_{ia} = \oint_{\partial P} (\zeta_a \cdot n) \phi_i^{(k)} ds - \oint_P \phi_i^{(k)} \nabla \cdot \zeta_a dx - \oint_P \zeta_a \cdot \nabla \phi_i^{(k)} dx,
$$

and

$$
M_{ab} = \oint_{\partial P} \zeta_a \cdot \zeta_b dx.
$$

Replacing $v$ and $p$ with $\phi_i^k$ and $\zeta_a$ in (12.9) yields the linear system of equations

$$
\sum_{a=1}^{n_P} S_{ia} M_{ab} = R_{ib} \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, n\mathcal{V} \quad \text{and} \quad b = 1, \dots, n_P.
$$

Therefore, the coefficient matrix is obtained as $S = RM^{-1}$.

In order to better understand the gradient correction scheme, let us denote by $p_v$ the perturbation to $\nabla v$ that gives the corrected gradients, that is,

$$
\nabla_{P,k}v = \nabla v + p_v.
$$

Inserting the above in (12.9), we get for any $p \in [\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(P)]^d$,

$$
\oint_P p_v \cdot p_v dx = \left[ -\oint_P v \nabla \cdot p \, dx + \oint_{\partial P} v (p \cdot n) ds \right] - \oint_P p \cdot \nabla v \, dx. \quad (12.10)
$$

Several observations can be made. First, the integral on the left-hand side of the above equation is exactly computed by the utilized quadrature (recall that we require the quadrature to be exact for polynomial functions up to order $2k-2$). Second, notice the right-hand side of the above expression represents the difference of the two quadrature approximations (the two quadrature approximations are the terms in the bracket and the last term, respectively) of $\int_P p \cdot \nabla v \, dx$. Therefore, (12.10) shows that the perturbations $p_v$ capture the difference between two approximations of $\nabla v$ when integrated against polynomials of order $k-1$. $p_v$ is, in fact, the “smallest” element in $[\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(P)]^d$ with respect to the $L^2$-norm that does so according to (12.7)–(12.8). Third, the smaller the error in the satisfaction of the discrete divergence theorem (i.e., right-hand side of (12.10)), the smaller the perturbation is to the gradient.

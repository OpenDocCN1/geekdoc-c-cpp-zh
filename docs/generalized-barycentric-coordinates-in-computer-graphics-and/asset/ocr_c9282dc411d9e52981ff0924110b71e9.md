For the special case of mean value mappings, this bound can provably be satisfied for mappings between any convex polygons (see Chapter 2). Figure 4.8 shows that with enough intermediate steps the mapping $f$ becomes bijective.

In particular, there exists some $m \in \mathbb{N}$ such that the uniform partition $t_k$ with $t_k = k/m$ gives a bijective composite barycentric mapping $f_{r_m}$. # 4.3.1 Limit of composite barycentric mappings

The idea of uniform composite barycentric mappings leads to the interesting question of the behavior in the limit. To this end, we consider the *infinite composite barycentric mapping* $f_\infty = \lim_{m \to \infty} f_{r_m}$ and its backward mapping $g_\infty : \Omega' \to \Omega^0$. Figure 4.10 shows the result of mapping a star with a uniform composite mapping $f_{r_m}$ composed with its backward mapping $g_{r_m} : \Omega^1 \to \Omega^0$ with the same number of steps. Computing the maximum distance $\|x - g_{r_m}(f_{r_m}(x))\|$ for one million random points $x \in \Omega^0$ indicates that this distance converges to zero and so $g_{r_m} = f_{r_m}^{-1}$ as $m \to \infty$. Consequently, the inverse of an infinite barycentric mapping is likely to be a infinite barycentric mapping itself, which is not the case for standard barycentric mappings.

This observation can be proven by considering the difference between two successive steps and taking its limit [145]. For any point $x^0 \in \Omega^0$ we evaluate the first step $f_0$ of the composite mapping $f$,

```
$$x^{t_1} = f_0(x^0) = \sum_{i=1}^n \phi_i^{t_0}(x^0) \psi_i(t_1).$$
```

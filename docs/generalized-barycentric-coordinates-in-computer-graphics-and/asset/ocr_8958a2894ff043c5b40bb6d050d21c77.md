by setting \(\boldsymbol{p} = \nabla_{P,k}q - \nabla q = \boldsymbol{p}_q\) in (12.10), we have

```
\(\int_P (\nabla_{P,k}q - \nabla q)^2 d\boldsymbol{x} = -\int_P q \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{p}_q d\boldsymbol{x} + \oint_{\partial P} q (\boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \boldsymbol{n}) ds - \oint_P \boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \nabla q d\boldsymbol{x}\)
\(= - \int_P q \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol{p}_q d\boldsymbol{x} + \oint_{\partial P} q (\boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \boldsymbol{n}) ds - \int_P \boldsymbol{p}_q \cdot \nabla q d\boldsymbol{x}\)
\(= 0,\)
```

which implies that the corrected gradient coincides with the exact gradient when applied to k-th order polynomials, i.e.,

```
\(\nabla_{P,k}q = \nabla q \quad \forall q \in \mathcal{P}_k(P).\) (12.11)
```

This property of the correction scheme plays an important role in ensuring that the resulting discretizations are polynomially consistent and satisfy the patch test.

To aid in better understanding (12.11), we consider the case with linear space \(\mathcal{V}_1(P)\), where an explicit expression for the corrected gradient can be readily obtained. Let \(\boldsymbol{p} \in [\mathcal{P}_0(P)]^d\) and \(v \in \mathcal{V}_1(P)\). We arrive at the following expression for the corrected gradient:

```
\(\nabla_{P,1}v = \nabla v + \frac{1}{|P|} \left( \oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds - \int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} \right),\)
```

which is similar to the corrected strain operator in the meshfree literature [296]. We can see from this expression that the perturbation is a constant field proportional to the error in satisfying the identity \(\int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} = \oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds\) by the volumetric and surface quadrature schemes. This implies that if \(v \in \mathcal{P}_1(P)\), then there is no correction to its gradient, i.e., \(\nabla_{P,1}v = \nabla v\). Additionally, in the 2D case, recall that the variation of \(v\) on \(\partial P\) is piecewise linear, and the boundary gradient is exact. In this case, \(\oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds = \oint_{\partial P} v \boldsymbol{n} ds = \int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x}\), and subsequently

```
\(\nabla_{P,1}v = \nabla v + \frac{1}{|P|} \left( \int_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} - \oint_P \nabla v d\boldsymbol{x} \right).\) (12.12)
```

The perturbation is simply the difference between the volume average of \(\nabla v\) and its approximations through the quadrature. An immediate observation from (12.12) is that

```
\(\oint_P \nabla_{P,1} v dx = \int_P \nabla v dx.\)
```

This shows that the corrected gradient \(\nabla_{P,1}v\) under the action of the quadrature behaves like \(\nabla v\) under exact integration.

Generally, it is expected from the definition of the correction that

```
\(\oint_P \psi \cdot \nabla_{P,K} v dx - \int_P \psi \cdot \nabla v dx = O(h_P^k) ||\nabla v||_{L^2(P)^d}\)
```

for \(v \in \mathcal{V}_k(P)\) when \(\psi\) is a sufficiently smooth vector field. Here, \(h_P\) is the diameter of element \(P\).

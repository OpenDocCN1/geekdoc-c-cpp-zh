To assist the formal definition of the gradient correction scheme for scalar problems, we consider a basis $\{\zeta_1, \dots, \zeta_n\}$ for $[P_{k-1}(P)]^d$ and replace the equality constraint (12.8) by the equivalent set of constraints:

$\oint_P \zeta_a \cdot \xi \, dx = -\int_P v \nabla \cdot \zeta_a \, dx + \oint_{\partial P} v(\zeta_a \cdot n) \, ds, \quad a = 1, \dots, n$

Introducing multipliers $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$, one obtains the Lagrangian:

$\mathcal{L}(\xi, \lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n) = \oint_P \|\xi - \nabla v\|^2 \, dx + \sum_{a=1}^n \lambda_a \left[ \oint_P \zeta_a \cdot \xi \, dx + \int_P v \nabla \cdot \zeta_a \, dx - \oint_{\partial P} v(\zeta_a \cdot n) \, ds \right].$

The optimality of $\nabla_{P,k}v$ requires that for any variation $\eta$,

$D_\xi \mathcal{L}(\nabla_{P,k}v, \lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)[\eta] = 2 \oint_P (\nabla_{P,k}v - \nabla v) \cdot \eta \, dx + \sum_{a=1}^n \lambda_a \oint_P \zeta_a \cdot \eta \, dx = 0,$

and therefore

$\oint_P \left( \nabla_{P,k}v - \nabla v + \sum_{a=1}^n \frac{1}{2} \lambda_a \zeta_a \right) \cdot \eta \, dx = 0.$

This shows that the perturbation $\nabla_{P,k}v - \nabla v$ coincides with an element of $[P_{k-1}(P)]^d$ at the location of the quadrature points, because the optimization problem (12.7)-(12.8) can only prescribe the values of $\nabla_{P,k}v$ at the quadrature points. Therefore, we can officially define the corrected gradient $\nabla_{P,k}v$ as the field satisfying the following two conditions:

+   \(\bullet \quad \nabla_{P,k}v - \nabla v \in [P_{k-1}(P)]^d.\)

+   \(\bullet \quad \text{For all } p \in [P_{k-1}(P)]^d,\)

\(\oint_P p \cdot \nabla_{P,k}v \, dx = -\oint_P v \nabla \cdot p \, dx + \oint_{\partial P} v(p \cdot n) \, ds. \quad (12.9)\)

We observe from these two conditions that $\nabla_{P,k}$ is a linear map, and so in practice only the action of $\nabla_{P,k}$ on the basis of $\mathcal{V}_k(P)$ must be computed.

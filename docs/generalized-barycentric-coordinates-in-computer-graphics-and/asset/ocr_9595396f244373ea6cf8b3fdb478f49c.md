```
\(\min_{f_1, \dots, f_n} \frac{1}{2} \int_{\Omega} |g''(w)|^2 ds,\)
\(\text{s.t. } g(r_j) = q_j, \quad j = 1, \dots, p,\)
(7.20)
```

where \(f_i \in \mathbb{C}\) are the variables, \(g(z)\) is the discrete Cauchy transform, and \(g''(z) = \sum_{k=1}^n C''_k(z)f_i\) is its second complex derivative. The choice of \(g''\) rather than \(g'\) in (7.20) is due to the fact that the second complex derivative of a similarity transformation \(S(z) = \alpha z + \beta\) is zero everywhere. Therefore, if the user transforms the P2P handles using a similarity \(S(z)\), the solution to the optimization is \(f_i = S(z_i)\) with the corresponding map \(g(z) = S(z)\) having zero energy. In particular, the map \(g\) reproduces the identity map if the P2P handles are fixed, that is, if \(g(r_j) = r_j, j = 1, \dots, p\), then \(g(z) = z\). Luckily, the derivatives of Cauchy coordinates possess simple closed-form expressions [414, Appendix C-E]. In particular the second derivative is given by

```
\(C''_k(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \left( \frac{1}{B_{k-1}(z)B_k(z)} - \frac{1}{B_k(z)B_{k+1}(z)} \right).\)
(7.21)
```

The boundary integral in (7.20), however, is approximated numerically. The simplest way to approximate the integral is to substitute it with a summation over a dense set of \(\mathbb{K} \gg n\) boundary samples, where the sampling should be as uniform as possible. This allows us to assume that the differential \(ds\) is approximately constant. While Cauchy coordinates have well-defined limits at the vertices [414, Appendix B], the second derivative in (7.21) is singular at the vertices. There are two possible ways to avoid these singularities. One is to exclude the cage vertices from the set of samples (note that (7.21) is well-defined on the open edges). A second choice is to offset the source cage in the outward normal direction by a very small amount, and to use the outward offset cage as our new cage. The original cage (which is strictly inside the offset cage) is then sampled. This choice also makes the evaluation of the Cauchy coordinates themselves simpler. With the integral being approximated as a sum, we can express (7.20) in matrix form

```
\(\min_{\boldsymbol{f}} \frac{1}{2} \|\boldsymbol{E}\boldsymbol{f}\|^2,\)
\(\text{s.t. } \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{f} = \boldsymbol{q},\)
(7.22)
```

where \(\boldsymbol{f} \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times 1}\) is the complex column vector with \(f_i\) as entries, \(\boldsymbol{E} \in \mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{K} \times n}\) is the matrix of second complex derivatives of the Cauchy coordinates evaluated at the samples, \(\boldsymbol{A} \in \mathbb{C}^{p \times n}\) is the matrix of Cauchy coordinates evaluated at the P2P handles, and \(\boldsymbol{q} \in \mathbb{C}^{p \times 1}\) is the vector with the user-specified target P2P positions.

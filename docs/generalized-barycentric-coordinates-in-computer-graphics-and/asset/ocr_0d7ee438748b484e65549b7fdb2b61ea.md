\(\frac{\partial^{n-1}u}{\partial e_1^{n-1}}(x + \rho_1 e_\theta) = \frac{\partial^{n-1}u}{\partial e_1^{n-1}}(x) + \rho_1 u^{(n)}(x) + \cdots + \frac{\rho_1^{2n}}{(2n)!} \frac{\partial^{2n}u}{\partial e_\theta^{2n}}(x),\)
\(\frac{\partial^{n-1}u}{\partial e_1^{n-1}}(x - \rho_2 e_\theta) = \frac{\partial^{n-1}u}{\partial e_1^{n-1}}(x) - \rho_2 u^{(n)}(x) + \cdots + \frac{(-\rho_2)^{2n}}{(2n)!} \frac{\partial^{2n}u}{\partial e_\theta^{2n}}(x).\)

Eliminating terms in \(\partial u(x)/\partial e_\theta, \dots, \partial^{2n-1}u(x)/\partial e_\theta^{2n-1}\) we arrive at

\(\frac{\partial^{2n}u}{\partial e_\theta^{2n}}(x) = \frac{(2n)!}{\rho_1^n \rho_2^n} [u(x) - \alpha_1 u(y_1) - \alpha_2 u(y_2) - \cdots\)
\(- \alpha_{2n-1} \frac{\partial^{n-1}u}{\partial e_\theta^{n-1}}(y_1) - \alpha_{2n} \frac{\partial^{n-1}u}{\partial e_\theta^{n-1}}(y_2)],\)

where \(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_{2n}\) are some weights. Combining with (3.29) and mimicking polyharmonic interpolation gives a high-order generalization of the modified Gordon-Wixom interpolation scheme.

# ## 3.4 GENERALIZED MEAN VALUE POTENTIALS AND DISTANCE FUNCTION APPROXIMATIONS

In this section we study properties of generalized mean value potentials, which are generalizations of the normalization function for the transfinite mean value coordinates; that is, the denominator in (3.4). We show that these generalized mean value potentials deliver accurate approximations of the distance function \(\text{dist}(x, \partial\Omega)\) near \(\partial\Omega\) and can be seen as smooth distance functions. Efficient computation of smooth distance functions is important for a number of applications, including pattern recognition [435], computational mechanics [157], medical imaging [338], and computational fluid dynamics [332, 399].

# ### 3.4.1 Generalized mean value potentials for smooth domains

Floater et al. [77, 133] observed some remarkable properties of the mean value normalization function

\(\Phi(x) = \int_{S_x} \frac{d\theta}{\|x - y\|},\)

which is the denominator in (3.4). In particular, for \(\Psi(x) = 1/\Phi(x)\), they show that

\(\Psi(y) = 0 \text{ and } \frac{\partial \Psi(y)}{\partial n_y} = \frac{1}{V_{d-1}} \quad \forall y \in \partial\Omega, \quad (3.30)\)

where \(n_y\) is the outer unit normal for the boundary of \(\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d\) at \(y \in \partial\Omega\) and \(V_{d-1}\) is the volume of the unit ball in \(\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\) (\(V_1 = 2\) and \(V_2 = \pi\)). In view of (3.30), the function \(V_{d-1}\Psi(x)\) delivers an accurate approximation of the distance function \(\text{dist}(x, \partial\Omega)\) near \(\partial\Omega\).

As a designer, it's your job to strike a balance between telling a good story and making a good game. To do that, you need to know there are some hidden parts of stories and games that make them good.

Now comes the part of the book when I throw a whole bunch of concepts at you. All of these play an important part in building good interactive fiction games. Don't stress yourself on trying to achieve all of them, but try to keep them in mind. As you build more games, there's no doubt you'll get better at incorporating them into your designs! For your first few designs, try to just focus on one or two elements. As you get more comfortable, expand your designs to incorporate more. Soon, you'll be a master!

# Story Concepts

There are five very important parts that help a story come together and allow the reader—who is, in our case, also a player—follow along. Those parts are character, setting, plot, conflict, and resolution.

+   - Characters (and items): These are the people and things that the players interact with as they move through your game. Some are important to the story, while others just add flavor.
- Settings: These are the places where your story takes place. Some stories have a lot of places, while others just use a few. The setting should make sense with what your story is about. If you're creating a game that takes place in space, the settings should match!
- Plot: This is the heart of your story; it is the main idea that your story is about and the sequence of events that happen. There may be smaller, unrelated events that happen in your story, but the plot is the main set of events that your game is really about.

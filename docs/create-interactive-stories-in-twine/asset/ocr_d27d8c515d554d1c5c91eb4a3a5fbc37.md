You’ll probably notice a green spaceship in the upper left corner of the passage. This is the story start icon. When your game launches, it will start with this passage—which explains the rocket. You can change which passage is the start passage if you need to, but for now, just leave it be. Go ahead and double click on the passage to bring up the edit screen. You should see this:

![](bbox=[0.198, 0.26, 0.796, 0.368])

There are three parts to a passage: the title, the tags, and the body.

+   **Title**: Big surprise—this is the name of the passage. It is not displayed to the player. Instead, it’s there to help you know what the passage contains and is how you link to the passage. When you name your passage, try to keep it descriptive and short!

+   **Tags**: These are little labels that you can attach to your passages. Usually, they just help you group or categorize your passages. Occasionally, you can use them when designing your game. The startup tag is one example we will explore later.

+   **Body**: This is where you will put your game. The story goes here. The little bits of code that make it interactive also go here. They get mashed together in a way that is natural and accessible, so don’t worry about having to learn how to write a whole bunch of complex code! You’ll need to learn some easy tools that will allow you to do cool things. We’ll cover those as we work on building our first game. For now, just know that this is the main work area for building your game!

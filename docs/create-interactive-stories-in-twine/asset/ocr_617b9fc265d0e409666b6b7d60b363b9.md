# Changing Text with Links and Clicks

You can also create clickable links in your story that do other things besides moving the reader on to the next passage. You can use them to reveal new information, shift, or change what is shown to the reader.

These changes are done with macros. That word might be scary in computer science, but don't worry. It's actually pretty simple! We will be using link and click macros, and they have a specific format that's a little different from what we have done so far. Check out this example:

```
(macro: "original displayed text\)[new text]
```

The macro is the portion surrounded by parentheses. The original text will be inside the rounded brackets and inside of quotation marks. When the text is recognized properly, it will be colored teal when editing.

The new text will be added to—or replacing—the original in square brackets right after your macro. What you put in the single square brackets is called the hook and is usually changed, modified, or affected by a macro. When editing, the square bracket text will display as black.

We discussed earlier how, with Twine, you can write how you normally would and add some snippets to expand or affect the game. We saw that with the style changes, but those were just a few extra characters added. Macros are the first example of code pieces that are added to passages and act on your story. Some macros get mixed right in your writing, while others just need to be in the passage and will have an effect on the entire thing.

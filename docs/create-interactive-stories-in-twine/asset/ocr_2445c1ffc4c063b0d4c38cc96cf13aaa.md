Welcome back—I hope you chose wisely! Though that's just a simple example, you really get to the heart of what interactive fiction games are all about. You have a story that presents the players with decisions. If players are thoughtful—or just lucky—their choices help move the story forward, and the players keep reading and playing. The wrong decisions can set the players back or end their game!

With a conventional story, you—the author—would have a lot of control. You know exactly how it is going to play out, when the reader is going to discover that the old lady is actually an alien, or when the two main characters are going to fall in love.

In CYOP, the players are like readers in a conventional story. Except, as they go through the story, they get to make decisions when prompted and turn to the right page to find out what happened—which, hopefully, moves them along in the story.

Whenever you're creating or designing something, having a sheet for writing down your thoughts and ideas for the project is good. It provides a space to collect all of the ideas that may come to you. You can organize and keep track of all them in one place.

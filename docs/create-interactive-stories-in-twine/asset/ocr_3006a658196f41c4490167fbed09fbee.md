So when you start coming up with characters for your story, you've got to remember that everyone you create will interact with the player as he or she moves through your game.

+   **Cues:** Cues are related to the challenges and conflict in your game. It's not a lot of fun—when you're playing a game—to just blindly guess what choice you need to make. Make the right choice and you live. Make the wrong choice and you die. That's obviously pretty important! You want players to have an idea of how to make an informed and interesting decision based on information they got earlier in the game. These can be hints and clues the player gets from items and characters they encounter in the story that they use to help make informed decisions. Going back to control, players may get to a point early in the game where their decisions aren't based on anything, and they choose blindly and die. But in a great game, players can go back and explore more to find the clues or items they need to move forward!

+   **Audience:** When designing games and writing stories, it's always important to keep in mind who your audience is going to be. You want the content, language, and challenges to be appropriate for who you're writing for.

+   **Decision points:** These are key to interactive fiction games. They are a main part of what make it a game! They should be challenging, interesting, fun, and engaging. Players should want to make these decisions to move the story forward!

+   **Paths:** As the name kind of implies, paths are the different choices and directions that your story can go as the readers play your game.

+   **Interactions:** Just like paths, interactions are pretty explanatory. They're what you can do to the things in your game: pull, push, talk ... anything you can imagine!

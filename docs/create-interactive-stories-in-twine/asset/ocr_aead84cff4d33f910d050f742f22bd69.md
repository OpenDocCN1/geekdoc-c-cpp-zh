+   - One-time use: You can also have objects that have special one-time uses that the players need to hold onto and use at the right moment. They may need a match to get them safely through a dark area, bait to catch a small animal, or a potion to give them temporary flight. In all of these cases, the players can only use the object once! So the trick is to get the players to think carefully about when time is, or they need to go back, get another item, or start over again.

Similar to objects the players need and carry with them, you can also have obstacles that require the players have done something first before they can get past. They might need to have talked with an important character, or learned a secret password before they can enter an area. They might need to have accomplished something, like rescuing the prince before they enter the castle. Passing these obstacles doesn't require that the players have something but rather that they know or have done something.

You won't put this information in the narrative parts that are read loud to the players. That wouldn't be fun at all! Instead, these are a part of the rules that will be outlined for the person running the game. Because these rules usually affect two locations—the place where the players find or learn something and the place where they use it—it is best to include it in both locations.

For example, let's say the players need to use a key they find in the study to later open the chest in the basement. When they find the key, under the rules, you can state:

+   - The key is small and ornate and seems important. The key will open the chest found in the basement.

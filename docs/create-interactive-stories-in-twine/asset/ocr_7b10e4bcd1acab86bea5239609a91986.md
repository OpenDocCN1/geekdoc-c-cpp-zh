So, unlike the CYOP game you created, where the players were always moving forward in the story, in a Parsely game, you can spread your clues, tools, and pieces around throughout the game. Now, it's all well and good to encourage exploration, but you don't want to confuse it with your story arc and the main things that you want to happen in the story.

For example, let's say you want the players to have their way past a rabid banana that is guarding the path to the city of Fruitopia. And to get past the banana, they will need to have a very hungry gorilla accompanying them. Who else is going to be able to take on and eat the rabid banana, right?! Without the gorilla, it will end horribly for the players.

This is where you, as the designer, get to have fun with your players. You don't want to find them just before they run into the banana. If you do, it becomes obvious what they will need to do and they are guaranteed to find the gorilla right before meeting the banana and will always be prepared to deal with it. Though success is fun, having to work for that success is even better!

Instead, the gorilla should be in a location a few rooms away, and may have its own challenge make it difficult to acquire. But keeping them apart creates a problem for the players to solve without an immediately obvious solution!

They may run the rabid banana first—before finding the gorilla—and die a horrible death at the hands of the yellow fruit. They can then load their game and explore other areas, looking for a way to help them get around the banana and discover the gorilla!

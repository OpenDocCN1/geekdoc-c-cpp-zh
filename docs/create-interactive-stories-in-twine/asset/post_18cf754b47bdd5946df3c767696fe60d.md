# Getting Loopy

- 10–15 minutes Idea sheet, sticky notes, pencil

## Activity Overview

So far, you've created some simple decision points in your game, letting the players choose between a few options. One choice moved the players forward, the other ended the game and they would need to start over.

There be times when things end so horribly that the players need to start over from the beginning. But if every wrong choice forced the players to have to start from the beginning, players might get tired of having to go through the same material again and again as they try to reach the end of your game. That's a problem!

## Instructions

Take a look at the block you placed just before your final block. Think of a decision that players could make that would not move them to the end but also wouldn't force them to start over.

For example, in the example right before I defeat the warlock, I need to speak the words of power to weaken him. Speaking the words of power moves forward in the story to where I defeat the wizard. Maybe at the block just prior, I can add another choice to throw a rock at the wizard. Because players are so close to winning, I don't want to just end the game by choosing wrong, so I can loop them back to the block where they need to choose what to do to defeat the wizard.

So, I can add a new block between the final block and the one where I made the choice. I have a new outcome, and so I would draw an arrow from the decision block to this new outcome block. In this block, I can write that the rock bounces harmlessly off the wizard and I better think of something more effective. I can then draw an arrow from this block back to the block that I made the decision. This, as you can probably tell, creates a loop!

If the players choose to throw the rock, they learn that it didn't work well and they are brought back to make a different decision. This is explained by the path of the arrows, so look at it.

- 10–15 minutes Idea sheet, sticky notes, pencil

# Activity Overview

Another approach is to start from the final event and work backward, thinking about what needs to happen just prior to a later event.

In the example of my story, I needed to defeat the warlock on top of the mountain. So I need to think: What happens before the warlock is defeated? Well, in order to get past the warlock, I need to speak the words of power. And before I can do that, I need to get to the top of the mountain.

# Instructions

Think about your ending and what the players might need to do just before they reach that ending. Place a block just to the left of your final block. Like your earlier blocks, give the block a name and fill in details about that moment and events. Because it is a decision point, draw a line from this block to your final block. What other decisions could you present to the reader that would take them away from defeating the wizard or would end in defeat?

As a designer—and you're a real one now—it can be helpful to be able to think forward and backward as you work on your game. Remembering your starting point as you fill in the rest of the events will make it easier to help your story connect and get from that starting point to the end of the game!

# Funky Fiction Flow

Up to now, you have been thinking about moving your game forward as the readers make the correct choices at their decision points.

This is a great start, but you can use some different tools that allow the players to maintain the progress they made while also introducing interesting choices into the game. The next three activities are meant to give you some more tools for your arsenal of awesomeness to draw from when building your game!

# Getting Loopy

- 10–15 minutes Idea sheet, sticky notes, pencil

## Activity Overview

So far, you've created some simple decision points in your game, letting the players choose between a few options. One choice moved the players forward, the other ended the game and they would need to start over.

There may be times when things end so horribly that the players need to start over from the beginning. But if every wrong choice forced the players to have to start from the beginning, players might get tired of having to go through the same material again and again as they try to reach the end of your game. That's a problem!

## Instructions

Take a look at the block you placed just before your final block. Think of a decision that players could make that would not move them to the end but also wouldn't force them to start over.

For example, in the example right before I defeat the warlock, I need to speak the words of power to weaken him. Speaking the words of power moves forward in the story to where I defeat the wizard. Maybe at the block just prior, I can add another choice to throw a rock at the wizard. Because players are so close to winning, I don't want to just end the game by choosing wrong, so I can loop them back to the block where they need to choose what to do to defeat the wizard.

So, I can add a new block between the final block and the one where I made the choice. I have a new outcome, and so I would draw an arrow from the decision block to this new outcome block. In this block, I can write that the rock bounces harmlessly off the wizard and I better think of something more effective. I can then draw an arrow from this block back to the block that I made the decision. This, as you can probably tell, creates a loop!

If the players choose to throw the rock, they learn that it didn't work well and they are brought back to make a different decision. This is explained by the path of the arrows, so look at it.

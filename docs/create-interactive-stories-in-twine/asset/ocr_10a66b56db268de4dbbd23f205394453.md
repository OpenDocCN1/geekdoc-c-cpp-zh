Exits: These are the directions that the player can go; north, south, east, and west are what you'll use most. You may also use up or down, if you want to have players climbing trees or using stairs. For simplicity's sake, when creating your map, try to keep the possible directions that players can go from a particular space down to a maximum of four. This makes it easier for you when designing and keeps it clear for the players when they are running the game!

If you start making "diagonal" exits, like northeast or southwest, it can get messy. Also, remember to keep paths between locations straight! Avoid including elbow corners on your map. When you are first creating your game, you may not have all of the directions on your map right away. You may still be adding locations as you continue working on your game, and that's all fine. Just make sure you can fit all your ideas in eventually!

Rules: The rules help define—for the person running the game—what the players can and can't do in the space. You don't need to think of every possible thing that can be done; just try to get down the ones that are most important for your story. The "computer" will come up with fun, interesting reactions to commands that don't make sense or aren't defined by your rules. That's part of the fun of being the computer!

Your rules don't have to be complex. You can use short, simple sentences to define them. And there's more than one way to write your rules! For example, if the players need to use a key to unlock a door, you could write any of the following rules:

The door is locked. It will open with the key from the chest.

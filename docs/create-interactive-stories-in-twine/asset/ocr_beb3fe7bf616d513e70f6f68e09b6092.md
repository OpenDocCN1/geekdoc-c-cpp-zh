Or—if you want to get fancy—your map can be decorative and elaborate, with all kinds of cool details. A decorative map isn't necessary, but it makes the experience more fun for the person running the game. The decorations can be strictly thematic, or they can give visual hints to the player running the game where things are located. No matter what, it's important to remember that the players never actually see the map. As they listen to and explore your story, they will have-to keep track of where they are and where they went in their heads.

Regardless of which map you decide to make, the map needs to have a few elements that are clear and legible for the person who will be running the game.

First, each location space on the map must have clear and readable names. These can be in the spaces themselves, or right next to them. However you label the spaces, the person running the game must be able to glance at the location and immediately know what the name of that location is.

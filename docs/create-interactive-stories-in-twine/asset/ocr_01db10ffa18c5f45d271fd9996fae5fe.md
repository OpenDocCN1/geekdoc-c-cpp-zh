You can include it either in the text leading to the macro or in the text being acted on by the macro. Just like the passage links, the link macros will show as blue to the readers, letting them know they can click on them.

+   - Link (link: "text a")[text b]
  "text a" will be shown to the reader and be clickable.
  When the reader clicks on "text a," it will be replaced where it is in the story by "text b."
- Example:
  - My memories of the exam leave me (link: "hopeful ...")broken!"
  - The sentence will first read: "My memories of the exam leave hopeful ..." When "hopeful" is clicked, the sentence will change and read: "My memories of the exam leave me broken!"
- Note: Notice there is a space after "me" and the start of the link macro—which leaves a space between "me" and the word "hopeful." I could have also typed: me(link: "hopeful..."), putting the space before the word "hopeful." Either would keep a space for the reader between the two words. But if you include the space in the link macro, you'll also need to include it in the replacement text, i.e. \[broken!]"

+   - Link reveal (link-reveal: "text a")[text b]
  "text a" will have [text b] added after it. Imagine that "text b" is there—hidden—and when you click on "text a," the sentence expands to reveal "text b," which was hiding there the whole time.
- Example:
  - I clicked the (link-reveal: "button.")[A door revealed itself behind the panel.

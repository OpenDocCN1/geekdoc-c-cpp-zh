## Text Me

10–15 minutes

## Activity Overview

Okay, so that was underwhelming, I admit. But hey, you've got to start somewhere, right? Close the tab that opened up with the game, and let's get back to the story grid!

I think this story game is missing some, well, story. So let's add some! You saw from the last activity that anything you type will show up as a part of your game. You don't have to do anything special—just type it.

You may want to add some extra styling and bling, though. There are some styles that you can add by simply adding a few characters around the text you want to change:

- **bold** can be added with two single apostrophes on both ends:
  - 'Look at my "bold statement"'
- *Italics* can be added with two forward slashes on both ends:
  - OI //really, really// like your hair.
- ~~Strikethrough~~ can be added with two tildes on both ends:
  - OI really, ~~hate~~ like your hair.
- *Superscript* can be added using two carets on both ends:
  - OIts the worst, like 1000^^2^^

## Instructions

Now it's your turn! Edit your starting passage and try a few different styles out. Remember, you'll need to put the proper characters on both sides of the text you want to change. If you wind up seeing the characters (like the tilde or the slashes) you are trying to use to change the text, that means you made a mistake. Luckily, that's easy to fix. If so, go back and edit the passage. Take a close look and try to fix what went wrong. This is called debugging an error. With everything in good order, press the play icon at the bottom to test out and see how your new styles look!

You'll also notice that writing a game in Twine uses a lot of natural language. You write how you would normally write, and add some extra bits to adjust it to the game. That is what is great about using Twine to create digital interactive fiction games. You can just write and add a few elements that make them look and play great!

## Our First Passage

5–10 minutes

## Activity Overview

Now that you've got the hang of styling, let's get the start of our practice game down. For the next few activities, you will follow my examples to begin creating a simple, playable game. The goal is to introduce you to the tools that you can use in Twine and give examples of how they look and act in the program.

## Instructions

Let's use the nice intro paragraph from the Parsely game example. Edit your starting passage and type in the following:

You are standing in an old, abandoned field that has been long forgotten. The stalks of grass whip in the wind. Ahead of you, a house stands in the field. The paint is chipped and peeling. The windows are all cracked and broken.

```
//No one has lived here in years//
```

Notice that there is a break between the first paragraph and the second line, with the italics. When you finish editing the passage, close it and press the play icon. That break between the two text parts is still there, right? Keep that in mind—when you type in a passage, it will keep the spacing that you use when you write. You don’t have to do anything special!

## Hit the Links

Now that you have the start of the story, it needs to go somewhere. You can make more passages that are the next steps in your game, but you need a way to get to them! And how do you do that? One word: links. Links will let you connect to other passages, or back to ones already made. Besides connecting, links also allow you to reveal new information. The next few activities will show you how to use links to expand and extend your game. Pretty exciting stuff!

Links will use double square brackets [[ ]. The text inside the brackets will define the link and the passage it connects to. The link will also display as blue text, so everyone knows it’s special!

## New Passage Links

10–15 minutes

## Activity Overview

Your story, at some point, is going to have to move forward. You want to your players to have some choices and new passages to explore and extend their games. These are like the decision points when we were building our CYOP game. Each decision will lead to a new passage, with a new part of your story. And we need to create a link between the two passages that will take us from a choice in the first passage to one of the new passages.
Thankfully, Twine has a quick and easy way to do this! When you add a new link, Twine will automatically create a passage with the title of the link. So let's make a link to a new passage and create the passage at the same time.

## Instructions

Open up to edit your starting passage. Leave a blank line after the last line and then enter the following:

```
[[Approach the House]]
[[Go around Back]]
```

Once you have that, close the passage. You should see two new passages. Can you guess what they're titled? One will be “Approach the House” and one will be “Go around Back.” There should also be two arrows, each leading from your starting passage to one of the new passages.

![](img/3760f220c9ee3ac581049b311bdc3cc4_84_0.png)

On the back end, this lets you know that there are links connecting the starting passage to both those passages. The direction of the arrow lets you know that you can get from the start passage to both the “Approach the House” and “Go around Back” passages. But you can't go back directly—nor can you go anywhere else.

Wait, I missed something. This would be a good time to introduce the step-back feature. It may be a little hard to see, but it is on the left near the top when you are playing the game and looks like a looped arrow.

![](img/3760f220c9ee3ac581049b311bdc3cc4_84_1.png)

After you click one of the links, use the arrow to step back to the start passage and try the other link. Pretty easy!

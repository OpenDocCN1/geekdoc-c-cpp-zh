In fact, creating a Parsely game is very much like computer programming. But don't let that scare you! That just means you'll be putting your information together in a certain way, and when it runs, that information will create a game experience for those playing. If the players looked at a Parsely game, it would not look like much of a game! And it definitely wouldn't be very fun for them to just read through. But if someone runs the game for them, a fantastic gaming experience can be created!

This is very similar to video game coding. If you look at the code for a video game, it wouldn't look anything like a game and would not be playable or fun for the person reading. But when that code runs through a computer, it creates something that transcends all code and becomes an awesome experience!

One of the big differences when designing a Parsely game is how much less control you have over how your game plays out. Don't worry—you still have control over the big things. But that control is much looser than the CYOP story you made.

You have control of what things need to happen to progress through the game, but you no longer have full control over when elements are discovered or if the players are able to figure out what they need to do next. Like CYOP, players may stumble across horrible demises or setbacks, but this system is modeled after computer games, so players can choose to save and restore, so they don't have to go all the way back to the beginning.

In a Parsely-style game, players are simply presented with what they see and the directions they may go. It is up to them to examine and explore to find the solutions to move forward. Although they may need to go left to find the key for the locked door, they may choose to go right—you don't have control of that. So the players may go right and find something else or get stuck, then have to backtrack until they finally go left and find the key.

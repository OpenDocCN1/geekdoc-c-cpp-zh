+   * Conflict: This is the thing (or *things*) the players must overcome in the game. Conflict creates the challenges and obstacles that the players must overcome. The players may need to defeat an evil wizard. To do that, they will need to craft a magical item, which requires collecting a number of items. The conflict should connect to the plot—they should drive each other. Stories, and games, can have more than one conflict!

+   * Resolution: This is how the players solve the main conflict in the game. In interactive fiction, there can actually be multiple resolutions depending on the choices made by the players in the game. They may defeat the evil wizard, or they may be turned into a pile of goo. You get to decide what happens—and why!

# # Game Concepts

There is a huge number of parts that go into making a game fun and engaging, but I will focus on five for you here: goals, challenges, exploration, progress, and choice.

+   * Goals: These are the things that the players need to accomplish in the game. These are pretty similar to the conflict and resolution story elements. If you think the player needs to defeat the evil wizard by collecting some different items to craft a magical item, you have identified your game goals! You can add in additional game goals that players need to do too, and it's up to you how simple or complex your game is going to be. The more goals, the more complex the game becomes. When you are first starting out, it is good to have just a few goals. As you get more comfortable designing games, you can begin to add more!

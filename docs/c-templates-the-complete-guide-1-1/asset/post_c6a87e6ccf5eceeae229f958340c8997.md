## About This Book

The first edition of this book was published almost 15 years ago. We had set out to write the definitive guide to C++ templates, with the expectation that it would be useful to practicing C++ programmers. That project was successful. It's been tremendously gratifying to hear from readers who found our material helpful, to see our book in use again and again (e.g., as a recommended reference, and to be universally well received).

That first edition has aged well, with most material remaining entirely relevant to modern C++ programmers, but there is no denying that the C++ landscape has changed dramatically in the intervening years. C++11, C++14, and C++17 have changed a fair amount of the material in the first edition.

With this second edition, our high-level goal has remained unchanged: to provide the definitive guide to C++ templates, including both a solid reference and an accessible tutorial. This time, however, we work with the "Modern C++" language, which is significantly bigger (and still growing) than the language available at the time of the first edition.

We're also acutely aware that C++ programming resources have changed (for the better) since the first edition was published. For example, several very good books have appeared that document the modern C++ language and standard library. Most important, far more information about C++ templates and template-based techniques is readily available online, as are examples of advanced uses of these techniques. In this edition, we have decided to emphasize a breadth of techniques that can be used in a variety of applications.

Some of the techniques we presented in the first edition have become obsolete because the C++ language now offers more direct ways of achieving the same effects. Other techniques have become unnecessary and, in fact, bad practice. We have incorporated current knowledge to offer a complete and modern book that can serve as a solid tutorial and reference.

We've now lived over 20 years with C++ templates, but the C++ programmer community still regularly finds new fundamental insights into the way they can be used to improve software design. Our goal with this book is to share that knowledge but also to help explain the rules to new and old programmers and, perhaps, foster the next major C++ technique.

## WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW BEFORE READING THIS BOOK

To get the most from this book, you should already know C++. We describe the details of a particular language feature, not the fundamentals of the language itself. You should be familiar with the concepts of classes and inheritance, and you should be able to write C++ programs using components such as IOStreams and containers from the C++ standard library. You should also be familiar with the basic features of "Modern C++" including C++11, C++14, and C++17, as well as the concept of templates. However, we review more subtle intricacies of the core areas, even when those issues extend beyond the templates. This ensures that the text is accessible to experts and intermediate programmers alike.

We deal primarily with the C++ language revisions standardized in 2011, 2014, and 2017. However, at the time of this writing, the ink is barely dry on the C++20 revision, so we expect that most of our readers will be familiar with C++20. We are also aware of a relevant proposal for standardization in 2023. We will use the term "Modern C++" to describe that language. We do not attempt to explain the language in detail (although we do not assume that readers are familiar with all of it). Instead, we focus on that part of the language that is relevant to template programming.

Our goal is to show the effect of the language features, and how they are used in practice, not to explain the language. We do not attempt to explain every detail of C++11, C++14, and C++17, but we do explain the parts that are relevant to templates. We also do not cover all the new language features of C++20, but we do describe some of them, particularly those that interact with templates. Furthermore, we do not cover the standard library in detail, but we do discuss many standard library components that are relevant to template programming.

## OUR ORGANIZATION OF THE BOOK

Our goal is to provide the information necessary to start writing templates and benefit from their power, as well as to provide information that will enable experienced programmers to push the limits of the state of the art. To achieve this, we decided to organize our text in two parts:

- Part I introduces the basic concepts underlying templates. It is written in a tutorial style.
- Part II presents the language details and is a handy reference to template-related constructs.

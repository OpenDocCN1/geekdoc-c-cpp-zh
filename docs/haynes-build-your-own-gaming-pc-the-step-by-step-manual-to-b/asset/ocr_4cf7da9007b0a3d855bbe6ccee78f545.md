# Form factor

There are something like 40 different form factors that have been sorted out by the computer industry to ensure that a given motherboard will fit inside a particular case and work with a certain power supply, but happily we can safely ignore any obsolete, obscure or irrelevant form factors since a gaming machine is far more standard than a PC built for large-scale data storage, for instance.

For gaming PCs, the mainstream form factor is ATX and will offer the most expansion slots – meaning spaces to install hardware like the graphics card. With this space it gives the manufacturer plenty of scope to design their motherboard to offer a fair amount of variety. A bigger board doesn’t mean you should fill it all, however, since the more space there is between hardware the better air can circulate inside the case and therefore avoid overheating. ATX is the standard for a reason but, of all the options available, it provides the most flexibility for installable parts while still allowing for decent cable management and air circulation.

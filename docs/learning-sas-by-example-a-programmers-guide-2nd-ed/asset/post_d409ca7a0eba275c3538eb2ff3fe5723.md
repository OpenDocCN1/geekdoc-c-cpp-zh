## 8.6 DO WHILE and DO UNTIL Statements

Instead of choosing a stopping value for an iterative DO loop, you can stop a loop when a condition is met or while a condition is true. Enter the DO UNTIL and DO WHILE statements.

Let's revisit the compound interest problem from earlier in this chapter. Instead of asking how much money you have after x years, want to know how many years you need to keep your $100 in the bank at 3.75% to double your money. The two programs solve this problem:

# Program 6.13: Demonstrating a DO UNTIL Loop

```sas
data Double;
  Interest = .0375;
  Total = 100;
  do until (Total ge 200);
    Year = Year + 1;
    Total = Total + Interest*Total;
    output;
  end;
  format Total dollar10.2;
run;

title "Listing of Double";
proc print data=Double noobs;
run;
```

The condition is placed in parentheses for the keyword UNTIL. In this example, the loop continues to repeat until the value of Total is greater than or equal to 200. Here is the output:

An alternative to DO UNTIL is DO WHILE. As you might expect, a DO WHILE loop iterates as long as the condition following WHILE is true. There is no difference between DO WHILE and DO UNTIL—the WHILE condition is tested at the top of the loop rather than at the bottom.

Unlike a DO UNTIL block that always iterates at least once, a DO WHILE block does not execute even if the condition is false. You can rewrite Program 8.13 using a DO WHILE statement, like this:

# Program 8.15: Demonstrating a DO WHILE Statement

```sas
data Double;
  Interest = .0375;
  Total = 100;
  do while (Total < 200);
    Year = Year + 1;
    Total = Total + Interest*Total;
    output;
  end;
  format Total dollar10.2;
run;
```

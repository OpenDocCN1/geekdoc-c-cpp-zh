And on a longer-term level, the creation of board and card games has always primarily been the development of process definitions—embodied in game rules—that determine how play moves forward.

In important ways these noncomputational media processes are like the processes of digital media: they are defined previously, but (at least in part) carried out during the time of audience experience. This is true as Tzara pulls a paper scrap from his sack, as the Zoetrope image flickers, as the poker hand goes through another round of betting, and as the image of a Pong ball bounces off the image of a Pong paddle. The processes of digital media, however, are separated from noncomputational media processes by their potential numerousness, repetition, and complexity.

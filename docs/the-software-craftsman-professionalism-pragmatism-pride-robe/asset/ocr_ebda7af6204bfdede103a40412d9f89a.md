# 11. Interview Anti-Patterns

Paying attention to detail during the selection process is one of the best ways to get insights into the culture and values of the company. Experienced and talented developers are very selective about the clients they want to work with. On top of the financial offer, autonomy, mastery, purpose, productive partnership, and talented people, and a good working environment are some of the things they will consider before accepting any offer. How they are interviewed, and most importantly, the people who are interviewing them can be decisive factors in them accepting the job.

In order to attract software craftsmen, as an interviewer it is important to know what to avoid when conducting interviews.

In this chapter we cover many interview anti-patterns that should be avoided if you want to attract software craftsmen to your organization.

# Don't Be a Smart-Ass Interviewer

Don't try to make yourself look smarter and better than the candidate. Don't intimidate the candidate by putting them in a tough situation for your pleasure. Don't try to impress the candidate with your title, responsibilities, and skills. Don't talk to the candidate as if you were the most powerful person in the world. Simply put, don't be an ass.

Don't try to look smart by asking tricky and irrelevant questions. Experienced and talented developers will notice this and won't be very nice to work with.

When recruiting for talent, you should be looking for a partner, a person who can join your team and help you to better, and not a subordinate developer who follows your orders.

Just be honest and humble. Treat candidates like professional developers, and conduct the interview as if you were having a great conversation with someone you. Most importantly, listen to the candidates and keep your open mind. You may learn a few things.

# Don't Use Beastayers

Avoid stupid questions that are totally unrelated to the job. How many golf balls can you fit into an airplane? A clerk at a butcher shop stands 5 feet 10 inches tall and size 13 sneakers. What does the weight to answer these These stupid beastayers has nothing to do with writing-coded code, being a good player, or having a professional attitude. Beastayers are a total waste of time, Google finally realized after using them for a long time. At least for them, later than never.

# Don't Ask Questions to Who You Know the Answers

Googling for questions and answers to ask candidates before the interview makes no sense. If you, as an interviewer, are not sure about the questions and answers they are probably not very important to the job. If you are a good interviewer, you will know what is important and what is not.

Don't try to confuse the candidate with misleading questions. If we don't do that to our colleagues, and even get mad when people do it to us why would we do it to likes?

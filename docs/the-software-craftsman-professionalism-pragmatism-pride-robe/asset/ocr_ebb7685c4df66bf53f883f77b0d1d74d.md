# A Tale from a Brazilian Teenager

As a middle-class teenager living in a small town in the Brazilian country, my dream was to live in London. I could never explain why, but there was something about London that fascinated me. My parents avoided conversations about it because they knew the chances of it happening close to zero; our ever-struggling financial situation would never allow me to get a good education or live in a better city. On top of that, the town we lived in didn't even have a European football team. To be a good professional, on top of that, I could not speak English well and I didn't have any experience in the UK market. How could my CV stand out in a country that has some of the best universities in the world?

Even so, when I finished high school, I had to decide which career I would follow. My dreams were limited because I couldn't afford the best universities. Taking into account the things I was passionate about, as a young teenager, I never had a very clear idea what to do but I wanted a best option. So back then, the dream to live in London was always there.

After a few years working as a software developer, I finally could afford to start a very long and expensive Italian course; my parents were Italians. I had a lot of money to pay people to help me find some documents that proved I had Italian citizenship. I had to translate all of them into Portuguese, and then make them legal in Brazil. For a Brazilian middle-class boy like me, that was hard, but having a European passport was almost essential if I had to pursue my dream.

In 2000, with my career in motion, I traveled to the UK on holidays. I was 21 years old and what I liked about it was that I had never wanted to travel to see London, and the rest of the UK, was exactly what I thought it was.

I wanted to know exactly what to do in order to be competitive in the UK market. For 30 days I traveled around the UK and spoke to some developers, who could then help me the trip via an online website similar to LinkedIn. And I realized that I had a few years of experience as a developer, Delphi was not enough for the UK market. I needed to speak to more developers to find jobs on job websites. The way I had back then. Then, as I worked as a software developer in London, I could find orders and direction at a manager's, but my English was not good enough to talk to other developers, for interviews, or any of the conversations we needed to have when working on software projects. I felt like a stranger to the people around me.

After a wonderful time in the UK, and back in Brazil, I knew what to do: find a job and to live. I hired a private English teacher who could teach me in the evenings; and I enrolled in a post-graduate course. It was a bit weird that my academic record wouldn't. be. I decided to take a few courses well-not that they would eventually prove but it would help in showing my CV on matters that London could recognize.

At that point, I realized that my career was important for me; I was not just preparing to move to the UK. I was preparing to have to move out of my comfort zone, take ownership of my career, and constantly look for opportunities live. I realized that I had to make my dream important. It was my life, and I had to make it my own.

For the next years, keeping an eye on the UK market, I prepared myself. In January 2004, a little later with a European passport in hand, I contacted few developers and companies in London, moved to London, and within two years I started my first job.

A journey that lasted more than a decade had come to an end. I finally living in London and working as a developer. I realized my dream was just starting, as new opportunities opened up. But something even deeper had happened to me. When I arrived in London I was not the same with a teenager anymore. I was a 27-year-old professional software developer who was still at the very beginning of my journey. I realized that my career was a very long road to mastery and that this exciting journey was just about to start.

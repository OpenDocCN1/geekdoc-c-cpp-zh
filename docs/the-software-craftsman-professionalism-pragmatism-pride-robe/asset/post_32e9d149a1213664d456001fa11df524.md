# Enlightened Managers

Good managers understand that they are part of the team and need to work with the developers to the common goal. There is no 'us' and 'them'. They must understand what the current state of the project is and work with the team to figure out what they will be able to achieve within a period of time.

Managers should appreciate when we can say no to certain features. Although that may be bad news, that information helps avoid a major mistake. Transparency helps managers and prepare for such tasks. Raising the red flag as early as possible, indicating that things are wrong, allows all the team and people involved to be pragmatic and up with alternative solutions. It frees people to be creative to solve the problem. If we deliver what we promised then managers will be more inclined to trust us when we say no.

Smart people know not to know everything and also know that in a team, we need to rely on all the members of the team. As the saying goes, 'a team is as strong as its weakest link.' Enlightened managers understand and expect that things are not easy to do or may take long time to be done. They appreciate when they are challenged and different options are suggested. This attitude shows that everyone is working as a team around a common goal. With more information and a few proposed solutions, managers feel more confident to choose the best solution to the problem, making the people that made the suggestions stand out.

Good managers are part of the team. They stay with the team during good and bad times. If everyone is committed to work extra hours, they should be there as well, even if just to buy the pizzas, crack a joke every now and again. The team feels the boat, feeling the pain or enjoying success. This type of environment helps people to focus on doing whatever they can to make things happen. Good managers shield developers from external pressure, whatever they can to remove any impediments the team may have, and help the team feel comfortable and confident to get their job done. Managers should be harmony ambassadors and it is their job to keep the team healthy, happy, and united.

# Summary

Being professional and satisfying our clients does not mean everything they ask us to. Our job is to help clients figure out what their needs are and also advise them on the best way to approach them. A professional has ethics code of conduct. A professional would never do something that could be harmful to a client just because the client is paying for it. Clients rarely understand the full impact of certain decisions on a software project, and it is our job to make them aware of it. It is our job to say no to our clients when they ask us for something we know is not going to work or that is not going to be done by a certain deadline. This is the thing we would expect from a project manager, as a professional who knows exactly what to do or how to do their job. They wouldn't let us anyway. We go to these professionals with a problem (or a need) and we expect them to use their experience to explain which options we have and what their respective risks are. We expect them to give us information so that we can make informed decisions. But if we push them to do something that goes against what they think is right, they would simply say no. And that's exactly what we should do.

# 6. Working Software

One of the first things said by the nonbelievers of the Software Craftsmanship movement is that good and clean code is not enough to guarantee the success of a project. And, yes, they are absolutely right. There are innumerable reasons what may cause a software project to fail, ranging from bad business strategy, competitors' superiority, bad project management, high costs to market, technical limitations, and integrations—just to mention a few. Due to the importance of things a software project, many organizations do not pay much attention to those that are concerned with less important factors, like the quality of the software being developed. Some believe that with a good project management plan, strict process, and a large amount of good documentation, the project will succeed. Many organizations see software development as a process where the workers (developers) are viewed as lesser than their "highly qualified" and well-paid masters. Software development is then outsourced to workers in cheaper countries, which, if they provide poor quality anyway. With a strong emphasis on command and control management, software development is treated as a detail. Very rarely companies believe that without quality, they can attract or retain good developers. Traditional managers believe that documentation is enough, but the truth is that in a software project the most important deliverable is the software itself.

In this chapter I discuss why working software is not enough and the impacts of bad software. Fortunately, we all realize the importance of quality quality. I also discuss how a change in attitude can help us deal with legacy systems.

# Working Software Is Not Enough

In the Agile Manifesto, we have two principles that mention working software:

- Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months, with a preference to shorter timescale.
- Working software is the primary measure of progress.

This is absolutely great and we all agree with these principles. The problem here is that over the years, the idea of working software moved away from quality. So, what does "working software" mean? Can we consider the applications described above as working software? Is working software enough? Is it acceptable to deliver something that takes a long time to build and go through a long period of manual testing and bug fixing? What about applications without an extensive test suite, where are we need to deploy under user acceptance testing (UAT) if we cannot run it locally? They are all working software.

# Looking After Our Garden

Code is not mechanical. A garden needs constant maintenance. For a garden to look beautiful anyway we need to tend to it, constantly water it regularly, remove some dead plants, plant new ones or rearrange existing so they can stay healthy and the garden stays whole. With basic and regular maintenance, the garden will always look great but if we neglect it, for a short period of time, it will start to look much less enough to make it look good again. The longer we neglect it, the harder we will need to bring it back to a state we can enjoy.

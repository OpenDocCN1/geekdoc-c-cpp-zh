# Not Only Customer, but Also Product Producers

The first thing to mention here is that we do not believe in the employee/employer type of relationship. What the contract says you are a permanent employee, contractor, vendor, paid per day, hour, or month is just for formality. We believe in partnership above all. If you are a permanent employee, you should treat your customer as customer, the same way as if you were a contractor or consultant. Employers, on the other hand, should also expect their employees to provide great service at all times, like a consultant and craftsman is supposed to. Turning up to work, keeping your head down, and doing what you are told to do is not what it means to be professional.

Software craftsmen are not factory. We want to actively contribute to the success of the project, questioning requirements, understanding the business, processes and product partnering with our customers or employers. This is a different approach to the traditional employee/employer model and the advantages for employers. A highly motivated team will make the project succeed. Passion and talented people want to succeed and will always find ways to overcome problems and boundaries.

Software craftsman want need successful projects to build their reputations. We want to be proud of what. Successfully delivering high-quality software and having satisfied customers are essential for a software craftsman's craft

Although extremely important, writing good code is one of the things we need to do to have a project to succeed. We help our customers to improve their processes, provide them with more viable options, help them to remove unnecessary bureaucracy, understand their business, question their requirements in the value they provide, provide them with good information and knowledge, help them to plan and prioritize work, and work on things, although they are not their own. Every detail is important to the project. Providing value to our customers at all levels is what we mean by having a productive partnership.

The core business of many of our customers is not building software, so it is our obligation to help them run a project in the best possible way. That's what we pay. For developers that says it is not their job if the task is not related to real software craftsmen.

# But Some Customers Are Not Ready for a Productive ...

Unfortunately, there are some customers that are not ready for this type of partnership. They see software development as an industrial process and the least important part of the project. For some customers, software developers are as factory workers, who are there to follow orders from smarter people.

Some of these companies focus on getting the cheapest developers they can find and always have technical managers to micro-manage them. They will not welcome or listen to developers when it comes to business decisions. Some will have no say in development skills, to the technical leader (or dictator)

Working for a company with this mentality is always very difficult for any software craftsmen. We always try to turn around this situation and try to prove to our customers that we could help them far more if they wanted.

However, in the same way that customers have to have good developers, software craftsmen also want to work for good companies. There is no point in spending all our energy and health to help those who do not want to. There is a limit to the efforts we make to help our customers. How to select clients (or employers) is an essential skill for a craftsman. Choosing for a client who is not interested in or does not value the skills of a software craftsman is pointless.

Choosing clients carefully is essential for software craftsmen to build their reputations and move forward with their careers. A partnership, by nature, is a two-way street. A partnership is a good partnership only if it is good for both parties involved. If you don't find that the partnership with your client is good for you, it is probably time for you to find another client.

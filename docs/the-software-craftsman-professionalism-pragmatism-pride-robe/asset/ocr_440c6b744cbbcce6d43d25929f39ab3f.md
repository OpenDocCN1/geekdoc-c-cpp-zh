# # 10. Software Craftsmen

Interviews are a two-way street. Companies try to find developers who can help them achieve their goals, and developers try to find out if the hiring company is a good fit for their career aspirations.

As companies generally conduct multiple interviews as part of their selection process, it is vital that developers use the interviews to learn as much as about the hiring company. Choosing a new job is an important career decision and can also have a direct impact on our personal lives; we may be spending more time at work than we spend with our loved ones.

In this chapter we look at how companies and developers can identify whether they will have a productive partnership. We will then look at some interview techniques and describe how to interview software craftsmen.

# ## A Business Negotiation

As technology evolves, the need for software professionals increases. In order to have a competitive advantage, companies now demand that they hire talented and passionate developers and, most importantly, pay them well. This is highlighted in an article in InfoWorld. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects a 30 percent job growth in the coming years, and CareerCraft deemed software engineering all-around best for 2012.

During an interview, it is important to remember that we are not begging for a job. We are doing a business negotiation. On one side we have needs and goals. On the other side we have a software perspective which could help the company negotiate better contracts; the most important aspect of providing a service, as a contractor or a permanent employee, is to understand and measure the rewards and risks of that negotiation.

Every craftsman has a reputation and anything that can damage that reputation must be a risk. An example would be to join a project that is behind schedule, with dubious business value, control and management, and in a company where developers are treated as factory workers—just following orders, coding a piece of software, with no say in, and with no contact with users and stakeholders. The chances of bringing a project like that back on track are close to zero, meaning that the only software craftsman who can save it is yourself. This is a frustrating situation, and the partnership must not be rebuilt by a software craftsman. However, if the project is in trouble but developers have total autonomy to do whatever is best to save the project, working with collaboration, then this project can be a great opportunity for a craftsman.

# ## Identifying Predictive Partnerships

Companies and developers are looking for the same thing: a productive partnership. However, during interviews, each side has a different perspective on what a productive partnership should be. Let's look at these different perspectives.

# ### A Hiring Company's Perspective

I have interviewed developers over the years and more often, when I ask the candidates if they have any questions, the answer was always no. On some occasions, I was so disappointed with this attitude that I decided not to hire them. As a hiring company, we expect to have partnerships with those whom we hire. We expect that the people working with us will show what they want to achieve, what they can do, and contribute to our success.

I want talents that, as a hiring company, share our values and want to improve the way we work. If the developers' opinions are not aligned, there will be no reason to hire talent. Collaboration and empowerment are key for any successful team. The developer's job (and also the types of questions) asked by an interviewee may be a good indicator of how much this person cares. If the interviewee asks many questions about the company or about the interview process, it may be that he is just looking for a job, regardless of which one. If the interviewee asks of questions, I can assume that she really cares about her career and find the right company. Always remember that when a candidate asks questions, it doesn't mean they are better than the ones who don't ask questions; but, it shows they care enough to be in it they will.

# Blogs

Blogs are popular and a great way to keep ourselves up to date. Quite a few good developers I know and respect just blogs. They have many abandoned blogs. Blogs tend to fit well in the Software Craftsmanship industry because they provide a place to share findings, opinions, successes, and failures in short snippets. Reading from more experienced professionals and subject experts is a good, quick, and free for us to learn from many different professionals at the same time. Blogs are like hashtags, just to mention, that we can use to keep track of blogs.

Blogs can be dangerous for the uninformed. The majority of blogs are written without much research or depth. Some blogs are just a dump of half-baked ideas, wants, or random thoughts. Some developers use their blogs to keep track of their work. Some people share their experiences in project results but does not necessarily mean they were able to solve their problems or even to identify their real problems. # Know Who to Follow

In every profession there is a group of people who contribute massively to promote that profession. We have many of these people, some on the technology-specific side and others on the generic, conceptual, and behavioral side. They are all important, so know who these people are. That helps us to filter the information we have online or in physical books. For example, if we use Java or Ruby, we should know who publishes the best material on. We should know who is helping the language to move forward or defining better ways to use it. We should know the people who are reshaping our industry are. When we hear terms like Agile and Software Craftsmanship, we should try to discover the people who are behind it. Look at their history. What they've published. Try to understand where they got their inspiration or what they are basing their ideas on. We may discover many of the things we talk about today back a few decades.

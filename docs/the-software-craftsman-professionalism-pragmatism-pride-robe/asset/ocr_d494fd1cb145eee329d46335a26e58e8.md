Trying to reach a consensus of when and where to run sessions can be one of the biggest reasons not to have the sessions at all. Although we should always try to accommodate everyone, we just need to say: "Guys, John and I will be in the meeting room Wednesday at 12:00 p.m. playing with Scala (or whatever technology). You are more than welcome to join us."

Many I have seen teams discussing for weeks, trying to reach consensus. It is sometimes just not possible to accommodate everyone. "I want to go to the gym at time," says one. "Wednesdays work from home," says the other. Just pick a day and run the session.

# Don't Ask for Authorization

You don't need authorization from your boss to learn and practice on your own time. This is true in many companies, even during working hours if you don't. You just need to be responsible. No sensible manager will complain when the team is practicing and getting better. 

Find an empty room and have the meeting at lunchtime, early in the morning, or late in the afternoon. Just keep a healthy balance between your learning activities and the things you promised yourself. Be sensible.

# Don't Complicate

"Our office layout is not suitable for group meetings. Our room does not have a projector. What about our teams in other locations? How can we include them? These are common challenges I hear from developers who are trying to create a culture of learning at their workplace. Because many of these problems cannot be resolved quickly, the sessions never happen

You dont say. If your office is not good enough, go to a coffee shop. If you don't have Internet access, ask people to install whatever they need on their laptops before coming to the meeting. If some of your teams are in different geographic locations, have the different teams running sessions locally.

Aim for sessions that don't require too much organization or preparation. If you choose to have a group discussion, there is nothing you need to book a room and calling everyone; the people attending the meeting can choose the topics when they get there.

# Establish a Rhythm

The secret to a healthy community, and keeping the sessions regularly is to have a rhythm. It is much easier for people to come to the sessions regularly Our open space will be every Wednesday at 5 pm in the board room. Regular meetings are also easier to organize. Find a meeting room and schedule a recurring meeting inviting everyone. Just make sure you find someone to run the meeting when you are not in the office. It is important that meetings happen even if you are away.

It is not difficult to build a culture of learning into an organization. The only thing needed is a passionate developer to start it. Stop making excuses and be this developer.

# Summary

Creating a culture of learning is not only a manager's or Agile coach's job. It's everyone's, including developers. Lack of time or an appropriate place for learning is not an excuse. With no support from management, developers can easily create a culture of learning, environment where they can learn and share with each other. Don't have a place? Go to a local coffee shop. Don't have time? Use your lunchtime or do it before or after working hours. It's not possible to do it every day? Do it once week.

Despite what many companies or managers think, creating a culture of learning is cheap and easy. Hire passionate developers and provide them with a good environment and support, and they will figure out many different ways in which they can learn, share, and bring innovation to the company.

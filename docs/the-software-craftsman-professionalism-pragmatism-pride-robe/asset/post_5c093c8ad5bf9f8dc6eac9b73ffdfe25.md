## Socialize

The idea that software developed in isolation is never totally useless. Finding developers we can bounce ideas off, pair, and network is almost essential for success. A great and easy way is to join your local groups and technical communities. They normally promote facts, activities, and social events. ## Deliberate Discovery

> I'm the smartest in Athens because I know I know nothing.
> - Socrates

The biggest mistake that software makes is not accepting that they don't know what they don't. What not knowing what they don't is called second-level ignorance. Accepting that they have to learn it is a sign of maturity and one of the first steps toward mastery. The vast majority of us have a natural tendency to be positive and optimistic. An example of this is estimating tasks. When tasks are completed we compare the time it took to our original estimations, and realize there is a much chance that things will not go as planned, which means there will be unforeseen and unpredictable results. Unfortunately, we have no idea when, where, or how.

There is not a magical way to completely solve this problem but we can try to minimize it. One way of doing this is to constantly expose ourselves to situations where we can learn something about the context we are in. This is very important, especially in the early days of a project or before building a major set of features—when we are most ignorant about what we need to do. Spending time trying to identify and minimize our ignorance across all the axes we can think of can be time well spent.

# Foreword

In 1973 Roberta Flack sang "Killing Me Softly." You've never heard it in elevators or on your grandmother's radio station. It's a soft, lilting ballad about a woman who goes to a concert and hears a young man singing a song that she so identifies with that she wonders the young man had found and read her letters. She expresses herself better than she feels when he strums his guitar, and it is the song of her life.

The book you are holding feels that may validate you. Sandro Caruso's career has been very different from mine. He is much better than I. We have lived and worked our careers on different continents and in different cultures. We share nothing but irony. I've met him a few times, and each time it was never for a few minutes.

Within the pages you are holding you will find a fascinating alternation between biographical anecdotes based on those experiences. If you are a programmer, you will feel these stories and recommendations resonate with you. If you are to say yourself, as I did, "Been there that." And you will feel the pain aching in your mind as he strums your pain.

And strum your pain you will feel, because this book is about it. It is about pain. The ache that you and I, and, all in an un-profession. It is the pain of feeling trapped constrained to do a poor job. It is the pain of wanting to do better and not knowing how.

This book also contains the antidote for that pain. I believe it contains the cure. You see, this is is about software professionalism. Not just individual professionalism, but the professionalism of the software organization. This is a book about Software Craftsmanship.

In these pages the author lays out a plan, a strategy, a set of attitudes, and a suite of principles that every programmer, team, and software organization can use to haul themselves out of the mire of mediocrity, to make themselves more professional, more productive, and more of the work they do like.

The scope of this book is midway wide. Topics range from design patterns, pair programming, and Test-Driven Development to how to conduct and manage deadlines, how to respond to tight deadlines, how to write job descriptions, and how to relate to coworkers and managers.

In short, this book is an encyclopedia of the behavior, attributes, and structure of an organization striving to grow in professionalism and adhere to the principles of Software Craftsmanship.

If you are the type of programmer, team lead, or manager who craves to be able to go home after a long day of work, look in the mirror, and say "Damn, I did a good job today?" then this is the book for you.

-Robert C. Martin
August 2014

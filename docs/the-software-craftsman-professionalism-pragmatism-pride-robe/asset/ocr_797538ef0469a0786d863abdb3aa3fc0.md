Unfortunately, the notion of seniority has not changed in the last 20 years. Software developers are normally considered according to the number of years they have worked in the industry and not according to their knowledge.

There is a difference between having years of experience and having experience repeated times. Years working on different projects, with different technologies, and for different companies is different from years working for the same company, on the same project, and with the same people same technology.

There is no such thing as a senior or junior developer. Developers may be very experienced in developing applications for large organizations but totally inexperienced writing games in JavaScript. They can be extremely experienced in a working or Agile environment but totally inexperienced dealing with the bureaucracy and cultural challenges of a large corporation.

# A New Reality

Good programming practices and techniques can be applied in many different environments and systems of types, coding is just one of the things that modern developers do. Beyond just about writing code in any language or framework, modern developers need to know how to provide to customers, automate tests and deployment, manage technical challenges that may affect the entire business, work in distributed teams, help clients define and prioritize requirements, report progress, manage changes and expectations, protect potential clients and work with pre-sales activities, estimate time and costs, interview new team members, design and evolve the architecture, with nonfunctional requirements and service level agreements (SLAs), understanding business goals, make decisions based on facts and find better ways to win users, about the value they deliver to customers, and many other things.

Some day developers have been around for the decades. Well, that is true for some senior developers, but it is definitely not true for the majority of them. In the past, the roles and responsibilities of the people involved in a software project were merged for many years. Developers were not involved in the design and architecture of the applications, rarely had access to users, and were never to be trusted to make business decisions.

The days when developers were specialized in only one area had - if it is not coding, it's no longer acceptable. Companies started to learn, flattening their hierarchies and replacing many-one-pony professionals with generalizing specialists. Agile is for developers who like to be flexible, adaptable, and ready at writing code at all times.

In the past two decades, with the Internet, mobile devices, cloud services, and general evolution, everything changed. Clients want more things, and companies are becoming more aggressive with their software offerings. Being able to easily change the software, with agility, the more we value the value provided to customers becomes. We now rely on software-like architectures-Amazon, Google, Twitter, Facebook, and many others-become the dominant patterns in the market. Agile replaces bureaucratic service companies with rigid and deep hierarchical structures with more agile companies win. The waterfall approach with a traditional management style often cannot keep up with the pace of change-we need to keep up with the game.

# Focus: The Pomodoro Technique

In order to use our time outside working hours, it is extremely important that we have a focus. A good technique is to decide before what we want to do we actually use this time. It is like deciding the agenda before scheduling a meeting. It does not need to be perfect, but we need at least have a good idea of what we want to achieve. Once this is done, we must ensure we can get it done. One way we can achieve this is by using the Pomodoro technique. There are five basic steps to implement this technique:

+   1. Decide on the task to be.

+   2. Set the Pomodoro (timer) to 25 minutes.

+   3. Work on the task until the timer rings.

+   4. Take a short break (normally 5 minutes).

+   5. Every four "Pomodoros", take a longer break (15-30 minutes).

During a Pomodoro, we focus on the task and nothing else. The breaks between Pomodoros are for a quick rest, checking emails, a phone call, checking your Twitter, or whatever you feel like. We do whatever to finish the Pomodoro with no interruptions but if it needs to be interrupted (there is an important call we need to take or we need to speak to someone), then the Pomodoro must be terminated and not counted. If so, there are many Pomodoro tools available out there. There are some sophisticated that can keep track of all the tasks you completed, interrupted Pomodoros, and many other statistics. I, personally prefer the simple ones but feel free to use that that suits you better.

# Balance

Whatever you do, a sustainable pace is key. Keeping a healthy-work-life balance is tough but not. If you are the type of person who says, "I don't want to work for a computer outside," you should think about your career choice; maybe balance development is not for you. For the majority of us, software development—besides being our job—is also a hobby, which makes it relatively easy to find enough time to practice and better ourselves.

Keeping your professional life is essential for a healthy life. An unhealthy professional life, where we are constantly worried about not being paid well, or we may not find another well-paid and interesting job if we are made redundant in the market, puts us in a good position not to worry too much about our professional lives and gives us our families and the attention they much deserve.


How we make sure we are not filtering good developers? The first thing is to define what a good developer means. This is totally context dependent. Different teams, with different cultures, developing different applications, will have different views of what a good developer means. I've had this discussion with experienced developers over the years and once good developers were defined according to specific technical, years of experience, or academic background. A good developer is not measured according to that, is why we keep looking for these things or using our criteria.

In our case, filter criteria were related to what we valued the most. We soon realized we could not measure it just looking at CVs, and that was not enough for starting developers. From that onward, we looked for CVs that at least contained information about their technical skills, community contributions and user groups with which they were active. In this mind, we asked for any projects, list of feature techniques, conferences where they gave talks or attended. From our perspective, a passionate junior developer should have some of these items. This approach was enough for us to identify the developers who were investing in their own working hours, which also adds passion.

Although we know that some developers might not have any of the things described above, the alternative would be to go back and interview every single candidate—which make the selection process very expensive and even prohibitive.

## Proactive Recruitment

When we started Codature, one of our first clients, an international company, asked us for a unusual service. They asked us to help reshape their recruitment process and interview candidate.

We were growing fast and had to hire more than 70 developers to cope with their demand. They needed developers of all levels—junior, senior, architects, employees, contractors. They also needed analysts, developers (DevOps), and testers. They were hiring for many new projects and needed developers in various languages and technologies.

One of the reasons we asked to help them is that they couldn't cope with the volume of interviews, and they were not able to quite how to recruit all developers. They needed us to get talented developers to join the team because these developers would work directly with their clients, developing bespoke software.

Before they hired us, they were in desperation. They had no time to fill all those roles in order to not lose business opportunities. The problem is that when you are in desperation, you increase the risk of making mistakes, especially when it comes to recruitment. You ended up lowering the bar and recruiting anyone you please.

It took us some time, but we managed to help them reshape their recruitment, with the advice we wrote in this book, and we manage to select some developers for them.

We learned a lesson which is never allow your company to be in this situation. When you are desperate you will bring the wrong people to your organization, although you may think that you are at least getting some developers in place which are going to end. They will end even before you expect, your clients will be very dissatisfied with the service provided by them bad. Besides damaging the reputation of the company, your company now has many developers on the bench, waiting for their next project.

At Codature, we decided to be proactive with our recruitment process. We did not want to wait until a bad developer came in to start hiring. Hiring good developers is very difficult when you are in a hurry; that are when you really need a good developer, you can only find one quickly with enough. The difference is that we make our specific process enough so that only the types of developers we are actually looking for apply. This helps us not only to reduce the number of applications, but also to increase quality.

When we are not ready to hire but still have applicants, we tell them at the start of the recruitment process that we are not hiring straightway. If they want to go through the selection process and pass, they will be the first ones we call as soon as we need to. As part of our selection process, we use code assignment that at least is a way to test and convince them to go through the process, even that we are not ready to hire, we provide them with a very comprehensive review of their code. With this approach, applicants at least get some valuable advice on their code, and we can build a pool of talented developers who have passed our process, and we can call them when we are ready to hire.

## Summary

Companies can benefit from engaging with local users and communities. It gives them a good chance to meet great and well-established professionals, as well as many young developers with high potential. Due to their conflict of interest, they do not always care about their clients' interests and values. Matching keywords in a job description to CVs is how usually they are filtering. Their lack of understanding about software development and technologies makes them very poor talent scouts.

If a job description is needed in order to reach a wider audience of developers, make sure the job description clearly states your company's culture and values; project details; expected responsibilities and technologies used (instead of required). Focus on passion instead of specific technologies and never use years of experience, academic background, certifications, or any other criteria that are not relevant to the job.

For a better predictability of future success, look for passionate developers because they are naturally open-minded and are constantly looking for ways to learn. They are also the ones who have a natural motivation to innovate and drive technical changes, instead of just trying to be safe—which will keep the company stagnant. Due to their passion and the time they spend trying to get better, even if they are not so experienced today, they have a great chance to become great professionals in a very short period of time.

If you think your developers are not good, don't blame them. First, ask yourself why and how they were hired. Maybe your recruitment process is not working as well as you thought it was. As a company, don't think you are the only one applying filters and looking for the best people. Good developers are also filtering bad companies out and looking for the best ones.

## 10. Software Craftsmen

Interviews are a two-way street. Companies try to find developers who can help them achieve their goals, and developers try to find out if the hiring company is a good fit for their career aspirations.

As companies generally conduct multiple interviews as part of their selection process, it is vital that developers use the interviews to learn as much as about the hiring company. Choosing a new job is an important career decision and can also have a direct impact on our personal lives; we may be spending more time at work than we spend with our loved ones.

In this chapter we look at how companies and developers can identify whether they will have a productive partnership. We will then look at some interview techniques and describe how to interview software craftsmen.

## A Business Negotiation

As technology evolves, the need for software professionals increases. In order to have a competitive advantage, companies now demand that they hire talented and passionate developers and, most importantly, pay them well. This is highlighted in an article in InfoWorld. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects a 30 percent job growth in the coming years, and CareerCraft deemed software engineering all-around best for 2012.

During an interview, it is important to remember that we are not begging for a job. We are doing a business negotiation. On one side we have needs and goals. On the other side we have a software perspective which could help the company negotiate better contracts; the most important aspect of providing a service, as a contractor or a permanent employee, is to understand and measure the rewards and risks of that negotiation.

Every craftsman has a reputation and anything that can damage that reputation must be a risk. An example would be to join a project that is behind schedule, with dubious business value, control and management, and in a company where developers are treated as factory workers—just following orders, coding a piece of software, with no say in, and with no contact with users and stakeholders. The chances of bringing a project like that back on track are close to zero, meaning that the only software craftsman who can save it is yourself. This is a frustrating situation, and the partnership must not be rebuilt by a software craftsman. However, if the project is in trouble but developers have total autonomy to do whatever is best to save the project, working with collaboration, then this project can be a great opportunity for a craftsman.

## Identifying Predictive Partnerships

Companies and developers are looking for the same thing: a productive partnership. However, during interviews, each side has a different perspective on what a productive partnership should be. Let's look at these different perspectives.

### A Hiring Company's Perspective

I have interviewed developers over the years and more often, when I ask the candidates if they have any questions, the answer was always no. On some occasions, I was so disappointed with this attitude that I decided not to hire them. As a hiring company, we expect to have partnerships with those whom we hire. We expect that the people working with us will show what they want to achieve, what they can do, and contribute to our success.

I want talents that, as a hiring company, share our values and want to improve the way we work. If the developers' opinions are not aligned, there will be no reason to hire talent. Collaboration and empowerment are key for any successful team. The developer's job (and also the types of questions) asked by an interviewee may be a good indicator of how much this person cares. If the interviewee asks many questions about the company or about the interview process, it may be that he is just looking for a job, regardless of which one. If the interviewee asks of questions, I can assume that she really cares about her career and find the right company. Always remember that when a candidate asks questions, it doesn't mean they are better than the ones who don't ask questions; but, it shows they care enough to be in it they will.

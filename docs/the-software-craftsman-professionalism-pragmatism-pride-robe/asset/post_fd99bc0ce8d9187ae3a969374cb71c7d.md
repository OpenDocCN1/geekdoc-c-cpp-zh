# Summary

In order to remain competitive, companies need to deliver software faster with better quality. Agile software development helps companies to shorten their feedback loop, giving them the opportunity to quickly adapt to changes and the demands of their clients. Unfortunately, during their Agile adoptions, many companies tend to pay a lot of attention to Agile processes but totally neglect technical practices.

Although the Agile Manifesto clearly states "individuals and interactions over process and tools," Agile transformations end up being all about process and tools adopting Scrum, having daily stand-up meetings, and having to manage backups in progress won't magically improve the quality of the software or make developers better. Improving the process without improving technical excellence is pointless.

For a full Agile transformation, we also need professional software developers who master technical practices, technologies, and tools. We need developers capable of constantly delivering quality software that is regularly deployed into production. We need developers who can deliver software that is fully tested and that can be easily changed. For a full Agile transformation, companies need to embrace Software Craftsmanship.

## 3. Software Craftsmanship

Before we discuss Software Craftsmanship, let's describe what it **is not:**

- Beautiful code
- Test-Driven Development
- Self-selected group of people
- Specific technologies or methodologies
- Certifications
- Religion

So, what is Software Craftsmanship then? This chapter presents a few definitions and discusses its origins and history. This chapter also covers the Software Craftsmanship Manifesto, with an in-depth explanation of what it really means.

## A Better Metaphor

In a very simple way, we can say that Software Craftsmanship is a better metaphor for software development than software engineering. Apprentices would work with more experienced blacksmiths, traveling from place to place and working for different masters, learning different tools and techniques, and improving their craft until the they were good enough to become a master themselves. There is more to it, but bear with me for now.

## What Does Wikipedia Say?

[Software Craftsmanship] "is an approach to software development that emphasizes the coding skills of the software developers. It is a response by software developers to the mainstream software industry"

I personally don't like this definition. It's very dry and I don't think it captures the essence of what being a software craftsman means to a software developer.

## A More Personal Definition

Software Craftsmanship is a long journey to mastery. It is a mindset where software developers choose to be responsible for their own craft, constantly learning new tools and techniques and constantly better themselves. Software Craftsmanship is all about putting responsibility, professionalism, pragmatism, and pride back into software development.

## A Shorter Defion

Software Craftsmanship is about professionalism in software development.

This is probably the most important thing about Software Craftsmanship. If you only remember one thing after reading this book, I you remember this.

## Beyond Definitions

I prefer to think of Software Craftsmanship as an ideology or a mindset. It is something to express and give a name to all the things I always believed. Many developers, including myself, may say that they are the majority of the things that Software Craftsmanship talks about: caring about what they do; trying to be better; sometimes being professional; delight customers by helping them achieve whatever they want; learning from other developers what they knew; mentoring less-experienced colleagues.

If you have valued the things above then great you are a software craftsman, even if you do not like to refer to yourself as such. Many people do not really like labels or do even agree with the Software Craftsmanship method. That is totally fine. The important things here are the things we should care about as software professionals.

## Craft, Trade, Engineering, Science, or Art

When I first got with the Software Craftsmanship movement, I remember having many discussions about what software development was. I originally liked to describe it as an art. Then, over the years, I preferred to treat it as a craft. Many I know, people whom I really respect, do not agree with this at all. For some, it is a trade. For others, it is engineering. Very few would say that software development is a science.

Every developer has many arguments why software development should be viewed as art, craft, engineering, or science. When listening to all the reasons why software development is one thing or the other, the reasons make sense—some more than others, but they all make some sense.

Another big debate about Software Craftsmanship is the name itself. Some people absolutely hate it. They cannot see the point of comparing the most innovative and fast-crafting industry to something that go back to medieval times. And it may have a point.

Over the years, I joined some of these debates and spent a lot of energy trying to convince people why Software Craftsmanship is a good method and why software development is art. As I was getting involved with it, I sud realized that it didn't really matter. This is by far the least thing we were discussing. I personally like the metaphor and treat software development as craft, but what I really import and care are the values of Software Craftsmanship.

At the end of the day, what we all care about is raising the bar of our profession and helping developers, including inothers, to professional developers and not just unskilled workers who do what they are told to.

## Software Craftsmanship Summit

In 2008, years since the Agile summit, it became apparent that things in the world were not going well as asexpected. The deviation from Agile methodologies like Extreme Programming (XP) and the commercialization of the process-oriented methodologies brought to some of the original Agile innovators.

There were lots of talks about Software Craftsmanship in the Agile community, which Michael Martin and Paul joined to put a public face on Software Craftsmanship in order to better and define it. The main goal was they invites were all people who expressed interest, to some extent, in Software Craftsmanship.

The summit took place on December 13, 2008, in Libertyville, Illinois, just a few kilometers from Chicago. The summit lasted for six hours and there were roughly 30 people, including Uncle Bob, Brian Mariak, Corey Haines, Dave Hoover, Doug Bradley, and Chelsea Thomas.

**Keyword matching**: Specifying acronyms and technologies filtering candidates by keyword matching, recruiters or members of the HR department, who do not understand all the subtleties of the job, become the gatekeepers.

**Listing technologies**: Talented developers may not select their CVs because they may not have experience with all the technologies listed. A lousy list of technologies is desirable—but not needed—some are also added to the list. In some cases, the list of technologies is created without any correlation to the skills and experiences of those applying for the job.

**Wrong cultural fit criteria**: Companies fail to state their values, the expected attitude and responsibilities, in their job descriptions. When stated, it is done in a very subjective way which makes it impossible for any developer to agree. Easier to learn, great team spirit, positive attitude, and being smart is better to define the specific role. Not one developer will think they don't tick off the boxes in the list.

**Listing the requirements**: Listing requirements for the job, instead of required skills, years of experience, and academic requirements, it is a better way to attract better developers. Time and again we see developers with all the skills (on paper), a university degree, years of experience do not do the job.

**Wrong jobs**: Job descriptions are dressed to weed out, the not attract the best. Top developers do not apply for experience-based job descriptions. They are usually more selective when choosing where to work. The company's culture, type of responsibility, and type of project are normally more important for them than the specific technologies. The approach to eliminate the clearly unqualified backfires fails to attract the best.

**No internal promotions**: Normally people get promoted into the new job because of their achievements, leadership, team skills, and other aspects related to the new. No one gets promoted because they know the APIs of a framework or have five years of experience. If job descriptions are not used for internal promotions, why would they effect to attract new talent?

There are many reasons why job descriptions are bad but the ones should be enough for us to avoid them.

# What if a Job Description Is Needed?

Job descriptions are totally discouraged, but if one is needed, the focus should be on detailing the expected attitude and responsibilities, types of projects, technologies used (not required), and values and culture of the company.
Let's compare two job descriptions. The first one presents a generic offer from a financial institution. The second one is from the 7digital website.

# Job Description 1

Java Developer—JSE/JEE—Financial Software

Java Developer (JSE or JEE) with SQL experience required for a permanent role with a growing and extremely successful Financial Software organization.

The ideal candidate for this job role will possess a passion for technology and are to have exposure to, and learn more about the Financial Services area.

Salary: $500–£600 plus benefits and bonus

# Skills and Experience

Applicants must have core Java skills gained in a commercial environment along the following technical skills and experience:

- 5+ years experience Java Development (JSE or JEE)
- 3+ years experience SQL (some knowledge of SQL Server and Oracle)
- Experience with web technologies (HTML 5, CSS 3, jQuery, Spring MVC)
- Strong OO analysis and design experience
- Experience of the full software development lifecycle (SDLC)
- Ability to communicate with peers, business analysts, and subject matter experts

The following skills would be beneficial but not essential:

- Development on high performance distributed systems (in Java)
- Experience with real time and batch systems
- Experience with distributed technologies such as Oracle Coherence
- Experience with Agile, Hibibernate (including TDD, JUnit, etc.)

The Java developer role will involve close interaction with the Systems Architect, Java Team, and other members of the development team and will demand a high level of design and coding to implement and deliver enhancements.

There will be ample opportunities for the successful Java candidate to quickly expand on their banking and funds management experience, with plenty of business development.

This job description was definitely created for the mass market. It defines the salary range, making every developer who applies roughly. We are very precise with what we need: a developer with SQL; five or more years of JSE or JEE; or more years of SQL. We measure seniority by using a technology and no knowledge. We know all of acronyms: JUnit instead of JEE. This means they are using an old version of the Java Enterprise Edition or did not. Either way, both are acceptable.
As beneficial as not, they "Experience in Agile (including TDD, JUnit, etc.)" here they Agile environment with Test-Driven Development and JUnit if you are. The "etc." after JUnit is reinforcement that they don't really care much about Agile or Extreme practices. It is just a bunch of stuff.

It implied they have a hierarchy of self-organizing team when they mention "close interaction with the Systems Architect, Java Team."

At the beginning and at the end of the job description, they emphasize the opportunity to learn more about the business: financial services, funds, fund management. Since we have in the banking industry before, I know that there is a very subtle message. It implies that, with the opportunity to understand more about the business, you can progress in your career and become a manager.

Last but not least, they say the ideal candidate "will possess a passion for technology." I could not see anywhere in the job description how this passion is appreciated, and aligned with the company's software development

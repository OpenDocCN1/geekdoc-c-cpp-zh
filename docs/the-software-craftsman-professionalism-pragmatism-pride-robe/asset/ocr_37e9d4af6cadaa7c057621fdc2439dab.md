# # Software Craftsmanship History

Although John J. Reeves suggested that software development is more than an craft in 1992, I
believed that Software Craftsmanship really begins with the Thematic Programmer: From Jouryman to Master
by Andy Hunt and Thomas Thomas Then Peter McConnell published Software Craftsmanship: The New Imperative,
the book that introduced the ideas of that we used to shape the Software Craftsmanship movement years
later.

In 2002, Ken hosted a Apprenticeship Summit in North Carolina, and invited, among
few others, Peter McConnell, Ron Jeffries, and Robert Martin (Uncle Bob). At the time, Uwe was already holding
apprenthips. The conclusion of the summit was a call to form a community around software apprenticeship,
the company founded by Uncle Bob. Then on, Object Mentor started having on apprentices. But there wasn't much formality or process around it, but
several years later we went through apprenticeship again.

In 2006, Michael Martin and Paul gained right as an embodiment of Software

Although there were few people in Chicago embraced Software Craftsmanship, nothing really happened
the field between 2001 and (when Peter McConnell's book came out) 2003. Agile was the new thing, with
dag, methodologies, and techniques shaping the foundations of the software development industry. Back then,
there was no real need for Software Craftsmanship because people believed that the biggest problems in our
industry could be addressed by one of the Agile methodologies and practices.

In August 2008, during his keynote at an Agile conference, Bob published a fifth value for the Agile
Manifesto: "craftsmanship over craft". He changed his proposal to "craftsmanship over execution." Also
in August 2008, he published Clean Code: A Handbook of Software Craftsmanship and in May 2011, The Clean
Code: A Craft of Conduct for Professional Program, two of the most, if not the most, influential books in the
Craftsmanship circle.

# Learning to How So

More than not, we have tight deadlines and a lot of pressure from our managers to meet them. Many of these deadlines are unrealistic and imposed on developers by managers who were very aggressive in trying to make what they are delivered on time. Developers, the younger ones, tend to succumb to pressure and say they will get things done by the imposed date, because they are afraid to go against their managers or because they want to avoid conflict. When they are unable to deliver all the features they imposed, developers end up ending with conflict.

The outcome of this is usually very bad. Systems are riddled with full of bugs, and people are unhappy and full of distrust. After working hard for many days, and sacrificing time with their loved ones, developers feel undervalued by some manager who pushed them to do what they knew impossible, while he was at home enjoying his weekend with his family, they blame their incompetence. "How the hell did you break the sign-up page? Why isn't the system performing well? How on earth is the system not able to process 10,000 transactions per minute? # An Epic Failure

I worked for a large marketing company. The team wanted to launch this brand-new mobile portal we were developing. They wanted to launch it in three different countries and this estimation was a small percentage of our customers would use the application from day. Even being a small percentage, the company had 20 million customers in the three countries combined, so we were talking about a few thousand customers. We did not know. One manager said that he wanted the website to go live by a certain date and we knew it. So, in one of our first meetings, we told him that we probably would not meet the deadline he was asking for. So he had to us and said that if we worked hard we would be able to pull it off. We were not sure, but he was being so insistent that we would do it and try our best. And it was a disaster.

Week by week and a business analyst asked for more features and the deadline did not move. More and more, then they told us we were struggling with the requirements, let alone taking on new ones. However, they would come back to us and the marketing team had already starting the campaign in all three countries.

We told the marketing team to get our first estimate and if we could speak to them in order so we could come up with an agreement on which features we had to deliver and negotiate the scope of a few features. We had a few ideas that could make some features smaller or less complex. The manager was our contact point and he was fully aware of all the team's needs, so there was no point speaking to him. "No, there is no time," he said. "We need everything," he said. After all the conversations we had with him, then our only option was to put in as much work and hours as we could. What could we do?

We spent five weeks working day and hours. Of course when I am talking about the developers. The manager and business analyst were nowhere to be seen after six o'clock and never available on the phone. Since the beginning of the project, we felt that there had some infrastructure to work in. We had to have a production-like environment and to prepare some load-testing infrastructure. That was so and that we should focus on building the infrastructure, but the marketing team let. Before the go-live date, they told us that we had to stop working on features and focus on some technical work to guarantee that the application would work well in production, but that meant we were not able to deliver some of the features we wanted. The answer was no. We had to finish all of the business features. Guys, this is the last day and I know you but we need to speak to the people on the marketing team, people we had not met once since the beginning of the project, but yet again the answer was no. The deadline approaching, and with so much work already into it, we said, "Sure, we will do it."

The final day was a Monday morning and the application went live. Our last deployment to production had been a few hours before. We were tired and wanted the whole thing to be over but at the same time we were extremely proud of ourselves, knowing that we saved the project. We would be considered heroes. We had few conversations in the middle of the night, during a coffee break, asking for raises and promotions

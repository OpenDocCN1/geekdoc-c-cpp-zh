# Summary

Delivering value is the obligation of any professional who is paid to do a job. But there is more to job than that. Work doesn't need to be the antonym of pleasure—we just need to know what to look for when choosing a job: autonomy, mastery, and purpose. These are the three things that a craftsman should look for in each job. Although it's not always easy to find jobs that can provide us that, they exist. Maybe we are not just ready to get these jobs yet.

The better we are the easier it is to find jobs that give us pleasure. But to reach a point where we can change the jobs we want we need a lot of effort and hard work to develop our careers. Our own careers should always take precedence over a career inside a company. It's great when a career inside a company is aligned with our own career aspirations but it is often a mistake to let a company control our careers. Jobs are an investment. # 9. Recruitment

The first thing to do when hiring a new person is to make we are not making our existing problem bigger. If we are unhappy with how our developers work or behave, there is no point in hiring more developers who work and behave in the exact same way. If we want developers to be constantly learning, bringing innovations, adopting more efficient practices, caring about the project and quality of the code, self-organizing to solve problems, and striving to be better than they do, why would we hire more developers who satisfy this criteria? Although this sounds obvious to many of us, it is not so obvious to hiring managers and the human resources department. Although every company would say they want the best people, they do not really know what that means. And a proof of that is they are averse to job openings.

In this chapter we look at how to attract software developers to our organizations and how to do proactive recruitment. We will also look at how to craft job descriptions and how to avoid descriptions altogether.

# Too Busy to Interview

The main mistake that makes interviewing so easy is that they say to interview: "We can't spend too much time. We have work to do."

We spend more time with our colleagues than we spent with them—which makes it even important that we trust and have relationships. Because the majority of businesses rely heavily on developers, developers play a big part in the success or failure of business. Developers are the ones who maintain the machine that enables businesses to stay alive.

Since hiring developers is key, the quality of the interviewers is extremely important. Job candidates are always judged from a biased perspective: they will be considered good or bad according to the interviewer's own views, values, and technical abilities. People who do not value themselves and believe in hierarchies will not hire anyone better than themselves. On the other hand, people who seek to do things in a better way will always look for people who are better or at least good as them.

Although we need to allocate time to interview, the whole recruitment process—from collecting CVs to interviewing—can take weeks or months. If not, we wasted a lot of time looking at all-the-other CVs and interviewing many unsuitable candidates.

Time-consuming activities or defining a formal recruitment process is not the solution. The wasted interviewing and wrong candidates is the problem we must address. Hiring interviewing to find four or five is not viable, and delegating the recruitment activity is too risky; the only alternative is to be more specific with our filtering criteria.

# No Job Descriptions

Job descriptions are anti-patterns. Lod Asher has a series of articles on the topic at the website itlt.com. He says that traditional skills and experience-based job descriptions are "anti-talent and anti-diversity, aside from being terrible predictors of future success.

Here are some reasons:

- Absolute numbers are: arbitrary, misleading, and confusing. Defining that a candidate needs to have five or more years of experience in Java or a certain university degree does not make sense. Having five years of experience is different from having five times one year of experience. Many developers have worked with the same technology for a long time but never learned anything after they were hired. A good university degree doesn't mean anything because the students are not ready to be a professional software developer when they graduate.

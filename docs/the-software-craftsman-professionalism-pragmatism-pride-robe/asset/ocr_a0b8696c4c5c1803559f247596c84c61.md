# # Preface

In the mid-twenties, two years after I started my professional career, a large international company in Sao Paulo announced that there were 60 developers in need. The selection process would take a few weeks and was divided into four phases: a three-hour technical; two weeks of training in their proprietary technology followed by a test; a full day of group dynamics; and a round of interviews. It announced in a newspaper and 900 applicants. I was working for a small software house at the time, a place where I was very happy, but I felt that I was looking for something better. Since the first few weeks would be on a Saturday, I had to apply a bit. Fewer than 300 candidates passed to the first phase, and I was nervous and confident but not. I had to resign from my current job in order to move forward with the selection process I would need to take many days off for the remaining days. Then my financial situation was so weak and I could not count on any monetary support from my family. It was quite hard to resign a job I liked in order to pursue a dream I had no idea would come true. Also, I had to learn everything in the new job. That was the type of company I wanted to work for. For that I wanted.

I was 21 years old, and I already had a few years of experience starting when I was 11 and started my professional career when I was 19. The problem is this mix of youth and a bit of cockiness that could easily lead me to arrogance. I was of an arrogant, young person you could know. I could beat anyone hands down. I thought of myself, better than any other person who studied with me at university, but I was better than the majority of developers who worked with me in my previous jobs.

After going through all four phases, the international company announced that they could not find 60 developers at the level they were expecting. They hired 32 and I was one of them. I was the more and more cocky each day. In my first few months, while speaking to different business modules, I was responsible for delivering one business modules of the system. We started talking about this team, a team that seemed the best team in the company. They were the "architecture" team, responsible for the core and for providing all architecture code used by the business.

The architecture team was led by this incredible guy, who also did the management work, was also a fantastic developer. He was a man, but he had always a few time to check code, code in his name, and review the code written by his team. His team was always working on interesting things and that their code was really good. It was exactly what I wanted.

After a few weeks, I was asked to be the manager of the architecture team, this guy I heard so much about. I didn't really know what to say or what I was expecting. I was only one of them. I had nothing to lose. The worst-case scenario he would say that he was not interested to be on his team. One day I saw him alone in the company I was shaking. I was nervous. He looked at me and smiled with a hand. "I'm Namce he said. Nice to meet you." He was calm and relaxed. "I want you to work for us," he said after a few seconds. I was a bit surprised but it turned out it in a very positive way. We then started talking about the selection process, why I applied, what I was expecting from the job. He asked me if I had projects to show. I mentioned some technologies I knew and some other things that I did. And after around 30 minutes of conversation the meeting had started. I was shocked. That was not what I was expecting. So we agreed to schedule a meeting, a formal interview and so on. It took me a long time to get used to the situation and I was very concerned about my professional development. I was wondering whether I was capable of doing the right thing.

My first day was a Monday. Namce came to my desk and started assigning me a task. He explained one of the applications and what I had to do. He said it would be the best way to check what I could do. It was short. That was my challenge. I had to show him how well I wanted to be there. I stayed in the office until midnight, felt shift, a few hours later every Tuesday, and around two o'clock in the afternoon. I had finished my assignment with less than half of the time I had given. I was feeling great. I had many ideas of how to be able to contribute to the project. It was a huge achievement for me.

I rushed to Namce's office and said it with excitement. "It's done. I finished it." and then he was stopping and turned to me. "Making things is the work I expect from someone who is part of me," he said. "What you say is finished, if it proves it." That was like a big slap in my face. My face faded a bit and I was embarrassed. Maybe I was too fast. I went back to my desk for a long day. He did not have much else to say. "Sit here and let's see what you do," he said. There and I watched him typing in the command line, checking the code from source and my opening .pas file containing all the code. He opened the file with this horrible black and green editor on the command line. That was the first time I had seen it. We were using Delphi instead of an integrated development environment (IDE). For me, seeing some Delphi files vi (a Unix text editor) was very alien. "Close it so we can have a look at it," he said. He positioned the cursor on the first and started looking at the code line.

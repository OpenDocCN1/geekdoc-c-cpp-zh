# Practice, Practice, Practice

How it is as important as getting it. If we want to be good at writing quality code, we need to practice how to write quality code. There is no other way. The practice should be on the techniques we are using and not in solving the problem. Solving problems is what we are paid for and what we do at work. However, we should focus on how we are solving the problem. For example, if you are trying to learn or get better at Test-Driven Development, this is a hard discipline to master. It is counterintuitive at first, and that is why we need to practice

Do you remember when you were learning to drive? I remember every time I was driving a car with a high hill and I tried to think, 'Damn, I need to stop and pull in first gear, the clutch be subtle, and that the car does not stall or roll away.' I also remember the first time I had friends with me and they were calling 'Car, watch out on that!' and I nervously glancing between the mirrors, my fingers gripping the steering wheel they would tell me.. 'I remember because when I was learning to drive, I was about to drive over. Well the gear shifting, I was worried about the mirrors, other cars, driving straight and in the lane, not driving into the other lane when turning, and so I could not divert my attention to the radio. Now, I do not even remember what the radio. We do not think about the mechanics of a car anymore. We focus on where we are going, what we are going to do when we arrive there; we listen to music, sing, have conversations while driving. Driving a car second nature and the car is now an extension of our bodies.

Technical practices and new technologies are the same thing. The more we practice, the more comfortable we become, to the point where we do not have to think about what we want to do.

When we are needing to focus on writing the code we could quickly. If it takes us minutes or a few hours, to write a simple test is it the best test we have written, that is OK. We should not worry if we take a long time to name a variable, method, or class. As long as we try our best to find the most appropriate name for it, we are great. We are practicing and when we do, we should strive for practice. With this approach, facing the deadline of a real software project, we can concentrate on finding a solution to the problem and not on how to write tests or commands to use.

# Katas

Katas are simple coding domain that can be understood in a few minutes and are complicated enough to be solved too quickly. They normally take from a few minutes to a few hours to be solved and as a practice to try new techniques and approaches. An example would be a kata where we need to calculate the score of a good game or bowling game tic-tac-toe. We can use these katas to practice things we think we are not good at, like Test-Driven Development, language, or a framework.

Katas have been criticized by some in the Agile world, 'Some people say that it is plain silly to solve the same thing over and over again.' Well, there is some degree of truth to it. Usually it is said because the term comes from martial arts and that it is done in karate. For the example we do something over and over again. However, solving katas with our current toolkit (the techniques and tools that we are comfortable with) does not make a lot of sense.

When doing, it is to stretch our outlets, practices, techniques, and technologies that we are not comfortable with, with the intent of getting better at. After practicing these things takes a few times, you may feel for weeks, days, and months before they feel like them. In a professional environment. About like this is exactly what we are trying to achieve.

Katas can be very beneficial when we know how to do them over and again but by using a different approach or technique each time. That allows us to experiment and get correct. The correct word to define this practice, according to martial arts, as developers of all the world have already adopted the word kata, is keeping it instead of introducing another term.

# Focus and Determination

Vojra said, "You've got to be careful if you don't know where you're going because you might not get there." We software craftsmen, value and control our careers. We understand that a career is a life-long journey, depending on the choices we make, we or may not lead us to mastery.

Defining the direction we want to go in our careers is important. However, this is a long-term goal and many things can change. Think about your career if you are managing a multilayer project. Once we understand the project vision —the ultimate goal—we take the most important requirements, break them down into smaller pieces, and iterate. While working on small iterations, we also re-evaluating the project goals and adapting when necessary. It's the same with our careers.

# But If We Don't Know Where We Are Going?

It sounds simple but we do not always know where to go next. Actually, defining the direction we want to take our careers can be an extremely difficult thing to do. We don't decide it once and for all. We need to constantly evaluate our career decisions based not just our passion but also all the events in our personal and professional lives.
We need to be where we are in relation to our goals and our dreams. We need to create situations where opportunities can be presented to us. No one will knock on our doors and offer us a opportunity if we keep ourselves isolated from the rest of the world, locked inside our houses and offices. No one will offer us a opportunity if they don't know who we are, what we do, and most importantly, how we work. We need to go out and speak to people. We need to show other people what we can do. We need to be comfortable with technical practices, and to talk about what we want to do next, create an environment where other people and companies can present us with some options. When we have a few doors, and a few options available to you, you will be in a better place to decide what to do.

Here are a few things we can do in order to create opportunities:

+   - Expand our technical knowledge, studying things that are out of our comfort zone, like new languages and technologies.
- Attend user groups and community events.
- Network with other developers and businesspeople.
- Blog about what we are learning and doing.
- Contribute to open source projects.
- Develop projects and make them publicly available.
- Attend conferences.
- Present at a conference.

The things described above create situations where we can receive options that we were not considering before, and this could help us decide what to do.

# Job as Investment

The definition of a great career in software development is very personal: each of us have a different dream, aspiration, and different person has it. However, the process of career aspirations is very similar. The most important thing is to treat every job as an investment, a step toward a bigger goal. Software craftsmen value their ever-increasing knowledge, passion, dedication, and professional attitude in every job they do. Therefore, for like any other type of work, we need to be clear about what we expect as a return, and we need to measure it regularly throughout the length of the investment.

After a few months of interviews and networking, I finally accepted a job in an investment bank. For many days, Well then I doubted myself that investment banking was totally compatible with Agile and Software craftsmanship principles, making them a no-go for me. I also thought that I would be totally constrained by their bureaucracy and their hierarchies. However, I realized I could help them adopt technical practices and bring new ideas into the existing ones. I was looking for a job where I could find some of the gaps that I had in my career. The return on investment I was looking for was experience in developing software at a very scale, with teams all around (the world I was not familiar with), and opens doors in an industry that was closed to me—usually you need to have previous experience or a financial background to be hired as a high in an investment bank. I wanted to see how effective of all my preferred software development projects and techniques would work. Exposing myself to an unfamiliar environment allowed me to learn and discover different ways of doing things. That was the return on investment I was expecting from this job.

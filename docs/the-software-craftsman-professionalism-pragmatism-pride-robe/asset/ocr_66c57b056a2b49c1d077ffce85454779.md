My way is to stay in a job for as long as it remains aligned with our career aspirations. Whenever it starts to diverge from our career aspirations, the partnership with employees gets weary and less beneficial for both. Whenever we are not moving forward, we are not learning or enjoying ourselves, we should move on. Loving the company and its colleagues is not enough to stay in a job. People move on, companies move on, and so we. Looking for a job that is more aligned with our career aspirations is a favor that we do in order to bring in new, with fresh ideas and energy to inspire the status quo and what we do. From experience, I believe that staff turnover of 15 to 30 percent a year is very healthy for a company. New people with new ideas help the company remain to date, competitive, and fresh.

# What If We Don't Know Where to Go?

At first, this is a little scary, but it happens to everyone. When we don't know where to go or what to do next, some things can get lost. A little bit of courage to adapt at that, but when we do to ourselves, things get better. Once we know where we are going, we can be more focused and objective in our way. That for- has certainly happened to me. In fact, it has happened three times in my career. There is a lot you can do when you want to meet people. Get out of yourself and open as many doors as you can. Start attending networking events. Starting to open new opportunities. In conversations in meetings. Look for people. Go out there and show people where you are going. You will see there is a world out there full of people, and with opportunities you never expected before. This will help you decide where to go next. Personally, I know a few stories from developers who met in technical communities and started their own companies, found great jobs, or changed their careers.

# Job Diversity

Software development is a very diverse profession, and skillful craftsmen have very broad experiences. Being a developer in startup and building games for mobiles is very different from being a developer building applications for a global investment bank. Developing a mass-market product is very different from developing software. Developing tools is very different from building bespoke software. Developing web applications is very different from developing applications with no user interface. Although you can argue that software should be treated as a product-internal tools or other systems as we are-clients is not always the case. By using your applications, you will learn many of the good practices that apply in many different types of software, the whole environment is different. People are different. The relationship with clients or users is different. The amount of pressure you are under in certain projects is different and experienced professionally. In certain applications, a bug can cause huge damage (millions of dollars in finance, or even endangerment of human life), while in others, a bug production might not be a big deal (some may get a bit annoyed but they will get it over soon, when the bug is fixed).

Working for a company can be tough if you are not exposed to different types of projects and environments. I'm not saying this is bad, but it's definitely no great deal. A consultancy company has many different environments and they provide us with many different opportunities. As you move from project to project, you have opportunities to work with different technologies, different types of software, different types of companies, different team dynamics, and different tools and processes. You may have all of these experiences while still working for a company with your career. On top of that, you can increase your network, which can be very handy as you move with your career to a new company. Working on various projects can be exciting; it makes you go to places you have never been before. Experiencing many different opening doors can lead you to places you have never considered before. As a consultant is not for everyone, but this is something to consider, mainly early in your career. Working for a few years can give you a lot of exposure, experience, and options, making it easier for you to decide where to take your career in.

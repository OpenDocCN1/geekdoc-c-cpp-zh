A question that is often asked during our Software Craftsmanship meetings is,"How can I convince my team [or manager, company] to adopt TDD [or pair programming, continuous integration]" Trying to sell technical practices for what they are pointless. It doesn't work. Don't focus the practices themselves when trying to convince managers or team members to adopt them. Focus the discussion on the benefit they bring and how they compare to the practices they currently have. A smaller feedback loop; a better understanding of requirements and cost; knowledge sharing; reducing of bugs; and quick and totally automated releases to production are examples of values added by technical practices.

# Automated Testing

Automated testing enables us, with a click of the button, to have our entire system tested within a few minutes. It gives us confidence to deploy approach into production minutes after the last change was made. The amount of time the business can save avoiding long quality assurance (QA) phases can be measured in days, if not weeks.

The feedback loop on the correctness of our code is reduced from weeks to minutes, enabling us to correct any mistakes almost immediately. In this reduction the feedback loop also prevents us from writing more code on top of erroneous code—reducing the overall bug-fixing cost.

As the system grows, testing phases become longer, the entire system must be manually tested. This may also mean more testers. Writing test before writing any production code makes us faster as the system grows. The increase in speed is due to our confidence to add features without breaking the system. The entire test suite normally takes a few minutes to run, it can be run as often as needed—reducing the need for lengthy testing phases and the number of people involved in them. Automated testing gives us measurable business value.

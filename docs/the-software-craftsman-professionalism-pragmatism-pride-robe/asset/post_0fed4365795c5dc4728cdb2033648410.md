
Unfortunately, the notion of seniority has not changed in the last 20 years. Software developers are normally considered according to the number of years they have worked in the industry and not according to their knowledge.

There is a difference between having years of experience and having experience repeated times. Years working on different projects, with different technologies, and for different companies is different from years working for the same company, on the same project, and with the same people and the same technology.

There is no such thing as a senior or junior developer. Developers may be very experienced in developing applications for large organizations but totally inexperienced writing games in JavaScript. They can be extremely experienced in a working or Agile environment but totally inexperienced dealing with the bureaucracy and cultural challenges of a large corporation.

## A New Reality

Good programming practices and techniques can be applied in many different environments and systems of types; coding is just one of the things that modern developers do. Beyond just about writing code in any language or framework, modern developers need to know how to provide to customers, automate tests and deployment, manage technical challenges that may affect the entire business, work in distributed teams, help clients define and prioritize requirements, report progress, manage changes and expectations, protect potential clients and work with pre-sales activities, estimate time and costs, interview new team members, design and evolve the architecture, with non-functional requirements and service level agreements (SLAs), understanding business goals, make decisions based on facts and find better ways to win users, about the value they deliver to customers, and many other things.

Some day developers have been around for the decades. Well, that is true for some senior developers, but it is definitely not true for the majority of them. In the past, the roles and responsibilities of the people involved in a software project were merged for many years. Developers were not involved in the design and architecture of the applications, rarely had access to users, and were never to be trusted to make business decisions.

The days when developers were specialized in only one area had—if it is not coding, it's no longer acceptable. Companies started to learn, flattening their hierarchies and replacing many-one-pony professionals with generalizing specialists. Agile is for developers who like to be flexible, adaptable, and ready to write code at all times.

In the past two decades, with the Internet, mobile devices, cloud services, and general evolution, everything changed. Clients want more things, and companies are becoming more aggressive with their software offerings. Being able to easily change the software, with agility, the more we value the value provided to customers becomes. We now rely on software-like architectures—Amazon, Google, Twitter, Facebook, and many others—become the dominant patterns in the market. Agile replaces bureaucratic service companies with rigid and deep hierarchical structures; more agile companies win. The waterfall approach with a traditional management style often cannot keep up with the pace of change—we need to keep up with the game.

The scale of projects has changed. Today, we really see an application that does not integrate with others. We don't see new projects built with a client-server architecture, with clients deployed in each machine, having a monolithic database as an integration point. Modern applications are built with multiple integrations. Where we had applications only used by employees, now we use millions of people around the world. For many companies, the cost of bugs in production became enough to push them out of business, or cause massive damage to them.

This reality in the software industry forced an evolution of the software development process. Companies are demanding professional software developers, not just cheaper ones who do what they are told to.

Furthermore, our industry is leaving behind the Industrial revolution inheritance—command and control; the roles and responsibilities of a software developer become wider and more important, making developers to provide better development capable of up-skilling and de-skilling looking forward with their careers. The problems encountered on projects with a rigid and flat plan, strictly defined process, are being erased. Flat hierarchies with talented and empowered professionals are becoming the norm. The need for agility and well-crafted software has never been higher.

In 2001, a revolution in the software industry happened: Agile companies all the world started adopting and embracing the Agile methodologies and mindset, changing their processes and internal structures. However, even with significant improvements in their processes, companies were still unhappy with their delivery capabilities: not responding to change fast enough, low-quality software, bugs, high maintenance costs, unhappy customers, and difficulties in hiring good developers. Developers are still struggling to maintain, test, and add new features to the software they work with, especially if they are new to the project. Why do we need Software Craftsmanship? Back in 2001, seventeen influential people met together in Utah to discuss what Agile is. They created the Agile Manifesto. The Agile Alliance formed.

They pioneered various methodologies and techniques like:
- Extreme Programming (XP)
- Scrum
- Dynamic Systems Development (DSD)
- Adaptive Software Development
- Feature-Driven Development
- Pragmatic Programming

Agile is not a single thing. Agile is a combination of methodologies and techniques that help teams adapt to the ever-changing nature of software and also reduce the risks associated with them. Agile disciplines are divided into two main groups: process-oriented and technical.

### Process-Oriented Disciplines

These disciplines affect how teams and organizations work, and organize. They normally prescribe or suggest, among many others, the types of methods teams should use; the roles people play; ways to capture requirements; how to measure velocity; how to work in an iterative way; how to plan and deliver.

Process-oriented disciplines help teams focus on what is really important and valuable to the business. Using these methodologies is a great way to make sure the team is building the right thing.

### Technical-Oriented Disciplines

These disciplines are more specific to the challenges of developing, growing, and maintaining software. They normally prescribe or suggest technical practices:
- Test-Driven Development (TDD)
- Continuous Integration
- Simple design

Technical-oriented disciplines help teams focus on the quality of the software they are producing and help teams to make sure they are building the right thing.

## What Is It to Be Agile?

> Agile means adapting successfully to circumstances.
> — Tom Gil

We don't go Agile. Either we are Agile or we are not. Agile methodologies are all about quick and short feedback loops. The quicker and faster our feedback loop, the more agile we can become. Every time we receive feedback, we have an opportunity to react to it, and the act of reacting (or not) to new information makes us (or less) agile. Narrowing the feedback loop helps us make mistakes visible sooner, allowing us to implement and adapt quickly. The faster we see a demo or any other feature to test, the earlier we get feedback on it. I'm using user here as a general term. If we are for a product owner, sponsor, or an end user. Feedback from multiple sources is extremely important. When a feature is commercially viable sooner helps mitigate the risks of our industry.

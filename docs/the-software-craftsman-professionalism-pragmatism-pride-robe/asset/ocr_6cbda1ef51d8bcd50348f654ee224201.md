# Narrow Focus

Another very misunderstanding is to think that Software Craftsmanship is TDD and "beautiful" code. Some very uninformed people, mainly from the Agile community, have made comments that software craftsmen are shortsighted and have a very narrow focus. I hope that the content of this book helps explain that Software Craftsmanship goes beyond TDD and well crafted code.

Lean, Agile and Craftsmanship are totally aligned and have similar values. They all focus on providing value to clients through transparency and feedback loops. The major difference is that their practices and disciplines are best applied in different spheres of an organization.

# Craftsmanship versus XP

Craftsmanship is an ideology, XP is a methodology. An ideology is about values, attitude, and behavior. A methodology is about practices that solve specific problems.

XP is also based on values (simplicity, communication, feedback, respect, and courage), but it is more commonly known for its practices.

Craftsmanship focuses on, a professional attitude is totally related to the practices we use, and currently, the majority of these practices are XP practices.

# Attachment to Practices

Software craftsmen are not attached to practices. They are attached to the practices that give them. For the vast majority of projects, XP practices have to be the practices that provide value to clients and development, and that is the reason why Software Craftsmanship advocates them.

Our industry is in constant evolution and we are always trying to uncover better and more efficient ways to deliver software. Practices are chosen according to the value they bring, and whenever we find practices that could give us more than the XP practices, we are more happy to adopt them.

# Agile Coaches and Managers

While reviewing this, I noticed that it was quite tough, unf own on, Agile coaches and managers. My professionalism, knowledge, and ability to express opinion about a specific subject, is not as easy to put down into the implied knowledge and personal experiences we have which serve as a context for that position.

I have the pleasure of working with some great Agile coaches and managers. Their ability to deal with tough situations (business or people-related), was so rare. Unfortunately, I worked with many bad Agile coaches and many managers who were useless, or at best, not very good at their jobs.

Due to their positions, power, and influence, these changes can damage not projects, but also an entire organization. Working under bad leadership and being advised by bad Agile coaches can destroy the motivation of a team, causing a lot of frustration, and even make the best leave the company.

In many parts of the book, when referring to Agile coaches and managers, I was thinking about all the bad ones I worked with and not the good ones.

Agile coaches and managers are very important in some organizations, and I have a lot of respect for great professionals, regardless of what type of work they do.

# Software Apprenticeships

Some people have asked why I started writing about software apprenticeship. For a Software Craftsmanship book, this is a fair question. There were a few reasons that me did not decide to it. The main reason was that software apprenticeship is a huge topic, with many different people and companies doing in a completely different way. Although I have my own views on the subject of a software apprenticeship program at Codars,, I felt the topic would take a big chunk of the book and would end up deviating from it core message. However, it is still my plan to write about it in the future. Maybe in another book or a series of articles.

## Lesson Learned

In the story above, we can inevitably say that the manager did not act properly. We can say he never cared about the team and was trying to get his own promotion while other people were doing the hard work for him. We can say it was his fault and he should be blamed. We can also say that the developers should have stood up to tell everyone how the manager worked and how they worked to make the project a success. Well, we can say whatever we wanted, but saying anything after that failure was of no use. It was too late. It didn't matter anymore.

Unfortunately, the developers could have avoided the situation, and we were blamed. We did not act properly. We should have said that we would try to explain something when the code was impossible. If we believed that it could be possible to have all the features deployed into production, we would not have all the tests in place and all the confidence that we would have been. Being not comfortable to confront a manager and having the whole situation, we were managing something that was not pushing us to accept it. Deep inside we should have said how good we were and what we could have done, but here, we didn't have the time to pull it off and what we were. We would have been recognized as the guys who made a successful campaign viable. In this situation, we wanted to give it a go.

In this situation, we would have said and we would never try, and even though we were heroes. We would have pressured him to speak to the marketing team in the beginning. We should have identified that our manager was not transparent with the marketing team when he constantly asked for more features, we did not have capacity. We had communicated via email every time we had a meeting where we did not have the capacity and that we had to prepare the system for production load. Although very badly, our manager was trying to do features without being realistic and not accepting responsibility of delivering something when it was of low quality and not ready for production. We had made it clear we could not commit to anything if we did not have a meeting, of all, with the marketing team, when the new features began to be introduced. We would have made very clear that a full system of features is what we wanted, but what could be used by anyone. It is not worth by anyone. We did not act professionally when we could be heroes. We should have said so.

## Being Professional

Often we have had to deal with very hard deadlines. The best way to deal with this is to analyze everything that the risks we see and concerns we make. We should raise our concerns and highlight all the uncertainty and unclear points as much as possible. Most important, we need to state how much could be confidently identified by the experts. By confidently identifying, mean code that is tested and intensively tried in a production-environment.

When they say we cannot deliver the features by an imposed manager, developers do not always take that seriously. A negotiation between managers and developers takes place, but with lack of negotiation skills and ill-prepared arguments, developers usually succumb to the pressure. Some managers can be very persuasive in ever convincing developers to commit to a tough deadline. "Guys, you know how important this is. It will be good if I trust you. We really need to get this done. I am sure if you work hard we will be able to deliver." In order to avoid confrontation or to demonstrate a can-attitude, respond: "OK, we will try our best."

When we are going to try, two things normally happen: the person or people we say that to understand that we are going to deliver. For them, "trying" is equal to "yes, it will be done." The other thing is we are explicitly saying that we do not normally work hard. It is almost like we are saving energy and we could use it to speed up at any point in time.

If we know it will be impossible to deliver all the features before a deadline, how can we tell our managers we are going to deliver them? What does try mean? Does it mean we can deliver if we work harder? Does it mean we are committed to work for more hours, including weekends? If yes, would that really make a difference? If not, is it better that we do not try?

Sometimes there are problems that we do not know how to solve. When this is the case, we should say it honestly and as quickly as possible. With new information we will be able to investigate the problem and do our best at solving it, we are not making false promises whatever we learn. Providing updates of as often as we can is our best; it enables the team to do what to do with new information and also help in areas where they might have a better understanding than we do. This will make the team really trust us when we say what we know. Honest, no matter how bad the situation is, it is a sign of professionalism

## Providing Options

Always saying no is not professional. Every time, we need a list of alternatives. Even if we are not able to come up with options, it helps. At the end of the day, we should always focus on solving the problem, regardless of which solution we proposed.

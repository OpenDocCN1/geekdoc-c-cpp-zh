# Who Owns Your Career?

What if the company we work for does not buy us? If the company never sends us to any training or courses? Or will it be that we could learn new things? Does it really mean the company is bad?

Imagine that you have a problem to solve in your house. Imagine that you need a lawyer for any legal issues, or a doctor or a dentist when you have an aching tooth. Normally you go to these professionals when you have a problem, so imagine turning them back and saying, "Can you make me a book? Can you send me on a course?" What would you think about? To make things even worse, imagine that you, for some reason, decide to buy the book or send them on a service, and they acquired the knowledge you gave them, they come back and charge you for their services. How does it sound to you?

These professionals need to invest in their own careers so they can do a good job, satisfy their customers, and hopefully receive other clients. They need to satisfy their current clients. Those that don't end up losing clients, receive fewer rewards for forever, and will slowly be forced out of their business.

On the other hand, factories work, for example rely on training. Factories need to train their employees to use new machines and perform work. Factory workers have no say in what machines the factory should buy they are just to be told what.

Clients pay with the expectation and experience of receiving a good service. They pay professionals to solve the problems in the best way possible. Clients don't pay for learning. Clients pay for their knowledge and skills. Professionals are expected to provide solutions, viable alternatives, and ideas to their clients. Professionals are expected to help their clients achieve whatever they want to achieve in the best way possible, and that is how they build their trust and reputation.

We have all wanted to be respected as professionals before we that we need to start behaving like professionals. That means that we should use our own time and money to get better at what we do. We should show our own careers and what we learn and what we earn. We should be in a position that we can help clients and employees to achieve their goals. Those who only rely on their company knowledge are not professional software developers. They are just factory workers in disguise

Companies should invest in people but software professionals do not see that as their obligation. That should be a bonus, a win-win situation. Companies that provide time to get better at what they do are much better and can become far more efficient. Passionate developers will always favor these companies when choosing who to work for.

There are no subscripts F(ju)—that is, it exists for all frequencies f(ju) from negative to positive energy. F(ju) has a magnitude and a phase. The unique signal F(ω) is called the spectrum. Figure 1.17(b) shows the magnitude of the Fourier integral that represents some arbitrary signal f(t) in Figure 1.17(a), this is a sketch and not mathematically exact. You can see that the spectrum can have both positive and negative amplitudes. We plot the magnitude or absolute value of the spectrum instead in Figure 1.17 (c). The dots at the points of the spectrum indicate that these theoretically extend out to positive and negative infinity.

When you see plots of spectrum in this book, you will always be looking at the magnitude plotted in dB). When you also see the spectrum and the magnitude are symmetrical about the y-axis. This is because the input signal f(t) is real (has no imaginary components)—this will be the case with all our audio signals. You may also be disturbed by the fact that there are indeed negative frequencies in the spectra—in fact, for each frequency component will have an identical frequency over the negative side of the plot. Figure 1.17 is intentionally drawn to show how the magnitude produces the same picture, all positive. It also shows peaks (resonances) and notches (anti-resonances) because these are common in music signals. Books will show a much more generalized spectrum, usually bell shaped as in Figure 1.18 (a). Lowpass filtering this signal to the Nyquist criteria produces a clipped version of signal, also common in DSP textbooks. The clipped spectrum is said to be bandlimited.

Equation 1.7 shows a relationship between a continuous time signal and its continuous frequency domain spectrum. We can also go in the other direction and represent the spectrum as an infinite sum of time functions with Equation 1.8.

```
F(ω) = \int f(t)e^{-jωt}dt
```

Equation 1.7 is called the Fourier transform and it converts a function in f(t) into a spectrum F(ω). Notably, Equation 1.8 is called the inverse Fourier transform since it performs the opposite function. The Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT) performs the integration over a finite time period between t1 and t2.

```
F(ω) = \int f(t)e^{jωt}dt
```

The Fourier Transform converts a signal or function whose dependent variable is t into a signal or function whose dependent variable is complex ju.

# # 11.5 The Moog AniMog Anisotropic Synthesizer

In 2011, Moog reintroduced vector synthesis to the world with its AniMog iOS app. The app has won multiple awards in both musical and technical categories. Like its predecessors, it excels at producing lush wavables such as searing, quickly modulated sounds. Moog names their brand of vector synthesis anisotropic. Merriam-Webster defines anisotropic as“exhibiting different values when measured in different directions”, which the vector vector emphasizes direction and magnitude. The control surface consists of a rectangular grid of 128 wavables comprising eight rows and 16 columns, as shown in Figure 11.17. There is no joystick, as the grid and touch surface replace it.

The rows are calibrated timbers. Each row consists of 16 wavables that are musically or sonically related. Each wavetable is a sample of a sound with a slight variation in timbre, noise or some other attribute the timbres in the same row. For example, one row may feature a sound bass sweep. Moving across the row from left to right reveals that successive wavetable is slightly brighter than the previous, as it were sampled with the filter knob in a slightly different but increasing position. The wavables get more or less resonant, noisy, bass-y, mid-range, transient, et., as you move from left to right. Each row is a complete set of sounds. When you assign the rows, you may assign similar or different sounds. You might populate the upper rows with harsh metallic sounds and the lower rows with soft mellow ones, or interleave them. The timbres themselves are named in a way that is reminiscent of the ProphetVS, such as “Distorted Primes” and “Saw Octave Dri?..” In other words, they are not named “Slap Bass” or “Liquid Lead”—rather, they are named after the waveform clips they contain.

AniMog allows you to create a vector path connecting up to 14 segments using 15 vertices by tapping on the iOS device. This results in a familiar vector path as shown in Figure 11.18—the obvious difference is that the path is stretched across a rectangle of 128 sounds rather than a diamond of only. Likewise, you can hear the wavet smoothly crossing into another as you move along the path. You are not allowed to program the time between each segment, as in the ProphetVS and Wastenv; instead you control a single rate value that controls the overall time from the beginning vertex to the end vertex. Thus, longer segments will have a longer time from beginning to end, and shorter segments will have a shorter time. You can also program the path mode as once, loop, or back-and-forth. There are not many looping options compared with the original vector sounds, but you can set up many more segments.

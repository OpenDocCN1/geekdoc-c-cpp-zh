Since it is still a SVF, the outputs will resemble the response plots from Chapter 4. The output varies from a perfect BSF to asymmetrical emphasizing either the high or low band. We can see that the notch frequency changes and that the emphasized band will gain. You can also make a resonant notch by filtering Q above 0.707.

# 7.3 Korg35 Sallen-Key Filter Model

Korg used several filter architectures in its early synthesizer designs, the Korg35 used in the MS-10 and early MS-20 synths stands out as an interesting and useful design. It has reappeared in several Korg products, including the more modern Monotone synths. The term CV actually refers to a sub-circuit where Control Voltage (CV) modifies the cutoff frequency of the main filtering circuit. Here is where we use to include the LPF or HPF circuit. The K35 LPF and HPF are controlled versions of the Sallen-Key filter topology. This is one of the early filter designs that use the SVF; some analog filter books refer to it as "Class 2a (Lindquist) and "PF1" (Wahhammer) Sallen-Key filters have a positive feedback path in a negative feedback path, and this allows the filter to self-oscillate. Korg took advantage of this design to produce a simple but highly resonant filter. Both LPF and HPF are second order types and will self-oscillate, but the Korg35 HPF has an interesting quirk; its roll-off is 6 dB/octave instead of the 12 dB/octave. This produces a HPF with more response.

# 7.4 Korg35 LPF Model

Figure 7.9 shows the block diagram of the two amplifier Sallen-Key LPF. Two LPFs are formed by the cascade of R1/C1 and R2/C2. K=K1=K2 is the strength of the amplifier blocks, and we can see the output is fed back into the filter via capacitor C1.

The reason for choosing the two-amplifier version is that the amps buffer the LPF stages to prevent loading. Our digital filters do not suffer from impedance loading, so we use this version. The original Korg analog version uses the single amplifier circuit. The difference in the two designs boils down to the self-oscillation gain value; you can find a complete derivation and implementation of the lossy single-amplifier K35 design at http://willinkle.com/synthbook/.

A simplified block diagram is shown in Figure 11.19. You can see that there is one LFO hardwired as a rotor, with normal and quad phase outputs connected to the vector mix generator. There are controls for rate, x-amplitude and y-amplitude. The rate is the LFO oscillator frequency, and the x and y values are the amplitudes of the normal and quad phase LFO outputs. In addition to the hardwired amp EG and filter EG, you also have a general purpose EG and LFO as modulation sources. The modulation matrix consists of four programmable rows, each with a source, control and destination.

With the slick touchscreen interface, the AniMooog takes the path/rotor concept up a notch by showing you the orbits of each note in different colors. The orbits chase each other around the path, looking like electrons orbiting nuclei that are constantly moving. For slow orbit rates, this produces a corkscrew trace; as you increase the orbit rate, the circular orbit becomes dodecahedral, then octagonal, and so on until it becomes a multi-pointed star. These visual cues aren't just there because they look cool—they tell you when the rate becomes so fast that wavables are being skipped as the (x,y) position moves quickly. The orbits are also bound just like in the previous synths, but in this case, the outer rectangle that contains the grid. Figure 11.20 shows a slow orbiting rotor. We will discuss more of the details when we get to the AniSynth project in a few sections. With the other vector synths, you can shift the path and its orbits by tapping on it and moving it, and likewise the same keep-out rules apply.

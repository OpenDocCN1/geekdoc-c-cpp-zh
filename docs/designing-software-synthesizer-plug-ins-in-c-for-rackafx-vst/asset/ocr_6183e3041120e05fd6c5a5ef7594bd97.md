$y(n)=Kx(n)\rightarrow Y(z)=KXz$$
H(z)=\frac{Y(z)}{X(z)}$
Y(z)=X(z)H(z)=KX(z) \quad (4.3)

This is fundamentally identical to the Laplace transform—the only thing that changes is the dependent variable from s to z. So, the two transforms seem to operate in parallel. The only real difference, is that analog circuit filtering equations are based on the mathematical operation of integration. Digital filtering algorithms are almost always based on the concept of delaying samples in time, rather than integration.

# 4.3 Digital Delay

The scalar multiplication and addition (or subtraction) operations are simple mathematical operations. Consider the M-sample delay line in Figure 4.4(a). Applying a discrete complex sinusoid $e^{ju\omeganT}$ (where n is the sample number, and T is the sampling period) at produces a M-sample delayed version at the output, $e^{ju(n-M)T}$. Using the polynomial property of $e^{a+b}=e^ae^b$, we can split the output term as:

$e^{ju\omeganT} = e^{ju\omeganT}e^{-ju\omegaMT} \quad (4.4)

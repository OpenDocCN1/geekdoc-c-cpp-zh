Our two oscillators will use some coupling, and we will need to know whether a reset operation is pending for the next sample period or occurring during the sample period. If there is no reset pending or occurring, we still have to check our position relative to the ordinary sawtooth discontinuity. The height of the reset discontinuity is found as the functional component of the ratio of the oscillation frequencies.

Figure 5.51: Hard syncing two hard sawtooth waveforms.

Figure 5.52: The spectrum of two hard sawtooth waveforms (a) and (b) application of two-point PolyBLEP with f (master) = 440 Hz and f (slope) = 600 Hz.

BLEP/PolyBLEP can be used to smear over the discontinuities, but there are some major issues to deal with. In fact, the Brand patent fails to disclose any implementation details or any real or simulated results. In a case where the master and slave oscillators operate at relatively low, semi-discrete results can be obtained as in Figure 5.52, where two-point PolyBLEP has been applied to the discontinuities. As Andrey points out, there are problems when the frequencies become high enough and BLEP correction be applied in overlapping pulses, or when master and slave both reset during the same sample interval (see the Lear/Bright patent in the Bibliography). This is especially bad when the master and/or oscillator frequencies become very high, at which point unacceptable aliasing will occur. The PolyBLEP oscillator used to produce Figure 5.52 suffers from this problem and does not produce acceptable results at higher master/slave frequencies or when the sync-ratio is modulated at high frequencies.

Leave the Lear/Bright patent and study how the authors handle the hard-sync situation. Use the blocks provided to implement aliasing free hard-sync oscillator part. The BLEP oscillators in this chapter are based on the first part of the patent, and you can certainly start with them as a basis for your work.

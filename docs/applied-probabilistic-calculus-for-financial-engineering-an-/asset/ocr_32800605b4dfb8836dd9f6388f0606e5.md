+   - d) A general formula for the minimum-variance portfolio may be derived as follows:

Starting with a reduced-form equation where the variance var_p of a two-asset portfolio is being expressed as a function of x_B, starting with var_{A-B} = var_A + 2x_B(covar_{A-B} - var_A) + x_B^2(var_A - 2covar_{A-B} + var_B) (3.54) for the two-asset portfolio, assets A and B.

Differentiating both sides of (3.54), with respect to x_B, namely, taking the ∂/∂x_B operation on both sides of (3.54), the result is d(var_{A-B})/dx_B = 0 + 2(covar_{A-B} - var_A) + 2x_B(var_A - 2covar_{A-B} + var_B) (3.55)

To obtain a stationary point, one sets (3.55) to zero, and solve for the value of x_B that will provide the minimum-variance portfolio, namely, solving for x_{B-min}. The result is 2(covar_{A-B} - var_A) + 2x_{B-min}(var_A - 2covar_{A-B} + var_B) = 0 from which x_{B-min} = (var_A - covar_{A-B})/(var_A - 2covar_{A-B} + var_B) (3.56)

This minimum-variance portfolio may have a lower risk than either of the two-component assets, and may also have a higher return!

More precisely, a 0-dimensional cellular quasi-manifold is a set of components, each component contains one or two vertices numbered 0. Each connected component of a 0-Gmap G = (D, α0) contains one or two darts: so, the orbits ⟨⟩[0]\{0} = ⟨⟩ correspond in the first case to one vertex numbered 0, and in the second case to two vertices numbered 0.

Assume an (n − 1)-dimensional cellular quasi-manifold Q is equivalent to an (n − 1)-Gmap G = (D, α0, …, αn−1). Let Q′ be the n-dimensional cellular quasi-manifold defined by constructing cellular cones for all connected components of Q; let G′ = (D, α0, …, αn−1, αn = Id), where Id is the identity on D, be the n-Gmap deduced from G by increasing its dimension. Then Q′ is equivalent to G′. In fact (cf. Fig. 8.11):

+   any i-simplex σ numbered {j0,…,ji} in Q is a simplex of Q′. Let d be a dart, such that σ corresponds to orbit O = ⟨α0,…,α̂j0,…,α̂ji,…,αn−1⟩(d) in G; σ corresponds also to orbit O′ = ⟨α0,…,α̂j0,…,α̂ji,…,αn−1,αn⟩(d) in G′, and O and O′ are equal since αn = Id;

+   a unique (i+1)-simplex σ′, numbered {j0,…,ji,n}, is associated with σ in Q′: σ′ corresponds to the orbit ⟨α0,…,α̂j0,…,α̂ji,…,αn−1,α̂n⟩(d) in G′, which clearly is equal to O;

+   at last, for each connected component of Q, a unique 0-simplex numbered n is created in Q′; corresponding to the unique orbit ⟨α0,…,αn−1⟩ in G′ associated with the corresponding connected component in G.

Assume an n-dimensional cellular quasi-manifold Q is equivalent to an n-Gmap G = (D, α0,…,αn−1,αn). Let c1 and c2 be two (n − 1)-cells, incident respectively to (n − 1)-simplices σ1 and σ2, such that σ1 ≠ σ2, σ1 (resp. σ2) is incident to a unique n-simplex, and an isomorphism φ associates c1 and c2, with φ(σ1) = σ2 (and φ = φ−1 if c1 = c2). Let d1 (resp. d2) be the dart of D corresponding to the n-simplex incident to σ1 (resp. σ2): note that ⟨αn⟩(d1) = {d1} corresponds to σ1 (resp. ⟨αn⟩(d2) = {d2} corresponds to σ2). Let Q′ be the n-dimensional cellular quasi-manifold defined by identifying c1 and c2 according to φ; let G′ = (D, α0,…,αn−1,α′n) be the n-Gmap deduced from G by n-sewing d1 and d2. Then Q′ is equivalent to G′. In fact:

+   isomorphism φ induces an isomorphism φ′ between orbits ⟨α0,…,αn−2⟩(d1) and ⟨α0,…,αn−2⟩(d2) (or an automorphism such that φ′ = φ′−1 on orbit ⟨α0,…,αn−2⟩(d1) if φ is an automorphism on c1);

+   identifying σ1 and σ2 (and all simplices corresponding by φ) corresponds to link d1 and d2 by α′n (and all darts corresponding by φ′), since ⟨α′n⟩(d1) = {d1,d2}.

The car physics are very simple in this game. In a commercial racing game, there may be all manner of physics scripts running to keep it upright around corners and behaving like a real car. Advanced physics are beyond the scope of this book, so instead we set the physics rigidbody's center of mass in such a way as to encourage the car to stay upright. If you were to comment out this line and play Metal Vehicle Doom, the car would roll onto its itself when coming around a corner. Changing the center of mass can have strange effects on the physics, although with a little trial and error, you may be able to reach a good level of stability without too much strangeness in terms of physical reaction:

```cs
// with this simple code, we set the center of mass
// low to keep the car from toppling over
myBody.centerOfMass= new Vector3(0,-3.5f,0);
```

At low speeds, it is difficult to turn the vehicle quickly. When the user crashes to a wall, slow turning speed can make the game a very frustrating experience. The aim of this overridden version of UpdatePhysics() is to add some turn helper force, to make the vehicle turn at lower speeds so that getting out of a crash may ier. It also means that the vehicle can do some run donuts on the track, but that another story...

Here we check the value of mySpeed (which, of course, holds the player's movement speed) and see whether it is below the value of TorqueHelperMaxSpeed. The intention is that its value will be set in the Unity Inspector window. If mySpeed gre than TorqueHelperMaxSpeed, no helper force will be applied to the vehicle.

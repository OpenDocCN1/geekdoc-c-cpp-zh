6 × 6 identity matrix I, leading to

∇∇'ᵗ ⎛ R_{|i|+i₀,2,f}(i⃗) ⎞
               ⎜ R_{|i|+i₀,1,1,f}(i⃗) ⎟
               ⎜ R_{|i|+i₀,2,0,f}(i⃗) ⎟
               ⎜ R_{|i|+i₁,0,1,f}(i⃗) ⎟
               ⎜ R_{|i|+i₁,1,0,f}(i⃗) ⎟
               ⎝ R_{|i|+i₂,0,0,f}(i⃗) ⎠ = Z⁻¹ I Z' = I,

as required.

Unfortunately, these functions are not yet basis functions, because their normal derivatives do not necessarily vanish at edge and side midpoints that lie by i⃗. They'll be modified later to be proper basis functions.

# 28.8 Edge-Midpoint Functions

Here we define the so-called edge-midpoint functions R_{i,f} (40 ≤ i < 52) in t. Consider the two degrees of freedom 40 ≤ i, i + 1 < 52 (for some even i) corresponding to the normal derivatives at the edge midpoint w ∈ e, where e is some edge in the reference tetrahedron T. (The basis functions corresponding to these degrees of freedom, p_i and p_{i+1}, are defined in T in Section 28.2.) Let us denote these normal directions by the three-dimensional unit vectors n⃗^(1) and n⃗^(2). Let n⃗^(3) be the unit vector that is tangent to e.

Let us show that both p_i and p_{i+1} vanish in the edge e in T. Indeed, in this edge, they are just polynomials of degree five in one variable, so they are determined uniquely by six degrees of freedom only. Now, both functions have six vanishing degrees of freedom in e: the function and its first and second derivative at the endpoints. Therefore, they must vanish in the entire edge e, as asserted.

As a result, their tangential derivative along e (and, in particular, at its midpoint w) must vanish as well:

∇' p_i(w) n⃗^(3) = ∇' p_{i+1}(w) n⃗^(3) = 0.

Define the 3 × 3 matrix formed by these three unit column vectors:

N⃗_e ≡ ( n⃗^(1) | n⃗^(2) | n⃗^(3) ).

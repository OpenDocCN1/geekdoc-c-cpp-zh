This is probably why poetry may have such a strong effect on the reader: it may reflect his/her own emotional state through words that really touch.

Poetry may thus be defined as any text that may give the reader a sense of aesthetics and insight. This definition is of course subjective: some text may seem as poetry to some readers and as mere prose to other readers. In fact, this may be relevant not only for texts written in a natural human language but also for texts written in a formal language such as a programming language or mathematics. For example, some readers may well see the beauty in a well-written scientific theory, mathematical proof, or computer program and consider it as poetry.

Below we observe how modern poetry can actually be considered as a glance into the subconsciousness.

# 4.10 Dreams and the Subconsciousness—Freud’s Theory

Poetry may well touch the reader as strongly and deeply as a dream. In fact, as in Freud’s theory, a dream too may give the dreamer a short glance into his/her own subconsciousness. The views seen in a dream have no obvious verbal representation. Still, once the dream is told by the dreamer to someone else, the words used for this purpose may reveal secrets from the very subconsciousness of the dreamer that have been completely unknown to him/her before.

Thus, the words used to tell the dream come from the same source as the dream itself: that is, subconsciousness. In fact, these words refer to the original memories in the original background of the dreamer that may have been locked up for years. These words may therefore be considered as subjective pointers or references to subjective memories in one’s mind.

# 4.11 Natural Human Languages

As discussed above, general objects and concepts used in natural human languages are so vague that no word can describe them precisely. Words can at best refer to or indicate the original concepts, so that they can be combined with other words in subsentences and sentences. These indications, however, are not objective but rather subjective: they depend strongly on the background of the person who uses them and on his/her personal associations and memories.

Fortunately, most speakers of a particular language have rather similar backgrounds, so they assign rather the same meaning to each word. This way, they can indeed communicate with each other and understand each other.

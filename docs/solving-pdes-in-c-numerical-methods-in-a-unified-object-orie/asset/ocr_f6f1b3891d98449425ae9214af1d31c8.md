7. Conclude that, for $q \ne 1$,

$$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1} q^j = \frac{1-q^N}{1-q}.$$

8. For $0<k<N$, set $q=\exp(2\pi i k/N)$ in the above formula to conclude that

$$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\exp(2\pi i j k/N)=\frac{1-\exp(2\pi i k)}{1-\exp(2\pi i k/N)}=0$$

(where $i=\sqrt{-1}$ is the imaginary number).

9. Multiply both sides in the above formula by $\exp(\pi i k/N)$ to obtain

$$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\exp(2\pi i(j+1/2)k/N)=0$$

as well.

10. Consider the real part in the above formula to have

$$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\cos(2\pi(j+1/2)k/N)=0.$$

11. Use the formula

$$\cos^2(\theta)=\frac{1+\cos(2\theta)}{2}$$

with $\theta=\pi(j+1/2)k/N$ to conclude that

$$\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\cos^2(\pi(j+1/2)k/N)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1+\cos(2\pi(j+1/2)k/N)}{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{2}=\frac{N}{2}.$$

is defined only relatively in terms of other people only and his emotions towards them. Indeed, from birth, the baby identifies with his/her mother, and later on with his/her father too. Furthermore, throughout life, the human sees not himself/herself but rather other people only, and always seeks their attention, appreciation, assurance, acceptance, and recognition of his/her valuable contribution to and place in society. In this view, each individual human mind exists only relatively as a reference or a mirror to others. Only two or more mirrors can share and reflect information and form a system or a society. An isolated mirror, on the other hand, would remain in the dark forever. As indicated in Zen Buddhism, a single hand can never clap.

It is particularly interesting to see how the above observations are also relevant in perhaps the most abstract form of language: poetry.

# 4.6 Verlaine: Impressionism in Poetry

Poetry, as well as other kinds of art, may indeed be viewed as the interface between the obscured ideas and impressions deep in one’s mind and the more explicit thoughts that can be expressed in any human language. The rhythm of poetry and the pauses and blank spaces used in between the lines are what communicates emotions, impressions, and ideas that cannot be said in any words. As the great poet Paul Verlaine advises poets in his poem “The Art of Poetry” (free interpretation of an interpretation by Aminadav Dikman):

> The words you use
> you have to choose
> with some degree of disregard.
> 
> Remember:
> treasure points at which
> implicit raw ideas meet
> the well defined.
> 
> And all the rest—
> is only prose.

In fact. Verlaine even recommends here to use inaccurate words. After all, there is no chance to express the pure idea or the deep emotion properly, so why bother to use precise words? It makes much more sense to go about the original idea and only indicate or hint it. To make the correct impression, the dramatic words and rhythm used in poetry are particularly suitable. All the rest is now up to the reader.

The rest of the edges. on the other hand, remain undivided in the next finer mesh as well. This completes the definition of the next finer mesh in the next finer level. It is also possible to replace j₁(m, v) by a more standard average:

```
j₂(m, v) ≡ ∑_{e∈m} j(e,v) / ∑_{e∈m} 1
```

This, however, would require us to index the edges in the entire mesh. (See Section A.30 in the Appendix.) Once the edges are indexed, it is also possible to form the list of edges in the entire mesh and order it in increasing-jump order: edges with a small jump at the beginning of the list, and edges with a larger jump at the end. With this list, one could divide only those edges with the largest jumps in the current mesh (say, only the edges in the last fifth of the list), leaving the remaining edges (which have a smaller jump) undivided in the next finer mesh as well.

In the present applications, however, we prefer to avoid this extra programming, and stick to the original j₀ and j₁ to decide which edges to divide in the adaptive algorithm.

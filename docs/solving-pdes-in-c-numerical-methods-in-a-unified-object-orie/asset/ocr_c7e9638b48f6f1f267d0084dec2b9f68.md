The correct (well-posed) form should use integration rather than differentiation. This form is called the "weak" formulation, because it requires weaker assumptions than the original "strong" formulation. In particular, it no longer requires u(x, y) to have continuous derivatives but only that the flux vector (Pu_x, Qu_y) be continuous. The weak formulation is the basis of the most useful finite-element discretization method.

In the original strong formulation of the diffusion problem as a PDE accompanied by boundary conditions, Pu_x and Qu_y must be differentiable. These assumptions may be too strong. For example, when P and Q are discontinuous, Pu_x and Qu_y may be continuous but not differentiable. In such cases, the strong formulation may have no mathematical solution at all.

Still, the physical phenomenon does exist and needs to be formulated mathematically. The mathematical model must be well posed in the sense that it has a unique solution, just like the original physical phenomenon. This model is provided by the weak formulation described below.

The weak formulation is based on integration rather than differentiation. Thus, it only requires the flux (Pu_x, Qu_y), as well as K and F, be square-integrable over Ω with respect to the area element dxdy. (We say that a function w is square-integrable over Ω if its square w² is integrable over Ω: that is, ∫_Ω w² dxdy < ∞.) We also assume that α and G are square-integrable over Γ with respect to the length element ds.

Let us now define the weak formulation in detail. For every function v that is square-integrable over Ω with respect to the area element dxdy (and also square-integrable over Γ with respect to the length element ds), define the linear functional f(v) by

```
f(v) = ∫_Ω F v dxdy + ∫_Γ G v ds.
```

For every two functions v and w with square-integrable derivatives in Ω, define the symmetric bilinear form a(v, w) by

```
a(v, w) = ∫_Ω (P v_x w_x + Q v_y w_y) dxdy + ∫_Ω K v w dxdy + ∫_Γ α v w ds.
```

The weak formulation is obtained from the strong one by using Green’s divergence formula in the PDE and boundary conditions as follows. Let v be a function in Ω that vanishes on Γ₀ and has square-integrable derivatives in Ω. Then, we have, from Green’s formula and the boundary conditions,

```
∫_Γ (G v - α u v) ds = ∫_Γ (P u_x v n₁ + Q u_y v n₂) ds
= ∫_{∂Ω} (P u_x v n₁ + Q u_y v n₂) ds
= ∫_Ω ((P u_x)_x + (Q u_y)_y) v dxdy
= ∫_Ω ((P u_x)_x v + (Q u_y)_y) v dxdy
+ ∫_Ω (P u_x v_x + Q u_y v_y) dxdy.
```

Here is yet another poem:

My son,
don’t read poetry.
Read railway lists,
they are more precise.
Read topographic maps,
before it is too late.
Open your eyes;
write no poem.

Furthermore, in his poem “Dante,” Enzensberger also wrote about identity. He says that nobody can determine your identity for you. Only you can decide what your identity really is, even though this may disagree with what everybody else expects from you.

In his book *The Notebooks of Malte Laurids Brigge* (1910), Rainer Maria Rilke writes that memories must be well processed and integrated in one’s mind before they can come out in the form of a poem. This is why only at old age can one interpret his/her entire life experience into at most one good poem. Furthermore, the entire history of the entirety of mankind is not as important as the life of one poet. Indeed, as Aristotle said, poetry is a greater import than history. After all, history can tell the past, but poetry may capture the unique moment of present.

# 4.8  Whitman and His Followers

In the above poems, one can find some general truth or observation rather than mere private introspection. The poet not only reveals to us readers his/her own psychological state of mind, but also reflects the entire natural or social landscape. This is indeed what characterizes valuable poetry, such as that of the great poet Walt Whitman and his followers, including the Israeli poets Avoth Yeshurun and Nadav Liniel.

The young poet Nadav Liniel is particularly influenced not only by Whitman but also by Aristotle and Wittgenstein. Indeed, it seems that Liniel realizes that there are no words that can express true, deep, raw emotion (free interpretation):

Iron remembers rain
with a touch of rust.
Rock misses the rain
that breaks it down.

The section about the control's life cycle has an exercise that shows you how to display the various events and the order in they occur. You could extend the example and write code for even more events. Additionally, you could add more controls to the page and handle their events as well to establish a solid understanding of these events. Because a good understanding of these events and their order is critical in writing applications, the time you put into this little research project is well spent.

Whenever you are writing pages that access a database or other slow resources like files or web services, consider whether they can benefit from caching. Although it's not hard to add coding at a later stage, it's best to put it in as early as possible.

Consider hard-coding data access pages. Though they are more difficult to write at first, you'll end up with pages that are easier to maintain in the long run.

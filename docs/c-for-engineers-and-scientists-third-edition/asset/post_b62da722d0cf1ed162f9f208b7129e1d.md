It occurs by transferring energy from atom to atom within a substance. With uniform temperatures on either side of equal-sized surfaces, the rate of heat flow through a substance is provided by Fourier's law of heat conduction which becomes the formula:

$Q = \frac{k(T_1 - T_2)}{w}$

$k$ is the thermal conductivity, which is a property of a substance that indicates its capacity to conduct heat (Watts/m·K or BTU/hft·F).
$T_2$ is the hotter temperature (°F or K).

$w$ is the width of the substance (ft or m).

## Engineering and Disciplines

### Civil Engineering

The field of civil engineering is concerned primarily with large-scale structures and systems used by a community. A civil engineer designs, constructs, and operates bridges, dams, railroads, airports, and is also responsible for the effects these systems have on society and the environment. The field can be subdivided into three categories:

- Structures: Design, construction, and operation of public works, such as dams, railroads, and roads. Properties of materials, geology, soil mechanics, and statistics are important elements of background training. For example, determining a building's maximum height before it buckles under its weight is a question involving all these subjects.

- Urban planning: Planning, designing, and constructing transportation systems (roads, railroads) and land use. Survey and mapmaking are necessary skills.

- Sanitation: Waste treatment, water supply, and sewage systems. Fluid mechanics, hydrology, pollution control, irrigation, and economics are important areas of study.

The programs you've examined so far have illustrated the programming concepts involved in input, output, assignment, and selection. By this time, you should have gained enough experience to be comfortable with these concepts and the mechanics of implementing them in C++. Many problems, however, require a repetition capability, in which the same calculation or sequence of instructions is repeated over and over, using different sets of data. Examples of this include continual checking of user data entries until an acceptable entry, such as a valid password; counting and accumulating running totals; and constant acceptance of input data and recalculation of output values that stop only at entry of sentinel value.

This chapter explores different ways programmers construct repeating sections of code and explains how they can be implemented in C++. Repetition is referred to as a loop because after the last statement in the code is executed, the program branches, or loops, back to the first statement and starts another repetition through the code. Each repetition is also referred to as an iteration or a pass through the loop.

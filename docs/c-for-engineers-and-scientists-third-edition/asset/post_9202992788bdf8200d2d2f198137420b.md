# Spotlight on Careers in Computing

Colin Dean of Volant, PA graduated with a B.S. in Computer Science from Westminster College in New Wilmington, PA in May 2007. He completed his M.S. in Business Education in July 2008 at Robert Morris University and is employed as a developer in Pittsburgh, PA.

You knew you were a Computer Science major when...
I had that feeling when, somewhat on a whim, I proposed to Westminster's information systems department that they lend me mine new, used computers in order to build a Folding@Home cluster in the Unix Lab. They approved, and that cluster ran 24/7 for about six months. (http://www.cs.webminster.edu/folding/).

One piece of advice for first year students: Ask questions. Don't be afraid to inundate a professor with questions. Remember, it's your education—get what you want out of it. If a professor is too busy to help you, find another one to help. Don't share your code with classmates—don't let them look at it unless you get permission from a professor: it's against every school's academic integrity policies. And finally, at least one weird (non-major) language like Smalltalk, Prolog, or even Haskell. You never know when it it might come in handy.

If you could have dinner with a famous computer scientist, living or dead, who would you choose? Tim Berners-Lee, the father of the World Wide Web. His creation has changed more lives directly than just any other technology has. It's enabled the dissemination of vast amounts of knowledge and enabled collaboration throughout the world.

What technology do you read on a regular basis? I find myself on Ars Technica, A List Apart, and The Daily WTF most often, as well as Engadget and a few politics and technology blogs.

Where do you see yourself in ten years? I hope to be running my own successful Internet-based company and contemplating a doctorate, and perhaps holding public office.

5. (Structural Eng.) Three of the most commonly used beams in structural engineering are the I-beam, rectangular, and cylindrical beam, in Figures 4.6 through 4.8. In determining the stress given weight places on a symmetrical beam, an important design parameter is the beam's rectangular moment of inertia, $I$, which is typically given in units of $in^4$. The computation of $I$ depends on the beam's geometry, and for the three beam types the values are calculated as follows:

For an I-beam: $I = \frac{BH^3 - bh^3}{12}$ where all measurements are in inches

For a rectangular beam: $I = \frac{bh^3}{12}$

For a cylindrical beam: $I = \frac{\pi r^4}{4}$

Figures 4.6 through 4.8 show the variables $b, h, B, H$, and $r$. Using this information, design, write, compile, and execute a C++ program that prompts the user for the type of beam and the necessary data (based on the input), and then computes and displays the beam's rectangular moment of inertia.

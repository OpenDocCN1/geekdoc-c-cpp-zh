# 14.6 The Trapezoidal Rule

An approximation to the area under a complicated curve is obtained by assuming the function can be replaced by simpler functions over a limited range. A straight line, the simplest approximation to a function, is to be first considered and to what's called the trapezoidal rule.

The area under the curve $f(x)$ from $x = a$ to $x = b$ is approximated by the area beneath a straight line drawn between the points $xa(a)$ and $xb(b)$ (see Figure 14.8). The lighter area is then the approximation to the integral and the area of a trapezoid, which is

$I \approx$ (average value of $f$ interval) (width of interval)

```
$I \approx \frac{1}{2} [f(a) + f(b)] (b - a) = T_0 \quad \text{(Equation 14.6)}$
```

This is the trapezoidal rule for one panel, identified as $T_0$.

**Figure 14.8** Approximating the area under a curve by a single trapezoid

To improve the accuracy of the approximation to the area under a curve, the interval is next divided in half, and the function is approximated by straight-line segments over each half. Half the area in this example is approximated by the area of two trapezoids, as illustrated in Figure 14.9.

```
$I = T_l = [\frac{\Delta x_l}{2} (f(a) + f_l) \Delta x_l] + [\frac{\Delta x_r}{2} (f_l + f(b)) \Delta x_r]$
```

or

```
$T_l = \frac{\Delta x_l}{2}[f(a) + 2f_l + f(b)] \quad \text{(Equation 14.7)}$
```

where

```
$\Delta x_l = \frac{(b - a)}{2}$
```

$f_l = f(x = a + \Delta x_l)$

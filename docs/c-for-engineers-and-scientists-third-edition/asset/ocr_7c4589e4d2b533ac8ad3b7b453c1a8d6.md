# Acknowledgments

The writing of this third edition is a direct result of the success (and limitations) of the previous two editions. In this regard, my heartfelt acknowledgment and appreciation is to the instructors and students who found the previous editions to be of service in their quests to teach and learn C++.

Next, I would like to thank Anya Pratt, my Senior Project Manager at Continuous Technology. In addition to her faith and encouragement, her ideas and partnership were instrumental in creating this book. After the writing was completed, the task of turning the final manuscript into a book depended on many people other than myself. For this, I especially want to thank my developmental editor, Lisa Lord, who provided an outstanding job. Her editing dealt so much with the spirit and idiosyncrasy of my own writing that it was an almost pleasing experience. She understood what I was attempting to achieve while patiently going through both the technical and grammatical content. A truly incredible feat! This editing supplemented by the equally detailed work of my colleague, Professor John Zucker Hoffman, with structural engineering applications provided by Professors Greg Bajaj and Al Branchi and moral support provided by Dr. John Becker of The Theology Department. Finally, I would like to thank the designers and Camille Kler at GEM Publishing, especially the interior designer, whose work was extremely important to me and I am very grateful to them.

The following reviewers provided extensive, extremely useful, and detailed information and corrections that made this edition better and clearer. No matter how careful I was, each reviewer pointed out that I missed or could be better. Naturally, all errors rest square on my shoulders, but these reviewers made them much easier; Henry Aki, California State University Marshall; Robert Smith, Salt Lake City Community. In addition I like to thank the instructors who reviewed the proposal for this edition and offered valuable feedback: Randy Bower, Jacksonville University; Harvey Clarence, Delaware City of New York; Cynthia Lister, Tuskegee University; and Sherman Wang, Barnard University.

With the first edition, special acknowledgement was given to Dr. G. J. Boris of Lehigh University, who provided material that was adapted for this book. His contribution includes almost all of Chapter 14, which he graciously permitted me to adapt from his FORTRAN 77 text (published by PW Publishing). I would also like to acknowledge, with extreme gratitude, the wonderful academic environment for learning and teaching created at Dickinson University—starting with the President, Dr. Michael Adams, followed through the academic departments of the university and campus prov, Dr. Joseph Kieran and Kenneth Greene, and finally to the direct encouragement and support provided by my dean, Dr. William Moore, and my chairperson, Dr. Paul Yoon. Without their support, this book could not have been written.

Finally, I deeply appreciate the patience, understanding, and love provided by my wife, and partner, Rochelle.

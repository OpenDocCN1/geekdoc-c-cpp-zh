**6. (Fluid Mechanics)** A key parameter used to determine the type of fluid flow a pipe is the Reynolds number, which is given by this formula:
$Re = \frac{V d}{\nu}$

$Re$ is the Reynolds number (a dimensionless value).
$V$ is the velocity (m/s or ft/sec).
$d$ is the diameter of the pipe (m or ft).
$\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity of the fluid (m/s$^2$ or ft/sec$^2$).

The viscosity, $
u$, is a measure of the fluid's resistance to flow and stretch. Except at extremely high pressures, a fluid's kinematic viscosity is dependent on temperature and independent of pressure. The following chart provides the viscosity of water at three different temperatures:

| Temperature (°C) | Kinematic Viscosity (m/s$^2$) |
|---||
| 5 | $1.49 \times 10^{-6}$ |
| 10 | $1.31 \times 10^{-6}$ |
| 15 | $1.15 \times 10^{-6}$ |

Duchamp had famously taken to playing chess as a form of art, even playing himself as chess-player in an experimental film directed by the American composer John Cage (1912-1992). In 1963, Duchamp played chess with nude challenger Eve Babitz at his first retrospective in Pasadena. Duchamp’s love affair with chess, according to many of his scholars, came from the beauty of the game and the “language” of chess. He played competitively in the 1920s, and certainly enjoyed winning.

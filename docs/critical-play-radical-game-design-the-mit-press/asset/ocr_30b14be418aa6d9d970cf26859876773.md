The gaze becomes, by default, the gaze of the other. “When I am looking at an object, the object is already gazing at me, and from a point at which I cannot see it.”109 For Žižek, the gaze “falls into ourselves, the spectators.”110 Because the player experiences a character’s daily routine from a perspective divorced from lived experience, the virtual doll’s human-like, autonomous gestures force the player to recognize that the “very procedure is, on a formal level, already ‘perverse.’”111

The perverse and subversive elements of The Sims play are among the most powerful alternate play strategies available to computer game players. Some players subvert the game by purposely making life miserable for the little consumer characters. Players can download negative or subversive homes such as that of “Mr. Sadistic,” a household where, instead of nurturing characters and getting them jobs, the Sims characters are tortured and frequently die.

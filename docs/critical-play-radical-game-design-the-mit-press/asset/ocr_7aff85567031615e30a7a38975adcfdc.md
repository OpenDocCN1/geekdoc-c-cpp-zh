The contribution of Lewis Carroll (Charles Lutwidge Dodgson, 1832–1898) to the study of language cannot be denied; philosophers from George Pitcher (1965) to Leila Silvana May (2007) have explored Wittgenstein’s concepts of language games through the complex work of Carroll. He specialized in the area of nonsense and the creation of language systems. ${}^{15}$ Most known for his children’s books, ${}^{16}$ Dodgson was obsessed with logic, and published many games from 1860 to 1896. Some examples were *Puzzles from Wonderland* (1870), *Miscbmash* (1882), and *Circular Billiards* (1890). ${}^{17}$ He also created a huge set of ciphers and codes. One might argue that such play with language is not particularly critical, and with all likelihood does not in itself foster social critique.

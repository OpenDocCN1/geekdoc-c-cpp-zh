+   3. The goto transitions for state \(i\) are constructed for all nonterminals \(A\) using the rule: If \(\mathrm{GOTO}(I_i,A)=I_j\), then \(\mathrm{GOTO}[i,A]=j\).
+   
+   4. All entries not defined by rules (2) and (3) are made “error.”
+   
+   5. The initial state of the parser is the one constructed from the set of items containing \([S' \to \cdot S]\).

□

The parsing table consisting of the ACTION and GOTO functions determined by Algorithm 4.46 is called the *SLR(1) table* for \(G\). An LR parser using the SLR(1) table for \(G\) is called the *SLR(1) parser* for \(G\), and a grammar having an SLR(1) parsing table is said to be *SLR(1)*. We usually omit the “(1)” after the “SLR,” since we shall not deal here with parsers having more than one symbol of lookahead.

**Example 4.47:** Let us construct the SLR table for the augmented expression grammar. The canonical collection of sets of LR(0) items for the grammar was shown in Fig. 4.31. First consider the set of items \(I_0\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
E' &\to \cdot E\\
E &\to \cdot E + T\\
E &\to \cdot T\\
T &\to \cdot T * F\\
T &\to \cdot F\\
F &\to \cdot (E)\\
F &\to \cdot \mathrm{id}
\end{aligned}
\]

The item \(F \to \cdot (E)\) gives rise to the entry ACTION[0, (] = shift 4, and the item \(F \to \cdot\, \mathrm{id}\) to the entry ACTION[0, id] = shift 5. Other items in \(I_0\) yield no actions. Now consider \(I_1\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
E' &\to E\cdot\\
E &\to E\cdot + T
\end{aligned}
\]

The first item yields ACTION[1, $] = accept, and the second yields ACTION[1, +] = shift 6. Next consider \(I_2\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
E &\to T\cdot\\
T &\to T\cdot * F
\end{aligned}
\]

Since FOLLOW(\(E\)) = {$, +, )}, the first item makes

\[
\mathrm{ACTION}[2,\$] = \mathrm{ACTION}[2,+] = \mathrm{ACTION}[2,)] = \mathrm{reduce}\ E \to T
\]

The second item makes ACTION[2, *] = shift 7. Continuing in this fashion we obtain the ACTION and GOTO tables that are shown in Fig. 4.31. In that figure, the numbers of productions in reduce actions are the same as the order in which they appear in the original grammar (4.1). That is, \(E \to E + T\) is number 1, \(E \to T\) is 2, and so on. □

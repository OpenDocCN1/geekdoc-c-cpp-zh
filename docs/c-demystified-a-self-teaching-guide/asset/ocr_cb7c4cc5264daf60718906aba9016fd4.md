# Introduction

C++ was my first programming language. While I have learned others, I've always thought C++ was the "best" programming language, perhaps because of the power it gives the programmer. Of course, this power is a double-edged sword, also being the power to hang yourself if you are not careful. Nonetheless, C++ has always been my favorite programming language.

C++ also has been the first choice of others, not just in the business world because of its power, but also in academia. Additionally, many other programming languages, including Java and C#, are based on C++. Indeed, the Java programming language was written using C++. Therefore knowing C++ also means learning other programming languages easier.

# Why Did I Write this Book?

Not as road to riches, fame, or beautiful women. I might be misguided, but I'm not completely delusional.

To be sure, there are many introductory level books on C++. Nevertheless, I wrote this book because I believe I bring a different and, I hope, valuable perspective.

As you may know from my author biography, I teach computer science at Los Angeles Valley College, a community college in the San Fernando Valley area of Los Angeles, where I grew up and have lived most of my life. I also write computer programs, but teaching programming has provided me with insights into how students learn I could never obtain from writing programs. These insights are gained not just from answering student questions during lectures. I spend each week in our college's computer lab helping students with their programs, and many hours each week reviewing and grading their assignments. Patterns emerge when teaching programming methods and, the level of difficulty at which to introduce a new topic, and so on. I learn from my students that they are my best testers in my never-ending attempt to become a better teacher, but there is much truth in this hope.

Additionally, my beta testers... err, students, seem to complain about the textbook matter book I wrote. Many ask me why I wrote a book they could use to learn C++. They may be saying this flatter me (I'm not saying it doesn't work), or for the more sinister reason that think they will be able to blame the teacher for a poor book as well as poor instruction. Nevertheless, having written other books, these questions planted in my mind of writing a book that, in addition to being sold to the general public, could also be used as a supplement to a textbook.

For those who do not know what this means, here is a brief explanation. You probably know that whole numbers are the numbers 0, +1, -1, +2, -2, and so on. You can do anything in this number range until you discover multiplication and the division that goes with it. You need the rational numbers; these are any two numbers in a fraction—so, for example, -3/4. You are happy with this number until you get the idea of adding very long fractions that get smaller and smaller. This is practical if you want to give all points on a line. The sum of such sequences in real numbers, which include $\pi$ and $\sqrt{2}$, for example. Do we have everything now? Yes—so long you don't calculate the square root of a negative number (or zero)—for example, $4^2 = 16$—it is not easy to define what the square root of a negative number should be. For example, what is $\sqrt{-1}$?

Such a gap ro mathematicians of sleep, which is why you define it as follows: $\sqrt{-1}=i$ or $i^2=-1$. Phew, that helps mathematicians: now they can solve equations that have no solution in real space. For example, $(x+1)^2=-9$ does not result in a true statement for any real number $x$. If we take the earlier definition, we see that $x$ has the two solutions: $-1+3i$ and $-3i$. It looks strange, but you get to use it. A complex number as $-1+3i$ always has two components: the *real* part without $i$, here $-1$, and the *imaginary* part, here $+3i$. If you look at the two parts as $x$ and $y$ coordinates on a plane, you can see the complex numbers as points in the plane—just as you can see the real numbers as points on a line.

Special rules apply when calculating complex numbers. They're not really anything special; you just have to keep in mind that every $i^2$ can be replaced with -1. Otherwise, the normal arithmetic rules apply:

+   * Addition: $(3+5i) \cdot (8-i) = (3+8) + (5-1)i = (11+4i)$
* Multiplication: $(-2+3i) \cdot (4+2i) = -2\cdot4 + 3\cdot4 - 2\cdot2i + 2\cdot i\cdot i = -8+12i-4i+2i^2 = -8+8i-6 = -14+8i$
* Exponent: $((-1+3i) \cdot (1i)^2) = (3^2) \cdot (i^2) = 9 \cdot (-1) = -9$

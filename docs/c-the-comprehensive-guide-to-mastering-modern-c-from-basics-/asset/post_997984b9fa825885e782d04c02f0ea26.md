If you look at the two parts as $x$ and $y$ coordinates on a plane, you can see the complex numbers as points in the plane—just as you can see the real numbers as points on a line.

Special rules apply when calculating complex numbers. They're not really anything special; you just have to keep in mind that every $i^2$ can be replaced with -1. Otherwise, the normal arithmetic rules apply:

- Exponent: $((-1+3i) \cdot (1i)^2) = (3^2) \cdot (i^2) = 9 \cdot (-1) = -9$

No sooner had this number space been discovered than physicists and mathematicians found all kinds of “practical” applications for it. In fact, complex numbers can be used to solve problems that were previously unsolvable.

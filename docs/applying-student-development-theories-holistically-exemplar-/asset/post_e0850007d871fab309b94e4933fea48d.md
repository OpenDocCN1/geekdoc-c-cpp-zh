As a reminder, involving student staff members in every part of the IAP process is critical. If supervisors are hesitant to relinquish some power in the working relationship to share the responsibility of creating an IAP, student staff can have a difficult time understanding how to take charge of their own experiences within their role. Some student staff members may expect to be told what to do and how to do it at all times.

## Explore Resources That Help Staff Advance the IAP Process

For professional staff members who implement IAPs, it is critical to continually evaluate the effectiveness of each staff member's IAP by journaling, narrative evaluation, and conversation. The following websites offer suggestions and ideas for creating effective reflective prompts:

- www.facultyfocus.com/articles/teaching-professor-blog/prompts-help-students-reflect-approach-learning/ (Weimer, 2014)
- https://ww2.kqed.org/education/2017/05/16/8-ways-for-students-to-reflect-on-their-learning/ (Chen, 2017)
- www.edutopia.org/discussion/scaffolding-student-reflections-sample-questions (Alrubail, 2015)

There are several books that are helpful for professional staff who are engaged in honoring, supporting, and helping students from diverse backgrounds thrive (particularly when attending Predominantly White Institutions). Two exemplars are *Student Engagement in Higher Education: Theoretical Perspectives and Practical Approaches for Diverse Populations*, edited by Quaye and Harper (2015), and *Creating Campus Cultures: Fostering Success among Racially Diverse Student Populations*, edited by Museus and Jayakumar (2012). Roper (2012) shared helpful information in “Supervising across Cultures: Navigating Diversity and Multiculturalism,” which is a chapter in the monograph *Supporting and Supervising Mid Level Professionals*.

IAPs are a strategy to maximize the developmental growth of student staff members, which can help them approach the adaptive challenges of their role. Through a process of co-creating an action plan with supervisor and supervisee, student staff members will have the opportunity to think critically about their growth, development, and working styles, and how that is informed by their identities and histories. The nature of the IAP allows student staff to think about their role in a complex way that suits their unique needs and contexts. Implemented with intention, IAPs can help student staff members approach the adaptive challenges of their complex positions in effective ways.

## Self-Authorship and Critical Praxis With Student Staff

*Chelsea Fullerton and Scott Burden*

This chapter describes a curricular framework that incorporates elements of self-authorship and critical praxis into a Learning Partnership Model (LPM; Baxter Magolda, 2004) used with undergraduate student staff members at a Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Transgender, Queer/Questioning, Intersex, and Asexual Plus (LGBTQIA+) center. The student development theory utilized is Baxter Magolda’s (1999) developmental process of self-authorship. Additionally, the framework utilizes Crenshaw (1989) and May (2015), who define and describe intersectionality to support the premise of moving beyond single-axis thinking to a more complex way of thinking. To supplement these theories, professional staff developed an Employability Traits Rubric that along with self-reflection, supervisor assessment, and a comprehensive review process, promotes self-authorship.

Lehigh University is a medium-sized, private, not-for-profit research institution in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, with approximately 5,000 undergraduates and 2,000 graduate students pursuing bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees. Lehigh is a Predominantly White Institution (PWI) with 26% domestic students of color and 9% international students. Students are primarily from the tristate area (i.e., New Jersey, New York, and Pennsylvania); 26% hail from other areas of the United States (Office of Institutional Research and Strategic Analytics, 2018). The student culture is heavily influenced by fraternity and sorority affiliation as Greek-affiliated students make up 40% of the undergraduate student body (Office of Fraternity and Sorority Affairs, 2017).

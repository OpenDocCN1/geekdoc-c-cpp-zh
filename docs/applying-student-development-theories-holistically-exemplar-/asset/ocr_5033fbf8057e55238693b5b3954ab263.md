# An Identity Development and Support Initiative for Black Men

Jonathan A. McElderry, Jordan Williams, Marcus Mayes, Donald D. Gilliam, and Curtis Taylor, Jr.

The Mizzou Black Men's Initiative is a program for first-year, Black male-identified students at the University of Missouri. The student development theories that informed the creation of the program are Black Identity Development (Cross & Phagen-Smith, 2001) and Sense of Belonging (Strayhorn, 2012). With theories as guides, the program aids in the successful transition of these individuals into the collegiate environment, with a focus on support, development, and involvement. This chapter provides a description of the program, guiding theories, and assessment and evaluation techniques. Additionally, discussion is included on lessons learned from applying theory as well as recommendations and resources necessary to impact the identity development and sense of belonging for Black males in the collegiate environment

The University of Missouri ("Mizzou" or MU) located in Columbia, Missouri, was established in 1839. It is a land-grant research university and was the first public university west of the Mississippi River. Mizzou has 19 colleges and schools with more than 300 degree programs and is one of five universities in the United States that offers law, medicine, veterinary medicine, and a nuclear research reactor (University of Missouri, 2014). According to the Fall 2016 Student Body Profile (University of Missouri, 2016), the total enrollment was 33,266. Mizzou is a Predominantly White Institution (PWI). A total of 20,273 of the undergraduates identify as White, and 9,814 as White men.

# Acknowledgments

A book on applying student development theories holistically in higher education environments can be successful only if it involves those who are most likely to use it: graduate students in academic programs that prepare emerging scholarly practitioners. Four graduate students directly enhanced this book’s contents by participating in the process of gathering examples of theory-to-practice programs and other initiatives, and by providing thoughtful feedback on chapter contents. University of Rhode Island master’s students Melissa Ferris, Haley McConville, and Maira Pantoja assisted us in soliciting proposals for possible chapters, reviewed submitted proposals, and participated in the final selection. Their analysis was invaluable as we selected examples demonstrating a kaleidoscope of perspectives using relevant theories, covering a range of functional areas, and examining environments at a variety of institutional types. University of Connecticut doctoral student Monique Golden joined this book project after we received chapters from contributors and began to refine content. Her attention to detail while also remaining focused on the overall final product made immeasurable contributions.

Additionally, we want to acknowledge the feedback, guidance, and support we received from Routledge/Taylor and Francis Group staff. Senior Editor Heather Jarrow’s insightful suggestions and patience while working with us were both learning-centered and inspiring.

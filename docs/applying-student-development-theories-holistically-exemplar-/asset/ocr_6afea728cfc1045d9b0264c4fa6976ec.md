Structured touchpoints included the kickoff, the closing potluck, and five more social events throughout the semester, both on campus (e.g., pumpkin carving) and off campus (e.g., visit to local organic farm). The Assistant Director collaborated with popular locally owned businesses in downtown Iowa City to offer discounts to buddies who visited the establishments as a pair (using a Buddy sticker on student ID cards). A Twitter competition awarded a significant prize (e.g., Beats by Dre® Headphones) to the buddy pair who tweeted the most photos of meet-ups during the semester. This generated enthusiasm for the program and a rich stock of photos for marketing purposes.

Changes to the program resulted in continued increased participation in the following semesters. These changes fostered more opportunities for interaction, encountering difference, and reflecting on one’s own ethnic identity. During the Fall 2014 semester, 66% of survey respondents reported meeting four times or more, with 26% meeting seven times or more. Open-ended comments included positive reflections about encountering difference such as, “It was a new experience and one that put me out of my comfort zone, which I hope was a chance for growth for both of us.”

The structure established in Fall 2014 has remained largely intact, with further improvements made over the past six semesters to encourage more and meaningful encounters with difference. By Spring 2017, the program had become streamlined with regard to events and programs offered, campus and community partnerships, and an enhanced training protocol for the Ambassadors. Ambassadors serve as the liaison between the program participants and the Assistant Director. Thus, training includes best practices for pairing participants, as well as intentional conversations regarding the overall goals and aims of the program. When planning events for the semester, Ambassadors are tasked with presenting evidence that the events support program and outcome goals. At events, Ambassadors also gauge these outcome goals through interactions with students. Finally, Ambassadors participate in the debrief process after each semester and are encouraged to help shape future iterations through reflections themselves as well as through review of the survey responses.

# A Pre-Orientation Retreat to Increase Retention and Develop Competence

Kelly Carter Merrill

Launch describes a pre-orientation retreat at Randolph-Macon College, a small private liberal arts institution in Virginia. Launch was designed with a psychosocial developmental focus and the intention of improving student retention at the college. This overarching goal was actualized by addressing four intended developmental goals: ease new students’ social transition, prepare them for academic challenges, encourage reflection on perseverance, and create a social community of peers who would value and protect their diversity. Four theoretical constructs guided the design and curriculum of Launch: developing competence (Chickering & Reisser, 1993), student involvement theory (Astin, 1999), mindset and perseverance (Dweck, 2012), and microaggressions and climates for diversity (Sue, 2010).

Randolph-Macon College (RMC) is a small (1,400 students), private, selective, liberal arts college in central Virginia with a largely regional draw (73% in state). It is a residential college, with 92% of first-year students, and 80% of all students, living on campus. Originally, RMC was an all men’s institution founded in 1830 with a Methodist mission. Today, the institution boasts 53% enrolled women (having gone coeducational in 1971) and is the oldest Methodist-related college in continuous operation (Office of Institutional Research, 2017; SCHEV, 2017). The college’s Carnegie classification is Baccalaureate College—Arts and Sciences, and the athletic conference is the Old Dominion Athletic Conference (ODAC) within the National Collegiate Athletic Association’s Division III.

Schlossberg’s work also is rooted in adult development theory, including age 18 and up, as well as various life experiences. However, community colleges are adopting programs to entice high school student enrollment such as honors programs that may have students younger than 18. Further, this theory does not take into account a student’s maturity level being one of an adult, regardless of age. A major limitation of Schlossberg’s transition theory is a lack of mindfulness about how intersectional aspects of identity influences students’ transitions. As a result, transitional theory may miss out in serving practitioners with a comprehensive understanding regarding the various barriers and considerations we encounter as we work with a progressively diverse student population.

Passport cannot entirely be confident of the extent to which transfer students have mastered the content shared during an appointment or transfer workshop since not all students who have appointments with Passport respond to the Passport survey. Additionally, Passport currently does not have a survey to evaluate the effectiveness of the transfer workshops. These are two opportunities for Passport to grow and potentially expand services to students.

Passport made many connections with transfer research such as Flaga’s (2006) dimensions of transition and Wyner et al.’s (2016) *The Transfer Playbook*. It can be argued that the last two dimensions of Flaga’s (2006) research, *negotiating* and *integrating*, may be more strongly connected with a student’s post-transfer experiences rather than Passport’s involvement as a pre-transfer resource to students. There is an opportunity for Passport to collaborate with IUPUI’s Office of Transfer Student Services to explore how students may be progressing through Flaga’s (2006) dimensions of transition and how services may be improved to help students establish a strong sense of connection with IUPUI prior to transferring.

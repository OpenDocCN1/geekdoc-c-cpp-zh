
They explored their identities, even throughout the process of determining which affinity groups they most identify. Students indicated that having a friend to turn to meant opportunities to reflect on the differences that made them unique and provided the support and encouragement necessary to see that these unique differences should be celebrated and embraced rather than ignored or viewed in a negative context.

## *Moving Beyond Difference to Reflection on Ethnic Identity*

While our survey data overall suggests students have meaningful exchanges with students from another culture, we have asked, “Does Tippie Buddies provide a platform for students to reflect on their own ethnic identities?” To answer our question, we sought open-ended feedback from participants with at least one semester completed in the program, and had at least one semester to reflect on their experiences. We found many students believed that participation in the program led to a greater understanding of their own ethnic identities:

- “The program was helpful in giving me a better understanding of my own ethnic identity by my buddy pointing out the ‘different’ and ‘strange’ things we do here. Our dialogue usually consisted of discussing similarities and differences among our cultures. Having my buddy bring certain norms and beliefs that I hold up as being unique to our culture gave me a better understanding of the ethnic identity I hold.”
- “It definitely made me aware of who I am and where I belong. It made me compare myself with other people around me and also made me realize that I’m somewhat different from other people in the way of mys.

Perhaps the greatest challenge is trying to balance the developmental outcomes of the program with nuances that arise from the institution. This University, these resources help staff who serve international students understand what they expect from higher education, and how they describe their experiences, which can inform the design and components of mentoring programs.

There are other institutions that have programs similar to Tippie Buddies, many designed to help ease the transition of new international students. Higher education and student affairs professionals who are considering domestic-international student buddy programs should consider examining Georgia State University’s International Exchange Student Buddy Program (https://cas.gsu.edu/international-exchange-student-buddy/) and the International Student Buddy Program at Columbia College Chicago (https://students.colum.edu/student-diversity-and-inclusion/international-student-scholar-services/buddy-program.php).

In this chapter, we proposed just one of many possible methods to create environments and opportunities for undergraduate students to explore their ethnic identities. We posit that our own success with the program is contextually (and potentially even institutionally) bound, but the relative ease with which a buddy program can be created makes it a feasible option across institutional settings.

Research suggests it is important to understand the multiple identities individuals hold and the holistic impact of different components of identity (e.g., Iwamoto & Liu, 2010). Ethnic identity exploration and development as a process may occur regardless of intentional programming, but our hope is that by offering the opportunity and structured, supportive environment which encourages students to interact and engage in dialogue, it allows the process to be both positive and meaningful.

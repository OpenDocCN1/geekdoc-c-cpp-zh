# # Foreword: It Is Not Magic

*Susan R. Komives*

My son is a rocket scientist—really! He grew up loving airplanes, drawing airplanes, visiting the Smithsonian Air and Space Museum, doing his high school senior project in the wind tunnel at the University of Maryland, joining the U.S. Air Force Reserve Officers Training Corps, and most recently earning his doctorate in aeronautical engineering at the University of Minnesota. He is now Major Jeffrey R. Komives, USAF, PhD, researching and teaching hypersonics at the Air Force Institute of Technology at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio. Meanwhile, I am his mother who sits in my window seat in a massive airplane loaded with hundreds of people as we are taking off thinking, “This is just magic.” Jeff has given me many lessons and I basically understand lift and thrust; yet, as illustrated in Figure 0.1 (Boyd, 2010), I still secretly think, “It just has to be magic that this massive plane lifts off and stays up!”

Between boarding the plane and landing, the process of how an airplane takes off and stays up is the mystery box for me (i.e., the box of unknowns; Figure 0.2). I do not fully understand what happens in that box but trust that it works. Fortunately for me and other passengers, there are experts like Jeff who know both how that works and how to make it happen! He chastises me, “Mom, it is not magic!”


## Gordon S. Louie
earned both a bachelor’s degree and a master’s degree in History and a Graduate Certificate in Higher Education from the University of Nevada, Las Vegas. While in graduate school, he began working in higher education through a grant for program development/assessment, and has since gained experience in academic support services, admissions/financial aid, academic advising, and international programs. Alongside his role as the Assistant Director, Global Community Engagement, in the Tippie College of Business at the University of Iowa, he is currently a PhD student in the Educational Policy and Leadership Studies program at Iowa. His current research interests and projects include ethnic identity exploration for international students, and the points of intersection/divergence in policies regarding campus diversity and internationalization.

## Marcus Mayes
graduated from the University of Missouri with his BS in Business Administration and a master’s degree in Education Leadership and Policy Analysis. He co-founded the Mizzou Black Men’s Initiative in 2009 and served as the program coordinator until 2013, providing guidance, support, and mentorship to program participants as they transitioned from high school to college. In his current role as the Coordinator of Student Activities at the University of Texas, Austin, Marcus coordinates development and recognition programs for registered student organizations on the 40 Acres, and co-advises Student Government, Senate of College Councils, and the Graduate Student Assembly.

## Jonathan A. McElderry
serves as the Assistant Dean of Students and Director of the Intercultural Center at Wake Forest University. His experience in higher education has focused on enhancing diversity, equity, and inclusion on college campuses. Additionally, his research has sought to raise awareness of the experiences of underrepresented students at Predominantly White Institutions and provide strategies to increase their academic and social success. He has presented both nationally and internationally, has held leadership positions within the American College Personnel Association, and has received several awards for his work in higher education. Jonathan earned a PhD in Educational Leadership and Policy Analysis with an emphasis in Higher Education from the University of Missouri and an MEd in College Student Personnel from Ohio University.

## Sharee L. Myricks
is the Assistant Director of the Indiana University-Purdue University Indianapolis (IUPUI) and Ivy Tech Coordinated Programs (Passport) Office. She completed her MEd at Texas A&M University within the Student Affairs Administration in Higher Education program. Within higher education, Sharee utilizes her diverse experiences to successfully collaborate with students, faculty, staff, and community partners. Sharee has authored several publications and presents regularly at National Association of Student Personnel Administrators conferences on topics focused to enhance the career development and organizational leadership of new professionals within higher education. Additionally, her research focuses on resiliency of women within higher education.

## Christine Nguyen
is originally from Iowa and earned her undergraduate degree in Psychology from Cornell College. She completed her master’s degree at Western Illinois University in College Student Personnel, where she gained paraprofessional experiences in career development, leadership development, and residence life. In her first professional position, she worked as Director of Health and Wellness Services at Trine University in Angola, Indiana. She currently works as an Academic Advisor for the College of Arts & Sciences Honors Program at The Ohio State University.

## Vanessa Perez
has been a higher education professional since 2012. She earned a Master of Public Policy degree from Rutgers and has served in student affairs roles at New York University (NYU), Abu Dhabi, and NYU, New York. She currently focuses on international initiatives and continuing education with senior leadership at the NYU College of Dentistry. Vanessa’s diverse experiences have afforded her the opportunity to apply theories to practice in a variety of professional settings, thus inspiring reflection, assessment, and innovation in her work. Vanessa is a New Jersey native who is passionate about equity and inclusion and plans to pursue a doctorate in the future.

## Lori K. Pyle
earned her PhD from James Madison University, where she is currently the Associate Chair of the Ethical Reasoning in Action program. She has been part of this curricular and co-curricular program to teach ethical reasoning skills and elevate the campus-wide understanding and discourse on ethical reasoning since its inception in 2013. Lori facilitates ethical reasoning workshops on campus and in the local and regional communities, advises an ethical reasoning peer education group, and plans the first-year student introductory ethical reasoning experience.

## Anna Randolph Lehnen
earned her MA in College Student Personnel at Bowling Green State University. She currently works with the University Program Board at James Madison University, where she serves as the Student Affairs Fellow for the Ethical Reasoning in Action program. She has experience in student activities, leadership programs, and residence life. Her research interests include understanding the effects of campus programming, leadership development, and the connections between critical thinking development and co-curricular involvement. She presents frequently about leadership development programs and trainings for college students.

## Sarah E. Schoper
earned a PhD from the University of Maryland, and she is a faculty member in the College Student Personnel Program at Western Illinois University. Dr. Schoper was previously a practitioner at various institutions where she worked in leadership development, fraternity and sorority life, student activities, student government, student organization conduct, commuter life, and new student programs. Sarah’s research areas include promoting transformative learning within and outside the classroom, the development of emerging professionals, the experiences of first-generation college students, and the experiences of student affairs professionals with disabilities. Sarah regularly presents at a variety of student affairs conferences and consults with institutions and organizations regarding how to become more learning-centered.

## Erik Sorensen
earned a master’s degree in Counselor Education, Georgia Southern University, and is currently a PhD candidate in the Student Affairs and Higher Education program at Miami University in Ohio. He has experience in counseling, crisis management, and suicide prevention. Erik served as a Graduate Resident Director at Georgia Southern University and a Complex Director at the University of Oregon. In his current role as an Assistant Director of Residence Education at Miami University, Erik supervises professional staff, oversees resident assistant training, and research and assessment in his office.

## Christine M. Wilson
serves as Assistant Vice President for Student Affairs and Director of Student Activities at the University of Connecticut. She earned her PhD in Education from the University of Rhode Island, where her research focused on preparing teachers for multicultural environments. Wilson earned a Master of Science degree and a Bachelor of Arts degree from Indiana State University. She recently co-authored the ethical practice chapter in the sixth edition of *Student Services: A Handbook for the Profession* with Sue Saunders (2017). She has 30 years of experience in higher education in both student affairs and academic affairs at Pennsylvania State University; the State University of New York, New Paltz; the University of Baltimore; the University of Rhode Island; New England College; and the University of Connecticut.

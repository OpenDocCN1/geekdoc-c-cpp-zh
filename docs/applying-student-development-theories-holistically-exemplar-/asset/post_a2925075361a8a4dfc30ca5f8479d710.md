As active members of the Mizzou community, participants not only support the efforts of their peers, but also advocate for and facilitate programs that enrich both the Black student experience and larger campus community. Harper and Quaye (2007) note that active engagement enables strong Black identities that incite productive activism on predominantly White campuses. The students’ dedication to giving back to their community while pursuing their education can be reflective of a healthy Black identity (Internalization/Internationalization-Commitment stage). The focus is a long-term commitment and vested interest in Black affairs on Mizzou’s campus.

To measure a sense of belonging, the leadership team tracks participants’ campus involvement, including student organization membership, community service programs, social activities, and attendance at events on campus. Involvement and sense of belonging is also considered in reflection papers and class discussions and social activities.

## Lessons from Applying Theory Artfully

Our goal for the continuation of MBMI does not rest solely on data analysis, but by the needs presented in Strayhorn and Terrell’s (2010) work. Educational settings that are incongruent to the experiences of Black males create formidable challenges to their matriculation experience (Strayhorn & Terrell, 2010). The necessity to carve out spaces for Black males to be successful in predominantly White spaces with a program like MBMI is acute. However, even with the efforts and resources we have utilized thus far, there is recognition that additional assessment and reevaluation is needed to enhance and sustain the program’s success.

This assessment could potentially resolve some of the challenges that the program faces and improve the experiences of participants. First, an expansion of the program to involve more participants could lead to a greater sense of belonging and identity discovery for Black males at Mizzou. The developmental opportunities that the MBMI program offers its participants, while impactful, only reaches a small percentage of the Black male community at the university. ## Relationship Building as a Pathway to Cross-Cultural Awareness

Jennifer A. Blair and Gordon S. Louie

Following the national trend in rapid growth of international (i.e., U.S. visa category F-1) student enrollment, the Tippie College of Business Undergraduate Program at the University of Iowa created a dedicated position charged with developing and coordinating programs. The goals of this position are centered on building community and promoting cultural awareness in the college. As part of these efforts, a "buddy" program was created. International and domestic students are matched for a semester-long friendship, and each buddy pair is provided the following: structured events to gain cultural knowledge and support large-group interactions, partnerships with local businesses to incentivize one-on-one meetings among buddy pairs, and a safe and semi-structured environment in which to engage in difficult, but reflective, conversations related to race and ethnicity. Now in its eighth semester, the program's creation and continued development is guided by Phinney's (1993) model for ethnic identity development. The program situates students (at all stages of the model) in cultural difference and aims to offer a supportive environment to interact and engage in dialogue and encourage critical self-reflection that connects experiences in (and out of) the program with the greater whole of their collegiate experience, professional aspirations, and personal goals.

The University of Iowa is a public research university located in Iowa City in eastern Iowa. With a student population of 33,564 and more than 200 academic areas of study, the University of Iowa attracts students from 114 countries and all 50 states. Of the 24,503 undergraduates enrolled in the Fall 2017 semester, 57.7% (14,160) are from Iowa, 26% (6,382) are from adjoining states, and 7.8% (1,899) are from other states and U.S. Territories. The remaining 8.4% (2,062) come from foreign countries. Of total enrollment, 17.8% identify with a minority group (University of Iowa Registrar, 2017a, d).

The Tippie College of Business (“Tippie”) is one of 11 colleges at the University of Iowa. Undergraduate student enrollment in Tippie is 2,726. Of these, 1,730 students are declared as Pre-Business. Of the total undergraduate enrollment in Tippie, 12.1% identify with a minority group (University of Iowa Registrar, 2017b, c).

Tippie’s undergraduate enrollment includes 365 international students (13.4%). A further 309 international students are Pre-Business (17.9%). Of the international students enrolled in Tippie, 80.5% (294) are from China, 7.9% (29) are from South Korea, and the remaining 11.6% are from approximately 15 other countries (University of Iowa Registrar, 2017a, d). While the international student population more than doubled at the University of Iowa between 2007 and 2014, international undergraduate student enrollment grew by nearly 10 times at Tippie, reflecting a national trend in increased enrollment from international undergraduates pursuing business degrees (Institute of International Education, 2014).

## Description of Tippie Buddies

In response to the rapid growth in international student enrollment, in 2012 the Undergraduate Program at Tippie created the position of Assistant Director of Global Community Engagement, with the goal of building greater community between domestic and international undergraduate students. In Fall 2013, the Assistant Director created and convened a new student group called the Global Engagement Student Advisory Board (GESAB), comprised of a balance of international and domestic students in Tippie. GESAB’s mandate was to create programming, raise awareness, and serve as an advocating body for cross-cultural interaction. GESAB members’ first priority was to create a buddy program in Tippie.

International Buddies at Tippie (Tippie Buddies) is the pairing program conceptualized by GESAB. Overseen by the Assistant Director and supported with two student assistants or Global Ambassadors (Ambassadors), the program matches international and domestic business students for a semester-long program to foster friendships across cultures and provide opportunity for reflection on racial and ethnic identity.

Basic job functions needed to be included in the IAP (e.g., RAs complete residence hall rounds, write incident reports as needed, build community; SOULS recruit, select, and train an orientation team). Many of these job parameters may be set by departmental or divisional expectations through the forms of policy or professional standards. RAs at Miami University have specific programmatic requirements and parameters but the way in which each parameter is met is the decision of each RA. Resident Assistants select how they would be held accountable for the timeliness and completion of the IAP. It is completely acceptable for each RA to have a plan unique to them, as noted in the “individualized” label of the accountability plan. The goal of IAPs is to encourage and celebrate the ways in which student staff members already know themselves and their strengths, challenges, and areas for growth. Through this approach, student staff members are more heavily invested in their role because they help create and drive how they will do their work. This allows them to identify experiences that help them make decisions using the knowledge and expertise that they already have as students, leaders, and campus staff members.

On a fundamental level, IAPs vary in structure and content based on the supervisor’s as well as the student supervisee’s needs and expectations. In the example provided in Table 7.1, one RA supervisee decided that they would like to make meaning of their experience, work role, and values by writing a blog. Another student made meaning of their role by tracking the progress toward their goals on a spreadsheet and then processing or celebrating their work verbally. Each of these evaluation tools was co-created by the supervisor and the supervisee to best meet the needs of the residential community, the staff team, and the RA themselves.

Although IAPs are co-created, it is the work of supervisors to decide on and communicate the parameters within which student staffs are able to be creative.

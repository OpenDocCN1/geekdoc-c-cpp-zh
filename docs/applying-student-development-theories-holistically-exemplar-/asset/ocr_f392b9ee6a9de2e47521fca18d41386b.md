This goal was a stretch for many undergraduate paraprofessional staff members, especially envisioning themselves as the facilitator or leader for this type of program. Connecting passion topics to a social justice program was usually possible, but was made more difficult depending on how related the initial “Props for Passion” (i.e., personal sharing of an important topic) topic was to social justice concepts. For example, some undergraduate paraprofessional staff members presented generically on “family” and had to critically examine to find a social justice connection. For others, the process was seamless because the passion topic related directly. For example, Rolah shared that he facilitated a program about disabilities called *Walk a Day in My Shoes*. He set up stations to emulate different disabilities. He said that it was a completely different program from anything he had ever done, seen, or attended. He indicated that it felt like a bigger accomplishment because he got people to think more about themselves and their lives and put things into perspective.

Over time, we observed this outcome was often achieved in their second year as undergraduate paraprofessional staff members engaged in subsequent cycles of *Passion to Action*. First, *Passion to Action* exposed the undergraduate paraprofessional staff members to new ideas, concepts, and topics around diversity, inclusion, and social justice. Second, consistently discussing the “Passion to Action Macro-Cycle” gave staff a roadmap to use outside of their roles. Eventually, returning undergraduate paraprofessional staff members were planning social justice programs concurrent with “Props for Passion” in the fall and completing their “Passion Projects” on new topics in the spring.

We define teamwork as one’s ability to interact effectively with peers and foster relationships with individuals and other units/offices across campus. To foster such relationships, communication skills must be effectively utilized. Critical thinking skills are developed throughout the student staff experience, and include making informed decisions, seeing problems from different perspectives, creating alternate interpretations of ideas, and identifying multiple approaches to solving problems. The fourth trait, intercultural competency, is deeply embedded in the student staff experience, and is grounded in an intersectional framework. We define a foundational component of intercultural competency as actively seeking opportunities to increase one’s personal understanding of cultures and lived experiences different from one’s own. We challenge student staff members to move beyond recognizing intercultural competency as an individual process to acknowledging institutional and systemic components of oppression and marginalization. We define the last trait, administrative and organizational management, as adhering to high standards of personal and professional conduct, responding positively to change, and demonstrating a willingness to learn new methods for organization and administrative tasks.

ETR is intended to support the learning and development of undergraduate student staff in the Pride Center and Office of Multicultural Affairs and is utilized throughout the year as both a reflective and evaluative tool. Throughout the year, student staff members are challenged to strengthen these five traits through their work in the Pride Center, in the classroom, in other leadership capacities, and personally. Student staff reflect on the five traits regularly in one-on-one meetings with other staff members and supervisors. ETR is also incorporated into biannual comprehensive reviews. The reviews are grounded in the concept of multi-rater feedback and challenge traditional unidirectional performance review approaches (Bracken, Dalton, Jako, McCauley, & Pollman, 1997), as well as the principles of the LPM, which views learning as co-creative and non-hierarchical (Baxter Magolda, 2004).

While student staff are held to high standards, we recognize the need to learn alongside the student staff and meet our own expectations within the various traits outlined in the rubric.

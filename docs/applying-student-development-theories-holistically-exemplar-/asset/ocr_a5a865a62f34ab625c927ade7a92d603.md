As anticipated in the cluster of theories identified primarily as psychosocial in nature, some proposals also incorporated ideas from frameworks that emphasize *influences* on development (versus theories that describe adult development sequences).

From among cognitive development theories, nine frameworks were used in practice: Perry’s (1970) Scheme of Intellectual and Ethical Development; Belenky, Clinchy, Goldberger, and Tarule’s (1986) categories from *Women’s Ways of Knowing*; Kohlberg’s (1981) Theory of Moral Development; Gilligan’s (1982) Theory of Women’s Moral Development; Kitchener and King’s (1981) Reflective Judgment Model; Kegan’s (1994) Theory of Evolution of Consciousness; and Baxter Magolda’s (1992) Epistemological Reflection Model, Theory of Self-Authorship (2001), and Learning Partnerships Model (2004). The cognitive cluster of developmental theories was the one in which proposal authors brought in frameworks not listed on our Call for Proposals (i.e., Kegan’s Theory of Evolution of Consciousness and Baxter Magolda’s Learning Partnerships Model), making it evident that scholarly practitioners understood the links among these theories’ authors as well as foundational cognitive constructs. Baxter Magolda’s scholarship was cited frequently; scholarly practitioners embraced the direct connections among research, theory, and a model for using the theory in practice (i.e., her Learning Partnerships Model).

In the theory cluster labeled *Influences on Development*, proposals were received that made pragmatic links to all seven of the frameworks listed on our Call for Proposals, and Astin’s (1999) Theory of Student Involvement. These were as follows: Bronfenbrenner (developmental ecology), Schlossberg (transitions), Kolb (experiential learning), Jung or Myers and Briggs (personality type), Clifton and Anderson (personality strengths), Sedlacek (non-cognitive variables), and Strayhorn or others (sense of belonging). Because of the prevalence of Schlossberg’s Transition Theory (1981, 1984) and Kolb’s Experiential Learning Theory (1984) across the proposals, we selected contemporary applications that emphasized applying those two theories holistically.

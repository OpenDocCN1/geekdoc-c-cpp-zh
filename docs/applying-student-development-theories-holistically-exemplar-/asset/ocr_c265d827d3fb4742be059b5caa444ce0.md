The process of assessing self-authorship can be a developmental activity in and of itself (Baxter Magolda & King, 2007). When assessing IAPs, we also are assessing student learning, developmental capacity, and meaning-making. As supervisors, we focus on the intentional reflection of student staff to increase their developmental capacity and understanding themselves as learners. We assess our student staff members, both RAs and SOULs, throughout their experience in their positions, during an academic course that relates to their positions, and in exit interviews as they leave positions.

# LESSONS FROM APPLYING THEORY ARTFULLY

Overall, in our experience, implementing IAPs with student staff is rewarding for both supervisors and students. Student staff members using IAPs are afforded more ownership for their developmental journey and work, creating an explicit learning environment for these students. For supervisors, the additional ownership and responsibility on the part of student staff created less direct constant oversight. RAs and SOULs were effective in their roles when faced with adaptive challenges, which occur often in these roles.

IAPs take time to develop and implement and must be living, breathing plans. Because initiatives and plans are so personalized, supervisors must be open to making IAPs a focal point of the staff dynamic, keeping IAPs at the forefront of the work. IAPs become meaningless if they are not revisited and prioritized. Supervisors must be open to change and edits over the course of a year. This is a critical connection back to how the LPM aligns with IAPs. In the LPM, meaning is mutually constructed, which means that it must be flexible and adaptable as new needs emerge or a student is prepared for greater challenge. If a supervisor is tied to the idea that they are a distributor of knowledge that their supervisee must simply receive in a transactional relationship, then opportunities for development are limited. As students grow and develop, and as environmental events impact students’ lives and understanding of the world they live in, it may make sense to adjust IAPs throughout the student staff member’s term.

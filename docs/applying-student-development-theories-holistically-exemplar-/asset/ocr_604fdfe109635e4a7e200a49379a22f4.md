Physical and Manual Competence: While the mentoring group experience provided opportunities for participants to develop interpersonal competence, it was the *content* of the challenges that focused on students’ physical and manual competency. Mentoring group challenges included: an introductory bulletin board decorating contest that was judged based on creativity and success at introducing the group members (the bunk cabins had eight bulletin boards); daily adventure challenges, including high ropes, low ropes, zip-line, and lake trampoline; planning, shopping, and preparing two meals for the entire *Launch* group and cleaning up after two different meals; and a concluding skit competition that was judged on the group’s creativity. Each of these challenges offered opportunity for a variety of students’ physical and manual strengths to emerge.

Intellectual Competence: Understandably, many *Launch* participants wondered if they had the intellectual competence to succeed in their first year of college. Three strategies were embedded to address this concern. It was important to help students set realistic GPA expectations for the fall semester. During one of the morning meetings, each student was given a piece of college letterhead with an envelope. The staff members facilitated a discussion about the difference in academic life from high school to college, and the potential impact of that change on GPA. Next, the participants were instructed to address the envelope to their home address and write a letter with their GPA goal for fall term and two messages to themselves: *What do you want to tell yourself if you make your goal? What do you want to tell yourself if you do not meet your goal?* The letters were mailed in December after fall grades were submitted.

Second, by utilizing student mentors and student tutors from the Academic Center as *Launch* mentors, *Launch* students were intentionally connected with students who were highly trained to assist with academic transitions. The intention was that *Launch* students would already be socially connected to the staff at the Academic Center, thus easing any anxiety they might have about reaching out to the Academic Center later in the year when they knew they needed help.

Third, *Launch* students were taught the technique of free writing to encourage their writing fluency. Students at RMC receive a lot of feedback on their writing.

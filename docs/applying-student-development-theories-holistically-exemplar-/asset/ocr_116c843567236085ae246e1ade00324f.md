# THEORY CLUSTERS

To maximize the utility of this book, theory-in-practice examples were organized into the three parts or theory clusters that have historical associations with the student affairs literature: psychosocial, cognitive, and influences on development (Evans, Forney, & Guido-DiBrito, 1998; Mayhew, Rockenbach, Bowman, Seifert, & Wolniak, 2016). Each cluster represents a developmental emphasis throughout affiliated chapters, is inclusive of student communities served, and uses one or more of the previously listed theories, models, or frameworks. To illustrate, Part I chapters were assigned the cluster label, Who Am I?: Psychosocial and Social Identity Development. Communities served through programs and initiatives described in the four affiliated chapters were incoming first-years, first-generation students, international students, students of color, and Black males. Central scholarship used to inform practice includes Chickering and Reisser’s Theory of Identity Development, Astin’s Theory of Student Involvement, Cross and Fhagen-Smith’s Model of Black Identity Development, Strayhorn’s Sense of Belonging, and Phinney’s Model of Ethnic Identity Development.

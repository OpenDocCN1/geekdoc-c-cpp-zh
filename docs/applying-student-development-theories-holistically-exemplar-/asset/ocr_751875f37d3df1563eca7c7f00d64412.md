Technical problems can be solved by expert knowledge, where adaptive challenges require new learning to solve. Kegan and Lahey (2009) suggest that approaching adaptive challenges with technical solutions often does not work to the benefit of those involved. These solutions are ultimately not optimized as more creative, learning-centered solutions. Also important to note is that adaptive solutions often lead to the growth of developmental capacity of the learner. Student staff roles manage a series of adaptive challenges, which includes the unpredictable element of working with other human beings. Student staff members who address adaptive challenges through their role often do much of their work without direct supervisory oversight at all times, and so these staff members need to be able to make complex and mature decisions on their own. A self-authored form of mind helps staff members make legitimately internalized decisions (Baxter Magolda, 2004).

A framework that supervisors can use to grow developmental capacity toward self-authorship in student staff members is through the use of the Learning Partnerships Model (LPM; Baxter Magolda, 2004). Three key principles of the LPM are to validate students as knowers, to situate learning within the learner’s experience, and to mutually construct meaning (Baxter Magolda, 2004). LPM allows for an environment that fosters challenge and support, but always in concert with the involvement of others in the partnership.

A specific strategy that both authors have used to engage in a learning partnership with student staff members is the development of Individualized Accountability Plans. IAPs provide an opportunity for student staff to take additional ownership and responsibility for their work. Details and recommendations on the implementation of IAPs with student staff members are the focus of the remainder of this chapter.

## Assessment and Evaluation

The *Passport Report* is annually prepared by the IUPUI Office of Institutional Research and Decision Support to report prior ITISA student degree completion, GPA, and retention rates at IUPUI. For instance, ITISA students have consistently brought in more credits, on average, than IUPUI transfer students have from other institutions. ITISA has accounted for 40.7% of the incoming transfer credits in the 2016–2017 academic year and 35.5% for the Fall 2017 semester (Passport Office & IUPUI IRDS, 2017). By being informed of credits brought in by ITISA students, Passport can advocate for continuous coordination between ITISA and IUPUI to increase course articulations and the creation of 2+2 articulation agreements. The report dismisses myths regarding ITISA students’ preparedness to IUPUI. Data for the 2016–2017 academic year shows that 77.7% of ITISA students who transferred to IUPUI as a junior or senior were retained into the following fall compared with the average of 76.5% of all external transfers entering as a junior or senior (Passport Office & IUPUI IRDS, 2017, p. 10).

To better inform the office on their services and resource offerings, as well as how students perceived their pre-transfer environment experiences in relation to Schlossberg’s (1981) theory, Passport created an Academic Advising Survey, which provides constructive feedback to improve Passport’s pre-transfer advising. In Fall 2017, Passport surveyed prior ITISA students currently enrolled in IUPUI to capture data on their transfer experience. Of the responses received, 56% rated their overall transfer experience to IUPUI as “Very Satisfied” and 24% were “Satisfied” (Passport Office, personal communication, October 30, 2017). These results suggest that the efforts of the Passport Office align with reducing Schlossberg’s (1981) concern of a deficit situation—unstable within the new environment and student status (p. 6)—for ITISA students transitioning to IUPUI.

## LESSONS FROM APPLYING THEORY ARTFULLY

By intentionally applying student development theory and recent research, Passport has yielded success in addressing student transition via its various services, support, and resources to ITISA and IUPUI of traditional student populations, which means emphasizing those students transitioning from one four-year institution to another. Schlossberg’s work also is rooted in adult development theory, including age 18 and up, as well as various life experiences. However, community colleges are adopting programs to entice high school student enrollment such as honors programs that may have students younger than 18. Further, this theory does not take into account a student’s maturity level being one of an adult, regardless of age. A major limitation of Schlossberg’s transition theory is a lack of mindfulness about how intersectional aspects of identity influence students’ transitions. As a result, transition theory may miss out in serving practitioners with a comprehensive understanding regarding the various barriers and considerations we encounter as we work with a progressively diverse student population.

## 连接体问题

在解决连接体问题时，我们常采用整体法与隔离法进行分析。整体法适用于求解外力或系统加速度，隔离法适用于求解物体间的相互作用力。

**例1.** 如图所示，两个质量分别为 $m_1=2\,\text{kg}$、$m_2=3\,\text{kg}$ 的物体 A、B 叠放在光滑水平面上，用水平恒力 $F=10\,\text{N}$ 拉物体 B。求 A、B 间的摩擦力大小。（已知 A、B 间动摩擦因数 $\mu=0.5$，$g=10\,\text{m/s}^2$）

![](img/c03cf8065037f42512bddd1febea4918_153_0.png)

**解析：**
先以整体为研究对象，由牛顿第二定律：
$$F=(m_1+m_2)a$$
代入数据得：
$$a=\frac{F}{m_1+m_2}=\frac{10}{2+3}=2\,\text{m/s}^2$$
再隔离物体 A，A 在水平方向只受摩擦力 $f$，由牛顿第二定律：
$$f=m_1 a=2\times 2=4\,\text{N}$$

因为 $f=4\,\text{N} < \mu m_1 g=10\,\text{N}$，所以 A、B 间摩擦力大小为 $4\,\text{N}$，二者未发生相对滑动。

> 注意：当外力超过临界值时，物体间将发生相对滑动，此时摩擦力为滑动摩擦力 $f=\mu N$，且两物体加速度不再相同。

- 整体法：适合求解系统加速度或外部作用力，不考虑系统内物体间的相互作用。
- 隔离法：适合求解物体间的内力（如摩擦力、弹力），需对单个物体进行受力分析。

*B. Ashley Staples, Ben Jones, and Vanessa Perez*

The Passion to Action program was designed using an experiential learning framework to develop paraprofessional student staff members’ ability to engage with social justice personally, and implement programs that achieve social justice learning outcomes. The program was implemented during a four-year period in an upper-level students’ residence hall at New York University (NYU).

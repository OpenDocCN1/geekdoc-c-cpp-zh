Issues that prevented them from engaging in these topics included a lack of common language (e.g., “inclusivity” used to describe being considerate and welcoming to others rather than acknowledging and validating others’ social identities), self-confidence (e.g., only external facilitators were secured for diversity-related programming), and ability to dialogue (e.g., intentionally disengaging from casual conversations about diversity because of discomfort).

We created and implemented “Props for Passion” to address these challenges with the hope that it would lead to more face-to-face discussions and ultimately enhance the social justice culture in the residential community. After participating in the intervention, our undergraduate paraprofessional staff members reported having more conversations oriented toward social justice, enhanced opportunities to collaborate in planning programs related to social justice, and a greater confidence in engaging with diversity topics with their residents and colleagues. Based on these assessments and the returning undergraduate paraprofessional staff members’ excitement for the program, we restructured the initiative into *Passion to Action*, a two-part program with “Props for Passion” (sharing a personal story) in the fall semester and “Passion Projects” (social justice activity to share with and bring to the community) in the spring semester.

# **Guiding Theories**

# Assessment and Evaluation

As a campus-wide program, student learning assessment occurs at the institutional level using a longitudinal design: Time 1 assessment occurs the last day of first-year orientation and Time 2 assessments are administered in February of the sophomore year. Three assessment instruments were developed in collaboration with JMU’s Center for Assessment and Research Studies; these measure the five Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs). Assessment for specific educational activities is designed by individual faculty and student affairs professionals to measure the effectiveness of the specific educational interventions.

Program assessment is centered on students’ ability to delineate among and apply the 8KQ concepts broadly, rather than an elusive evaluation of student “ethical behavior.” The Defining Issues Test-2 (Rest, Narvaez, Thomas, & Bebeau, 1999), the dominant tool for measuring moral development, is not used because it focuses too narrowly on justice-oriented moral judgment to be a fit for Ethical Reasoning in Action assessment needs. Essay assessment scoring rewards the recognition of an ethical situation, the broad (and accurate) application of Key Questions, an explanation of each KQ’s relevance or irrelevance to the decision, and the demonstration of weighing and balancing ethical considerations. The JMU Ethical Reasoning Rubric© (2014) provides parameters for assessment specific to 8KQ proficiency.

Overall, ethical reasoning skills have improved since the program began, as indicated by a cross-sectional analysis comparing Fall 2012 baseline data collected prior to any ethical reasoning interventions to data collected in subsequent years where students have at a minimum experienced the It’s Complicated introductory program (Bashkov, Smith, Fulcher, & Hawk, 2014; JMU, 2016; Pyburn, Smith, & Ames, 2016; Smith, Fulcher, & Pyburn, 2015). However, longitudinal scores on the recall instrument, the Ethical Reasoning Recall Test (measuring SLO One) decline slightly or show no significant change between Time 1 and Time 2 as indicated in yearly assessment reports (Bashkov et al., 2014; Pyburn et al., 2016; Smith et al., 2015).

The activities in this stage required critical thinking, conceptualization, and implementation, and were informed with higher-order outcomes on Bloom’s revised cognitive and knowledge dimensions, including *carry out, use, integrate, design,* and *create* (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001). For many undergraduate paraprofessional staff members, the higher-order activity of “integration” was challenging, especially with the two seemingly disparate concepts of “my passion” and “what I do with my residents.” For some, this stage required more vulnerability than presenting in front of the team because it required sharing with the wider audience of residents within the building. CK reflected, “Connecting my passion to my work with residents wasn’t something I expected to do before becoming an RA, but something I think many residence halls could benefit from.”

Full staff (i.e., professional staff, graduate students, faculty fellows) involvement was key for providing support to the undergraduate paraprofessional staff members. A faculty fellow shared, “It was also very helpful to discover commonalities with other members of the staff, including undergraduate paraprofessional staff members, through their own passions, and it actually led me to approach some of them with ideas to program together.” Some undergraduate paraprofessional staff members quickly connected their passion to programming ideas, like Riya who reflected that she knew she wanted the program to “serve as a vehicle for educating others on the critical gaps present in the healthcare system.” For others it involved working through the intimacy of sharing their passion, issues conceptualizing the passion into a structured activity with residents, or gaining skills in program preparation.

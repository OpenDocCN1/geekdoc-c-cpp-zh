# Fostering Engagement With Social
Justice Through Experiential Learning

*B. Ashley Staples, Ben Jones, and Vanessa Perez*

The Passion to Action program was designed using an experiential learning framework to develop paraprofessional student staff members’ ability to engage with social justice personally, and implement programs that achieve social justice learning outcomes. The program was implemented during a four-year period in an upper-level students’ residence hall at New York University (NYU). Passion to Action was a two-part program that built confidence, knowledge, and skills through a “Props for Passion” activity, and the subsequent implementation of social justice programming that met departmental and institutional priorities through a “Passion Projects” activity. This program was structured according to Kolb’s (1984) Stages of Experiential Learning, and facilitated across an academic year timeline; the learning outcomes at each stage were constructed using Bloom’s Taxonomy of Educational Objectives, as updated by Anderson and Krathwohl (2001).

Passion to Action fostered engagement with social justice topics in a residence hall at New York University (NYU). NYU is a private research university enrolling nearly 60,000 students from diverse demographic backgrounds enrolled in programs across all degree levels (NYU at a Glance, n.d.). Residential Life and Housing Services (RLHS) houses more than 11,000 students and employs approximately 350 students and 100 professional staff members.

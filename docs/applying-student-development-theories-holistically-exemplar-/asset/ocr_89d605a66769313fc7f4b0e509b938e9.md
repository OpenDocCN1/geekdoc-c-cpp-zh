Miami University is situated primarily in the small, Midwestern town of Oxford, Ohio. Founded in 1809, the charter of the institution preceded the establishment of Oxford. Thus, Miami University’s Oxford campus is highly residential. It is a four-year public university that has a large undergraduate population of approximately 17,000 students. Approximately 40% of Miami students are in-state students. Demographically, 51.5% of students are female, and 48.5% of students are male. Domestic students are 87% of the population, while international students represent 13% of the student population. Domestic students of color are 14.3% of the student body. Because of strong Miami University tradition, legacy students (e.g., sons, daughters, siblings of alumni) are 11.7% of students (Miami University Admissions, 2017).

Moll (1985) referred to Miami University as a “Public Ivy,” noting schools in this category offer an Ivy League education at a public school financial cost. Its Carnegie basic classification is a doctoral university with higher research activity. Miami University holds strong traditions and values in academia and co-curricular life, making it a great place to challenge students to find their voices, values, and passions. This can include partnering with students in co-creating experiences inside and outside of the classroom.

# DESCRIPTION OF INDIVIDUALIZED ACCOUNTABILITY PLANS

IAPs were originated by the Office of Residence Life as a tool for Resident Directors to actively engage student learning and personal growth in the Resident Assistant (RA) position. IAPs have since been applied in multiple contexts with different student staff positions in order to closely align human development with student affairs practice. The goal of using IAPs is to help student staff members make meaning of their jobs and leadership roles on an individualized level that propel them toward the construct of self-authorship.

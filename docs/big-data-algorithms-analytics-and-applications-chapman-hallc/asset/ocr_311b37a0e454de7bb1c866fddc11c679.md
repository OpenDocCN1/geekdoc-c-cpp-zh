not satisfy the satisfiability condition, and (ii,jj) Q^F is the query of QWL^P having the greater negative distance from the satisfiability condition, that is, Q^T is the query of QWL^P that is in most need of new samples; (3) move enough samples from Q^T to Q^F in such a way as to satisfy the accuracy/privacy constraint on Q^T while, at the same time, ensuring that Q^T does not violate the constraint; and (4) repeat steps 1, 2, and 3 until the current representation of A' satisfies, as much as possible, the accuracy/privacy constraint with respect to QWL, within the maximum number of iterations bounded by L_{A,QWL}. Regarding step 3, moving ρ samples from Q^T to Q^F means (1) removing ρ samples from R(Q^T), thus obtaining an additional space, named B(ρ); (2) allocating B(ρ) to R(Q^F); and (3) resampling R(Q^F) by considering the additional number of samples that have become available—in practice, this means extracting from R(Q^F) further ρ samples.

Let S*(Q_i) be the number of samples of a query Q_i ∈ QWL satisfying the accuracy/privacy constraint. From the formal definitions of E_Q(Q_i) (see Section 16.5), I(Q_i), Ī(Q_i), and E_i(Q_i) (see Section 16.6), and the satisfiability condition, it could be easily demonstrated that S*(Q_i) is given by the following formula: S*(Q_i) = (1 - Φ_O_i)/(1 - Φ_P_i) |Q_i|.

Let S_eff(Q^F) and S_eff(Q^T) be the numbers of samples effectively extracted from R(Q^F) and R(Q^T), respectively, during the previous sampling phase. Note that S_eff(Q^F) < S*(Q^F) and S_eff(Q^T) ≥ S*(Q^T). It is easy to prove that the number of samples to be moved from Q^T to Q^F such that Q^T satisfies the accuracy/privacy constraint and Q^T does not violate the constraint, denoted by S_mov(Q^T, Q^F), is finally given by the following formula: S_mov(Q^T, Q^F) = S*(Q^T) - S_eff(Q^T), under the constraint S_mov(Q^T, Q^F) ≤ S_eff(Q^T) - S*(Q^T).

Without going into detail, it is possible to demonstrate that, given (1) an arbitrary data cube A, (2) an arbitrary query workload QWL, (3) an arbitrary pair of thresholds Φ_O and Φ_P, and (4) an arbitrary storage space B, it is not always possible to make QWL satisfiable via the refinement process. From this evidence, our idea of using a best-effort approach makes sense perfectly.

# 16.9.4 The computeSynDataCube Algorithm

Main greedy algorithm computeSynDataCube is described by the pseudocode listed in Figure 16.3, wherein (1) allocateStorageSpace implements the proportional storage space allocation scheme (Equation 16.1), (2) sampleDataCube implements the sampling strategy, (3) check tests the satisfiability of the target query workload against the current representation of the synopsis data cube, and (4) refineSynDataCube is in charge of refining the current representation of the synopsis data cube.

```
computeSynDataCube(A,QWL,Φ_O,Φ_P,B,L_{A,QWL})
W ← allocateStorageSpace(A,QWL,B)
A' ← sampleDataCube(A,Φ_O,W)
while (check(A',QWL,Φ_O,Φ_P) && L_{A,QWL} > 0)
    (A',currSwapMemorySize) ← refineSynDataCube(A',QWL,Φ_O,Φ_P,W,L_{A,QWL})
    L_{A,QWL} ← L_{A,QWL} - currSwapMemorySize
return A'
```

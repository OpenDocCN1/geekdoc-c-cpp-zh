the family. To determine the family of the current plant, Mary could start by describing a cypress to Ralph. She could first ask if the stem was woody. If the answer were yes, she could ask if the position was upright. If the answer were no, she would conclude that the family was not cypress. She could then proceed to ask questions concerning pines. Although this procedure would work, there is a lot of redundancy in the questions. Because all trees have upright position, once Mary ascertained that the position was not upright, she would know that the type was not tree. So a better strategy would be to first ask questions that narrowed down the type. For instance, if Mary learned that the stem was woody and the position was upright, she would then ask of there was one main trunk. If the answer to this question was then yes, she would know that the type was tree. She would then ask questions that would determine the type of the plant.

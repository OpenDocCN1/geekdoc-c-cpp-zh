reaches a point of equilibrium. During the day, sunlight warms the helium trapped inside the balloon, which causes the helium to expand and become less dense; thus, the balloon will rise higher. During the night, however, the helium in the balloon cools and becomes more dense; thus, the balloon will descend to a lower altitude. The next day, the sun heats the helium again and the balloon rises. Over time, this process generates a set of altitude measurements that can be approximated with a polynomial equation. Assume that the following polynomial represents the altitude or height in meters during the first 48 hours following the launch of a weather balloon:

```
alt(t) = -0.12t^4 + 12t^3 - 380t^2 + 4100t + 220,
```

where the units of t are hours. The corresponding polynomial model for the velocity in meters per hour of the weather balloon is

```
v(t) = -0.48t^3 + 36t^2 - 760t + 4100.
```

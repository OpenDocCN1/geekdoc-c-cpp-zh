These articles discuss the fact that libraries are adopting IT service models including formal models such as Information Technology Infrastructure Library (ITIL) (www.itil-officialsit e.com) and less formal models such as ITSM (www.itsm.info/ITSM.htm). Even for libraries that have not used such models, there is use of service management portals (McHale 2010) and a growing awareness of the responsibility of community-driven software development (Reese 2012).

As our exploration of cloud approaches emphasizes, there is a need for formal IT management practices. For libraries turning mostly to SaaS solutions, the emphasis on IT management should be on maintaining an intranet of service providers and support information. For libraries turning more to PaaS and IaaS solutions, a more formal support strategy helps make the implementation and management of solutions sustainable. There are a variety of models from which to choose, including ITIL, the Systems Development Lifecycle (Department of Justice 2003), Control Objects for Information and Related Technology (ISACA 2011), and Microsoft Operations Framework (Microsoft 2011). These models help IT organizations approach system selection, development, implementation, management, and decommissioning of IT services in systematic ways and are helpful for libraries of any type. Although each model has its own distinct features, there have been attempts to find parallel processes and common ground (Pollard et al. 2009; Susanto et al. 2011) among them. Critics of these models point out that IT service model adoption has not demonstrated improvements in IT service and assert that they create administrative overhead and can lead to the creation of arbitrary boundaries (Addy 2007, 4).

Service management is a broad concept, defined as “a set of specialized organizational capabilities for providing value to customers in the form of services” (ITIL 2011, 11). The ITIL service life cycle describes the process of developing, delivering, and managing services and focuses in particular on knowledge management, SLAs, and standardization. Service management models can provide standardized measures of success including time to resolution, ratio of open and closed incidents, number of supported services, and time commitment of IT staff.

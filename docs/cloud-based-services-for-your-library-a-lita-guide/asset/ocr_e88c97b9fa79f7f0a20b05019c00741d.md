In addition, many products such as Heroku, RedHat OpenShift Express, and Google Apps Engine offer free levels of service that may appeal to small and medium-sized libraries. Dedicated PaaS providers tend to charge on the bases of add-ons and IT resources used. It can be difficult to compare costs across providers because each provider uses different measures. For example, Heroku uses a measure of front-end and back-end processes called “dynos” that would be difficult to compare to Amazon’s Elastic Beanstalk instances. In contrast, Google has a multifaceted charging model that includes CPU time, bandwidth, and data storage elements. Although there has been some research comparing the total cost of ownership of cloud services, this form of PaaS environment introduces new elements and is in need of deeper exploration.

# Service Portability and Interoperability

The complex customization requirements of dedicated PaaS solutions mean that they can be difficult to migrate to new providers. Certain platforms such as Heroku and Amazon’s Elastic Beanstalk allow application packages to be deployed on other platforms. In contrast, platforms that use a customized technology stack such as Google App Engine make migrating to another platform difficult if not impossible without additional application development.

# CONCLUSION

This chapter explores two broad platform types important to consider when exploring cloud computing and virtualization. The review of SaaS providers focuses largely on library-specific solutions but also highlights SaaS services for broader information uses. Providers such as WordPress and Salesforce may very well be suitable solutions for libraries but should be considered within the overall IT framework required. Indeed, if libraries are seeking to use SaaS solutions to replace all of their information service infrastructure, these options should be considered as ways of bridging the gap between library-specific and general web-based information services. At the very least, however, libraries that turn to SaaS solutions need to consider how they plan to monitor these services, manage contact and access information, and prepare for migration away from SaaS providers.

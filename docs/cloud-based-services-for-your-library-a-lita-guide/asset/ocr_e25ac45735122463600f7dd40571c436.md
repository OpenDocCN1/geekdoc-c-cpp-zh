Three research questions examined the adoption of cloud computing and virtualization in libraries and explored the factors IT librarians consider when evaluating these technologies and their use in libraries:

+   - What is the landscape and state of cloud computing and virtualized technology adoption in libraries? What services are libraries providing using cloud- and virtual-based platforms? What types of cloud- and virtual-based platforms are being used in libraries?
+   - What enabling and inhibiting factors are identified by librarians in relation to the adoption of cloud computing?
+   - What actions would enhance the adoption of cloud computing in libraries? How could these actions be designed or implemented?

# METHODS

The study was designed and administered using an online survey platform (see www.alaeditions.org/webextras for the full survey). Participants were solicited from a range of library IT discussion boards including Code4Lib, Lita-L, SYSLIB-L, and WEB4LIB. The total number of participating libraries was 111. Participants answered a range of questions including demographic information on library size and focus, questions about the implementation of information services (e.g., ILS and OpenURL resolver), and motivations/concerns regarding cloud computing adoption. A mixed-method approach was valuable in this study because it provided both baseline descriptive data on the state of cloud computing and virtualized technology adoption in libraries and higher-level data surrounding the subjective motivations and enabling/inhibiting factors for librarians making decisions about technology adoption. The survey included quantitative data on the type of services provided by libraries using cloud platforms and qualitative data on the enabling and inhibiting factors related to decision making in IT for libraries.

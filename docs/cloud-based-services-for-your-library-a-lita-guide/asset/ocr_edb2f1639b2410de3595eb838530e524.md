Libraries may desire dedicated physical servers if cost, subscriber privacy, or other issues outweigh the anticipated benefits of cloud providers. Libraries that want to operate on dedicated physical servers may also want to consider options in which the library purchases hardware and pays a subscription fee for hosting its hardware in a data center. This approach often comes with higher levels of service, which may include forms of system administration for additional fees, but also enables libraries to benefit from robust data center features such as network redundancy, environment quality, and off-site backup redundancy without giving up the benefits of owning the hardware on which their web services operate. This may be particularly attractive if libraries have adopted an IT service that operates on a platform with which they are not familiar.

It is worth noting that other PaaS solutions such as Salesforce, Heroku, or GoogleApps are not included in the three types of PaaS hosting mentioned above. These solutions focus on supporting highly customized information services such as customer management, site publishing, and content management without the overhead of server management tasks. As a result, these features are more scalable than the server-focused solutions mentioned here but require a tighter coupling between application and hosting environment. These PaaS solutions are reviewed later in this chapter.

When you are selecting a provider it is important to look for objective reviews of each company and to choose a provider based on elements important to your local organization. These elements likely include supported server software, platform security, system management tasks, and SLA options.

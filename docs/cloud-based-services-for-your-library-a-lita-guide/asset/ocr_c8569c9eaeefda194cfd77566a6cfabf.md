al. 2011). Bertot and colleagues also found considerable increases in library IT service use in the previous year including public workstations, Wi-Fi, electronic collections, and training. This gap between patron demand demonstrated by Bertot and the allocation of resources discussed by McKendrick indicates that there may be room for libraries to leverage cloud computing and virtualization platforms to save resources and improve services.

# CONCLUSION

This survey of virtualization, cloud computing, and outsourcing adoption in libraries both underscores the current interest in cloud computing and virtualization and demonstrates which views are prevalent among adopters. The gaps found between library and IT literature and survey participants indicate that the questions about cloud computing and virtualization may be different for different audiences or perhaps are shifting as cloud computing matures. The survey also demonstrates a growing reliance on outsourced library IT as opposed to PaaS or IaaS approaches and indicates a slow but growing trend to migrate older and core library systems (e.g., the ILS) to these environments. These survey results should not be overgeneralized, but the evaluative model represented in figure 2.1 and the grouped themes presented in tables 2.4 and 2.5 may help libraries understand their own IT environment in more detail.

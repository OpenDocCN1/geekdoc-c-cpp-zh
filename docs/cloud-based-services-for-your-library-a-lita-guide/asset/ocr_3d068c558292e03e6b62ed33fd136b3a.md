# Budgeting and Service Subscription

Libraries turning to library-specific virtualization and cloud solutions often find that the budgeting and subscription terms provided by the vendor recognize the approaches typical of that industry, but they may also find that cloud providers from other areas are not used to working with libraries and other nonprofit institutions. In many of the cloud services explored in chapters 4 and 5, subscription terms are measured in hours or months rather than annually based. This can make budgeting and cost prediction more difficult and challenging. In addition, many cloud providers including Amazon and Microsoft rely primarily on credit cards as a form of payment and do not support purchase orders or other long-term subscription models by default.

Several PaaS and SaaS solutions are specifically for libraries. These include for-profit companies such as SerialsSolutions (http://serialssolutions.com), LibLime (http://liblime.com), Equinox (http://esilibrary.com), and ByWater Solutions (http://bywatersolutions.com/) as well as nonprofit organizations such as Lyrasis (http://lyrasis.org), OCLC (http://oclc.org), DuraCloud (http://duracloud.org), and Omeka (http://omeka.net). These nonprofit organizations (with the exception of OCLC) support the implementation of open-source platforms (e.g., DSpace and Omeka) that might not otherwise be implementable by libraries. For libraries interested in non-library-specific application hosting, there are companies such as LisHost (http://lishost.org) that focus on providing more generalized web hosting services for libraries. Selecting one of these cloud providers sidesteps some of the budgeting and payment issues libraries are likely to encounter with other cloud providers but also limits the platforms available for cloud adoption. Whether this is an issue depends entirely on organizational factors and preferred services.

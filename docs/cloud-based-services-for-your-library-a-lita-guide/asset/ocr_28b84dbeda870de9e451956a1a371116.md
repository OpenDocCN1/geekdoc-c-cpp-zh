Figure 2.1 includes four categories of virtualized/cloud computing that were created using the NIST model. These four models (nonvirtualized, internal noncloud or private cloud virtualization, public or community cloud virtualization, and outsourced) represent a mix of deployment contexts (e.g., public, community, private) and service models (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS) and were selected because they have common real-world examples in library IT environments.

One of the challenges with this study framework was finding a suitable way to categorize cloud platforms that made sense to a large population of participants. Although the study focused on library IT personnel, the population surveyed included a mix of highly technical and nontechnical librarians. To control for this variation, the categories were informally tested with technical and nontechnical librarians and refined to be as understandable as possible. In this study framework, nonvirtualized refers to traditional library IT systems that run on dedicated hardware within the organization. In contrast, internally virtualized or private cloud refers to IT systems that are run within the organization but use a specific form of virtualization. It is important to differentiate the virtualization of multiple machines on a single hardware stack (virtualization) from a new range of software platforms that “unify server, storage and network resources into a common resource pool” (Marko 2012, 4). The study grouped external cloud computing adoption into two genres, “public/community cloud” and “outsourced.” In category testing with librarians the term outsourced, though not truly representative of the SaaS technical concept, was more representative of the form of SaaS solutions adopted by libraries and was a commonly understood concept among nontechnical librarians.

Lyrasis started its work in virtualized hosting of these services for libraries based on the results of a member survey that found both an interest in open-source software and a need from libraries for infrastructure and hosting support for these software platforms. This work, which has also grown into an open-source site registry known as FOSS4LIB (http://foss4lib.org), has been directed at supporting library adoption of open-source software and filling member demand for specific platform needs.

It is less typical for providers like Lyrasis to provide on-demand or rapidly elastic services. Instead, Lyrasis works with clients to define a customized SLA, negotiate specific access terms and application management agreements, and work with more traditional payment and subscription terms than may be available in other cloud or virtualized platforms. These techniques are much more common from community cloud providers such as Lyrasis. These benefits can be a motivating factor for libraries seeking to leverage cloud-based and virtualized resources to implement new services or manage existing services. Another important advantage of community-specific cloud and virtualization providers of open-source software is improved ability to migrate between providers. For example, FOSS4LIB has four providers listed for the open-source ILS Koha, and Evergreen has multiple providers. Libraries seeking to change providers would likely not have significant issues with data migration and service setup as libraries faced with choosing new systems in addition to new providers.

# Issues

Despite the fact that there are a wide range of SaaS platforms for libraries seeking to move to the cloud, adoption of SaaS platforms is not without its own issues. Moving large elements of IT services to SaaS solutions can lead to a fragmented IT environment in which it is difficult to make systems work well together. This can lead to loss of data but more likely to loss of service control and data ownership or access. Likewise, as libraries move toward more data-rich systems, their ability to migrate away from these solutions diminishes. Because there are few standards governing the exiting of a library from these cloud services, vendor lock-in is becoming a significant concern, particularly given the fact that a new breed of tightly integrated knowledge bases and service layers is being developed and licensed to libraries as “black boxes.” In this licensing model it is easy for libraries to lose control of the resources that are included and excluded in the subscription.

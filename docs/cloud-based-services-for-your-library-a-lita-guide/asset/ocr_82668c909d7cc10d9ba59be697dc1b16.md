These four approaches emphasize different strengths and weaknesses in cloud solutions and can serve as a starting point for libraries turning to the cloud. Approach one emphasizes the simplicity and control that comes with using a single cloud provider but recognizes the risk associated with being locked in through extensive use of provider APIs. Approach two seeks to mitigate some of this risk by using multiple providers and leveraging SaaS solutions to monitor, manage, and audit these providers. This approach illustrates the benefit of keeping track of cloud infrastructure and expenditures and suggests that SaaS solutions are a way to provide infrastructure management support. Approach three focuses on services that seek to abstract system design and implementation from IaaS providers and demonstrates the benefits of being able to use multiple cloud platforms easily; still, it is complex and likely to require IT and monetary resources beyond those of approaches one and two. Approach four recognizes that libraries may very likely turn to entirely SaaS-based solutions to address technology needs. In this case operational, administrative, and service quality issues are more important than technical issues.

In the real world, it is likely that libraries will employ more than one of these approaches. In fact, most libraries already rely to some extent on an SaaS provider or outsourced information service. In deciding whether to expand those roles or to consider other approaches when moving new services to the cloud, issues of local resources, expertise, IT support capacity, organizational policy, and service need are useful factors that can make one approach more viable than another. Regardless of the approach selected, IT service elements such as capacity planning, quality of service monitoring, service integration, and resource utilization monitoring are important elements of a sustainable IT infrastructure. The remainder of this chapter examines these issues and provides suggestions for getting started with cloud computing.

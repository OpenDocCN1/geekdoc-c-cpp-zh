# Selecting and
Implementing Solutions

In this chapter we look at technical, organizational, legal, and staffing/expertise issues in more detail and consider library-specific factors that influence adoption of cloud-based services. This examination is based on the responses of participants in the chapter 2 survey, on approaches described in the literature on cloud computing (e.g., Reese 2009; Rittinghouse and Ransome 2009), and on case studies of library adoption of cloud computing and virtualization (Fox 2009; Han 2010; Mitchell 2010).

Our survey found variation in cloud computing adoption rates and different motivating and inhibiting factors with regards to organizational issues, technical/functional requirements, and perceived benefits. This points to the likelihood that there is not a “one-size-fits-all” solution for cloud computing in libraries but rather a need to find the best fit for each institution. Although it was difficult to compare the context of cloud adoption between libraries and other information organizations, there was a clear emphasis on privately virtualized or completely outsourced (e.g., SaaS) approaches in both our survey and other research reported in chapter 2. There were common themes mentioned that contextualized this trend, including lack of IT support or local expertise and concerns over the ability to sustain complex IT systems. These concerns are significant given the fact that as libraries are adopting new information services they are often faced with choices that require distinguishing between cloud, virtualized, and traditional IT approaches.

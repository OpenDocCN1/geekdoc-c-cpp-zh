## ORGANIZATION OF MATERIAL

Chapter 1 defines virtualization and cloud computing, examines specific solutions for these platforms, and discusses some common approaches to platform adoption. This chapter examines cloud computing models and uses these models to set up the discussion in the remainder of the book.

Chapter 2 reports the results of a survey of virtualization and cloud computing adoption in libraries. The survey was conducted in the fall of 2011, and the data were analyzed using technology adoption models. The findings of this chapter are an empirical grounding for the model used to examine specific cloud computing solutions in the remainder of the book.

Chapter 3 explores factors and issues related to IT adoption in detail. The chapter is grouped by technical, organizational, and environmental factors and takes the reader through system evaluation and implementation planning. This chapter also includes a focus on the role of IT service management in system design.

Chapter 4 examines software-as-a-service (SaaS) and platform-as-a-service (PaaS) solutions that are relevant to library information systems. The chapter includes an overview of relevant technical concepts, an exploration of information systems that use SaaS, and an examination of factors that influence decision making for these services.

Like chapter 4, chapter 5 examines cloud services but focuses specifically on infrastructure-as-a-service (IaaS) and multiplatform solutions. The chapter includes an overview of relevant technical concepts, an exploration of information systems that use IaaS, and an examination of factors that influence decision making for these services.

Chapter 6 reviews the models explored in the book and the systems examined in chapters 4 and 5 and presents four generic models that can be used in system design: an entire SaaS environment, a pure IaaS environment, and two models that explore the creation of a mixed-model approach. The chapter concludes with suggestions for incorporating cloud computing and virtualization into your IT environment.

## Cloud Computing and Virtualization in Libraries

The adoption of cloud computing in libraries is part of a larger trend of library innovation that is changing how libraries allocate financial, IT, and personnel resources. This shift both is driven by and is influencing other areas of library operations, including cataloging and metadata management, digital information services, community contribution to information spaces, and digital collections management. Like all of these trends, cloud computing and virtualization can fundamentally transform not only how libraries provide services but also how they define their core and value-added functions. Just as the delivery of the Internet as a public service in libraries transformed patron views of libraries, new trends of networked bibliographic data, an increasing demand for digital reference services, and the emergence of electronic collections are again changing both the services and their delivery method. These changes are motivating libraries to find ways to make information technology more efficient, more diffused throughout library operations, and more flexible in a changing environment. Cloud computing and its parent—virtualization—are helping libraries shape their services and collections toward these goals and, as this book shows, pose unique challenges and opportunities for libraries.

The opportunities that come from these technologies are unique for libraries because libraries are themselves unique from other information institutions. Libraries balance the roles of public service and cultural preservation. They often operate under the auspices of parent organizations and often find themselves functioning within the context of local technical, budgetary, and operational

constraints. Libraries frequently license or purchase services with the sole intent of giving them away in the most efficient and customer-friendly way possible. Just as there is no one type of library, there is no single reason why libraries turn to virtual or cloud-based services. Perhaps the most common motivation comes from resource constraints or an interest in new and innovative service. Other times it comes from a desire to deliver high-quality information services that surpass the capacity of local IT infrastructure or expertise. In these approaches libraries are embracing the spirit of start-up organizations, adapting to changing business models, taking a “permanent-beta” approach to service deployment, focusing on underserved populations and opportunities, and shifting resources toward successful experiments.

Over the past twenty years library services have become increasingly tied to Internet-based technologies. Though beneficial to library patrons, the delivery of Internet and other computing resources along with traditional library services is increasingly difficult to sustain (Bertot et al. 2011). In efforts to build on IT-rich services and reach diverse populations in new ways, libraries are experimenting with social media and mobile applications (Blowers 2010), seeking new IT platforms for services (Schrier 2011), migrating traditional systems to cloud-based environments (Breeding 2011b; Fitzpatrick 2010), using cloud-based services to engage student populations (Behrend et al. 2011), and investigating new approaches to digital libraries that include network-scale information systems (Kimpton and Baker 2011).

## OVERVIEW OF CLOUD COMPUTING AND VIRTUALIZATION

Cloud computing and virtualization are complementary technologies which, although technically very different, are often seen as similar approaches to solving a common problem. This book examines both concepts because a decision to either virtualize an IT service or deploy it in a cloud environment comes down to issues of service scale, organizational factors, or institutional capacity rather than to issues of the technologies themselves.

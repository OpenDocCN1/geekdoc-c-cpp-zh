**Regions:** IaaS providers may offer services in multiple cloud regions. A cloud region should be independent both physically and virtually from other regions in a cloud provider's network but should also be accessible via high-speed private networks for data and service replication. Providers may use region replication as a method to assure a high level of uptime in their SLA.

**Security groups:** IaaS providers typically offer some form of infrastructure-based security. Some limit this to delegated user access to view, modify, or create IaaS resources; others offer a fully functional firewall service.

**Server instance:** A server instance is an operational server in an IaaS environment. It utilizes an operating system, storage resource, and CPU/memory resources. It may or may not be located on its own hardware (see discussion of public and private clouds in chapter 3).

**Snapshots:** Snapshots are common in IaaS environments and set a “point in time” backup of a server or storage resource. In the Amazon environment, snapshots are incremental, meaning that each successive snapshot stores only the changes from the previous snapshot. This speeds backup times and saves considerable storage space.

**Virtual private network (VPN):** IaaS providers may support the creation of a virtual network that limits access to servers based on IP addresses. Users may want to leverage VPN resources in their organizational environment to limit access to sensitive ports (e.g., port 22—SSH).

# USING IAAS IN A TRADITIONAL IT APPROACH

Although considering IaaS from “traditional” and “cloud-aware” perspectives may seem artificial, it provides a means to explore the functional implications of different approaches to IT service management in an IaaS environment. As with the wide range of PaaS platforms, IaaS can include a confusing array of options and certainly requires a close examination of the issues explored in chapters 2 and 3, not least of which are the staff expertise, organizational policy, and legal issues associated with moving to cloud services.

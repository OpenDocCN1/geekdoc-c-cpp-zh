# ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS

# Infrastructure, Platform, and Service Costs

The ease with which costs can be projected varies with cloud platform, but estimates often involve more than accounting for direct run-time and licensing costs. Determining direct costs for SaaS solutions and entirely local virtualized or nonvirtualized solutions may prove to be easier to calculate than for public or community PaaS and IaaS solutions, if only because the purchasing approach for private IT service and SaaS service follows capital investment or yearly subscription models. In contrast, nonprivate PaaS and IaaS solutions are often charged at per-hour or per-bit levels, and estimating the costs for each of the elements (e.g., CPU time, network traffic, storage, backups, reserved IP addresses) can affect the reliability of these projections.

# Considerations for Calculating Cost

Financial factors relevant to moving to cloud environments include a need to support ongoing instead of capital costs, an ability to pay for cloud services using the payment terms available from a given cloud provider, and the ability to plan for and support long-term costs associated with cloud computing (e.g., long-term preservation of digital resources and migration costs). The ability to pay for cloud services using a supported payment method, as it turns out, can be significant for some organizations. As of this writing, Amazon EC2 services are available only via credit card transactions, except for large subscribers. This can create problems in small organizations where a payment method might be tied to a specific individual's corporate card; it can be a deal breaker in organizations where credit card transactions are not supported at all. In these cases, alternate cloud brokers can be selected that support a common payment method.

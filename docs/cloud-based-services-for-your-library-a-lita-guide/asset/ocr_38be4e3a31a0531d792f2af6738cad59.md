Issues of expertise, local capacity, organizational policy, and desired end-user service outcomes are also heavily influencing these choices. This trend toward SaaS poses both opportunities for libraries and challenges to open-data and open-source communities in the library world. In fact, as libraries turn increasingly to cloud solutions, they are helping redefine our industry’s view of distinctive library services and shaping the design of new information systems. If nothing else, the shift to SaaS platforms is demonstrating that just about every library service can be turned into a generalized commodity whose primary purpose is to support high-quality, user-facing services. Still, issues of data ownership, resource sharing, and interoperability pose continued challenges for these communities as library IT vendors seek to offer more comprehensive service frameworks that discourage multivendor adoption.

In contrast, PaaS and IaaS solutions provide a great level of flexibility, independence, and assurance of privacy at lower cost but at the expense of more local investment and expertise. There are few ready-to-use library-specific applications in these environments. This points to a real opportunity for open-source communities, consortia, and commercial providers to expand their support for PaaS and IaaS solutions and to help libraries make use of a wider range of cloud services.

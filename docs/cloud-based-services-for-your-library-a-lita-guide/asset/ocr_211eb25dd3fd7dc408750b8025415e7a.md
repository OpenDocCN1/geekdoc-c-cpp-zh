Just as there is no one type of library, there is no single reason why libraries turn to virtual or cloud-based services. Perhaps the most common motivation comes from resource constraints or an interest in new and innovative service. Other times it comes from a desire to deliver high-quality information services that surpass the capacity of local IT infrastructure or expertise. In these approaches libraries are embracing the spirit of start-up organizations, adapting to changing business models, taking a “permanent-beta” approach to service deployment, focusing on underserved populations and opportunities, and shifting resources toward successful experiments.

Over the past twenty years library services have become increasingly tied to Internet-based technologies. Though beneficial to library patrons, the delivery of Internet and other computing resources along with traditional library services is increasingly difficult to sustain (Bertot et al. 2011). In efforts to build on IT-rich services and reach diverse populations in new ways, libraries are experimenting with social media and mobile applications (Blowers 2010), seeking new IT platforms for services (Schrier 2011), migrating traditional systems to cloud-based environments (Breeding 2011b; Fitzpatrick 2010), using cloud-based services to engage student populations (Behrend et al. 2011), and investigating new approaches to digital libraries that include network-scale information systems (Kimpton and Baker 2011).

# OVERVIEW OF CLOUD COMPUTING
AND VIRTUALIZATION

Cloud computing and virtualization are complementary technologies which, although technically very different, are often seen as similar approaches to solving a common problem. This book examines both concepts because a decision to either virtualize an IT service or deploy it in a cloud environment comes down to issues of service scale, organizational factors, or institutional capacity rather than to issues of the technologies themselves. For example, although many libraries choose to outsource link resolution services, libraries that have complex internal data needs that do not fit the data models of hosted OpenURL service providers often run this service in-house.

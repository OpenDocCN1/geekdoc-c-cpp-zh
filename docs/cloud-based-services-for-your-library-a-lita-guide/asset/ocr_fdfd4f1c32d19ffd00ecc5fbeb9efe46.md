# Service Capacity

Libraries are unique adopters of cloud computing in that their main focus is on providing information services rather than on managing information technology. Libraries also tend to adopt multiple platforms to fill the needs of their patrons and may encounter challenges when attempting to gain support for these systems within their organizations. Though virtualized and cloud solutions can help with this situation, they can also create an environment in which library IT service is fragmented onto multiple providers and platforms.

IT service organizations familiar with service management approaches such as ITIL or ITSM may have ways of measuring the impact of a selected information system on an organization's IT support capacity. Common measures include available IT infrastructure, IT staff support availability, and application administration capacity. These departments may view virtualization and cloud computing as a way to improve local capacity from the perspective of either infrastructure or IT staff time. As a library or IT department turns toward cloud or virtualized solutions that involve direct system or application management, it is important that it take a systematic approach that leverages literature on IT service management. For example, before implementing multiple IaaS or PaaS solutions, libraries should also design a service management and replication plan that accounts for staff resources, IT resources, and ongoing expenses. A core part of this consideration is workflow integration and operational overlap. In regards to service capacity, it is important to consider challenges to sharing data, compatibility issues in workflow integration, and loss of efficiency that may come when working with multiple virtualized or cloud solutions. For example, data access over an Internet-based network connection is likely to see more latency than when accessed over an internal network connection. This may not be problematic for short bursts of data, but it can be challenging when interacting with large amounts of data or when streaming audio or video materials over a cloud connection.

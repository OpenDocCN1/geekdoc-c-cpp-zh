Measuring the quality and impact of these services can be more difficult than measuring cost or ROI, but they may be better measures of an organization’s view of the value of an information service and can be helpful in guiding decision making for IT platform adoption. Measures that can be useful in quantifying service value include desired service uptime as compared to real-world availability, the opportunity cost of service downtime, absolute or relative service utilization measures (e.g., through log file analysis or Google Analytics), and an analysis of system relationships and dependencies.

Several issues in table 3.1—such as access levels, administration, accountability, responsibility boundaries, coadministration, and coaccess—likely require complex agreements in cases where the library seeks to share administrative roles with an organizational or external IT department. In addition, negotiating SLAs that are tuned toward assuring a level of support focused on end-user or public operations (e.g., evening and weekend support for research tools) as opposed to staff-focused or business operations (e.g., Monday–Friday business hours) can be key to high-quality service deployment. Finally, it is worth noting that the factors listed in table 3.1 also need to be considered within the context of the service or service component being analyzed. All IT services include infrastructure (e.g., computing hardware, operating systems, networking, power, and cooling), platform (e.g., operating system, software stacks, development, and deployment frameworks), application (e.g., an information system that fills a single or multiple roles), and service elements (e.g., an end-point resource that provides some form of information service based on the functions of the application). Depending on the type of IT system being examined, one or more of these areas will need to be considered from the perspectives discussed in table 3.1. This can make comparing a traditional nonvirtualized environment (in which each of the four technology levels may be administered by the library) with an outsourced or SaaS application difficult. The following sections explore some of these factors in more detail and can serve as a starting point for libraries considering IT alternatives.

# TECHNICAL FACTORS

Of all issues to consider when exploring cloud-based and virtualized services, technical issues are likely to take center stage. Understanding the technical work required when a library takes on virtualized and cloud computing is an important first step that can help guide decisions about capacity and long-term sustainability.

# Genres of Cloud-Based or Virtualized Information Services

Cloud computing can be grouped into categories by service type (e.g., SaaS, PaaS, IaaS, DaaS), described using specific features (e.g., subscription, metered, scalable), and used in different deployment models (e.g., public, private, community, hybrid). As our initial exploration of these relationships shows, it can be difficult to compare IT systems from a functional perspective, let alone understand the implications of choosing an IT platform along with a system. In addition, libraries turning to outsourced and hosted solutions need to be prepared to evaluate the technological foundation of those services. Therefore, having a firm understanding of the basic functions of these cloud platforms is crucial in making an informed decision.

Libraries support a wide range of information systems including ILSs, link resolvers, digital asset management systems (DAMS), websites, research guides, internal intranets, and archival information systems. Cloud computing works well for most libraries and library services, but the right balance of platform, deployment environment, and adoption goals naturally drives libraries toward different solutions. It can be difficult to understand how virtualized and cloud services support functional IT needs such as preservation-quality network storage, development and production computing platforms, and acquisition and data management needs.

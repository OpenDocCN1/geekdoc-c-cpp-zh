# The Landscape of Cloud Computing and Virtualization Adoption

Libraries are at a crossroads in IT adoption because of rapid changes in purchasing, collection development, information management, and public service expectations (Attis et al. 2012). These changes have been accompanied by changing approaches to metadata standards, authority control, and information management systems (Seikel and Steele 2011). The shift in IT environments has included the proliferation of systems for different services (e.g., digital reference, resource management, digital libraries) and an emergence of new vendor and open-source community services that are virtualized, hosted, or cloud-based (Breeding 2011). In particular, LIS vendors are redefining their services to leverage cloud computing in the “next-generation” of LIS systems. In addition to the vendor-driven shift of the ILS to the cloud, libraries are turning to cloud computing to address a wider range of information system needs.

Much of the research in library adoption of cloud computing focuses on specific uses or platforms. For example, Malpas (2011) explored the role of cloud computing in large-scale digital text collections and Schonfeld and Housewright (2010) explored the impact of “network-scale” learning and resource use on academic teaching and scholarship. Both studies found a growing reliance on network-based technologies and Internet-accessible digital text collections by both academic libraries and their constituents. Some research has focused on how to incorporate cloud computing into IT planning (Reeves 2010), and academic institutions are increasingly incorporating virtualized technologies in strategic plans (Furman University 2011).

Although it is difficult to quantify the service capacity in an IT service department, the measures and frameworks that are part of IT service models can help measure the use of applications and resources. Likewise, when applied judiciously, these IT service models can expand the strengths of an IT department, enabling a small IT staff to manage an onslaught of requests more easily. Perhaps the most important principle of these service models is the view that IT service providers set boundaries and expectations that work for both customers and service providers. Although this is often formalized in SLAs, those agreements do not necessarily need to be complex and at the least can help avoid miscommunication and mismatches in customer expectations.

# Make Room for New Services

For organizations exploring the cloud, one of the important questions is how cloud computing can help libraries support new or different services. There are several important IT and organizational issues to consider here, including service requirements, user expectations, IT platform options, cost considerations, and legal/organizational policies. Understanding where constraints and surpluses exist may help guide the discussion and help organizations understand where their energy is best allocated. For example, staffing constraints and lack of expertise may mean that libraries are tempted to turn to SaaS solutions. As the previous section points out, the use of SaaS solutions does not eliminate the need for IT service management tasks. In contrast, as libraries turn to more SaaS solutions, the need for a cross-departmental service catalog and knowledge/ticket management system increases. This need is even greater in organizations where the licensing, management, and deployment of SaaS services are distributed among multiple departments.

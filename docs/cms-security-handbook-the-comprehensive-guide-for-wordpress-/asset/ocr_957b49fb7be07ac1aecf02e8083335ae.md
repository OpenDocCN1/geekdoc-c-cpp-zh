The second portion of the certificate is information you generate on your server, which is a combination of some software and some credential information. These two combined parts make up your SSL certificate.

# Working with Your Hosting Company

If you have managed support or a VPS situation, your host more than likely handles the certificate setup for you. Check whether the host requires that you purchase certificates from it, or allows you to shop around. In many cases, hosts will allow a third party to issue a certificate. That gives you a lot of options for choosing the best certificate for your budget and needs.

# Installing an SSL Certificate Yourself

If you are not using a host, you need to generate a SSL certificate for your server. Locate the method in your server setup that allows you to generate a certificate. Figure 5-21 shows an example using WHM.

After it’s filled out, this particular form generates three different pieces of information, as shown in Figure 5-22. The resulting generated text file contains several sections. You’ll provide portions of that to the SSL certificate issuer. From this point, follow the specific directions provided by your certificate provider to complete the setup.

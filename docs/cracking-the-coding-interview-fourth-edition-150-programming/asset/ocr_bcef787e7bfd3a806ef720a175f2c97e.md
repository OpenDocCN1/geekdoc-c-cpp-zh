# Interview War Stories

# The View from the Other Side of the Front, by Peter Bailey

For the eager candidate getting ready for a big job interview, Cracking the Coding Interview is an invaluable reference, containing excellent coaching and practice material that gives you an inside edge on the interview process. However, as you go over your old data structures textbook and drill with homemade discrete math flashcards, don't make the mistake of thinking the interview as a kind of high-pressure game show – that if you just give all the right answers to the tech questions, you too can win a shiny new career (this week, on, Who Wants to be a Software Engineer)?

![](bbox=[0.235, 0.175, 0.435, 0.305])

While the technical questions on computer science obviously are very important, the most important question is not covered in this guidebook. In fact, it's often the most important question in your interviewers' minds as they grill you in that little room. Despite the questions on polymorphism and virtual machines, the question they really want answer to ...

Would I have a beer with this guy?
Don't look at me like that, I'm serious! Well, I may be embellishing a little, but hear me. The point I'm trying to make is that interviewers, especially those that you might work with, are just as anxious as you are. Nonsense, say, as a nervous young professional, checking your pants for lint while you rip you apart, laugh at your shriveled corpse, waiting for the interview team to show up in the front lobby. After all, this is the big leagues, and these guys are just waiting for you to slip up so they can rip you, and grind your dreams to dust beneath the heels of their boots.

Right? Just like pledge week, back in freshman year? Right? Hmmm
Nothing could be further from the truth. The team of developers and managers interviewing you have their own tasks and projects waiting for them, back at their own desks. Believe me, they're hoping that every interview is going to be the last one. They'd rather doing anything. There might be a batch of projects looming on their calendar, and they need more manpower if they're going to have a prayer of making their deadline. The last guy the agency sent over was a complete flake who railed about Microsoft's evil for half an hour. And the one before that couldnt code out of a wet paper bag without copying-and-paste. Sheesh, they think, is HR getting these guys? How hard can it be to hire one lousy person?

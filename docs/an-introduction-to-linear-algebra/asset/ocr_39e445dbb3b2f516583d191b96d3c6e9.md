The concept of linear independence allows us to distinguish when the given functions are “essentially” different.

**Example 8.3.** The functions $1,x,\ldots,x^{m-1}$ are linearly independent in every interval $J$. For this, $c_1+c_2x+\cdots+c_mx^{m-1}\equiv 0$ in $J$ implies that $c_1=\cdots=c_m=0$. If any $c_k$ were not zero, then the equation $c_1+c_2x+\cdots+c_mx^{m-1}=0$ could hold for at most $m-1$ values of $x$, whereas it must hold for all $x$ in $J$.

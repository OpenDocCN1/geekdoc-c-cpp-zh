PROOF. The set $\emptyset$ is an isolated point of $\mathcal{F}(U)$. Hence it is sufficient to show that $g^*$ is l.s.c on $\mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$. For $A \in \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$, there exists $a \in A$ such that $g(a) = g^*(A)$. Consider now a sequence $A_n \in \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$, and $A_n \to A$. Let $a_n \in A_n$, such that $g(a_n) = g^*(A_n)$. Let us consider a subsequence $a_{n_k}$ such that $g(a_{n_k}) \to \liminf g(a_n) = \liminf g^*(A_n)$.

We can assume that $a_{n_k} \to \bar{a}$ (otherwise we take a converging subsequence). By l.s.c we have $g(\bar{a}) \le \liminf g(a_n) = \liminf g^*(A_n)$.

Since $d(\bar{a}, A_{n_k}) \ge d(\bar{a}, A_{n_k})$, it follows that $d(\bar{a}, A_{n_k}) \to 0$. Hence $\bar{a} \in \liminf A_{n_k}$. But $A_{n_k} \to A$, hence $\liminf A_{n_k} = A$. Therefore $\bar{a} \in A$. It follows that $g^*(A) \le g(\bar{a}) \le \liminf g^*(A_n)$, and thus the property has been proven. $\square$

Lemma A.3. Let U be metric compact and $g : U \to ]-\infty, +\infty]$ l.s.c. One defines for $A \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ and $c \in ]-\infty, +\infty[$ the function

$G(A, c) = \{u \in A | g(u) \le c\}$

then G is (K) u.s.c from $\mathcal{F}(U) \times ]-\infty, +\infty]$ into $\mathcal{F}(U)$. 

Proof. Let $A_n \to A$ and $c_n \to c$, we must prove that

(A.2.4) $\limsup_{n \to \infty} G(A_n, c_n) \subset G(A, c)$. 

Let $y \in \limsup_{n \to \infty} G(A_n, c_n)$. By definition, $\liminf_{n \to \infty} d(y, G(A_n, c_n)) = 0$.

It follows that there exists $y_{n_k} \in G(A_{n_k}, c_{n_k})$ such that $y_{n_k} \to y$. Note that $y_{n_k} \in A_{n_k}$. Therefore $y \in \liminf A_{n_k} = A$. Also $g(y_{n_k}) \le c_{n_k}$ and since $g$ is l.s.c. we get $g(y) \le c$. Hence $y \in G(A, c)$. This proves (A.2.4) and the desired result. $\square$

Lemma A.4. Let U be metric compact and $g : U \to ]-\infty, +\infty]$ l.s.c. Let $g^*$ be defined by (A.2.3). We define

$G^*(A) = G(A, g^*(A))$,

then $G^*$ is a Borel map from $\mathcal{F}(U)$ to $\mathcal{F}(U)$. 

Proof. According to Lemma A.3, G is a Borel map and from Lemma A.2 $g^*$ is also a Borel map. By composition $G^*$ is also a Borel map, which proves the result. $\square$



We can also write this formula as

(15.6.43) \( u_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t f(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + c E [\exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (s - y_x^{*,S}(\tau_x^S - 0))] + \frac{E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S}{1 - E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S} [ E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t f(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + K + c(S - s) + c E [\exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (s - y_x^{*,S}(\tau_x^S - 0))] ], \)

and thus

(15.6.44) \( u_\alpha^{*,S}(S) = -(K + c(S - s)) + \frac{1}{1 - E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S} [ E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t f(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + K + c(S - s) + c E [\exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (s - y_x^{*,S}(\tau_x^S - 0))] ]. \)

So we may write

(15.6.45) \( u_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t f(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + c E [\exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (s - y_x^{*,S}(\tau_x^S - 0))] + E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (u_\alpha^{*,S}(S) + K + c(S - s)). \)

If we introduce

\( G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = u_\alpha^{*,S}(x) + cx, \)

then we get

\( G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t f(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + c(x - E [\exp - \alpha \tau_x^S y_x^{*,S}(\tau_x^S - 0)]) + E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (K + G_\alpha^{*,S}(S)). \)

We use the relation

\( x - E[\exp - \alpha \tau_x^S y_x^{*,S}(\tau_x^S - 0)] = E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t (\nu + \lambda \xi + \alpha y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt, \)

which implies

(15.6.46) \( G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t g_\alpha(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S (K + G_\alpha^{*,S}(S)), \)

in which

\( g_\alpha(x) = f(x) + c\nu + c\lambda\xi + \alpha c x; \)

(15.6.47) \( G_\alpha^{*,S}(S) = \frac{E \int_0^{\tau_x^S} \exp - \alpha t g_\alpha(y_x^{*,S}(t)) dt + K E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S}{1 - E \exp - \alpha \tau_x^S}, \)

and we recover that \( G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) \) is solution of the problem

(15.6.48) \( \alpha G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) + \alpha G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = g_\alpha(x) \quad \forall x > s \quad G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = K + G_\alpha^{*,S}(S) \quad \forall x \le s \)

### 15.6.6. OPTIMIZATION.

Can we optimize among s, S values? We know from the Q.V.I. approach that there exists a unique pair s, S which minimizes \(G_\alpha^{*,S}(x)\) for any x. We recall that it is the solution of the following algebraic system. Set

\(H_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = (G_\alpha^{*,S})'(x),\)

which is not continuous for x = s. The conditions are

(15.6.49) 
\(H_\alpha^{*,S}(s) = 0 \quad H_\alpha^{*,S}(S) = 0,\)

and the corresponding function becomes C^1. The second condition is clear from the general formula (15.6.26). Indeed S enters only through the term \(G_\alpha^{*,S}(S)\) which should be minimized. Note indeed that in the integral part of the process \(y_S^{*,S}(t)\) before the stopping time \(\tau_x^s\) does not depend on S.

The second condition is less obvious. Finding a unique s which minimizes \(G_\alpha^{*,S}(x)\) for any x does not seem obvious from formula (15.6.26). In particular it should minimizes \(G_\alpha^{*,S}(S)\). We then state the

**Proposition 15.3.** There exists a pair s, S which minimizes \(G_\alpha^{*,S}(S)\). It satisfies conditions (15.6.29) and minimizes \(G_\alpha^{*,S}(x)\) for any x.

Proof. We will proceed with direct checking, since we have explicit formulas. We note from (15.6.27) that

\(G_\alpha^{*,S}(S) = -K + \frac{E \int_0^{\tau_S^y} \exp -\alpha t g_\alpha(y_S^{*,S}(t)) dt + K}{1 - E \exp -\alpha \tau_S^y},\)

so we have to minimize the expression

\(G_\alpha^{*,S}(s) = \frac{E \int_0^{\tau_s^y} \exp -\alpha t g_\alpha(y_s^{*,S}(t)) dt + K}{1 - E \exp -\alpha \tau_s^S}.\)

We then recall that

\(z_{\alpha,s}(x) = E \int_0^{\tau_x^s} \exp -\alpha t g_\alpha(y(t)) dt,\)

where \(y(t) = x - D(t)\) is the solution of

\(Az_{\alpha,s}(x) + \alpha z_{\alpha,s}(x) = g_\alpha(x), \quad x > s \quad z_{\alpha,s}(x) = 0, \quad x \le s\)

and is given by the formula

\(z_{\alpha,s}(x) = \frac{2}{\sigma^2} \int_x^\infty \Gamma_\alpha(x - \xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta - \xi) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi,\)

and

\(E \int_0^{\tau_x^s} \exp -\alpha t g_\alpha(y_S^{*,S}(t)) dt = z_{\alpha,S}(s).\)

After rearranging the quantity to optimize becomes

\(\Theta_\alpha(s, S) = \frac{\int_s^S \Gamma_\alpha(S - \xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta - \xi) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi + K \frac{\sigma^2}{2}}{\int_0^{S-s} \Gamma_\alpha(x) dx}.\)

The algebraic equations coming from the Q.V.I., recalling the expression of $H_{\alpha,s}(x)$, see (15.5.5) and (15.5.6) amount to

$$ \int_s^S \Gamma_\alpha(S-\xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g'_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi = 0, $$

and

$$ \int_s^S \left[ \int_s^x \Gamma_\alpha(x-\xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g'_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi \right] dx + K \frac{\sigma^2}{2} = 0. $$

So what we have to do boils down to checking that in equaling to 0 the derivatives of $\Theta_\alpha(s,S)$ with respect to $s$ and $S$ we obtain indeed (15.6.30) and (15.6.31). This is a pure algebraic equivalence. It is first useful to check that

$$ \int_s^x \Gamma_\alpha(x-\xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g'_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi - \int_s^x \Gamma_\alpha(x-\xi) g_\alpha(\xi) d\xi - \beta_{2\alpha} \int_s^x \Gamma_\alpha(x-\xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi. $$

Therefore (15.6.30) and (15.6.31) become

$$ \int_s^S \Gamma_\alpha(S-\xi) g_\alpha(\xi) d\xi + \beta_{2\alpha} \int_s^S \Gamma_\alpha(S-\xi) \cdot \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi = 0, $$

and

$$ K \frac{\sigma^2}{2} - \int_s^S \left( \int_s^x \Gamma_\alpha(x-\xi) g_\alpha(\xi) d\xi \right) dx - \beta_{2\alpha} \cdot \int_s^S \left[ \int_s^x \Gamma_\alpha(x-\xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi \right] dx = 0. $$

But this last relation is equivalent to

$$ K \frac{\sigma^2}{2} - \int_s^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-s) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \int_0^{S-s} \Gamma_\alpha(y) dy + \int_s^S \Gamma_\alpha(S-\xi) \left( \int_\xi^{+\infty} \exp \beta_{2\alpha}(\eta-\xi) g_\alpha(\eta) d\eta \right) d\xi = 0. $$

If we now take the derivatives of $\Theta_\alpha(s,S)$ with respect to $s$ and $S$, we can check easily after rearrangement and combining equations that we get indeed the relations (15.6.52) and (15.6.54).

Since we recover the values which allow to solve the Q.V.I. we know that the value of $s$ is uniquely defined, and $S$ is also uniquely defined, provided we take the smallest minimum. Since we have solved the Q.V.I. $G_\alpha^{*,S}(x)$ is minimized for any $x$. This concludes the proof. $\square$

### 15.6.7. ERGODIC CASE.

We can now easily treat the ergodic case, as $\alpha \to 0$. We can check immediately that

$$ \lim_{\alpha \to 0} \alpha u_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = \lim_{\alpha \to 0} \alpha G_\alpha^{*,S}(x) = \lim_{\alpha \to 0} \alpha G_\alpha^{*,S}(s) \\ = c\nu + c\lambda\xi + \frac{K + E \int_0^\tau f(y_s(t)) dt}{E \tau_x^s} = \rho^{*,S}. $$

Moreover $G_\alpha^{s,S}(x) - G_\alpha^{s,S}(s) \to G^{s,S}(x)$ uniformly on compact sets and $G^{s,S}(x)$ is the solution of

(15.6.56) 
\(AG^{s,S}(x) + \rho^{*,S} = cv + c\lambda\xi + f(x), \quad x > s\)
\(G^{s,S}(x) = 0, \quad x \le s\)
\(0 = K + G^{s,S}(S)\)

From this equation we can verify that

(15.6.57) 
\(\rho^{*,S} = cv + c\lambda\xi + \int_s^{+\infty} f(x)m^{s,S}(x)dx\)
\(+ K\frac{\sigma^2}{2}((m^{s,S})'(S-0) - (m^{s,S})'(S+0)),\)

which is also expression (15.6.55). Minimizing $\rho^{*,S}$ with respect to $s, S$ leads to conditions

\((G^{s,S})'(S) = 0 \quad (G^{s,S})(s) = 0,\)

and we recover the Q.V.I. of the ergodic case, see section 15.5.

## MEAN-REVERTING INVENTORY CONTROL

### 16.1. INTRODUCTION

In this chapter, we consider a situation in which the control of inventories is done partly through impulse controls as in the previous chapter, and partly through a smoothing procedure called mean-reverting. The mean-reverting procedure is a given continuous feedback, which continuously increases or decreases the inventory when it is below or above a given level. This action is not optimized and its cost being fixed is omitted. The impulse control, which remains the decision, is thus a complement. We will check that the s, S policy remains valid, with naturally different values of the quantities r, s, S. The demand is a continuous diffusion, without out jumps. The case of jumps raises mathematical difficulties. The mean-reverting inventory model has been considered in [13] and [14], with different techniques. In fact, in these papers a two-band impulse control problem is considered. Unexpectedly, this situation turns out to be simpler, because one needs to solve differential equations in a bounded domain, instead of differential equations in unbounded domains as in the present case. There is also a difference in the cost function with these papers, in which a quadratic cost is considered, whereas here our traditional holding and shortage costs are considered. The quadratic cost is justified by the fact that one tries to remain close to the targeted inventory and thus one minimizes the distance to the target.

### 16.2. DESCRIPTION OF THE PROBLEM

### 16.2.1. THE MODEL

The demand process is composed of two parts: first a deterministic part with constant rate \(\nu\). We next consider a probability space \(\Omega, \mathcal{A}, P\) on which is defined a Wiener process \(w(t)\). The demand on an interval \((0, t)\) is then given by

\(D(t) = \nu t + \sigma w(t)\)

where \(\sigma\) is a positive coefficient. Let \(\mathcal{F}^t = \sigma(D(s), s \le t)\). An impulse control is a sequence

\(\theta_n, \upsilon_n\)

where \(\theta_n\) is a stopping time with respect to the filtration \(\mathcal{F}^t\) and \(\upsilon_n\) is a random variable \(\mathcal{F}^{\theta_n}\) measurable. Denoting by \(V\) an impulse control, the corresponding inventory is described by the formula

\(y_x(t; V) = x + k \left( \gamma t - \int_0^t y_x(s; V) ds \right) - D(t) + M(t; V),\)


PROOF. The set $\emptyset$ is an isolated point of $\mathcal{F}(U)$. Hence it is sufficient to show that $g^*$ is l.s.c. on $\mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$. For $A \in \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$, there exists $a \in A$ such that $g(a) = g^*(A)$. Consider now a sequence $A_n \in \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$, and $A_n \to A$. Let $a_n \in A_n$, such that $g(a_n) = g^*(A_n)$. Let us consider a subsequence $a_{n_k}$ such that $g(a_{n_k}) \to \liminf g(a_n) = \liminf g^*(A_n)$.

We can assume that $a_{n_k} \to \bar{a}$ (otherwise we take a converging subsequence). By l.s.c. we have $g(\bar{a}) \le \liminf g(a_n) = \liminf g^*(A_n)$.

Since $d(\bar{a}, A_{n_k}) \le d(\bar{a}, a_{n_k})$, it follows that $d(\bar{a}, A_{n_k}) \to 0$. Hence $\bar{a} \in \liminf A_{n_k}$. But $A_{n_k} \to A$, hence $\liminf A_{n_k} = A$. Therefore $\bar{a} \in A$. It follows that $g^*(A) \le g(\bar{a}) \le \liminf g^*(A_n)$, and thus the property has been proven. $\square$

### Lemma A.3.
Let U be metric compact and $g : U \to ]-\infty, +\infty]$ l.s.c. One defines for $A \in \mathcal{F}(U)$ and $c \in ]-\infty, +\infty[$ the function

$G(A, c) = \{u \in A | g(u) \le c\}$

then G is (K) u.s.c from $\mathcal{F}(U) \times ]-\infty, +\infty]$ into $\mathcal{F}(U)$.

Proof. Let $A_n \to A$ and $c_n \to c$, we must prove that

(A.2.4) $\limsup_{n \to \infty} G(A_n, c_n) \subset G(A, c)$.

Let $y \in \limsup_{n \to \infty} G(A_n, c_n)$. By definition, $\liminf_{n \to \infty} d(y, G(A_n, c_n)) = 0$.

It follows that there exists $y_{n_k} \in G(A_{n_k}, c_{n_k})$ such that $y_{n_k} \to y$. Note that $y_{n_k} \in A_{n_k}$. Therefore $y \in \liminf A_{n_k} = A$. Also $g(y_{n_k}) \le c_{n_k}$ and since $g$ is l.s.c. we get $g(y) \le c$. Hence $y \in G(A, c)$. This proves (A.2.4) and the desired result. $\square$

### Lemma A.4.
Let U be metric compact and $g : U \to ]-\infty, +\infty]$ l.s.c. Let $g^*$ be defined by (A.2.3). We define

$G^*(A) = G(A, g^*(A))$,

then $G^*$ is a Borel map from $\mathcal{F}(U)$ to $\mathcal{F}(U)$.

Proof. According to Lemma A.3, G is a Borel map and from Lemma A.2 $g^*$ is also a Borel map. By composition $G^*$ is also a Borel map, which proves the result. $\square$

### Lemma A.5.
Let U be metric compact. There exists a map $\sigma : \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset \to U$, which is Borel and satisfies

$\sigma(A) \in A, \forall A \in \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$.

Proof. Let us consider a sequence $g_n$ of functions which are uniformly continuous on U and bounded and separates the points of U. This means that if $u \neq v$ then there exists $g_n$ such that $g_n(u) \neq g_n(v)$. There exists such a sequence. Indeed, pick a sequence $u_k$ dense in U. Define

$F_{kn} = \left\{v \in U | d(u_k, v) \le \frac{1}{n} \right\}$.

Consider two sets $F_k$ and $F_{k'n'}$. We define a function $g_{k,k:n'}$ from $U$ to $[0, 1]$, which is continuous (hence uniformly continuous, since $U$ is compact) and satisfies:
if $F_k \cap F_{k'n'} \neq \emptyset$ then $g_{k,k:n'} = 1$
if $F_k \cap F_{k'n'} = \emptyset$ then $g_{k,k:n'}(F_k) = 0, g_{k,k:n'}(F_{k'n'}) = 1$

The set of functions $g_{k,k:n'}$ is a separating sequence. Indeed if $u \neq v$ there exists $F_{k,n}$ and $F_{k'n'}$ such that $u \in F_{k,n}, v \in F_{k'n'}$.

To the function $g_n$ we associate $g^*_n$ as in Lemma A.2 and $G^*_n$ as in Lemma A.4.

Next define the sequence $H_n : \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset \to \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$ by the formula
$H_n(A) = G^*_n(H_{n-1}(A)); H_0(A) = A$

Since $A \neq \emptyset$, one has
$A = H_0(A) \supset H_1(A) \cdots \supset H_n(A)$.

Since
$H_n(A) = \left\{ u \in H_{n-1}(A) \mid g_n(u) = \min_{a \in H_{n-1}(A)} g_n(a) \right\}$,

the sets $H_n(A)$ are not empty and compact. We can assert that
\(\cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A) \neq \emptyset.\)

Indeed, there exists a sequence $a_n \in H_n(A)$. We can extract a subsequence $a_{n_k} \to a$. Necessarily $a \in \cap^\infty_{n=0} H_{n_k}(A) = \cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A)$.

If $u, u' \in \cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A)$, then by construction
$g_n(u) = g_n(u') = g^*_n(H_{n-1}(A)),$

and since the functions $g_n$ separate the points of $U$, we have $u = u'$. Therefore $\cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A)$ contains a single point, which is denoted by $\sigma(A)$. Let us check that

(A.2.5) \(H_n(A) \to \{\sigma(A)\}.\)

Indeed, the sequence $H_n(A)$ being decreasing, we can assert that
\(u \in \cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A) \implies d(u, H_n(A)) = 0, \forall n\)

therefore

(A.2.6) \(\cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A) \subset \liminf_n H_n(A) \subset \limsup_n H_n(A).\)

On the other hand, if we consider an element \(u \in \limsup_n H_n(A)\), there exists a sequence \(u_{n_k} \in H_{n_k}(A)\) such that \(d(u, u_{n_k}) \to 0\). We have \(n_k \le n_{k+1}\), hence for fixed \(k, u_{n_k} \in H_{n_k}\). This implies \(u \in H_{n_k}\), therefore \(u \in \cap^\infty_{n=0} H_n(A)\), which with (A.2.6) implies the result (A.2.5).

Now the functions \(G^*_n\) being Borel, the functions \(H_n\) are also Borel, \(\forall n\). Therefore the function \(\nu : \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset \to \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset\) defined by \(\nu(A) = \{\sigma(A)\}\) is Borel. To conclude that $\sigma$ is Borel, we note that $\sigma = \tau^{-1} \circ \nu$, where $\tau : U \to \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$ is defined simply by $\tau(u) = \{u\}$. The map $\tau$ is point to point and continuous. Since $U$ is compact, $\tau(U)$ is also compact, hence closed in $\mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset$. The inverse is Borel, and therefore $\sigma$ is Borel, which concludes the proof.

We now prove Theorem 4.1:

PROOF. Consider the map \( F^*: X \times [-\infty, +\infty] \to \mathcal{F}(U) \) defined by
\[ F^*(x, c) = \{u \in U | F(x, u) \le c\}, \]
then \( F^* \) is (K) u.s.c. Indeed if \( x_n \to x, c_n \to c \), we must show that
\[ \limsup F^*(x_n, c_n) \subset F^*(x, c). \]
Let \( u \in \limsup F^*(x_n, c_n) \), by definition \( \liminf d(u, F^*(x_n, c_n)) = 0 \). There exists a subsequence \( u_{n_k} \in F^*(x_{n_k}, c_{n_k}) \), such that \( u_{n_k} \to u \). This implies
\[ F(x_{n_k}, u_{n_k}) \le c_{n_k}. \]
Since \( F \) is l.s.c., we obtain \( F(x, u) \le c \), hence \( u \in F^*(x, c) \). Define next
\[ F^*(x) = F^*\left(x, \inf_{u \in U} F(x, u)\right): X \to \mathcal{F}(U). \]
Since \( F^*(x, c) \) is Borel and \( x \to \inf_{u \in U} F(x, u) \) is l.s.c., the map \( F^*(x) \) is Borel. Note that
\[ F^*(x) = \left\{u \in U | F(x, u) = \inf_{v \in U} F(x, v)\right\}. \]
Moreover \( F^*(x) \neq \emptyset, \forall x \), hence \( F^*: X \to \mathcal{F}(U) - \emptyset \). The map \( \hat{v}(x) = \sigma(F^*(x)) \) is Borel from \( X \) to \( U \) and satisfies \( \hat{v}(x) \in F^*(x) \). Therefore we have
\[ F(x, \hat{v}(x)) = \inf_{v \in U} F(x, v) \forall x, \]
which completes the proof. \(\square\)

\[ G(x_n) \to \liminf G(x_n), \]

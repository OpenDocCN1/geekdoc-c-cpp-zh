# 8.1.2. ASSUMPTIONS.

We now define a cost function. Let \( l(x, v) \) satisfying (4.3.2), we set

\( l(x, v, w) = l(x + v, w) + c(w). \)

We consider only \( w \in (R_+^d) \) in accordance with the applications we have in mind. We then assume the special form

\( c(w) = K \mathbf{1}_{w \neq 0} + c_0(w) \)
\( c_0(w) \text{ is sublinear positive continuous, } c_0(0) = 0 \)
\( c_0(w) \to \infty \text{ as } |w| \to \infty \)

We note that (4.3.2) is satisfied by the function \( l(x, v, w) \).

**Remark.** Of course the important element in the structure of \( c(w) \) is the presence of \( K \), which is only there when an action is taken, \( w \neq 0 \). It is called a _fixed cost_ (in the inventory literature it is also called _set up cost_). We note also that, even when \( (x, v) \) is continuous, the function \( (x, v, w) \) will remain l.s.c and not continuous.

We consider a decision rule \( V, W \) extending the usual definition for \( V \). We associate a probability \( P^{V,W}_x \) on \( \Omega, \mathcal{A} \) for which \( y_1 = x \) a.s. We will consider the pay-off function

\( J_x(V, W) = E^{V,W}_x \left[ \sum_{n=1}^\infty \alpha^{n-1} l(y_n, v_n, w_n) \right], \)

and, as usual, we are interested in the value function

\( u(x) = \inf_{V,W} J_x(V, W). \)

We shall need a ceiling function. We cannot use the symbol \( w_0(x) \) for the ceiling function, since we have a new control \( w_n \). By taking \( w_n = 0 \) and \( v_n = v_0 \) where \( v_0 \) is a fixed element of \( U \), the space of constraints, a closed subset of a metric space. Define

\( v_0(x) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \alpha^{n-1} (\Phi^{w_n} \circ^n - I_{l_\epsilon}(x), \)

where \( l_\epsilon(x) = l(x, v) \). We make the assumption

\( \psi_0(x) < \infty, \forall x. \)

We take \( \psi_0 \) as the ceiling function. It is the same as for the control problem, without impulses.

We can now write the Bellman equation corresponding to the pay-off (8.1.3). This is the functional equation

\( u(x) = \inf \left\{ \begin{aligned} & [l(x, v, w) + \alpha \Phi^{v,w} u(x)]. \\ & v \in U \\ & w \ge 0 \end{aligned} \right\} \)

The preceding equation is equivalent to

\( u(x) = \inf_{v \in U} \left\{ \begin{aligned} & [l(x + v, w) + c(w) + \alpha \Phi^u u(x + w)] \\ & w \ge 0 \end{aligned} \right\} \)

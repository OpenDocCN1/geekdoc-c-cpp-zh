
Consider now \( x \le S \) and the following sequence
\( y_1 = x, v_1 = S - x; \)
\( y_2 = S - D_1, v_2 = D_1; \)
\( \dots \)
\( y_{n+1} = S - D_n, v_{n+1} = D_n. \)

If we discount with rate \( \alpha \) we obtain the cost
\( u_\alpha(x) = hx^+ + px^- + c(S-x) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \alpha^n [\bar{c}D + hE(S-D)^+ + pE(S-D)^-], \)

therefore
\( u_\alpha(x) = hx^+ + px^- + c(S-x) + \frac{\alpha \rho}{1 - \alpha}, \)

and
\( u_\alpha(x) - \frac{\rho}{1 - \alpha} \to hx^+ + px^- + c(S-x) - \rho. \)

Consider now the case \( x > S \). We define
\( y_{n+1} = y_n - D_n, y_1 = x. \)

Let
\( \tau = \inf \{ n \ge 1 | y_n \le S \}. \)

Starting at period \( \tau \) we are back in the situation where the stock is below \( S \) and we proceed as follows
\( y_\tau = y_\tau, v_\tau = S - y_\tau, \)
\( y_{\tau+n} = S - D_{\tau+n-1}, v_{\tau+n} = D_{\tau+n-1}. \)

The corresponding cost is given by
\( u_\alpha(x) = E \sum_{n=1}^{\tau-1} \alpha^{n-1} h y_n + \)
\( + E \alpha^{\tau-1} [-c y_\tau + c S + h y_\tau^+ + p y_\tau^- \)
\( + \sum_{n=1}^\infty E \alpha^{\tau-1+n} [c D_{\tau+n-1} + h(S - D_{\tau+n-1})^+ + p(S - D_{\tau+n-1})^-] \)

hence also by appropriate conditioning, taking into account that \( D_{\tau+n-1} \) is independent from \( \tau \), for \( n \ge 1 \)
\( u_\alpha(x) - \frac{\rho}{1 - \alpha} = E \sum_{n=1}^{\tau-1} \alpha^{n-1} (h y_n - \rho) \)
\( + E \alpha^{\tau-1} (-c y_\tau + c S - \rho + h y_\tau^+ + p y_\tau^-) \)

and letting \( \alpha \) tend to 1 we obtain
\( u_\alpha(x) - \frac{\rho}{1 - \alpha} \to E \sum_{n=1}^{\tau-1} (h y_n - \rho) \)
\( + E (-c y_\tau + c S - \rho + h y_\tau^+ + p y_\tau^-), \)

and the right hand side is the probabilistic interpretation of \( u(x) \), as defined by (6.3.8), (6.3.9).

## 6.4. DETERMINISTIC CASE

The deterministic case can be treated as a particular case of the stochastic case. However we can look directly at the formulas obtained in section 5.3. In the no shortage case we have the formulas

(6.4.1)
$$
u_\alpha(x) = \begin{cases} (h-c)x + \frac{cD}{1-\alpha}, & \forall x \le D \\ hx + \alpha u_\alpha(x-D), & \forall x \ge D \end{cases}
$$

The Base stock \(S_\alpha = D\), does not depend on \(\alpha\), so \(S = D\), and \(\rho = cD\). We obtain that

(6.4.2)
$$
u_\alpha(x) - \frac{cD}{1-\alpha} \to u(x),
$$

with \(u(x)\) solution of

$$
u(x) = \begin{cases} (h-c)x, & \forall x \le D \\ hx - cD + u(x-D), & \forall x \ge D \end{cases}
$$

Similarly in the backlog case we have the relations

$$
u_\alpha(x) = \begin{cases} (h-c)x + \frac{cD}{1-\alpha}, & \forall x \le D \\ hx + \alpha u_\alpha(x-D), & \forall x \ge D \end{cases}
$$

so \(S = D, \rho = cD\). We have again (6.4.2), with

$$
u(x) = \begin{cases} hx^+ + px^- - cx, & \forall x \le D \\ hx^+ + px^- - cD + u(x-D), & \forall x \ge D \end{cases}
$$

## OPTIMAL STOPPING PROBLEMS

We consider in this chapter a situation where decisions are stopping times. They can be reduced to the general case, but it is preferable to use the specific aspects of this type of problems. We will begin by defining the functional equation of interest, which results from Dynamic Programming, study it directly then obtain the interpretation as a control problem with stopping times as decisions.

## 7.1. DYNAMIC PROGRAMMING

We present the functional equation of Dynamic Programming, after presenting the control problem of interest.

### 7.1.1. FORMULATION OF THE PROBLEM.

We consider the framework of Chapter 4. We recall that a decision rule is an infinite sequence

\(\hat{V} = \{\hat{v}_1, \cdots, \hat{v}_n, \cdots\},\)

in which

\(\hat{v}_n = \hat{v}_n(x_1, \cdots, x_n),\)

is a Borel map with values in \(U\). From the decision rule we construct the probability \(P^{V,x}\) of the canonical stochastic process \(y_n\), given \(y_1 = x\), representing the state trajectory. The state space is \(X = \mathbb{R}^d\). We recall the filtration

\(\mathcal{Y}^n = \sigma(y_1, \cdots, y_n).\)

In the present situation, there will be a new decision variable of a different nature, namely a stopping time \(\tau\) with respect to the filtration \(\mathcal{Y}^n\).

We consider the function \(l(x, v)\) such that

(7.1.1) \(l(x, v) \ge 0; \quad \text{l.s.c.}\)

In addition we shall consider a function \(\psi(x)\) such that

(7.1.2) \(\psi(x) \ge 0; \quad \text{l.s.c.}\)

The cost objective is defined by

(7.1.3) \(J_x(V, \tau) = E^{V,x} \left[ \sum_{n=1}^{\tau-1} \alpha^{n-1} l(y_n, v_n) + \alpha^{\tau-1} \psi(y_\tau) \right]\)

### 7.1.2. DYNAMIC PROGRAMMING EQUATION.

The functional equation giving the value function of the preceding problem is defined by

(7.1.4) $$u(x) = \min \{ \psi(x), \inf_{v \in U} [l(x, v) + \alpha \Phi^v u(x)] \}, \forall x.$$

This functional equation combines the nonlinear operator studied in Chapter 4, with the function $\psi(x)$. We call this function, the obstacle. This refers to the fact that the solution $u(x)$ must remain below the obstacle. As we see in equation (7.1.4), it represents in the cost functional the payoff received at the decision time $\tau$.

We shall consider the ceiling function $w_0(x)$, defined in equation (4.3.4).

We state the main result

### Theorem 7.1. Under the assumptions (7.1.1), (7.1.2) (4.3.1), (4.3.2) and (4.3.6) then the set of solutions of the functional equation (7.1.4) satisfying

$$
0 \le u(x) \le w_0(x) \quad \forall x,
$$

is not empty and has a minimum and a maximum solution, denoted respectively $\underline{u}(x)$ and $\overline{u}(x)$. The minimum solution is l.s.c. and the maximum solution is u.s.c. if the functions $l(x, v)$ and $\psi$ are continuous.

PROOF. Let $z$ be l.s.c. and positive. Define

$$
Tz(x) = \inf_{v \in U} [l(x, v) + \alpha \Phi^v z(x)].
$$

We know from Chapter 4 that $Tz$ is also l.s.c. and positive. Set

$$
Sz(x) = \min[\psi(x), Tz(x)].
$$

We use the following exercise to claim that $S(z)$ is also l.s.c. and positive.

The function $u$ solution of equation (7.1.4) is a fixed point of the map $S$. The map $S$ is increasing, i.e. if $z_1 \le z_2$ then $Sz_1 \le Sz_2$. If $z \ge 0$ then $Sz \ge 0$. Suppose $z \le \tilde{w}$ then

$$
Sz(x) \le l(x, v) + \alpha \Phi^{w_0} w_0(x) = w_0(x).
$$

We have checked that $S$ maps the interval $[0, w_0]$ into itself. Consider next the decreasing scheme

$$
u^{n+1} = Su^n, \quad u^0 = w_0.
$$

Clearly

$$
u^0 \ge u^1 \ge \dots \ge u^n \dots \ge 0.
$$

We can state that $u^n \downarrow u$ where $u$ is a positive Borel function. Clearly $u^{n+1} \ge Su$, hence also $u \ge Su$. Also

$$
\begin{aligned} u^{n+1}(x) &\le \psi(x) \\ u^{n+1}(x) &\le l(x, v) + \alpha \Phi^v u^n(x), \quad \forall v \end{aligned}
$$

Letting $n$ tend to $+\infty$ yields

$$
\begin{aligned} u(x) &\le \psi(x) \\ u(x) &\le l(x, v) + \alpha \Phi^v u(x), \quad \forall v \end{aligned}
$$

Therefore $u \le Su$. So the limit $u$ is a fixed point of $S$. Moreover it is the maximum solution in the interval $[0, w_0]$. It is u.s.c. as decreasing limit of a sequence of continuous functions.

hence also

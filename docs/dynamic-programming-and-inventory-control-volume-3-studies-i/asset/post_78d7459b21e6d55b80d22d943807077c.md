
Hence

$$
m_n(f) \le m_{n+1}(f),\\
M_n(f) \ge M_{n+1}(f),\\
m_n(f) \le M_n(f).
$$

We have successively

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{n+1}(f) - m_{n+1}(f) &= \sup_x \Phi^{n+1} f(x) - \inf_x \Phi^{n+1} f(x)\\
&= \sup_{x, \xi} (\Phi^{n+1} f(x) - \Phi^{n+1} f(\xi))\\
&= \sup_{x, \xi} [\Phi(\Phi^n f)(x) - \Phi(\Phi^n f)(\xi)]\\
&= \sup_{x, \xi} \left[ \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta)) \Phi^n f(\eta) \mu(d\eta) \right].
\end{aligned}
$$

It follows

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{n+1}(f) - m_{n+1}(f) &\le \sup_{x, \xi} \left[ \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta))^+ \Phi^n f(\eta) \mu(d\eta) \right.\\
&\qquad \left. - \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta))^- \Phi^n f(\eta) \mu(d\eta) \right]\\
&\le \sup_{x, \xi} \left[ M_n(f) \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta))^+ \mu(d\eta) \right.\\
&\qquad \left. - m_n(f) \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta))^- \mu(d\eta) \right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
\int_X \varpi(x, \eta) \mu(d\eta) = \int_X \varpi(\xi, \eta) \mu(d\eta),
$$

the coefficients of \(M_n(f)\) and \(m_n(f)\) are the same.

So we get

$$
M_{n+1}(f) - m_{n+1}(f) \le (M_n(f) - m_n(f)) \sup_{x, \xi} \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta))^+ \mu(d\eta).
$$

Next

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta))^+ \mu(d\eta)
&= \int_X (\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta)) \mathbb{1}_{\{ \varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) \ge 0 \}} \mu(d\eta)\\
&= 1 - \int_X \varpi(x, \eta) \mathbb{1}_{\{ \varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) < 0 \}} \mu(d\eta)\\
&\qquad - \int_X \varpi(\xi, \eta) \mathbb{1}_{\{ \varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) \ge 0 \}} \mu(d\eta)\\
&\le 1 - \int_{X_0} \varpi(x, \eta) \mathbb{1}_{\{ \varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) < 0 \}} \mu(d\eta)\\
&\qquad - \int_{X_0} \varpi(\xi, \eta) \mathbb{1}_{\{ \varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) \ge 0 \}} \mu(d\eta),
\end{aligned}
$$

and from assumption (3.5.2) we deduce

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\le 1 - \delta \int_{X_0} \mathbb{1}_{\{\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) < 0\}} \mu(d\eta)\\
&\qquad - \delta \int_{X_0} \mathbb{1}_{\{\varpi(x, \eta) - \varpi(\xi, \eta) \ge 0\}} \mu(d\eta)\\
&= 1 - \delta \mu(X_0).
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore we have obtained

$$
M_{n+1}(f) - m_{n+1}(f) \le (M_n(f) - m_n(f))(1 - \delta \mu(X_0)),
$$

hence

$$
M_n(f) - m_n(f) \le (1 - \delta \mu(X_0))^{n-1} ||f||.
$$

We deduce that

$$
M_n(f) \Downarrow m(f); \quad m_n(f) \Uparrow m(f).
$$

Also we have the inequality

$$
M_n(f) - m_n(f) \ge \Phi^n f(x) - m(f) \ge m_n(f) - M_n(f).
$$

We have obtained

$$
|\Phi^n f(x) - m(f)| \le M_n(f) - m_n(f) \le (1 - \delta \mu(X_0))^{n-1} ||f||.
$$

When \(f\) has no sign, using the decomposition of \(f\) between its positive and negative part, we obtain

$$
|\Phi^n f(x) - m(f)| \le M_n(f) - m_n(f) \le 2(1 - \delta \mu(X_0))^{n-1} ||f||,
$$

which is the desired result (3.5.5) with \(\beta = 1 - \delta \mu(X_0)\).

It remains to prove that \(m(f)\) defines an invariant measure, and that such an invariant measure is unique. The map \(f \mapsto m(f)\) is linear and \(|m(f)| \le ||f||\). If \(f \ge 0\) then \(m(f) \ge 0\), and \(m(1) = 1\). So \(m(f)\) defines a probability on \(X, \mathcal{X}\). Clearly

$$
m(f) = m(\Phi f).
$$

Applying with \(f = \mathbb{1}_{\Gamma}\) yields

$$
m(\Gamma) = \int_X m(dx) \pi(x, \Gamma),
$$

which proves that \(m\) is an invariant measure. If \(\tilde{m}\) is another invariant measure, we have by definition

$$
\tilde{m}(f) = \tilde{m}(\Phi^n f) \to m(f),
$$

hence \(\tilde{m} = m\). The proof has been completed.

### 3.5.3. ANALYTIC PROBLEM.

Theorem 3.4. Under the assumptions (3.5.1), (3.5.2), there exists one and only one pair \(v \in B, \lambda \in R\) up to a constant for \(v\) such that

$$
\begin{aligned}
v - \Phi v + \lambda &= f, \quad \lambda = m(f)\\
m(v) &= 0,\\
m(v) &= m(\Phi v) = 0,\\
v(x) &= \lim_{\alpha \to 1} \left( u_\alpha - \frac{\lambda}{1 - \alpha} \right),\\
u_\alpha - \alpha \Phi u_\alpha &= f, \quad \alpha < 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

PROOF. One has also

$$
\lambda = \lim_{\alpha \to 1} (1 - \alpha) u_\alpha,
$$

and

$$
\lambda = \lim \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N \Phi^j f, \text{ in } B.
$$

From equation (3.5.6) we deduce

$$
m(v) - m(\Phi v) + \lambda = m(f).
$$

Since \(m\) is invariant, we have \(m(v) - m(\Phi v) = 0\), hence the second property (3.5.6). So \(\lambda\) is unique. If there are two solutions \(v_1, v_2\), then one has

$$
v_1 - v_2 = \Phi(v_1 - v_2).
$$

So also

$$
v_1 - v_2 = \Phi(v_1 - v_2) \Rightarrow m(v_1) = m(v_2).
$$

So \(v_1\) and \(v_2\) differ only by a constant. If we impose the condition \(m(v) = 0\), then the solution is unique. To prove existence we start with \(u_\alpha\) defined in equation (3.5.6). Moreover

$$
u_\alpha = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \alpha^n \Phi^n f.
$$

So

$$
u_\alpha - \frac{\lambda}{1 - \alpha} = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \alpha^n \Phi^n (f - \lambda).
$$

From property (3.5.5) we deduce that

$$
||\Phi^n(f - \lambda)|| \le 2 ||f|| |\beta|^{n-1}.
$$

It follows that the series defined in equation (3.5.11) is absolutely convergent in \(B\). Moreover

$$
u_\alpha - \frac{\lambda}{1 - \alpha} \to v = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \Phi^n (f - \lambda).
$$

Then \(v\) is the solution of equation (3.5.6). Finally property (3.5.8) follows from the fact that the series on the right hand side of (3.5.11) is absolutely convergent.

## 3.6. EXAMPLES

### 3.6.1. INVENTORY WITH NO BACKLOG.

We will consider the classical inventory problem with no backlog

$$
y_{n+1} = (y_n + v_n - D_n)^+,
$$

where \(D_n\) is the demand modeled as a sequence of independent identically distributed variables with density \(f\). The quantity \(v_n\) is the order, for which we assume the following policy

$$
v_n = S \mathbb{1}_{y_n=0}.
$$

So if we start with a stock which is less or equal to \(S\), the stock remains always between \(0\) and \(S\). So \(X = [0, S]\). We already see that this model defines a Markov chain on \(X\) equipped with the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra, with transition probability

$$
\pi(x, d\eta) = \begin{cases} 
\overline{F}(x)\delta(\eta) + \mathbb{1}_{0 \le \eta < x} f(x - \eta) d\eta, & \text{if } x > 0 \\
\overline{F}(S)\delta(\eta) + \mathbb{1}_{0 \le \eta < S} f(S - \eta) d\eta, & \text{if } x = 0 
\end{cases}
$$

So it is a mixture of a Dirac probability at 0 and probability density on positive inventories.

Assume \(\overline{F}(S) > 0\) then the conditions of Theorem 3.3 are satisfied. We have

$$
\mu(d\eta) = \delta(\eta) + d\eta,
$$

and

$$
\varpi(x, \eta) = \begin{cases} 
\overline{F}(x)\mathbb{1}_{\eta=0} + \mathbb{1}_{0 < \eta < x} f(x - \eta), & \text{if } x > 0 \\
\overline{F}(S)\mathbb{1}_{\eta=0} + \mathbb{1}_{0 < \eta < S} f(S - \eta), & \text{if } x = 0 
\end{cases}
$$

We can take \(X_0 = 0\) and \(\delta = \overline{F}(S)\) (this \(\delta\) should not be confused with the Dirac measure \(\delta(x)\)).

We can in fact find the invariant measure directly. It is a probability measure \(m(dx)\) on \([0, S]\), which we write as follows

$$
m(dx) = A\delta(x) + B(x)dx, \quad A + \int_0^S B(x)dx = 1.
$$

To find \(A\) and \(B(x)\) we must write the relation

$$
\int_0^S \phi(x)m(dx) = \int_0^S \phi(x)m(dx), \forall \phi.
$$

We obtain the system

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= A\overline{F}(S) + \int_0^S \overline{F}(x)B(x)dx,\\
B(\eta) &= A\overline{F}(S - \eta) + \int_\eta^S B(x)f(x - \eta)d\eta.
\end{aligned}
$$

then we have

**Theorem 3.5.** Assume \(f(x) > 0\) and continuous. The system (3.6.4) with the normalization condition (3.6.3) has a unique solution.

PROOF. We note that

$$
A = \frac{\int_0^S \overline{F}(x)B(x)dx}{F(S)}.
$$

Replacing \(A\) in the second equation and taking \(\eta = S\) yields

$$
B(S) = f(0) \frac{\int_0^S \overline{F}(x)B(x)dx}{F(S)}.
$$

Calling

$$
u(\eta) = \frac{B(\eta)}{B(S)},
$$

the second equation reduces to

$$
u(\eta) = \frac{f(S-\eta)}{f(0)} + \int_\eta^S u(x)f(x-\eta)dx.
$$

Then equation (3.6.6) has one and only one solution. Indeed the map

$$
T : u \longrightarrow \frac{f(S-\eta)}{f(0)} + \int_\eta^S u(x)f(x-\eta)dx,
$$

is a contraction on \(C([0, S])\), since

$$
\|Tu_1 - Tu_2\| \le \|u_1 - u_2\| \int_0^S f(x)dx,
$$

and

$$
\int_0^S f(x)dx < 1,
$$

and \(B(S)\) is defined by

$$
B(S) \left[ \frac{\int_0^S \overline{F}(x)u(x)dx}{F(S)} + \int_0^S u(x)dx \right] = 1.
$$

The proof has been completed. In the case of a Poisson distribution \(f(x) = \lambda \exp(-\lambda x)\), one finds that \(u(\eta) = 1\) and

$$
B(\eta) = \frac{\lambda}{1 + \lambda S}, \quad A = \frac{1}{1 + \lambda S}.
$$

### 3.6.2. INVENTORY WITH BACKLOG.

The model is

$$
y_{n+1} = y_n + v_n - D_n.
$$

So negative inventories are permitted. The orders follow an \(s, S\) policy, with \(s < S, S > 0\). Namely

$$
v_n = 0, \text{ if } y_n > s,
$$

$$
v_n = S - y_n, \text{ if } y_n \le s.
$$

Clearly if \(y_1 \le S\) then \(y_n \le S, \forall n\).

We have

$$
\begin{cases} 
\pi(x, dy) = f(S-\eta) \mathbb{1}_{\eta \le S} d\eta, & \text{if } x \le s \\
\pi(x, dy) = \mathbb{1}_{s \le x} f(x-\eta) d\eta, & \text{if } x > s 
\end{cases}
$$

We have \(X = (-\infty, S]\) and \(\mu(dy) = dy\), with

$$
\varpi(x, \eta) = \begin{cases} 
\mathbb{1}_{\eta \le s} f(S-\eta), & \text{if } x \le s \\
\mathbb{1}_{\eta \le x} f(x-\eta), & \text{if } x > s 
\end{cases}
$$

Assume \(f(x) > 0\) and continuous. Then the assumptions of Theorem 3.3 are satisfied. We take \(X_0 = (s-1, s)\) and

$$
\delta = \inf_{0 \le x \le S - s + 1} f(x).
$$

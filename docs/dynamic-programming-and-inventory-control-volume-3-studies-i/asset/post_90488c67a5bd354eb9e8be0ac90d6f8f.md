
## 16.3.4. FUNCTION $G_s(x)$

Define first

(16.3.27)
$$
Q(x) = \frac{2}{\sigma^2} \int_x^{+\infty} g(\eta) \frac{\chi(\eta)}{\chi(x)} d\eta.
$$

So the function $H_s(x)$ solution of (16.3.3) ($s$ is a fixed parameter) can be written as follows

(16.3.28)
$$
H_s(x) = \int_s^x \frac{\Phi(\eta)}{\Phi(\xi)} Q(\xi) d\xi.
$$

From the function $H_s(x)$ we define the function $G_s(x)$ by the formula

(16.3.29)
$$
G_s(x) = \begin{cases} G_s(s) + \int_s^x H_s(\xi) d\xi, & x > s \\ G_s(s), & x \le s \end{cases}
$$

with the choice

(16.3.30)
$$
G_s(s) = \frac{g(s) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} Q(s) + c(\nu - k\gamma)}{\alpha}.
$$

We then have the

### **Lemma 16.2.** The function $G_s(x)$ is $C^1$ and satisfies

$$
AG_s(x) + \alpha G_s(x) = g(x) + c(\nu - k\gamma), \quad x > s
$$

### PROOF. The fact that it is $C^1$ is a consequence of $H_s(s) = 0$. Next the equation of $H_s(x)$ (16.3.3) can be written as follows

$$
-\frac{\sigma^2}{2} H_s''(x) + \frac{d}{dx}[(\nu + k(x - \gamma)) H_s(x)] + \alpha H_s(x) = g'(x).
$$

Integrating between $s$ and $x$ and rearranging we get for $x > s$

$$
-\frac{\sigma^2}{2} G_s''(x) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} H_s'(x) + (\nu + k(x - \gamma)) G_s'(x) + \alpha G_s(x) - \alpha G_s(s) = g(x) - g(s),
$$

and from the definition of $G_s(s)$ the result follows, noting that $H'_s(s) = Q(s)$.

### **Lemma 16.3.** The function $G_s(x)$ attains its infimum for $x \ge s$.

### PROOF. From the definition of $Q(x)$ (16.3.27) and the value of $g'(x)$ we get that $Q(x) > 0$ for $x \ge 0$. For $x < 0$ we have the formula

$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{2} Q(x) \chi(x) = (-p + c(\alpha + k)) \int_x^0 \chi(\eta) d\eta + (h + c(\alpha + k)) \int_0^\infty \chi(\eta) d\eta,
$$

which is clearly an increasing function. We recall, see Lemma 16.1, that $\chi(x) \to +\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$. Therefore

$$
Q(x) \chi(x) \to -\infty, \quad \text{as } x \to -\infty
$$

It follows that there exists a unique point $x_0 < 0$ such that

$$
Q(x_0) = 0, \quad Q(x) > 0, \forall x > x_0, \quad Q(x) < 0, \forall x < x_0 < 0
$$

For $s \ge x_0$, $H_s(x) > 0$, hence $G_s(x)$ increases. Therefore its minimum is at $s$.

Assume next $s < x_0 < 0$.

We then can write

$$
AH_s(x) + (\alpha + k) H_s(x) = h + c(\alpha + k),
$$

therefore we can also write

$$
H_s(x) = \frac{h+c(\alpha+k)}{\alpha+k} + \Phi(x) \left( H_s(0) - \frac{h+c(\alpha+k)}{\alpha+k} \right), \quad \forall x \ge 0.
$$

This implies

$$
H_s(x) \to \frac{h+c(\alpha+k)}{\alpha+k}, \quad \text{as } x \to +\infty
$$

and thus

$$
G_s(x) \to +\infty, \quad \text{as } x \to +\infty
$$

Since $H'_s(s) = Q(s) < 0, (H_s)$ remains strictly negative for $x$ close to $s$, larger than $s$. Therefore $G_s(x)$ decreases for $x$ close to $s$, larger than $s$.

Since it goes to $+\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$, it attains its minimum for $x \ge s$, at points $\neq s$.

We can define $S(s)$ to be the smallest minimum of $G_s(x)$ for $x \ge s$. From the proof of Lemma 16.3 we see that

(16.3.31)
$$
S(s) = s, \forall s \ge x_0, \quad S(s) > x_0 \quad \forall s < x_0
$$

## 16.3.5. OBTAINING $s$

The point $s$ is the solution of (16.3.4) which means also

(16.3.32)
$$
0 = K + \int_s^{S(s)} H_s(\eta) d\eta.
$$

### **Proposition 16.1.** There exists a unique $s < x_0$ solution of (16.3.32).

### PROOF. We study the function

$$
\gamma(s) = \int_s^{S(s)} H_s(\eta) d\eta.
$$

Clearly

$$
\gamma(s) = 0, \quad \forall s \ge x_0.
$$

For $s < x_0$, we have, noting that $H_s(s) = H_s(S(s)) = 0$,

$$
\gamma'(s) = \int_s^{S(s)} \frac{\partial}{\partial s} H_s(\eta) d\eta
$$

and from formula (16.3.28) we get

(16.3.33)
$$
\gamma'(s) = -\frac{Q(s)}{\Phi(s)} \int_s^{S(s)} \Phi(x) dx,
$$

and since $Q(s) < 0$, we see immediately that $\gamma'(s) > 0$. The important step is to show that

(16.3.34)
$$
\liminf_{s \to -\infty} \gamma'(s) \ge \frac{p - c(\alpha + k)}{\alpha + k}.
$$

This will be done in the next Lemma. If (16.3.34) holds, then

(16.3.35)
$$
\gamma(s) \to -\infty, \quad \text{as } s \to -\infty.
$$

Since then, $\gamma(s)$ increases from $-\infty$ to $0$, as $s$ increases from $-\infty$ to $0$. Therefore there exists a unique $s < x_0$ such that $\gamma(s) = -K$. This concludes the proof.

The function $G_s(x)$ defined by (16.3.29) and (16.3.30) satisfies (16.3.1), (16.3.2). It remains to show the

### **Lemma 16.4.** The property (16.3.34) is satisfied.

### PROOF. We first notice that

$$
\gamma'(s) \ge -\frac{Q(s)}{\Phi(s)} \int_s^\infty \Phi(\eta) d\eta.
$$

Also

(16.3.36)
$$
\gamma'(s) \ge \frac{Q(s)}{\Phi(s)} \int_s^0 \Phi(\eta) d\eta \left( 1 - \frac{\int_{-\infty}^0 \Phi(\eta) d\eta}{\int_s^\infty \Phi(\eta) d\eta} \right).
$$

Since

$$
\int_s^\infty \Phi(\eta) d\eta \to +\infty, \text{ as } s \to -\infty
$$

the second term in the parenthesis goes to 0 as $s \to -\infty$. So we focus on the function

$$
Z(x) = -\frac{Q(x)}{\Phi(x)} \int_x^0 \Phi(\eta) d\eta, x \le 0
$$

We next note that

$$
Q(x) = \frac{2}{\sigma^2}(-p + c(\alpha + k)) \int_x^\infty \frac{\chi(\eta)}{\chi(x)} d\eta + \frac{2}{\sigma^2}(h + c(\alpha + k)) \int_0^{+\infty} \frac{\chi(\eta)}{\chi(x)} d\eta,
$$

and since $\chi(x) \to +\infty$, as $x \to -\infty$, the second term goes to 0 as $x \to -\infty$. Consider next the equation (16.3.22) for $\chi(x)$ for $x < 0$. We integrate between $x$ and 0 and divide by $\chi(x)$. We easily obtain

$$
\int_x^0 \frac{\chi(\eta)}{\chi(x)} d\eta = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2(\alpha+k)} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \frac{2}{(\alpha+k)\chi(x)} (\chi'(0) + \chi(0)(\nu - k\gamma)),
$$

Similarly, considering the equation for $\Phi(x)$ for $x < 0$, see (17.2.1), we integrate between $x$ and 0 and obtain

$$
\int_x^0 \frac{\Phi(\eta)}{\Phi(x)} d\eta = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2\alpha} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \frac{\nu + k(x - \gamma)}{\alpha} + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(0) - \Phi(0)(\nu - k\gamma)}{\alpha \Phi(x)},
$$

and the third term goes to 0 as $x \to -\infty$.

We are going to prove the two following properties

(16.3.37)
$$
-\frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} \to +\infty, \text{ as } x \to -\infty
$$

(16.3.38)
$$
\nu + k(x - \gamma) - \frac{\alpha^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} \ge \frac{1}{2} B(x), \forall x < \bar{x},
$$

for some $\bar{x} < 0$ and

$$
B(x) = \frac{\nu + k(x - \gamma) + \sqrt{(\nu + k(x - \gamma))^2 + 2\alpha \sigma^2}}{2}.
$$

Clearly

(16.3.39)
$$
-B(x)(\nu + k(x - \gamma)) \to \frac{\alpha \sigma^2}{2}, \text{ as } x \to -\infty.
$$

Suppose we have obtained properties (16.3.37), (16.3.38). In considering the behavior of $Z(x)$ as $x \to -\infty$, we look for the dominating term. From the considerations above, we can write

$$
Z(x) = \frac{1}{\alpha} \left[ \frac{2}{\sigma^2} \frac{p - c(\alpha + k)}{\alpha + k} \left( -\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\chi'(0) + \chi(0)(\nu - k\gamma)}{\chi(x)} \right) - \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\chi'(0) + \chi(0)(\nu - k\gamma)}{\chi(x)} \right] \left[ \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \nu + k(x - \gamma) + \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(0) - \Phi(0)(\nu - k\gamma)}{\Phi(x)} \right].
$$

In the first bracket, from (16.3.37) it is clear that the dominating term is

$$
\frac{p - c(\alpha + k)}{\alpha + k} \left( - \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} \right).
$$

In the second bracket the dominating term is not immediate. However

$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(0) - (\nu - k\gamma)}{\Phi(x)} = \frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(0) - (\nu - k\gamma)}{\chi(x)} \vartheta(x).
$$

Recall that

$$
\vartheta(x) = \exp -\frac{2}{\sigma^2} \left[ k \frac{(x - \gamma)^2}{2} + \nu x \right],
$$

then

$$
\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(0) - (\nu - k\gamma)}{\Phi(x)} = B(x) \left[ \frac{\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \Phi'(0) - (\nu - k\gamma) - \vartheta(x)(\nu + k(x - \gamma))}{\chi(x)} \right].
$$

It follows from (16.3.39), the properties of $\chi(x)$ and the value of $\vartheta(x)$ that the function in the brackets tends to 0 as $x \to -\infty$. From (16.3.38) it follows easily that the dominant term is

$$
-\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \nu + k(x - \gamma),
$$

therefore, collecting results we can assert that the dominant term in $Z(x)$ is

$$
\tilde{Z}(x) = \frac{p - c(\alpha + k)}{\alpha(\alpha + k)} \left( - \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} \right) \left( -\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \nu + k(x - \gamma) \right).
$$

Define

$$
u(x) = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2} \frac{\Phi'(x)}{\Phi(x)} + \nu + k(x - \gamma),
$$

then

(16.3.40)
$$
\tilde{Z}(x) = \frac{2}{\sigma^2} \frac{p - c(\alpha + k)}{\alpha(\alpha + k)} u(x - (\nu + k(x - \gamma))u).
$$

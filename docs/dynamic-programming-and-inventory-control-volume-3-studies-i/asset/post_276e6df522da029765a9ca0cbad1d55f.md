
### Remark 10.1.
Let \( J_{\alpha, x}(v(\cdot)) \) be the cost functional with discount \(\alpha\) for a controlled Markov chain, with control defined by a feedback \( v(\cdot) \) belonging to \( \mathcal{U}_b \). From Ergodic Theory we can claim that

(10.2.34) \[
(1 - \alpha) J_{\alpha, x}(v(\cdot)) \to \int l(\xi, v(\xi)) m_{v(\cdot)}(d\xi).
\]

Therefore we have also

(10.2.35) \[
\rho = \inf_{v(\cdot) \in \mathcal{U}_b} \lim_{n \to \infty} (1 - \alpha) J_{\alpha, x}(v(\cdot)).
\]

Let us also give the interpretation of the function \( u(x) \). Let us pick a feedback in \( \mathcal{U}_b \). Let us denote by \( y_n, v_n \) the corresponding trajectory, with

\[
y_{n+1} = (y_n + v_n - D_n)^+ \quad v_n = v(y_n); \quad y_1 = x.
\]

### Lemma 10.2.
We have the property

(10.2.36) \[
Eu(y_n) \to \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

**PROOF.** The integral is well defined because the function \( u(x) \) is continuous and the range of \( m_{v(\cdot)}(dx) \) is \( [0, M] \). In fact (10.2.36) will hold for any continuous function. By taking positive and negative parts of \( u(x) \) we may as well assume that \( u(x) \ge 0 \). Consider the sequence \( u_\epsilon(x) = \frac{u(x)}{1 + \epsilon u(x)} \). We note that \( 0 \le y_n \le \max(x, M) \). So \( Eu(y_n) < \infty \) and we can write

(10.2.37) \[
Eu(y_n) = Eu_\epsilon(y_n) + \epsilon \frac{u^2(y_n)}{1 + \epsilon u(y_n)}.
\]

and \( \frac{u^2(y_n)}{1 + \epsilon u(y_n)} \le C = \max_{\xi \in [0, \max(x, M)]} u^2(\xi) \). If the initial inventory \( x > M \), we note that

\[
\{y_{n+1} > M\} = \{D_1 + \cdots + D_n < x - M\}.
\]

Therefore

\[
P(\{y_{n+1} > M\}) = F^{(n)}(x - M) \le F^n(x - M).
\]

Now

\[
\limsup_{n \to \infty} Eu_\epsilon(y_n) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} Eu_\epsilon(y_{n+n_0}) \le \frac{1}{\epsilon} P(y_{n_0} > M) + \limsup_{n \to \infty} E I_{\{y_{n_0} \le M\}} u_\epsilon(y_{n+n_0}).
\]

From Ergodic Theory, using the fact that \( u_\epsilon(x) \) is bounded (by \( \frac{1}{\epsilon} \)) we have

\[
E I_{\{y_{n_0} \le M\}} u_\epsilon(y_{n+n_0}) \to \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx), \quad \text{as } n \to \infty.
\]

Therefore

\[
\limsup_{n \to \infty} Eu_\epsilon(y_n) \le \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx) + \frac{1}{\epsilon} P(y_{n_0} > M).
\]

Letting \( n_0 \to \infty \) we obtain

\[
\limsup_{n \to \infty} Eu_\epsilon(y_n) \le \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

On the other hand, we have also

\[
\liminf_{n \to \infty} Eu_\epsilon(y_n) = \liminf_{n \to \infty} Eu_\epsilon(y_{n+n_0}) \ge \liminf_{n \to \infty} E I_{\{y_{n_0} \le M\}} u_\epsilon(y_{n+n_0}).
\]

Therefore

\[
\liminf_{n \to \infty} E u_\epsilon(y_n) \ge \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

Hence

\[
E u_\epsilon(y_n) \to \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx),
\]

as \( n \to \infty \). Letting now \( \epsilon \to 0 \) and using Fatou's Lemma we deduce

\[
\int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx) \to \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

On the other hand, from (10.2.37), we have

\[
|Eu(y_n) - Eu_\epsilon(y_n)| \le C\epsilon,
\]

hence

\[
\limsup_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \le \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx) + C\epsilon.
\]

Letting \( \epsilon \to 0 \), we get

\[
\limsup_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \le \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

Now,

\[
\liminf_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \ge \liminf_{n \to \infty} E u_\epsilon(y_n) = \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

Letting \( \epsilon \to 0 \), and using Lebesgue's Theorem we obtain

\[
\liminf_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \ge \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx),
\]

and the result follows.

Let us consider a feedback \( v(\cdot) \in \mathcal{U}_b \) and let us consider \( y_n, v_n \) the corresponding trajectory Define

(10.2.38) \[
J_x^n(v(\cdot)) = \sum_{j=1}^n E(l(y_j, v_j)) - n\rho.
\]

We then assert

### Theorem 10.4.
We make the assumptions of Theorem 10.2. Then \( J_x^n(\hat{v}(\cdot)) \) has a limit as \( n \to \infty \) and

(10.2.39) \[
u(x) = \lim_{n \to \infty} J_x^n(\hat{v}(\cdot)) + \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx).
\]

Moreover, for \( v(\cdot) \in \mathcal{U}_b, J_x^n(v(\cdot)) \) is bounded below, and on has

(10.2.40) \[
u(x) = \inf_{v(\cdot) \in \mathcal{U}_b} \left[ \liminf_{n \to \infty} J_x^n(v(\cdot)) + \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx) \right].
\]

PROOF. From equation (10.2.29) we check easily

\[
u(x) = J_x^n(\hat{v}(\cdot)) + Eu(\hat{y}_{n+1}),
\]

and from (10.2.36) we deduce easily the property (10.2.39). Similarly, considering any feedback, which belongs to \( \mathcal{U}_b \), we can write using (10.2.31) (10.2.31)

\[
u(x) \le J_x^n(v(\cdot)) + Eu(y_{n+1}).
\]

Using again (10.2.36), we obtain that

\[
u(x) \le \liminf_{n \to \infty} J_x^n(v(\cdot)) + \int u(x) m_{v(\cdot)}(dx),
\]

and the property (10.2.40) follows.

We see that the function u(.) enters in the definition of the objective function.

## 10.2.4. PARTICULAR CASE.

We consider the particular case of an exponential distribution \( f(x) = \beta \exp -\beta x \). The solution \( \Gamma(x) \) of the renewal equation (10.2.13) is easily seen to be

(10.2.41) \[
\Gamma(x) = 1 + \beta x.
\]

Next we have

(10.2.42) \[
\mu(x) = h - (p - c + h) \exp -\beta x,
\]

and

(10.2.43) \[
g(x) = hx + \frac{p}{\beta} - \frac{p - c + h}{\beta} (1 - \exp -\beta x).
\]

We then compute, for \( x > s \)

(10.2.44) \[
H(x) = \int_s^x \Gamma(x - \xi) \mu(\xi) d\xi
= h(x - s) + \frac{\beta h}{2} (x - s)^2 - (p - c + h)(x - s) \exp -\beta s
\]

The number \( \bar{s} \) satisfies \( \mu(\bar{s})=0 \), hence

(10.2.45) \[
\exp -\beta \bar{s} = \frac{h}{p - c + h}.
\]

We thus consider \( 0 \le s \le \bar{s} \). We define \( S(s) \) by \( H'(S) = 0 \), hence

(10.2.46) \[
h + \beta h(S - s) - (p - c + h) \exp -\beta s = 0.
\]

This equation has a unique solution \( S = S(s) \), for \( s \le \bar{s} \), and one easily checks that \( S \ge s \). We next define the value of \( s \). We need a condition, see (10.2.19). Let \( S_0 = S(0) \). From (10.2.46) applied with \( s = 0 \), we obtain \( S_0 = \frac{p-c}{\beta h} \) and next, from formula (10.2.44), with \( s = 0 \), we see that \( H_0(S_0) = - \frac{(p-c)^2}{2\beta h} \) and the condition on \( K \) is

(10.2.47) \[
K < \frac{(p-c)^2}{2\beta h}.
\]

To define \( s \), we must write the equation \( H_s(S) = -K \). Using formula (10.2.44) and (10.2.46) we check that

\[
H_s(S(s)) = - \frac{[(p - c + h) \exp -\beta s - h]^2}{2\beta h},
\]

and we obtain easily the value of \( s \), by

(10.2.48) \[
\exp -\beta s = \frac{h + \sqrt{2\beta h K}}{p - c + h}.
\]

Thanks to the condition (10.2.47) the right hand side of (10.2.48) is less than one, so \( s < \bar{s} \) is well defined. Using (10.2.48) in (10.2.46), we can check the formula

(10.2.49) \[
S - s = \sqrt{\frac{2K}{\beta h}} = \sqrt{\frac{2KD}{h}}.
\]

### Remark 10.2.
If we compare with the deterministic case (10.1.3) where we had an s, S policy with \( s = D \) and \( S = D(k + 1) \), so \( S - s = D\bar{k} \). The integer \( \bar{k} \) minimizes \( K/(k+1) + hDk/2 \). Assimilating \( \bar{k} \) and \( k + 1 \) to the same real number, we obtain \( \bar{k} = \sqrt{2K/(hD)} \) and thus \( S - s = \sqrt{2KD/h} \), which is exactly (10.2.49) with of course \( D = \bar{D} \). So the ordering quantity is the same as in the deterministic equivalent, but the ordering point \( s \) is not \( D \). It takes into account the randomness, and also the penalty when some demand is not satisfied. This cannot occur in the deterministic case.

We can next obtain the number \( \rho = g(s) \). Using (10.2.48) we obtain

\[
\rho = hs + \frac{c}{\beta} + \frac{\sqrt{2\beta hK}}{\beta},
\]

and thus also

(10.2.50) \[
\rho = \frac{c}{\beta} + hS,
\]

which is also equivalent to the deterministic case, see (10.1.4). Let us recover this formula, by the probabilistic interpretation, Theorem 10.3. We will not take any feedback in \( \mathcal{U}_b \), but only those defined by an s, S policy. So consider a feedback defined by an s, S policy. We call it \( v_{s,S} \). We consider the invariant measure of the corresponding controlled Markov chain. We call it \( m_{s,S}(dx) \), see (9.2.82)

(10.2.51) \[
m_{s,S}(dx) = \frac{\exp^{-\beta s}}{1 + \beta(S - s)}\delta(x - s) + \frac{\beta \exp^{-\beta(x - s)}}{1 + \beta(S - s)} dx.
\]

Let us call \( l_{s,S}(x) = l(x, v_{s,S}(x)) \). We check easily the formula

(10.2.52) \[
l_{s,S}(x) = (K - (p - c)(S - x))\mathbb{1}_{x \le s} + pD + (h - p)x
\]

(10.2.53) \[
+ p\mathbb{1}_{x > E}(x - D)^+ + p\mathbb{1}_{x \le s} E(S - D)^+.
\]

This formula is general and is not limited to the particular case of exponential distribution. We then compute the ergodic cost

\[
J_{s,S} = \int_0^s l_{s,S}(x)m_{s,S}(dx).
\]

Making use of the property of the invariant measure, when integrating the last part of relation (10.2.52), we obtain

(10.2.54) \[
J_{s,S} = pD + (K - (p - c)S) \int_0^s m_{s,S}(dx) + (p - c) \int_0^\infty x m_{s,S}(dx) + h \int_0^\infty x m_{s,S}(dx).
\]

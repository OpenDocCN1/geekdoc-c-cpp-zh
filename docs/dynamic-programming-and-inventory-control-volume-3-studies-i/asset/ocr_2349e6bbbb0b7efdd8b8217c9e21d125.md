Therefore

$$ \liminf_{n \to \infty} E u_\epsilon(y_n) \ge \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx). $$

Hence

$$ E u_\epsilon(y_n) \to \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx), $$

as $n \to \infty$. Letting now $\epsilon \to 0$ and using Fatou's Lemma we deduce

$$ \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx) \to \int u(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx). $$

On the other hand, from (10.2.37), we have

$$ |Eu(y_n) - Eu(y_n)| \le C\epsilon, $$

hence

$$ \limsup_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \le \int u(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx) + C\epsilon. $$

Letting $\epsilon \to 0$, we get

$$ \limsup_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \le \int u(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx). $$

Now,

$$ \liminf_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \ge \liminf_{n \to \infty} E u_\epsilon(y_n) = \int u_\epsilon(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx). $$

Letting $\epsilon \to 0$, and using Lebesgue's Theorem we obtain

$$ \liminf_{n \to \infty} E u(y_n) \ge \int u(x) m_{\epsilon(\cdot)}(dx), $$

and the result follows.

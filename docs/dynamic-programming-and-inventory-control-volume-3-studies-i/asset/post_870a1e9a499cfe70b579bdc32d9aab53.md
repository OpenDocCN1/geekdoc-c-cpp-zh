
## 17.4. SOLUTION OF THE Q.V.I.

From the $a, A, B, b$ policy, we are going to construct $G^+(x), G^-(x)$ satisfying (17.2.11), (17.2.12), (17.2.13). We define

(17.4.1)
$$
G^+_{ab}(x) = G^+_{ab}(a), \forall x \le a; \quad G^+_{ab}(x) = G^+_{ab}(a) + \int_a^x H^+_{ab}(\xi) d\xi, \forall x \ge a
$$

(17.4.2)
$$
G^-_{ab}(x) = G^-_{ab}(b), \forall x \ge b; \quad G^-_{ab}(x) = G^-_{ab}(b) - \int_x^b H^-_{ab}(\xi) d\xi, \forall x \le b,
$$

with

(17.4.3)
$$
G^+_{ab}(a) = \frac{g^+(a) - c^+ \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}(H^+_{ab})'(a)}{\alpha};
$$

(17.4.4)
$$
G^-_{ab}(b) = \frac{g^-(b) + c^- \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}(H^-_{ab})'(b)}{\alpha}.
$$

We first check that

(17.4.5)
$$
G^+_{ab}(x) - G^-_{ab}(x) = (c^+ + c^-)x.
$$

Since

$(G^+_{ab})'(x) - (G^-_{ab})'(x) = H^+_{ab}(x) - H^-_{ab}(x) = c^+ + c^-$,

it is sufficient to check that

(17.4.6)
$$
G^+_{ab}(a) - G^-_{ab}(a) = (c^+ + c^-)a.
$$

Using

$$
\begin{aligned}
G^-_{ab}(a) &= G^-_{ab}(b) - \int_a^b H^-_{ab}(\xi) d\xi \\
&= G^-_{ab}(b) - \int_a^b H^+_{ab}(\xi) d\xi + (c^+ + c^-)(b - a),
\end{aligned}
$$

and formulas (17.4.3), (17.4.4) we must check the relation

$$
\begin{aligned}
g^+(a) - c^+ \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}(H^+_{ab})'(a) \\
= g^-(b) + c^- \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}(H^-_{ab})'(b) - a \int_a^b H^+_{ab}(\xi) d\xi + \alpha(c^+ + c^-)b,
\end{aligned}
$$

or

$$
-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}((H^-_{ab})'(b) - (H^+_{ab})'(a)) + \alpha \int_a^b H^+_{ab}(\xi) d\xi - (c^+ + c^-) \mu = f(b) - f(a) + \alpha c^+(b - a),
$$

which follows immediately from (17.3.6) after integrating between $a$ and $b$. The functions $G^+_{ab}(x)$ and $G^-_{ab}(x)$ satisfy (17.3.1), (17.3.2), (17.3.3). We then define

We note that

$$
\begin{aligned}
AG^+(x) + \alpha G^+(x) &= g^+(x) - c^+ \mu, & \forall a < x < b \\
G^+(x) &= G^+_{ab}(a), & \forall x \le a \\
G^+(x) &= G^-_{ab}(b) + (c^+ + c^-)x, & \forall x \ge b
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore for \( x < a \)

\( AG^+(x) + \alpha G^+(x) = g^+(a) - c^+ \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}(H_{ab}^+)'(a) \).

We use next \( (H_{ab}^+)'(a) < 0 \) and

\( g^+(x) = f(x) + \alpha c^+ x = (-p + \alpha c^+)x \)
\( \ge (-p + \alpha c^+)a = g^+(a) \)

to conclude that

\( AG^+(x) + \alpha G^+(x) \le g^+(x) - c^+ \mu, \quad \forall x < a \).

For \( x > b \), we have

\( AG^+(x) + \alpha G^+(x) = -\mu(c^+ + c^-) + \alpha(c^+ + c^-)x \)
\( + g^-(b) + c^- \mu + \frac{\sigma^2}{2}(H_{ab}^+)'(b) \).

We then use \( (H_{ab}^+)'(b) < 0 \) and

\( g^-(b) = f(b) - \alpha c^- b = (h - \alpha c^-)b \le (h - \alpha c^-)b \)
\( = g^-(x) = g^+(x) - \alpha(c^+ + c^-)x \),

to conclude also that

\( AG^+(x) + \alpha G^+(x) \le g^+(x) - c^+ \mu, \quad \forall x > b \).

Next we note that, from the choice of \( a, b \) we have

\( G_{ab}^+(a) = K^+ + \inf_{a < \eta < b} G_{ab}^+(\eta) \)

For \( x \le a \),

\( G^+(x) = G_{ab}^+(a) = K^+ + \inf_{a < \eta < b} G^+(\eta) \)
\( = K^+ + \inf_{x < \eta < b} G^+(\eta) = K^+ + \inf_{x < \eta} G^+(\eta) \).

For \( x > a \), to prove the same inequality we have to check that

(17.4.7)
$$
0 \le K^+ + \inf_{x < \eta} \int_x^\eta H_{ab}^+(\xi) d\xi.
$$

This is obvious for \( x > A \), since the integral is positive. So we may consider the situation \( a \le x \le A \). But then

\( K^+ + \inf_{x < \eta} \int_x^\eta H_{ab}^+(\xi) d\xi = K^+ + \int_x^A H_{ab}^+(\xi) d\xi \)
\( \ge K^+ + \int_A^A H_{ab}^+(\xi) d\xi = 0 \).

Therefore (17.4.7) is obtained. Considering (17.2.11) we see that the two first inequalities are satisfied. In a similar way, we check that the two first inequalities of (17.2.12) are also satisfied. Finally, considering the three intervals, \( (-\infty, a) \), \( (a, b) \), \( (b, +\infty) \) we see that the third relations in (17.4.7) and in (17.2.12) are also satisfied. Therefore we have found a solution of the Q.V.I. (17.2.11), (17.2.12), (17.2.13) and thus also of (17.2.6) through an $(a, A, B, b)$ policy.

## 17.5. COMPUTATIONAL ASPECTS

In this section we focus on computational aspects related to obtaining the four numbers $a, A, B, b$.

### 17.5.1. SIGN OF A AND B.

We know that $A < B$ and $a < 0, b > 0$ but we cannot guarantee the sign of $A, B$. It is interesting to further study this question. It also leads to simplifications in the computation of the four numbers. Consider finding $a(b)$ for fixed $b$. We have to solve $F(a(b), b) = 0$. But looking at formula (17.5.13) we see that $F(a(b))$ depends on $b$ only through $A(a, b)$. So for $b$ fixed the pair $a(b), A(a(b), b)$ is obtained via a system

(17.5.1)
$$
F(a, b) = 0, H_{\alpha}^{+}(a) = 0.
$$

In solving this system, we have different calculations depending on the sign of the solution $A$ that we look for. If we postulate $A < 0$, then the pair $a, A$ becomes the solution of the following system (recall that we omit to write $b$ which is a fixed positive parameter in this framework)

$$
K^{+} - \frac{1}{\alpha}(p - \alpha c^{+})(A - a) + \frac{1}{\alpha} \frac{p - \alpha c^{+}}{\beta_1 \beta_2} (\beta_1 - \beta_2) \frac{(\exp \beta_1(A - a) - 1)(\exp \beta_2(A - a) - 1)}{\exp \beta_1(A - a) - \exp \beta_2(A - a)} = 0
$$

$$
h - \alpha c^{+} - (p + h) \frac{\beta_1 \exp \beta_2 b - \beta_2 \exp \beta_1 b}{\beta_1 - \beta_2} = (p - \alpha c^{+}) \frac{\exp \beta_1(b - a)(\exp \beta_2(A - a) - 1) - \exp \beta_2(b - a)(\exp \beta_1(A - a) - 1)}{\exp \beta_1(A - a) - \exp \beta_2(A - a)}.
$$

Moreover this system is sequential. The first equation defines $A - a$ and is independent of $b$. The second equation defines $a$ as a function of $b$ and the value of $A - a$ obtained from the first one. If we define the function

(17.5.2)
$$
Z(x) = -x + \frac{\beta_1 - \beta_2}{\beta_1 \beta_2} \frac{(\exp \beta_1 x - 1)(\exp \beta_2 x - 1)}{\exp \beta_1 x - \exp \beta_2 x},
$$

then the first condition is equivalent to

(17.5.3)
$$
Z(A - a) = \frac{-K^{+}\alpha}{p - \alpha c^{+}}.
$$

We have the

**Lemma 17.3.3.** There exists a unique number $\varpi > 0$ such that

(17.5.4)
$$
Z(\varpi) = \frac{-K^{+}\alpha}{p - \alpha c^{+}}.
$$

Proof. We compute

$$
Z'(x) = -1 + \frac{\beta_1 - \beta_2}{\beta_1 \beta_2} \frac{\beta_2 \exp \beta_2 x (\exp \beta_1 x - 1)^2 - \beta_1 \exp \beta_1 x (\exp \beta_2 x - 1)^2}{(\exp \beta_1 x - \exp \beta_2 x)^2}
$$
$$
= \frac{[\beta_2 (\exp \beta_1 x - 1) - \beta_1 (\exp \beta_2 x - 1)]}{\beta_1 \beta_2 (\exp \beta_1 x - \exp \beta_2 x)^2} \frac{[\beta_1 \exp \beta_1 x (\exp \beta_2 x - 1) - \beta_2 \exp \beta_2 x (\exp \beta_1 x - 1)]}{\beta_1 \beta_2 (\exp \beta_1 x - \exp \beta_2 x)^2}
$$

We check easily that for $x > 0$
$$
\beta_2(\exp \beta_1 x - 1) - \beta_1(\exp \beta_2 x - 1) < 0
$$
$$
\beta_1 \exp \beta_1 x (\exp \beta_2 x - 1) - \beta_2 \exp \beta_2 x (\exp \beta_1 x - 1) < 0
$$
therefore $Z'(x) \le 0$. Moreover $Z(0) = 0$ and $Z( +\infty ) = -\infty$. This implies the result.

From (17.5.3) we can assert that
(17.5.5)
$$
A(a(b), b) - a(b) = \varpi \quad \text{if } A(a(b), b) \le 0.
$$

Going back to the second condition we can write
(17.5.6)
$$
\begin{aligned}
h - \alpha c^- - (p+h) \frac{\beta_1 \exp \beta_2 b - \beta_2 \exp \beta_1 b}{\exp \beta_1 b - \exp \beta_2 b} &= (p - \alpha c^+) \ \frac{\exp \beta_1 b (a-b) (\exp \beta_2 \varpi - 1) - \exp \beta_2 b (a-b) (\exp \beta_1 \varpi - 1)}{\exp \beta_1 \varpi - \exp \beta_2 \varpi}
\end{aligned}
$$

which defines $a$ as a function of $b$. Let us introduce the number
(17.5.7)
$$
\varphi(\varpi) = \frac{\beta_1(\exp \beta_2 \varpi - 1) - \beta_2(\exp \beta_1 \varpi - 1)}{\exp \beta_1 \varpi - \exp \beta_2 \varpi},
$$

then we have
$$
\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_1 = \frac{(\beta_1 - \beta_2)(\exp \beta_1 \varpi - 1)}{\exp \beta_1 \varpi - \exp \beta_2 \varpi}; \quad \varphi(\varpi) + \beta_2 = \frac{(\beta_1 - \beta_2)(\exp \beta_2 \varpi - 1)}{\exp \beta_1 \varpi - \exp \beta_2 \varpi},
$$

and (17.5.6) becomes after rearranging
(17.5.8)
$$
L_\varpi(b-a) = \frac{\alpha(c^+ + c^-)(\beta_2 - \beta_1) + (p+h)[\beta_2(\exp \beta_1 b - 1) - \beta_1(\exp \beta_2 b - 1)]}{p - \alpha c^+},
$$

with
(17.5.9)
$$
L_\varpi(x) = (\exp \beta_1 x - 1)(\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_2) - (\exp \beta_2 x - 1)(\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_1), \quad x \ge 0.
$$

We can also give another formulation of equation (17.5.8). Recalling the definition of $Z^+(a, b)$ we check easily that (17.5.8) is equivalent to

(17.5.10)
$$
\frac{Z^+(a, b)}{\exp \beta_1 b (a - b) - \exp \beta_2 b (a - b)} = \frac{\varphi(\varpi) - \alpha c^+}{\alpha(\beta_2 - \beta_1)}.
$$

Now, we check easily that $\varphi(0) = 0$. From (17.3.13) we see also that $a_0(b)$ is the unique solution of (17.5.10) when $\varpi = 0$. More generally, we state the

**Lemma 17.4.** For any $\varpi \ge 0$, there exists a unique $a_\varpi(b)$ solution of (17.5.10).

Proof. It is of course equivalent to consider the equation (17.5.8). The function $L_\varpi(x)$ satisfies

$$
\begin{aligned}
L'_\varpi(x) &= \beta_1(\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_2) \exp \beta_1 x - \beta_2(\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_1) \exp \beta_2 x; \\
L''_\varpi(x) &= (\beta_1)^2(\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_2) \exp \beta_1 x - (\beta_2)^2(\varphi(\varpi) + \beta_1) \exp \beta_2 x.
\end{aligned}
$$

We note that

(17.5.11)
$$
\varphi(\varpi) > 0, \quad \varphi(\varpi) + \beta_2 < 0,
$$
hence $L''_\varpi(x)$ is decreasing and
$$
L'_\varpi(0) = (\beta_1 - \beta_2)\varphi(\varpi), \quad L'_\varpi( +\infty ) = -\infty.
$$

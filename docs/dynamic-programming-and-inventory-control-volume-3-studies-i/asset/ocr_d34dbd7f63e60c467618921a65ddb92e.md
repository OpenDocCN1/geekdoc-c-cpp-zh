In other words, we can exchange the two operators \( E_{D_1} \) and inf. The reason is because the decision variables \( v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots \) are functions of all the random variables, including \( D_1 \). More specifically, we can first assert that

\( E_{D_1} \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} E_{D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots, \cdots) \le E_{D_1 D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots, \cdots) \)

where, in the right hand side, the decision variables are arbitrary. Therefore the left hand side of equation (4.27) is smaller than its right hand side. On the other hand for any \( \epsilon \) and any fixed \( D_1 \) we can find a policy \( v_2^\epsilon, \cdots, v_n^\epsilon \cdots \) depending of course of \( D_1 \) such that

\( E_{D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2^\epsilon, \cdots, v_n^\epsilon, \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots, \cdots) \le \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} E_{D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots, \cdots) + \epsilon. \)

Therefore

\( E_{D_1 D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2^\epsilon, \cdots, v_n^\epsilon, \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots, \cdots) \le E_{D_1} \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} E_{D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots) + \epsilon. \)

Therefore also

\( \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} E_{D_1 D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots, \cdots) \le E_{D_1} \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} E_{D_2 D_3 \cdots K}(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots) + \epsilon, \)

and since \( \epsilon \) is arbitrary, the right hand side of (4.27) is smaller than the left hand side. Hence the relation (4.27) holds. This relation means also that

\( Eu(g(x, v_1, D_1)) = \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} EK(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots). \)

But then

\( u(x) = \inf_v [l(x, v_1) + \alpha \inf_{v_2 \cdots v_n \cdots} EK(g(x, v_1, D_1), v_2 \cdots, v_n \cdots ; D_2 \cdots, D_n \cdots)], \)

which implies the result.

# 4.3. FUNCTIONAL EQUATION

We begin with assumptions. The space \( X = R^d \). The set of controls \( U \) is a closed subset of \( R^k \). If \( \phi(x, v) \) is a function of two arguments, we write \( \phi_v(x) = \phi(x, v) \). We shall assume that

(4.3.1) \( \Phi^* \phi_v(x) \) is uniformly continuous in \( v, x \) if \( \phi_v(x) = \phi(x, v) \) is uniformly continuous and bounded in \( x, v \).

We also assume

(4.3.2) \( l_v(x) = l(x, v) \text{ l.s.c.} \ge 0; l(x, v) \text{ is bounded on bounded sets; } \Phi^* l_v(x) \text{ is bounded on bounded sets; } \{ v \in U | l(x, v) \le L, |x| \le M \} \subset \{ |v| \le K_{LM} \}. \)

outcomes are not targeted, intended, or valued by the sponsoring organization. They are, however, powerful and individually valuable and are lasting artifacts of adventure experiences. None of these behaviors would generally be described as transfer, but they might be described as transformational turning points in one’s life and direct results of an adventure experience.

We believe that learning transfer does not provide a sufficient explanation of the potential and power of adventure programs for human growth and development. The past 25 years of theoretical and empirical work have left our field without a clear understanding of how transfer operates. Simultaneously, other ideas (transformative learning, educational turning points) have emerged that offer strong alternatives to transfer. By broadening our perspective on the lasting outcomes of adventure programming and no longer constraining ourselves to a historical alliance with the concept of learning transfer, we will be more able to understand, explain, and share the power of our programs.

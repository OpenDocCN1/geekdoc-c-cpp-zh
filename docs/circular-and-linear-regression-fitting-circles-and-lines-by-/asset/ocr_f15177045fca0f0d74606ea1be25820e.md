To avoid the trivial (and irrelevant) solution $\Theta=\mathbf{0}$ one can impose a constraint on $\Theta$. For example, one can set one of the coefficients of the polynomial to one, e.g., one can put $\theta_{1}=1$ in (6.9). Alternatively, one can require $\|\Theta\|=1$, or more generally $\Theta^{T}\mathbf{N}\Theta=1$, where $\mathbf{N}$ is some symmetric *constraint matrix*. (Note that the constraint $\theta_{1}=1$ is equivalent to $\Theta^{T}\mathbf{N}\Theta=1$ with $\mathbf{N}=[1, 0, \ldots, 0]^{T}\times[1, 0, \ldots, 0]$. The matrix $\mathbf{N}$ may be constant (as in the Kasa and Pratt fits) or data-dependent (as in the Taubin fit).

Now one arrives at a constrained minimization problem:

$\hat{\Theta}=\operatorname{argmin}\Theta^{T}\mathbf{M}\Theta,\quad\text{subject to}\quad\Theta^{T}\mathbf{N}\Theta=1.$
(6.11)

Introducing a Lagrange multiplier $\eta$, as in Section 5.5, gives

$\mathbf{M}\Theta=\eta\mathbf{N}\Theta$
(6.12)

thus $\Theta$ must be a generalized eigenvector of the matrix pair $(\mathbf{M}, \mathbf{N})$. As the parameter $\Theta$ only needs to be determined up to a scalar multiple, we can solve (6.12) subject to additional constraint $\|\Theta\|=1$.

**Gradient-weighted algebraic fit (GRAF)**. Unfortunately, for curves other than circles, the simple algebraic fit (6.8), or a modified algebraic fit (6.10) with any constraint matrix $\mathbf{N}$, has poor statistical properties, because the values $[P(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta)]^{2}$ minimized by (6.8) have little or no relevance to the geometric distances from $(x_{i}, y_{i})$ to the curve, thus the resulting estimates have little or no relevance to the MLE.

To improve the accuracy of (6.8), one usually generalizes it by introducing weights $w_{i}$ and minimizes

$\mathscr{F}_{2}(\Theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left[P(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta)\right]^{2}$
(6.13)

This modification of (6.8) is called the *weighted algebraic fit*. The weights $w_{i}=w(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta)$ may depend on the data and the parameters.

A smart way to define weights $w_{i}$ results from the linear approximation

$\frac{\left|P\left(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta\right)\right|}{\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} P\left(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta\right)\|}=d_{i}+O\left(d_{i}^{2}\right)$

where $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} P=\left(\partial P / \partial x, \partial P / \partial y\right)$ denotes the gradient vector. Thus one can construct an “approximate geometric fit,” to the leading order, by minimizing

$\mathscr{F}_{3}(\Theta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left[P\left(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta\right)\right]^{2}}{\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} P\left(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta\right)\|^{2}}.$
(6.14)

This is a particular case of the weighted algebraic fit (6.13) with weights set to

$w_{i}=\frac{1}{\left\|\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} P\left(x_{i}, y_{i}; \Theta\right)\right\|^{2}}.$
(6.15)

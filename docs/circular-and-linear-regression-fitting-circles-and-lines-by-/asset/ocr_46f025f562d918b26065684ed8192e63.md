**Theorem 3** $([73, 118, 143])$ For the Type $B$ model, the estimate $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ is asymptotically normal, and its limit distribution is described by

$\sqrt{n}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}) \rightarrow_L N\left(0, \frac{\sigma^2}{s_*}\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2}{s_*}\right)\right). \tag{2.22}$

*Its variance satisfies*

$\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\frac{\sigma^2}{n s_*}\left(1+\frac{\sigma^2}{s_*}\right)+\mathcal{O}(n^{-3/2}). \tag{2.23}$

Recall that the estimate $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ is unbiased, i.e., $\mathbb{E}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$, cf. Section 2.4.

*Remark.* In the Type $A$ model, the asymptotic normality $(2.20)$ is ensured by the assumed normal distribution of errors $\delta_i$ and $\varepsilon_i$. If errors have a different distribution, then the asymptotic normality of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$ may be lost. In that case one can still derive an asymptotic formula for $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})$ similar to $(2.21)$ assuming that the errors $\delta_i$ and $\varepsilon_i$ have finite moments of order four.

*Remark.* In the Type $B$ model, the asymptotic normality $(2.22)$ follows, as usual, from the central limit theorem, and one does not need to assume any specific distribution of errors $[73]$. The formula $(2.23)$ is derived under the assumption of normally distributed errors, but perhaps that assumption can be relaxed.

**Practical remark.** A more pragmatic question is: which approximation to $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})$, $(2.21)$ or $(2.23)$, should one use in practice, where $\sigma>0$ and $n$ is finite? Well, note that the leading terms in these formulas are identical and give a *first order approximation*:

$\operatorname{Var}_1(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\frac{\sigma^2}{S}, \tag{2.24}$

as we should obviously set $s_*=S/n$. The second order terms are almost identical; they differ only by the factor $n-1$ in $(2.21)$ versus $n$ in $(2.23)$. Thus both formulas give essentially the same result in practice, we record it as

$\operatorname{Var}_2(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\frac{\sigma^2}{S}\left(1+\frac{(n-1)\sigma^2}{S}\right). \tag{2.25}$

**Numerical test.** We verified the accuracy of the approximations $(2.24)$ and $(2.25)$ to $\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})$ numerically. We generated $10^6$ random samples of $n$ points whose true positions on a line were equally spaced on a stretch of length $L$, i.e., the distances between the points were $L/(n-1)$. The noise level $\sigma$ changed$^2$

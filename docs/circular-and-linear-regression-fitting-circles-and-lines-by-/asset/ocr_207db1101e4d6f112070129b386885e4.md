This is another classical least squares problem, and its solution is

\begin{equation}
\Delta_2\boldsymbol{\hat{\Theta}} = \left(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{F}, \tag{7.8}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{F} = (f_1, \ldots, f_n)^T$; of course this is a quadratic approximation which does not include $\varrho_B(\sigma^2)$ terms. In fact, the contribution from the first three (linear) terms in (7.7) vanishes, quite predictably; thus only the last two (quadratic) terms will matter.

Taking the mean value gives, to the leading order,

\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}(\Delta\boldsymbol{\hat{\Theta}}) = \mathbb{E}(\Delta_2\boldsymbol{\hat{\Theta}}) \equiv \frac{\sigma^2}{2R}\left[\left(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{1} + \left(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{S}\right], \tag{7.9}
\end{equation}

where $\mathbf{1} = (1, 1, \ldots, 1)^T$ and $\mathbf{S} = (s_1, \ldots, s_n)^T$, here $s_i$ is a scalar given by

$s_i = [-\tilde{v}_i, \tilde{u}_i, 0](\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W})^{-1}[-\tilde{v}_i, \tilde{u}_i, 0]^T.$

The second term in (7.9) is of order $\varrho(\sigma^2/n)$, thus the essential bias is given by the first term only:

$\mathbb{E}(\Delta\boldsymbol{\hat{\Theta}}) \stackrel{\text{ess}}{=} \frac{\sigma^2}{2R}(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W})^{-1}\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{1}.$

In fact, this expression can be simplified. Since the last column of the matrix $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W}$ coincides with the vector $\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{1}$, we have $(\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{W})^{-1}\mathbf{W}^T\mathbf{1} = [0, 0, 1]^T$; hence the essential bias of the geometric circle fit is

\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}(\Delta\boldsymbol{\hat{\Theta}}) \stackrel{\text{ess}}{=} \frac{\sigma^2}{2R} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \tag{7.10}
\end{equation}

The same result can also be obtained from the general formula (6.39). Thus the estimates of the circle center, $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{b}$, have *no essential bias*, while the estimate of the radius has essential bias

\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}(\Delta\hat{R}) \stackrel{\text{ess}}{=} \frac{\sigma^2}{2R}, \tag{7.11}
\end{equation}

which is independent of the number and location of the true points.

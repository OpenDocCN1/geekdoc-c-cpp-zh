and
\begin{equation}
\bm{P}_{\mathbf{x}_{i}} = 2( (\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i} - \bar{\bm{a}}), (\bar{\mathbf{y}}_{i} - \bar{\bm{b}}) )^{T}.
\end{equation}
By using (6.32) we can derive from (6.33) the asymptotic covariance matrix of the MLE:

\begin{equation}\label{6.35}
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}) = n_{m}^{-1} \bm{\sigma}_{m}^{2} \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{MLE}}^{*} + o(n_{m}^{-1} \bm{\sigma}_{m}^{2}).
\end{equation}

The existence of the limit in (6.34) effectively means that, as $n_{m} \to \infty$, there is a certain pattern in the distribution of the true points on the true curve.

**Finite sample variance of the MLE.** In our model, where $n$ is a constant, we would like to describe the MLE for a fixed $n$ (and fixed locations of the true points). Comparing (6.35) to (6.34) suggests that for finite sample size $n$ we have

\begin{equation}\label{6.36}
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}}) = \bm{\sigma}^{2} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{P_{\Theta_{i}} P_{\Theta_{i}}^{T}}{\|P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\|^{2}} \right)^{-1} + o(\bm{\sigma}^{2}/n),
\end{equation}
i.e., the matrix $\mathbf{V}$ for the MLE, in the notation of (6.31), is

\begin{equation}\label{6.37}
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{MLE}} = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{P_{\Theta_{i}} P_{\Theta_{i}}^{T}}{\|P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\|^{2}} \right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
This formula was indeed proved by Fuller, see Theorem 3.2.1 in [66]; it was independently derived in [42].

**Bias of the MLE.** Amemiya and Fuller also computed the asymptotic bias of the MLE, see (2.11) in [5], which in the above notation is

\begin{equation}\label{6.38}
\mathbb{E}(\hat{\Theta}_{\mathrm{MLE}} - \Theta) = \frac{\bm{\sigma}_{m}^{2}}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{n_{m}} \operatorname{tr}\left[ P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}} \left( \mathbf{I} - \frac{P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}} P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{T}}{\|P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\|^{2}} \right) \right] \frac{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{MLE}} P_{\Theta_{i}}}{\|P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\|^{2}} + o(\bm{\sigma}_{m}^{2}).
\end{equation}
Here $P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}$ is the $2 \times 2$ matrix of the second order partial derivatives of $P$ with respect to $x$ and $y$, taken at the point $\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}$. For example, in the case of fitting circles we have

\begin{equation}\label{6.39}
\bm{P}_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{i}} = 
\begin{bmatrix}
2 & 0 \\
0 & 2
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

Note that the main term in (6.38) is, in our notation, of order $\mathcal{O}(\bm{\sigma}^{2})$, i.e., it represents the *essential bias*. The remainder term $o(\bm{\sigma}_{m}^{2})$ includes, in our notation, all the $\mathcal{O}(\bm{\sigma}^{2}/n)$ and $\mathcal{O}(\bm{\sigma}^{4})$ terms, i.e., it contains the nonessential bias. Thus we can rewrite (6.38) as

\begin{equation}\label{6.39}
\mathbf{B}_{1,\mathrm{MLE}} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \operatorname{tr}\left[ P_{\mathbf{x}\mathbf{i}} \left( \mathbf{I} - \frac{P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}} P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}^{T}}{\|P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\|^{2}} \right) \right] \frac{\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{MLE}} P_{\Theta_{i}}}{\|P_{\mathbf{x}_{i}}\|^{2}}.
\end{equation}

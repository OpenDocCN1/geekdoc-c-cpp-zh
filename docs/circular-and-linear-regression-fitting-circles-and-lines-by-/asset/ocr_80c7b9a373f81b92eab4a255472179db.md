symmetric matrix, we have $\mathbf{H}_m = \mathbf{Q}_m^T \mathbf{D}_m \mathbf{Q}_m$, where $\mathbf{Q}_m$ is an orthogonal matrix and $\mathbf{D}_m = \text{diag}\{d_1, \ldots, d_{2n}\}$ is a diagonal matrix. The vector $\mathbf{E}_m = \mathbf{Q}_m \mathbf{E}$ has the same distribution as $\mathbf{E}$ does, i.e., the components of $\mathbf{E}_m$ are i.i.d. normal random variables with mean zero and variance $\sigma^2$. Thus

$\mathbf{E}^T \mathbf{H}_m \mathbf{E} = \mathbf{E}^T \mathbf{D}_m \mathbf{E}_m = \sigma^2 \sum d_i Z_i^2$, &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (6.21)

where the $Z_i$'s are i.i.d. standard normal random variables. One can also regard (6.21) as a generalization of the $\chi^2$ distribution; though it is not always positive, as we may have $d_i < 0$ for some $i$'s. In any case, the mean value of (6.21) is

$\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{E}^T \mathbf{H}_m \mathbf{E}\right) = \sigma^2 \text{tr}\mathbf{D}_m = \sigma^2 \text{tr}\mathbf{H}_m.$

Therefore, taking the mean value in (6.20) gives the bias of $\hat{\theta}_m$:

$\mathbb{E}(\Delta \hat{\theta}_m) = \frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 \text{tr}\mathbf{H}_m + \mathscr{O}(\sigma^4). &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (6.22)

We use the fact that the expectations of all third order terms vanish, because the components of $\mathbf{E}$ are independent and their first and third moments are zero; thus the remainder term is of order $\sigma^4$. In fact the expectations of all *odd* order terms vanish, because the distribution of the components of $\mathbf{E}$ is symmetric about zero.

Next, squaring (6.20) and again using (6.21) give the mean squared error

$\mathbb{E}\left( (\Delta \hat{\theta}_m)^2 \right) = \sigma^2 \mathbf{G}_m^T \mathbf{G}_m + \frac{1}{4} \sigma^4 \left( [\text{tr}\mathbf{H}_m]^2 + 2\|\mathbf{H}_m\|_F^2 \right) + \mathscr{R}. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (6.23)

Here

$\|\mathbf{H}_m\|_F^2 = \text{tr}\mathbf{H}_m^2 = \|\mathbf{D}_m\|_F^2 = \text{tr}\mathbf{D}_m^2 &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (6.24)

where $\|\cdot\|_F$ stands for the Frobenius norm (it is important to note that $\mathbf{H}_m$ is symmetric). The remainder $\mathscr{R}$ includes terms of order $\sigma^6$, as well as some terms of order $\sigma^4$ that contain third order partial derivatives, such as $\partial^3 \hat{\theta}_m / \partial x_i^3$ and $\partial^3 \hat{\theta}_m / \partial x_i^2 \partial x_j$. A similar expression can be derived for $\mathbb{E}\left( \Delta \hat{\theta}_m \Delta \hat{\theta}_{m'} \right)$ for $m \neq m'$, we omit it and only give the final formula below.

Comparing (6.22) and (6.23) we conclude that

$\text{Var}\left( \hat{\theta}_m \right) = \sigma^2 \mathbf{G}_m^T \mathbf{G}_m + \frac{1}{2} \sigma^4 \|\mathbf{H}_m\|_F^2 + \mathscr{R}. &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (6.25)

Thus, the two forth order terms in the MSE expansion (6.23) have different origins: the first one, $\frac{1}{4} \sigma^4 [\text{tr}\mathbf{H}_m]^2$, comes from the bias, and the second one, $\frac{1}{2} \sigma^4 \|\mathbf{H}_m\|_F^2$, comes from the variance.

**Covariances.** The above formulas can be easily extended to the cross product terms:

$\mathbb{E}\left( \left(\Delta \hat{\theta}_m \right)\left(\Delta \hat{\theta}_{m'} \right) \right) = \sigma^2 \mathbf{G}_m^T \mathbf{G}_{m'} + \frac{1}{4} \sigma^4 \left( [\text{tr}\mathbf{H}_m]\left(\text{tr}\mathbf{H}_{m'}\right) + 2\langle \mathbf{H}_m, \mathbf{H}_{m'} \rangle_F \right] + \mathscr{R}, &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp; (6.26)$

and

```
$$\mathbf{L}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}-R \sin \varphi_{1} & R \cos \varphi_{1} & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ -R \sin \varphi_{n} & R \cos \varphi_{n} & 0\end{array}\right].$$
```

As usual, \(\mathbf{I}_{n}\) denotes the identity matrix of order \(n\). Next we apply a standard block matrix inversion lemma (see, e.g., page 26 of [128]) we obtain

```
$$\left[\begin{array}{cc}\mathbf{K} & \mathbf{L}^{T} \\ \mathbf{L} & R^{2} \mathbf{I}_{n}\end{array}\right]^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\left(\mathbf{K}-R^{-2} \mathbf{L}^{T} \mathbf{L}\right)^{-1} & -\left(R^{2} \mathbf{K}-\mathbf{L}^{T} \mathbf{L}\right)^{-1} \mathbf{L}^{T} \\ -\mathbf{L}\left(R^{2} \mathbf{K}-\mathbf{L}^{T} \mathbf{L}\right)^{-1} & \left(R^{2} \mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{L K}^{-1} \mathbf{L}^{T}\right)^{-1}\end{array}\right].$$
```

One can easily verify this formula by direct multiplication. The \(3 \times 3\) top left block of the last matrix is the most interesting to us as it corresponds to the principal parameters \(a,b,R\). In terms of the matrix \(\mathbf{W}\) introduced earlier in (7.4) we have \(\hat{u}_{i}=\cos \varphi_{i}\) and \(\hat{v}_{i}=\sin \varphi_{i}\), hence

```
$$\mathbf{K}-R^{-2} \mathbf{L}^{T} \mathbf{L}=\mathbf{W}^{T} \mathbf{W},$$
```

thus the Cramer-Rao bound reads

```
$$\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\Delta \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)\left(\Delta \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\right)^{T}\right] \geq \sigma^{2}\left(\mathbf{W}^{T} \mathbf{W}\right)^{-1}.\tag{7.13}$$
```

This result is known to the statistics community, though it is not clear who was the first to derive it. It was apparently known to Chan in 1965, see page 53 of [31], though he did not state it explicitly. In an explicit form, it was published by Chan and Thomas [35] in 1995, but their argument was somewhat flawed. A correct argument was given in 1998 by Kanatani [96] and, independently, in 2006 by Zelniker and Clarkson [196].

**The unbiasedness restriction.** A few comments are in order regarding the practical relevance of the above result. The Cramer-Rao theorem only establishes the minimal variance for *unbiased* estimators. In the case of fitting nonlinear curves to data, there is no such luxury as unbiased estimators; in particular *all* circle fitting algorithms are biased to some extent (sometimes heavily); see Section 6.10. Hence it is not immediately clear if the bound (7.13) has any practical value, i.e., if it applies to any circle fit.

It actually does, as we explained in Section 6.9, if one restricts the analysis to the leading terms (of order \(\sigma^{2}\)). The right-hand side of (7.13) gives the lower bound for the *leading term* of the covariance matrix, i.e.,

```
$$\mathbf{V} \geq\left(\mathbf{W}^{T} \mathbf{W}\right)^{-1}.\tag{7.14}$$
```

This is true for any geometrically consistent circle fit, i.e., any fit satisfying (6.19). Thus (7.14) holds for the geometric circle fit and all known algebraic circle fits.

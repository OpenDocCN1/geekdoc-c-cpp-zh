The conditions \(\bar{x}=\bar{y}=0\) and (5.73) hold easily. Now we see that

Hence the center of the best fitting circle lies below the x axis, while every algebraic circle fit returns a center on the opposite side (above the x axis).

This example demonstrates that in nearly singular cases one cannot blindly rely on algebraic circle fits to initialize a geometric fitting routine. For safety, one should check whether the “algebraic” circle abides by the rules (a) and (b), see above. If it does not, one should change the sign of \(b\), for example, before feeding it to the geometric fitting procedure.

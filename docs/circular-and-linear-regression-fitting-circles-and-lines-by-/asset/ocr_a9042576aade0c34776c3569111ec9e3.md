Thus far we discussed two sides of the the circle fitting problem: its theoretical analysis (Chapter 3) and its practical solutions (Chapters 4 and 5). In a sense, the theoretical analysis of Chapter 3 was “pre-computational,” it prepared us for practical solutions. Chapters 4 and 5 were “computational.” Now we turn to the third, and last (“post-computational”) side of the problem: assessment of the accuracy of the circle fits. This brings us to statistical analysis of circle fits.

In this chapter we survey basic statistical properties of curve fitting algorithms when both variables are subject to errors. This is a fairly advanced topic in modern statistics, so we assume the reader is familiar with the basic concepts of probability and statistics. Our discussion need not be restricted to lines and circles anymore, it applies to general curves; though for illustrations we still use lines and circles.

**Gauss-Jordan Elimination.** The Gauss-Jordan transformation is similar to the Gauss transformation, simply adding to \(G_{x,k}\) the transvections corresponding to the rows of index \(i<k\); this has the effect of eliminating all coefficients, above and below the pivot. If in addition each row is divided by its pivot, this leads to the so called reduced echelon or Gauss-Jordan form. It is a normal form: for every equivalence class, there is a unique such reduced echelon form.

**Definition.** A matrix is in reduced echelon form if:

+   - all zero rows are at the bottom;
+   - the leading coefficient of every non-zero row, called a pivot, is a 1 and is to the right of the pivot of the row above;
+   - pivots are the only non-zero elements in their column.

**Theorem.** For every \(m\times n\) matrix \(A\) over a field, there is a unique \(m\times n\) matrix \(R\) in reduced echelon form and an invertible \(m\times m\) matrix \(U\) such that \(UA=R\).

# ## 12.1 Algebraic Equations

An algebraic equation is an equation of the form $p(x) = 0$, where $p$ is a polynomial in one variable with coefficients in an integral domain $A$. We say that an element $α ∈ A$ is a *root* of the polynomial $p$ if $p(α) = 0$.

Let $α$ be an element of $A$. Polynomial long division of $p$ by $x − α$ yields a constant polynomial $r$ such that

$p = (x − α)q + r.$

Upon evaluating this equation at $x = α$, we get $r = p(α)$. So the polynomial $x − α$ divides $p$ if and only if $α$ is a root of $p$. This remark leads to the notion of *multiplicity* of a root $α$ of the polynomial $p$: it is the largest integer $m$ such that $(x − α)^m$ divides $p$. We note that the sum of the multiplicities of the roots of $p$ is less than or equal to the degree of $p$.

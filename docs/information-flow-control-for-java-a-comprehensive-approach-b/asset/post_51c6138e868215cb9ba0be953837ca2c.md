When programs contain statements in procedures, Sinha et al. [SHR01] found that interprocedural control dependences with all control dependences do not suffice to guarantee semantic preservation during slicing. Based on Podorski and Clarke's [PC90], they define *interprocedural control dependences* to capture the effects of non-terminating procedure code. They prove that these guarantee semantic preservation, their approach captures context-insensitive slicing. They propose a second algorithm that is practically feasible as it relies on method inining for recursive methods. Their approaches are claimed to extend we control dependence, without any proof.

Exception Handling Sinha and Harrold [SH00] take a different approach for modeling exception handling in Java: They add interprocedural control dependences from throw statements to possible catch blocks in methods in the call graph. However, this approach does not handle implicit exceptions and requires non-standard interprocedural control dependence edges, which may render context-sensitive slicing with summary edges. Allen and Horowitz [AH03] show that this problem may lead to incorrect slices for certain programs, where the call chain is greater than one. Sinha and Harrold ignore data dependences to exceptions, rendering their approach unsound.

Allen and Horowitz [AH03] model try, *throw* and catch statements in analysis to jump statements of traditional language [BH93, CF94] as synthetic predicates. In our approach, possibly calling statements as predicates that introduce control dependences on their successors, as these depend on whether the method terminates normally or abnormally, however, there may be many successors, each type of exception that may be thrown. Their modeling of interprocedural dependences for the return or throw values is non-standard, as they insist on having a control dependence path from a throw statement to all vertices whose execution may be affected. They need many edges between parameter-in nodes and non-parameter nodes. In contrast, our approach handles these values with traditional interprocedural data dependences (parameter-out edges); this induces the same transitive dependencies with standard techniques.

## Extensions for Object-Oriented Languages While programing and dependence graphs are relatively old, extensions for object-oriented features have not yet reached the maturity of procedural analysis. One early approach to object-oriented slicing was the work by Larsen and Harroll [LH96] This approach represents fields of objects as extra (scalar) parameters and merges all fields of different objects. This results in a conservative approximation, as the approach is not object-sensitive. As already proposed by Malay [MKM94], individual dependencies and dependences, are only interesting for executable slices. Several others proposed Java implementations based on Larsen and Harroll's Kovač et al. [KMG96] based their implementation of a Java slicer that represents, with a slight adaptation for Java. Zhao [Zh00] also bases on his approach or insufficient. Eventually Liang [LH98] pointed out that Larsen's approach is insufficient: As an object passed as a parameter to another object's methods or by using another object's field cannot be represented. Apart from that, its model of polymorphic choice at invocation sites may give incorrect results. It remains unclear how the approaches based on Larsen's work circumvent these defects.

Tonelli [TAFM97] proposed to use the flow-insensitive points to analyze the runtime types of an object but represent an object as a single vertex when one is used as a parameter. Thus his approach is field-insensitive and lacks precision.

Liang and Harold [LH98] correct several flaws and impression of previous approaches. They introduce SDG, and can full object-sensitivity in their (polymorphic) objects that are passed as parameters or used as members in other objects. They soundly represent polymorphism with their parameters and inheritance. They represent objects as tree structures, however, they limiting their depth to a fixed number and it remains unclear how dependences depend to deeper levels are achieved. This problem inspired this work, which gives a precise condition for unrolling these trees together with a soundness. Liang and Harold discuss a language without exceptions, our work also integrates exception handling. Liang and Harold do not report an implementation of their approach.

Walkshaw [WRW03] implemented a SDG generator for sequential Java using the SOOT framework. Threads, exceptions and uncontrolled flow are not yet represented. Like our approach, it object sensitive for field depen dences, no algorithm to compute the object trees was given. In addition to our SDG, his graph contains membership dependencies and inheritance dependences as proposed in [MMKM94]. For non-executable slices, the latter are not necessary as they do not increase the precision of the slice. Thus in our SDG membership and inheritance dependences are omitted.

Nanda's approach [NR03] models fields of objects as extra parameter nodes escape nodes. The concept is similar to our approach but they build no nodes which makes it harder to find the dependence between a field and the containing object.

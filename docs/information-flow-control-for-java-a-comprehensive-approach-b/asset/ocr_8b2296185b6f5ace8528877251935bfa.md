Again, we define the functions GRef(m) = {t | (m,t) \in GRef} and GMod(m) = {t | (m,t) \in GMod}. CRef and GRef are mutually recursive, and the minimal fixed point gives the desired solution. It is again sufficient to consider representatives of the ~ relation in GRef and GMod definitions, as this fixed point is guaranteed to exist.

Considering our running example, Figure 2.17 shows the first iteration of the GRef and GMod computation: GMod \supseteq {(B.init,this), (B.init,this,a), (B.init,this,x)} and GRef \supseteq {(foo(this),this), (foo(this),this,a), (foo(this),this,x)}.

After the second iteration, CRef CMod contains the locations depicted in Figure 2.15: {(main(i), foo(b), b.a), (main(i), newB(b), b.x)} and CRef \supseteq {(main(i), foo(l), b.a), (main(i), (main), b.x)}. Since bound_main,foo is constantly empty (for main only having args as formal parameters), the fixed point iteration ends here.

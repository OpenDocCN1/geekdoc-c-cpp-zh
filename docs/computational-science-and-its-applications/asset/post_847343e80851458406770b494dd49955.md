
+   - $U_{ad} := \mathbb{L}_2^{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}(I,U)$, which is a Hilbert space of all functions in $\mathbb{L}_2(I\times\Omega,U)$ that are $\{\mathcal{F}_t\}_{t\ge0}$-adapted and endowed with the norm,
+   
+   $$\|u\|_{U_{ad}}^2 = E\left(\int_0^{\tilde{T}} \|u(t)\|_U^2 dt\right).$$

+   - $\mathbb{L}_2(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_0,X)$, which is the Hilbert space of all $\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable square integrable random variables with values in $X$.

+   - $\mathbb{L}_2(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_T,X)$ is a closed subspace of $\mathbb{L}_2(\Omega,X)$ consisting of all $\mathcal{F}_T$-measurable square integrable random variables with values in $X$.

Similar to the conventional controllability concept, the controllability of the stochastic fractional dynamical system is defined as follows:

The set of all states attainable from $x_0$ in time $t>0$ is given by the set

$$\mathscr{A}_T(x_0) = \{x(t;x_0,u): u \in U_{ad}\}.$$

Definition 18.1. [7] The stochastic fractional dynamical system is said to be completely controllable on the interval $I$ if for every $x_1 \in \mathbb{L}_2(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_T,X)$, there exists a control $u \in U_{ad}$ such that the solution $x(t)$ satisfies $x(T)=x_1$.

In other words,

$$\mathscr{A}_T(x_0)=\mathbb{L}_2(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_T,X).$$

## 18.3 Controllability Result for Stochastic Fractional Dynamical Systems

In this section we obtain the controllability result for the stochastic fractional dynamical system given in Equation (18.3). Let us further assume the following:

+   (H3) The operator $B:U \to X$ is a bounded linear operator satisfying $\|B\|_{L(U,X)}^2 \le K_1$.

+   (H4) The linear operator $\mathscr{V}_T:U_{ad} \to X$, 
+   
+   $$\mathscr{V}_T u = \frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)} \int_0^{\tilde{T}} (\tilde{T}-s)^{\alpha-1} B u(s) ds$$
+   
+   induces an invertible operator $\mathscr{V}_T^{-1}$ defined on $U_{ad} / \ker(\mathscr{V}_T)$ and there exists a positive constant $K$ such that $\|\mathscr{V}_T^{-1}\|^2 \le K$.

+   (H5) There exists $r>0$ such that
+   
+   $$\frac{4\tilde{T}^{2\alpha-1}}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2(2\alpha-1)}\left(1+5K_1K\frac{T^{2\alpha+1}}{(2\alpha-1)}\right)$$
+   
+   $((6N\tilde{T}+4N)(r+||x_0||^2)+2\tilde{T}sup_{t\in I}||f(t,0)||^2+2sup_{t\in I}||\sigma(t,0)||^2)\le r$

(H6) Let

$$\rho_1 = \frac{5\tilde{T}^{2\alpha-1}}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2(2\alpha-1)}\left(\frac{\tilde{T}^{2\alpha}K K_1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}+1\right)(L_1\tilde{T}r+N\tilde{T}+L_2+L_3),$$

be such that $0 < \rho_1 < 1$.

### Theorem 18.1. If the hypotheses (H1)–(H6) are satisfied then the stochastic fractional dynamical system is completely controllable.

Proof. Let $x_1$ be an arbitrary random variable in $L_2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}_{\tilde{T}}, X)$. Define the operator $\Lambda$ on $L_2^{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega, C(I,X))$ by

$$\Lambda x(t)=x_0+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}A(s,x(s))x(s)ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}Bu(s)ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,x(s))ds+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,x(s))dW(s).$$

The invertibility of $\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{T}}$ in (H4) allows us to choose the control process $u$ as

$$u(t)=\mathcal{B}_{\tilde{T}}^{-1}\left(x_1-x_0-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^{\tilde{T}}(\tilde{T}-s)^{\alpha-1}A(s,x(s))x(s)ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^{\tilde{T}}(\tilde{T}-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,x(s))ds-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^{\tilde{T}}(\tilde{T}-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,x(s))dW(s)\right).$$

The control $u$ steers the nonlinear system from the initial state $x_0$ to $x_1$ at time $\tilde{T}$, provided we can obtain a fixed point of the nonlinear operator $\Lambda$. First we show that $\Lambda$ maps functions in $L_2^{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega,C(I,X))$ having values in $Ba$ into itself. Consider

$$\|\Lambda x(t)-x_0\|^2 \le 4\left(\frac{1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left\|\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}A(s,x(s))x(s)ds\right\|^2+\frac{1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left\|\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}Bu(s)ds\right\|^2+\frac{1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left\|\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1}f(s,x(s))ds\right\|^2\right)$$

$$+ \frac{1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left\|\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1} \sigma(s,x(s))\mathrm{d}W(s)\right\|^2\right)$$

$$\le \frac{4}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left[\left(\sup_{t\in I}\|A(t,x(t))\|_{L(X,X)}^2 \sup_{t\in I}\|x(t)\|_X^2 + \sup_{t\in I}\|f(t,x(t))\|^2 + T^{-1}\int_0^t \|B\|_{L(U,X)}^2 \|u(s)\|_U^2 \mathrm{d}s\right]\frac{\tilde T^{2\alpha}}{(2\alpha-1)} + \left\|\int_0^t (t-s)^{\alpha-1} \sigma(s,x(s))\mathrm{d}W(s)\right\|^2\right).$$

Therefore using Itô isometry and hypotheses (H1), (H2), and (H3) we have

$$\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\|\Lambda x(t)-x_0\|^2 \le \frac{4}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left[\left(\mathrm{EC}_1\sup_{t\in I}\|x(t)\|_X^2 + 2\mathrm{EL}_2\sup_{t\in I}\|x(t)\|^2 + 2\sup_{t\in I}\|f(t,0)\|^2 + T^{-1}K_1\mathbf{E}\int_0^{\tilde T}\|u(s)\|_U^2 \mathrm{d}s\right]\frac{\tilde T^{2\alpha}}{(2\alpha-1)} + \frac{\tilde T^{2\alpha-1}}{(2\alpha-1)}\left[2\mathrm{EL}_3\sup_{t\in I}\|x(t)\|_X^2 + 2\sup_{t\in I}\|\sigma(t,0)\|^2\right]\right).$$

Using the definition of stopping time, Equation (18.5), and the fact that $\|x(t)-x_0\|_X^2 \le r$ we obtain

$$\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\|\Lambda x(t)-x_0\|^2 \le \frac{4}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\left(\frac{\tilde T^{2\alpha}}{(2\alpha-1)}\left[6N\left(r+\|x_0\|^2\right)+2\sup_{t\in I}\|f(t,0)\|^2 + K_1\mathbf{E}\int_0^{\tilde T}\|u(s)\|_U^2 \mathrm{d}s\right]+\frac{\tilde T^{2\alpha-1}}{(2\alpha-1)}\left[4N(r+\|x_0\|^2)+2\sup_{t\in I}\|\sigma(t,0)\|^2\right]\right)(5\tilde T K+1). \tag{18.6}$$

Also we have

$$\mathbf{E}\int_0^{\tilde T}\|u(t)\|_U^2 \mathrm{d}t \le 5\tilde T K\left(\|x_1\|_X^2+\|x_0\|_X^2+\left[(6N\tilde T+4N)(r+\|x_0\|^2) +2\tilde T\sup_{t\in I}\|f(t,0)\|^2+2\sup_{t\in I}\|\sigma(t,0)\|^2\right]\frac{\tilde T^{2\alpha-1}}{(2\alpha-1)}\right). \tag{18.7}$$

From the above inequalities, Equations (18.6) and (18.7), and (H5) it follows that

$$\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\|\Lambda x(t)-x_0\|_X^2\leq \frac{4\widetilde T^{2\alpha-1}}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2(2\alpha-1)}\left(1+5K_1K\frac{T^{2\alpha+1}}{2\alpha-1}\right)\left((6N\widetilde T+4N)(r+\|x_0\|^2)+2\widetilde T\sup_{t\in I}\|f(t,0)\|^2+2\sup_{t\in I}\|\sigma(t,0)\|^2\right)\leq r.$$

Thus $\Lambda$ maps functions in $L_2(\Omega,C(I,X))$ having values in $\mathrm{Ba}$ into itself. Now for $x_1,x_2\in L_2(\Omega,C(I,X))$ having values in $\mathrm{Ba}$, we have

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\|\Lambda x_1(t)-\Lambda x_2(t)\|_X^2
&=\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\Bigg\|\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}[A(s,x_1(s))x_1(s)-A(s,x_2(s))x_1(s)]\,ds\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}[A(s,x_2(s))x_1(s)-A(s,x_2(s))x_2(s)]\,ds\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}B\widetilde W_{\widetilde T}^{\alpha-1}\left[\int_0^T(T-s)^{\alpha-1}[A(s,x_1(s))x_2(s)-A(s,x_1(s))x_1(s)]\,ds\right.\\
&\qquad+\int_0^T(T-s)^{\alpha-1}[f(s,x_2(s))-f(s,x_1(s))]ds\\
&\qquad+\int_0^T(T-s)^{\alpha-1}[\sigma(s,x_2(s))-\sigma(s,x_1(s))]dW(s)\right]\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}[f(s,x_1(s))-f(s,x_2(s))]ds\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}[\sigma(s,x_1(s))-\sigma(s,x_2(s))]dW(s)\Bigg\|_X^2\\
&\leq \frac{5\widetilde T^{2\alpha-1}(L_1\widetilde T\|x_1\|_X^2+N\widetilde T+L_2+L_3)}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2(2\alpha-1)}\left(\frac{\widetilde T^{2\alpha}KK_1}{(\Gamma(\alpha))^2}+1\right)\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\|x_1(t)-x_2(t)\|_X^2\\
&\leq \rho_1\mathbf{E}\sup_{t\in I}\|x_1(t)-x_2(t)\|_X^2.
\end{aligned}$$

By (H6) we conclude that $\Lambda$ is a contraction mapping and hence there exists a unique fixed point $x\in L_2(\Omega,C(I,X))$ having value in $\mathrm{Ba}$ for $\Lambda$. Therefore the system in Equation (18.3) is completely controllable.

## 18.4 Stochastic Dynamical Systems

Now we consider the special case of stochastic quasilinear evolution system for $\alpha=1$ with additive cylindrical Wiener noise of the form

$$dx(t)+A(t,x(t))x(t)dt=f(t,x(t))dt+Bu(t)dt+\Phi dW(t),\quad t\in J=[0,T],$$
$$x(0)=x_0,$$
$$\tag{18.8}$$

where $A(t,x)$ is the infinitesimal generator of a $C_0$-semigroup in $X$ and $\Phi$ is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator from $H$ to $X$. Let $Y$ be a separable Hilbert space densely and continuously embedded in $X$, i.e., there is a constant $c>0$ such that $\|\cdot\|_X\le c\|\cdot\|_Y$. We recall some definitions and known facts from Pazy [18].

### Definition 18.2. Let $A$ be a linear operator in $X$ and let $Y$ be a subspace of $X$. The operator $\widetilde A$ defined by $D(\widetilde A)=\{x\in D(A)\cap Y:Ax\in Y\}$ and $\widetilde Ax=Ax$ for $x\in D(\widetilde A)$ is called the part of $A$ in $Y$.

### Definition 18.3. Let $\mathrm{Ba}$ be a subset of $X$ and for every $t\in J$ and $b\in\mathrm{Ba}$, let $A(t,b)$ be an infinitesimal generator of a $C_0$-semigroup $S_{t,b}(s),s\in J$ on $X$. The family of operators $A=\{A(t,b)\}_{(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{Ba}}$ is said to be stable if there are $M\ge1$ and $\beta$ such that

$$\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda+A(t_j,b_j))^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal L(X)}\le M(\lambda-\beta)^{-k},\quad \lambda>\beta,$$
$$\tag{18.9}$$

for every finite sequence $0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_k\le T$, $b_j\in\mathrm{Ba}$; $1\le j\le k$. The pair $(M,\beta)$ are called stability index for $\{A(t)\}$. In (18.9), the operator product is time-ordered, i.e., if $t_i>t_j$, then the operator $A(t_j)$ will be on the left of the operator $A(t_i)$.

### Definition 18.4. Let $S_{t,b}(s),s\in J$, be the $C_0$-semigroup generated by $A(t,b),(t,b)\in J\times\mathbb B$. A subspace $Y$ of $X$ is called $A(t,b)$-admissible if $Y$ is invariant subspace of $S_{t,b}(s)$ and the restriction of $S_{t,b}(s)$ to $Y$ is a $C_0$-semigroup in $Y$. Here we make the following assumptions.

+   (H7) For every $(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{Ba}$, $A(t,b)$ is the infinitesimal generator of a $C_0$-semigroup $S_{t,b}(s), s\in J$ on $X$. The family $\{A(t,b)\},(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{Ba}$ is stable with stability constants $(M,\beta)$.
+   
+   (H8) $Y$ is $A(t,b)$-admissible for $(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{Ba}$ and the family $\{\widetilde A(t,b)\},(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{Ba}$ of parts $\widetilde A(t,b)$ of $A(t,b)$ in $Y$, is stable in $Y$.
+   
+   (H9) For $(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{Ba}$, $D(A(t,b))\supset Y$, $A(t,b)$ is a bounded linear operator from $Y$ to $X$ with $C_2>0$ such that $\|A(t,b)\|^2_{\mathcal L(Y,X)}\le C_2$ and $t\mapsto A(t,b)$ is continuous in the $\mathcal L(Y,X)$ norm for every $b\in\mathbb B$. There exists a constant $L_3>0$ such that

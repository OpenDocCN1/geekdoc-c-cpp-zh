for each $t\in[0,I)$. We assume that the mapping $\mathcal{A}_t$ is a homeomorphic function, i.e., $\mathcal{A}_t\in C^0(\hat{\Omega})$ is invertible with a continuous inverse $\mathcal{A}_t^{-1}\in C^0(\overline{\Omega(t)})$. Furthermore, we assume that the mapping

$t\rightarrow X(Y,t),\quad Y\in\hat{\Omega}$

is differentiable almost everywhere in $t\in[0,I)$. Let us consider a scalar function $v$ on the Eulerian frame. Then, we have

$\hat v:=v\circ\mathcal{A}_t,\quad \hat v:\hat\Omega\times(0,I)\to\mathbb{R},\quad \hat v(Y,t)=v(\mathcal{A}_t(Y,t)),$

which is the corresponding function on the ALE frame. Furthermore, the time derivative of $v$ as seen from ALE frame (but expressed in Eulerian coordinates) is defined by

$\left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_{\hat\Omega}:\Omega(t)\times(0,I)\to\mathbb{R},\quad \left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_{\hat\Omega}(X,t)=\frac{\partial\hat v}{\partial t}(Y,t),\quad Y=\mathcal{A}_t^{-1}(X)$

Here, $\left.\right|_{\hat\Omega}$ is used to indicate that the time derivative is as seen from ALE frame. Further, the time derivative as seen from Eulerian frame is indicated by $\left.\right|_X$. The domain velocity $\mathbf{w}$ is defined by

$\mathbf{w}(X,t)=\left.\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}\right|_{\hat\Omega}.$

Now, we apply the chain rule to the time derivative of $v\circ\mathcal{A}_t$ on the ALE frame and obtain

$\left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_{\hat\Omega}=\left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_X+\left.\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}\right|_{\hat\Omega}\cdot\nabla_Xv=\left.\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right|_X+\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla_Xv,$ (10.9)

where $\nabla_X$ denotes the gradient with respect to the Eulerian coordinate. Using the above relation in Equation (10.9) in the momentum equation in Equation (10.5), we get

$\left.\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}\right|_X+\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{u},p)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}}\mathbf{e}.$ (10.10)

Note that the domain velocity term is the only difference between Equations (10.5) and (10.10).

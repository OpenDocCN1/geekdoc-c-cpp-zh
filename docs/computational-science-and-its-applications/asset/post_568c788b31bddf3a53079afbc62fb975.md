## 18.1 Introduction

Quasilinear partial differential equations such as symmetric hyperbolic systems of the first order, wave equations, the Korteweg–de Vries equation, Navier–Stokes, and Euler equations, equations for compressible fluids, magnetohydrodynamic equations, coupled Maxwell and Dirac equations, etc. occur in mathematical physics. The issue of controllability of such quasilinear equations has been well established for deterministic equations. See for instance [1, 2, 3] and references therein. When a physical state is modeled, external random noise can be a serious disturbance and affects controllability of the model. At the same time fractional derivatives are effectively used to model complex phenomenon by incorporating non-local relations of space and time [4, 5]. This motivates the study of qualitative property, namely controllability of the stochastic fractional quasilinear system of the form

$${}^{C}D^\alpha x(t)=A(t,x(t))x(t)+Bu(t)+f(t,x(t))+\sigma(t,x(t))\frac{dW(t)}{dt},\quad t\in[0,T],$$
$$x(0)=x_0,$$

(18.1)

where $0\leq\alpha<1$. For finite-dimensional systems, the notion of controllability of stochastic differential equations (SDEs) has been studied by many authors [6, 7]. The controllability of stochastic fractional dynamical systems in infinite spaces with $A(t,x(t))=A$ and

A(t,x(t)) = A(t) being a bounded linear operator is studied in [8, 9–11]. In particular when α = 1 the system (18.1) takes the form

$$dx(t)+A(t,x(t))x(t)dt=f(t,x(t))dt+Bu(t)dt+\Phi dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],$$
$$x(0)=x_0$$

(18.2)

and the controllability results of Equation (18.2) in some separable Hilbert space X with A(t,x(t)) = A in finite and infinite dimensional settings are studied in [6] and [7] respectively. Here A generates a C₀-semigroup and W(t) is a Wiener process with finite trace. In this chapter, we discuss the controllability of the above system with the linear operator A depending on t and x(t,ω) ∈ X, generating a C₀-semigroup (hyperbolic case) and perturbed with cylindrical Wiener process.

Kato [12, 13] presented a unified treatment of the Cauchy problem (local in time) for various quasilinear equations that appear in mathematical physics, based on the theory of abstract evolution equations. The stochastic counterpart of the same is considered in the Hilbert space setting [14] and in reflexive UMD Banach space [15], wherein the existence of local-time mild solutions is studied. There are many papers that have discussed the controllability of specific quasilinear partial differential equations [16]. In this chapter we obtain the controllability results for stochastic fractional system (18.1) by assuming that the operator A(t,b) is a bounded linear operator for every (t,b) ∈ J × Bₐ, where Bₐ is a ball in X. An equivalent integral equation to Equation (18.1) is obtained and Banach’s contraction principle is used to obtain the desired controllability result. For the particular case of α = 1 we study the controllability results by assuming that A(t,b) generates a C₀ semigroup for every (t,b) ∈ J × Bₐ. An equivalent integral equation to the quasilinear system is obtained by showing the existence of random evolution system as in [15] and applying Banach’s contraction principle to arrive at the controllability result.

## 18.2 Preliminaries

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{J},(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\geq 0},P)$ be given probability space. Let X, H, U be separable Hilbert spaces. For any Banach spaces Z the norm of Z is denoted by $\|\cdot\|_Z$. The space of all bounded linear operators from X to Y is denoted by $\mathcal{L}(X,Y)$ and $\mathcal{L}(X)$ is denoted by $\mathcal{L}(X)$.

Consider the quasilinear stochastic fractional system of the form

$$^{C}D^{\alpha}x(t)=A(t,x(t))x(t)+Bu(t)+f(t,x(t))+\sigma(t,x(t))\frac{dW(t)}{dt},$$
$$x(0)=x_0,$$

(18.3)

where 0 < α ≤ 1, the state variable x(t,ω) is random and takes values in a separable Hilbert space X, and the control function u(t,ω) is from Uₐdᵣ, a Hilbert space of admissible control functions and takes values in a separable Hilbert space U. A(t,x) is a bounded linear operator on X for every (t,x) ∈ J × X, B: U → X is a bounded linear operator, f: J × X → X is a continuous function, W(t) is a H-valued Wiener process, and σ(t,x) is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator from H to X for every (t,x) ∈ J × X. Taking Iᵅ on both the sides we get,

$$x(t)=x_0+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}A(s,x(s))x(s)\,ds$$
$$+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}Bu(s)\,ds$$
$$+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^t(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\sigma(s,x(s))\,dW(s).$$

The above integral equation is equivalent to the system given in Equation (18.3). Let $\mathrm{B}a:=\{x\in X:\|x-x_0\|_X^2\le r\}$. Since $A(t,b)$ is a bounded linear operator there exists a constant $C_1>0$ such that $\|A(t,b)\|_{\mathcal L(X,X)}^2\le C_1$, for every $(t,x)\in J\times\mathrm{B}a$. Assume the following hypotheses:

(H1) For every $(t,b)\in J\times\mathrm{B}a$ there exists a constant $L_1>0$ such that the bounded linear operator $A(t,b)$ satisfies

$$\|A(t,b_1)-A(t,b_2)\|_{\mathcal L(X,X)}^2\le L_1\|b_1-b_2\|_X^2$$

for every $b_1,b_2\in\mathrm{B}a$.

(H2) The functions $f:J\times X\to X$ and $\sigma:J\times X\to L_2^0$, where $L_2^0$ denotes the space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators (see [17]) are continuous and there exist constants $L_2,L_3>0$ such that

$$\|f(t,b_1)-f(t,b_2)\|_X^2\le L_2\|b_1-b_2\|_X^2$$

$$\|\sigma(t,b_1)-\sigma(t,b_2)\|_{L_2^0(H,X)}^2\le L_3\|b_1-b_2\|_X^2$$

for all $b_1,b_2\in\mathrm{B}a$.

Define the sequence of stopping times $\tau_N$ by,

$$\tau_N:=\inf_{t\ge 0}\{t:C_1\vee L_1\vee L_2\vee L_3\ge N\}$$

and assume $\tau_N>0$. Since $\chi()$ is random, the constants $C_1,L_1,L_2$ in the assumptions depend on $N$, where $N$ is from stopping time. We use the notation $\widehat{T}=T\wedge\tau_N$ and $I$ to denote the time interval $[0,\widehat{T}]\subset J$.

Let us consider the following space settings. Denote,

- $L_2^\infty(\Omega,C(I,X))$ is the space of $(\mathcal F_t)_{t\ge0}$-adapted processes having continuous paths a.s. and endowed with the norm,
  
    $$\|\phi\|_{L_2^\infty(\Omega,C(I,X))}^2=\mathbb E\left(\sup_{t\in I}\|\phi(t)\|_X^2\right),$$
  
    where $\mathbb E$ denotes expectation with respect to $\mathbb P$.

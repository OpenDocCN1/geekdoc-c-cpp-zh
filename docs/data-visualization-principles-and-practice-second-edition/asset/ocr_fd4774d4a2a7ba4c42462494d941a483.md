An equivalent definition of the divergence of v at a point p is

```
\(\text{div } \mathbf{v}(p) = \lim_{\Gamma \to p} \frac{1}{|\Gamma|} \int_{\Gamma} (\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{n}_r) ds.\) (6.2)
```

Here, \(\Gamma\) is a closed hypersurface (a curve for 2D vector fields and a surface for 3D vector fields) around the current point p, \(|\Gamma|\) is the area (2D) or volume (3D) of the space enclosed by \(\Gamma\), and \(\mathbf{n}_r\) is the outward normal of \(\Gamma\) (see Figure 6.1(a)). The integral in Equation (6.2) computes the flux that the vector field transports through the imaginary boundary \(\Gamma\). The limit \(\Gamma \to p\) describes a curve that shrinks around the current point p until it becomes infinitesimally short.

Red areas indicate high positive divergence, or sources. Two such sources are clearly visible. Blue areas indicate high negative divergence, or sinks. Within the dark blue area, two pronounced sinks are visible. If we correlate the divergence and vector glyph visualizations, we get the image of a flow field that emerges from the sources and ends up in the sinks.

Vorticity. Given a vector field \(\mathbf{v} : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3\), the vorticity of v, also called the curl or rotor of v\(^2\), is the vector quantity:

```
\(\text{rot } \mathbf{v} = \left( \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial z}, \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y} \right).\) (6.3)
```

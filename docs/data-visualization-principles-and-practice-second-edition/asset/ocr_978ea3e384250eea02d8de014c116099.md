Figure 5.19 shows a different use-case for height plots. The left image shows a 2D scalar plot of a brain CT slice, displayed with a grayscale colormap. The image is similar to Figure 5.2(a). Here, scalar values encode tissue density, with white corresponding to the hardest structures (bone) and black corresponding to air. Since a linear scalar-to-grayscale colormap was used here, high-contrast borders between regions of different densities are easily seen. Also, large differences of absolute data values are easily seen—we can, for instance, locate the hardest and softest tissues within the given scan by looking for the brightest, respectively darkest, areas in the image. However, small-scale data variations are not easily visible, since the dynamic range of a grayscale colormap on a typical computer screen has only 256 different values (8 bits of luminance, see Section 3.6.3).

The choice of $ω_i$ uniformly on the sphere is analogous to choosing an index $k = 1 … n$, except for one thing: When we chose $k$, it was possible that $m_k = 0$, that is, the particular path we were following in the Markov chain would contribute nothing to the sum. But when we choose $ω_i$, we're going to estimate the arriving radiance at $P$ in direction $−ω_i$, with a ray cast. We'll then know that $P$ is visible from whatever point happens to be sending light in that direction toward $P$. This was one of the great insights of the original path tracing paper: that using ray casting amounted to a kind of importance sampling for a certain integral over all surfaces in the scene. (Kajiya performed surface integrals rather than hemispherical or spherical ones.)

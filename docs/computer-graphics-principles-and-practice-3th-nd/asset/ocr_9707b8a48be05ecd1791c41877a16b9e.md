We need one more definition: We let $\Omega_{jk}$ denote the solid angle of directions from patch $j$ to patch $k$ (see Figure 31.13), assuming that they are mutually visible; if they’re not, then $\Omega_{jk}$ is defined to be the empty set.

Now let’s use these assumptions to simplify the rendering equation. Let’s start with a point $P$ in some patch $j$. The rendering equation says that for a direction $\omega_o$ with $\omega_o \cdot \mathbf{n}_j > 0$, that is, an outgoing direction from patch $j$,

$$L(P,\omega_o)=L^e(P,\omega_o)+\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega^+(\mathbf{n}_j)} f_r(P,\omega_i,\omega_o)L(P,-\omega_i)(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i. \tag{31.33}$$

![](bbox=[0.711, 0.113, 0.928, 0.231])

*Figure 31.13: Patch $k$ is visible from patch $j$; when it’s projected onto the hemisphere around $C_j$, we get a solid angle called $\Omega_{jk}$.*

We now introduce some factors of $\pi$ to simplify the equation a bit. We let $B_j=L(P,\omega_o)/\pi$. Since $L(P,\omega_o)$ is assumed independent of the outgoing direction $\omega_o$, the number $B_j$ does not have $\omega_o$ as a parameter. Similarly, we define $E_j=L^e(P,\omega_o)/\pi$. And substituting $f_r(P,\omega_i,\omega_o)=\rho_j/\pi$, we get

$$\pi B_j=\pi E_j+\frac{\rho_j}{\pi}\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega^+(\mathbf{n}_j)}L(P,-\omega_i)(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i. \tag{31.34}$$

The inner integral, over all directions in the positive hemisphere, can be broken into a sum over directions in each $\Omega_{jk}$, since light arriving at patch $j$ must arrive from some patch $k$. The equation thus becomes

$$\pi B_j=\pi E_j+\frac{\rho_j}{\pi}\sum_k\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega_{jk}}L(P,-\omega_i)(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i. \tag{31.35}$$

The radiance in the integral is radiance leaving patch $k$, and is therefore just $\pi B_k$. Substituting, and rearranging the constant factors of $\pi$ a little, we get

$$\pi B_j=\pi E_j+\frac{\rho_j}{\pi}\sum_k\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega_{jk}}\pi B_k(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i \tag{31.36}$$
$$=\pi E_j+\frac{\rho_j}{\pi}\sum_k\left(\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega_{jk}}(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i\right)B_k \tag{31.37}$$
$$=\pi E_j+\rho_j\pi\sum_k\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega_{jk}}(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i\right)B_k. \tag{31.38}$$

Dividing through by $\pi$, we get

$$B_j=E_j+\rho_j\sum_k\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega_{jk}}(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i\right)B_k. \tag{31.39}$$

The coefficient of $B_k$ inside the summation is called the **form factor** $f_{jk}$ for patches $j$ and $k$. So the equation becomes

$$B_j=E_j+\rho_j\sum_k f_{jk}B_k, \tag{31.40}$$

which is called the **radiosity equation**. Before we try to solve it, let’s look at the form factor more carefully. For patches $j$ and $k$, it is

$$f_{jk}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\omega_i\in\Omega_{jk}}(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_j)\,d\omega_i. \tag{31.41}$$

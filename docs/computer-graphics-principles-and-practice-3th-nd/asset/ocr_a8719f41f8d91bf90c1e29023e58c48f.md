where $H(\omega_o)$ is the (finite) set of directions $\omega_i$ that undergo mirrorlike scattering at $P$ to direction $\omega_o$, and $f_s^o(\omega_o, \omega_i)$ denotes the constant by which incoming radiance is scaled to produce outgoing radiance, which we've previously called the **magnitude of the impulse**.

◆ There's a slight subtlety here. The form given in Equation 29.17 is only valid if $L^m$ is continuous at $(P, -\omega_i)$. Otherwise, the value must be determined by a limit. As far as we know, this detail is generally ignored in graphics, perhaps because almost all physical processes involve convolution, and convolution tends to produce continuous functions. That is to say, perhaps our purely mathematically modeled $L^m$ has a discontinuity, but if we were in the real world, things like volumetric scattering would tend to make it actually continuous. Any picture whose appearance *depended* on the discontinuity of $L^m$ would be nonphysical anyhow!

# ## 29.6 A Worked Example

Consider the situation in Figure 29.3. The surface is 50% Lambertian and 30% a mirror reflector (so 20% of arriving light is absorbed). We'll compute the light reflected from the point $P = (0,0,0)$ under two different lighting conditions.

![](bbox=[0.7, 0.305, 0.95, 0.435])

+   1. The surface is bathed in light from all points of the positive-$x$ hemisphere (see Figure 29.4). The radiance $L^{in}(P,-\omega_i)$ is $6\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}$ for all $\omega_i$ with $\omega_i \cdot [1\ 0\ 0]^T \ge 0$.
+   2. The surface is illuminated by a uniformly radiating sphere (see Figure 29.5) of radius $r < 1$ at position $Q = (1,1,0)$; the total power of the luminaire is 10 W.

![](bbox=[0.7, 0.458, 0.95, 0.566])

![](bbox=[0.7, 0.615, 0.95, 0.717])

We'll examine the behavior of the second case as $r \to 0$ as well.

In each case, we'll compute the reflected radiance in the direction $\omega_o = S([-1\ 1\ 0]^T)$.

We start with situation 1. Let's begin by computing the diffusely reflected light. This is

$$L^{ref,0}(P,\omega_o)=\int_{S^2(P)} f_s^o(P,\omega_o,\omega_i)L(P,-\omega_i)(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_P)\,d\omega_i, \tag{29.18}$$

Rewriting $\omega_i$ in polar coordinates, $(x,y,z) = (\cos\theta \sin\phi, \cos\phi, \sin\theta \sin\phi)$, the radiance field $L(P,-\omega_i)$ is zero unless $x > 0$, that is, $\cos\theta > 0$, so we can restrict to $-\pi/2 \le \theta \le \pi/2$. Similarly, since we're only considering reflectance, we can restrict to $0 \le \phi \le \pi/2$. Thus, our integral becomes

$$L^{ref,0}(P,\omega_o)=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_0^{\pi/2} f_s^o(P,\omega_o,\omega_i)L(P,-\omega_i)(\omega_i\cdot\mathbf{n}_P)\,\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta, \tag{29.19}$$

where the factor of $\sin\phi$ comes from the change to polar coordinates. Within this restricted domain, the value of $L$ is the constant 6, so the integral becomes

$$L^{ref,0}(P,\omega_o)=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_0^{\pi/2} f_s^o(P,\omega_o,\omega_i)6\cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta, \tag{29.20}$$

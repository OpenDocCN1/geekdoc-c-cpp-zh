(at least for surfaces with no mirror terms or transmissive terms) for the scattered light is roughly proportional to \(f(\omega_o, \omega_i)\). In an ideal world, it would be exactly proportional. In ours, it's generally not, but the returned coefficient contains a \(f(\omega_o, \omega_i)/p(\omega_i)\) factor, where \(p(\omega_i)\) is the probability of sampling \(\omega_i\), which compensates appropriately.

What happens if there is a mirror reflection? Let's say that 30% of the time the incoming light is absorbed, 50% of the time it's mirror-reflected, and the remaining 20% of the time it's scattered according to a Lambertian scattering model. In this situation, `scatter()` will return `false` 30% of the time. Fifty percent of the time it will return `true` and set \(\omega_o\) to be the mirror-reflection direction, and the remaining 20% of the time \(\omega_o\) will be distributed on the hemisphere with a cosine-weighted distribution (i.e., with high probability of being emitted in the normal direction and low probability of being emitted in a tangent direction).

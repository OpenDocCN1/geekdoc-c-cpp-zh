where we’ve replaced the dot product with $\cos\phi$. Finally, the finite part of the BSDF is the constant $\frac{0.5}{\pi}\ \mathrm{sr}^{-1}$, so the reflected radiance becomes

$$L^{\mathrm{ref}}(P,\omega_o)=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{0.5}{\pi}\,6\cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta \tag{29.21}$$

$$=\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta \tag{29.22}$$

$$=\frac{3}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\phi\sin\phi\,d\phi \tag{29.23}$$

$$=\frac{3}{2},\ \text{so that} \tag{29.24}$$

$$L^{\mathrm{ref}}(P,\omega_o)=\frac{3}{2}+I\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}, \tag{29.25}$$

where $I$ is the impulsively reflected (i.e., mirror-reflected) radiance. Since the surface is 30% mirror-reflective, the incoming radiance of $6\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}$ is multiplied by the magnitude 0.3 to get the outgoing mirror-reflected radiance, $1.8\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}$. Thus, the total reflected radiance is $\frac{3}{2}+1.8\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}=3.3\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,sr^{-1}}$.

As a result, we’ve converted the handling of an impulse in the scattering function from an integral to a simple multiplication by a constant, the impulse magnitude.

Now, as we look at the second situation, with illumination provided by a 10 W radiating small sphere, we’ll see how each term (the diffuse and the impulse) behaves when there’s an “impulse” in the incoming light field (i.e., a point light), by seeing what happens as the radius of the sphere approaches zero.

As we showed in Section 26.7.3, a uniformly radiating sphere of radius $r$ and total power $\Phi$ produces radiance $\frac{\Phi}{4\pi^2r^2}$ along every outgoing ray, and subtends a solid angle approximately $\frac{\pi r^2}{R^2}$ at a point at distance $R$, with the approximation growing better and better as $R$ increases or $r$ decreases.

The integral to compute the Lambertian-reflected light from this small spherical source is essentially the same as the one above, except that instead of integrating over all directions $\omega_i=[x\ y\ z]^\mathrm{T}$ with $x\ge 0$, we now must integrate over just the small solid angle $\Omega$ subtended at $P$ by the small spherical source. So

$$L^r(P,\omega_o)=\int_{\Omega}f_s^0(P,\omega_o,\omega_i)L(P,-\omega_i)(\omega_i\cdot n(P))\,d\omega_i+I, \tag{29.26}$$

where $I$, as before, represents the impulse-reflected radiance and $f_s^0$ again is the constant function $0.5/\pi\ \mathrm{sr}^{-1}$. Furthermore, for $\omega_i$ in the solid angle subtended by the luminaire, $\omega_i\cdot n(P)$ is well approximated by $u\cdot n(P)$, where $u$ is the unit vector from $P$ to the center $Q$ of the radiating sphere, that is, $u=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}[1\ \ 1\ \ 0]^\mathrm{T}$. Since the normal $n(P)$ points in the $y$-direction, this dot product is just $\sqrt{2}/2$. Hence,

$$L^r(P,\omega_o)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\frac{0.5}{\pi}\int_{\Omega}L(P,-\omega_i)\,d\omega_i+I. \tag{29.27}$$

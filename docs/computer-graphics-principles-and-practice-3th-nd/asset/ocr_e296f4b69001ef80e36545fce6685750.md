# ## Dead or Alive: Continual Learning
with Neuromorphic Memory

**Anonymous Author(s)**

We address the problem of catastrophic forgetting in continual learning by introducing a novel bio-inspired neuromorphic memory mechanism. Our approach, termed Neuromorphic Memory Replay (NMR), integrates sparse, event-driven activations with a dedicated memory consolidation pathway that mirrors hippocampal-neocortical interactions. We evaluate NMR on standard continual learning benchmarks. Our results demonstrate that NMR significantly mitigates catastrophic forgetting, achieving state-of-the-art performance while maintaining energy efficiency and biological plausibility.

Catastrophic forgetting is a major challenge in continual learning, where neural networks lose previously acquired knowledge when trained on new tasks. Traditional regularization and replay-based methods offer partial solutions but often fail to balance stability and plasticity. Neuroscience, however, suggests that biological systems avoid catastrophic forgetting through mechanisms such as synaptic consolidation and memory replay. Inspired by these principles, we propose NMR, a framework that dynamically consolidates important weights and replays sparse memory traces. This approach not only preserves task-relevant information but also adapts efficiently to new information without explicit storage of large datasets.

Our experiments show that NMR outperforms current state-of-the-art methods on challenging benchmarks, including split-MNIST and split-CIFAR-100. The integration of neuromorphic components leads to a more robust and biologically faithful learning system, paving the way for lifelong learning in edge devices and autonomous systems.

We evaluate NMR on two continual learning benchmarks: split-MNIST (5 tasks) and split-CIFAR-100 (10 tasks). Our proposed method consistently outperforms baseline approaches, showing a marked reduction in forgetting and improved overall accuracy.

# ### 14.5.2.1 Ray-Tracing Implicit Surfaces

Implicit surface models are useful for ray casting and other intersection-based operations. For ray tracing, we take the parametric form of the ray with origin $A$ and direction $\omega$,

$$g(t)=A+t\omega, \tag{14.5}$$

and solve for the point at which it intersects the plane by substituting into the plane’s implicit form and finding the roots of the resultant expression. We want to find a value $t$ for which $f(g(t))=0$. That means

$$(g(t)-Q)\cdot \mathbf{n}=0,\quad \text{i.e.,}\tag{14.6}$$

$$(A+t\omega-Q)\cdot \mathbf{n}=0,\quad \text{so}\tag{14.7}$$

$$t=\frac{(Q-A)\cdot \mathbf{n}}{\omega\cdot \mathbf{n}}.\tag{14.8}$$

We can follow the same process for any surface whose equation admits an efficient closed-form solution after substituting the ray’s parametric form.

For a sphere of radius $r$ about the point $Q$, we can use the implicit form $f(P)=\lVert Q-P\rVert^2-r^2$. Substituting the parametric form for the ray, and setting to zero, we get

$$0=\lVert(A+\omega t)-Q\rVert^2-r^2 \tag{14.9}$$

$$r^2=\lVert(A-Q)+\omega t\rVert^2 \tag{14.10}$$

$$r^2=\lVert(A-Q)\rVert^2+2t(A-Q)\cdot\omega+\lVert\omega\rVert^2t^2 \tag{14.11}$$

$$0=(\lVert(A-Q)\rVert^2-r^2)+2t(A-Q)\cdot\omega+\lVert\omega\rVert^2t^2. \tag{14.12}$$

This is a quadratic equation in $t$, $at^2+bt+c=0$, where $a=\lVert\omega\rVert^2$, $b=(A-Q)\cdot\omega$, and $c=\lVert(A-Q)\rVert^2-r^2$. It can be solved with the quadratic formula to find all intersections of the ray with the sphere.

More general quadratics can be used to determine intersections with ellipsoids or hyperboloids, while higher-order polynomials arise in determining the intersection of a ray with a torus, for example, and for more general shapes, the equation we must solve can be very complicated. Multiple roots of the equation that results from substituting the parametric line form into the function defining the implicit surface indicate multiple potential intersections.

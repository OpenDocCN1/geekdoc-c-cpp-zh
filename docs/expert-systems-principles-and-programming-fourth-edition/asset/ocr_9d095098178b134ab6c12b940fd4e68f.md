Before any evidence is known, the prior probability of H is P(H). After the evidence is known the probability of H changes by Bayes' Theorem:

```
P(H | E) = \frac{P(H \cap E)}{P(E)} = \frac{P(H \cap E)}{P(E \cap H) + P(E \cap H')}
```

```
P(H | E) = \frac{P(E | H)P(H)}{P(E | H)P(H) + P(E | H')P(H')}
```

where P(E) is the probability of observing E.

Situation 2: Two pieces of known evidence conclude H.

This is a more complex case than situation 1 and corresponds to rules of the form:

```
IF E1 and E2 then H
```

After E1 and E2 are observed, the probability of H changes from the prior P(H) to:

```
P(H | E1 \cap E2) = \frac{P(H \cap E1 \cap E2)}{P(E1 \cap E2)}
```

```
= \frac{P(H \cap E1 \cap E2)}{P(E1 \cap E2 \cap H) + P(E1 \cap E2 \cap H')}
```

+   (1) P(H | E1 \cap E2)

```
= \frac{P(E1 \cap E2 | H) P(H)}{P(E1 \cap E2 | H) P(H) + P(E1 \cap E2 | H') P(H')}
```

This formula cannot be reduced further unless a simplifying assumption is made. If the evidence E1 and E2 are assumed conditionally independent of each other, then:

```
P(E1 \cap E2 | H) = P(E1 | H) P(E2 | H)
P(E1 \cap E2 | H') = P(E1 | H') P(E2 | H')
```

and so (see Problem 4.12 for conditional independence):

+   (2) P(H | E1 \cap E2)

```
= \frac{P(E1 | H) P(E2 | H)}{P(E1 | H) P(E2 | H) + P(H') P(E1 | H') P(E2 | H')}
```

Equation (2) is a major simplification of equation (1) since the probabilities are now all expressed in terms of the individual probabilities rather than the joint

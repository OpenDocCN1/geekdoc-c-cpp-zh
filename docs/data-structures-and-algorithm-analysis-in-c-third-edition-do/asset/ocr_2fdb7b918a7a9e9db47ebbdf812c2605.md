# Using the Book: Data structures and algorithms textbooks tend to fall into one of two categories: teaching texts or encyclopedias. Books that attempt to do both usually fail. This book is intended as a teaching text. I believe it is more important for a practitioner to understand the principles required to solve or design the data structure that will best solve some problem than it is to memorize a list of textbook implementations. Hence, I have designed this as a teaching text that covers most standard data structures, but not all. A few data structures that are not widely adopted are included to illustrate important principles. Relatively new data structures that should become used in the future are included.

Within an undergraduate program, this textbook is designed for use in either an advanced lower (sophomore or junior level) data structures course, or for a senior level algorithms course. New material has been added in the third edition to support its use in an algorithms course. Normally, this text would be used in a course beyond the freshman level "CS2" course the introduction to data structures. Readers of this book should typically have two semesters of the equivalent of programming experience, including at least some exposure to C++. Students who are already familiar with recursion will have an advantage. Students of data structures will also benefit from having first completed a good course in Discrete Mathematics. Nonetheless, Chapter 2 attempts a reasonably complete survey of the prerequisite mathematical topics at the level necessary to understand their use in this book. Readers may wish to refer back to the appropriate sections when encountering unfamiliar mathematical material.

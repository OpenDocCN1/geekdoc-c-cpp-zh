Function swap(A, i, j) exchanges $i$ and $j$ in array $A$. To see that this works, notice that each pass through the `for` loop will move at least the integer with $i$ to its correct position in the array, and during this iteration, the value of $A[i]$ must be greater than or equal to $i$. A total of at $n$ swap operations takes place, because an integer cannot be moved out of its correct position once it has been placed, and each swap operation places at least one integer in its correct position. Thus, this code fragment has cost $\Theta(n)$. However, it requires more time than the first fragment. A second principle for the relationship between a program's space and time requires programs that process information stored on disk, as discussed in Chapter 8 and thereafter. Strang enough, the disk-based space/time tradeoff is the reverse of the space/time tradeoff for programs using main memory.

The disk-based space/time tradeoff states that the smaller you can make your storage requirements, the faster your program will run. This is because the time to read information from disk is enormous compared to computation time, so any amount of additional computation to unpack the data is going to be less than the reading time saved by reducing the storage requirements. Naturally this principle does not hold true in all cases, but it is good to keep in mind when designing programs that process information stored on disk.

# 3.10 Speeding Up Your Programs

In practice, there is not such a difference in running time between an algorithm with growth rate $\Theta(n \log n)$ and another with growth rate $\Theta(n)$. There is, however, an enormous difference in running time between algorithms with growths of $\Theta(n \log n)$ and $\Theta(n^2)$. As you will see during the course of your study of common data structures and algorithms, it is not unusual that a problem whose solution requires $\Theta(n^2)$ time also has a solution requiring $\Theta(n log n)$. Examples include sorting and searching, two of the most important computer problems.

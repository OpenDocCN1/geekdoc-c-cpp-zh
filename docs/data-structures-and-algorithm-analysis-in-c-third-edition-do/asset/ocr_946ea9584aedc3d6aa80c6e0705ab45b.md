In other notation, we can think of matrix multiplication using divide and conquer in the following way:

```
$$\begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{bmatrix} egin{bmatrix} B_{11} & B_{12} \ B_{21} & B_{22} \end{bmatrix} = egin{bmatrix} A_{11}B_{11} + A_{12}B_{21} & A_{11}B_{12} + A_{12}B_{22} \ A_{21}B_{11} + A_{22}B_{21} & A_{21}B_{12} + A_{22}B_{22} \end{bmatrix}$.
```

Of course, each of the multiplications and additions on the right side of this equation are recursive on arrays of half size, and additions of arrays of half size, respectively. The recurrence relation for this algorithm is

```
$T(n) = 8T(n/2) + 4(n/2)^2 = \Theta(n^3)$.
```

This closed form can easily be obtained by applying the Master Theorem 14.1.

Strassen's algorithm rearranges the way that the terms are multiplied and added together. It does so in a particular order, as expressed by the following equation:

```
$$\begin{bmatrix} A_{11} & A_{12} \ A_{21} & A_{22} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} B_{11} & B_{12} \ B_{21} & B_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} s_1 + s_2 - s_4 + s_6 & s_4 + s_5 \ s_6 + s_7 & s_2 - s_3 + s_5 - s_7 \end{bmatrix}$.$
```

In order, the multiplication of an $n \times n$ array is obtained by a different series of matrix multiplications and additions for $n/2 \times n/2$ arrays. Multiplications between subarrays requires $\Theta(n^2)$. The subfactors are defined as follows:

```
$s_1 = (A_{11} - A_{22}) \cdot (B_{11} + B_{22})$\ns s_2 = (A_{11} + A_{22}) \cdot (B_{11} + B_{22})$\ns s_3 = (A_{11} - A_{21}) \cdot (B_{11} + B_{12})$\ns s_4 = (A_{11} + A_{12}) \cdot B_{22}$\ns s_5 = A_{11} \cdot (B_{12} - B_{22})$\ns s_6 = A_{22} \cdot (B_{21} - B_{11})$\ns s_7 = (A_{21} + A_{22}) \cdot B_{11}$
```

With a little effort, you should be able to verify that this peculiar combination of operations does in fact produce the correct answer!

Now, looking at the list of operations to compute the $s$ factors, and counting-the additions/subtractions to put them together to get the final answers, we see that we need a total of seven (array) multiplications and 18 (array) additions/subtractions to do the job.

```
$T(n) = 7T(n/2) + 18(n/2)^2$\ns T(n) = \Theta(n^{\log_2 7})$
```

# 2. Derivation of an Autonomous System Approximating the Original Non-autonomous System

In order to examine the general behavior of the horizontal circular motion in which the fundamental component with period 2π/ω predominates over all other components. The approximate solution of Eq. (3) is given by

```
x(t) = a(t) cos ωt - b(t) sin ωt
 y(t) = b(t) cos ωt + a(t) sin ωt
 z(t) = c(t) (10)
```

where the amplitudes a(t), b(t) and c(t) are slowly varying functions of time t as compared with cos ωt and sin ωt. This implies that their derivatives are sufficiently small quantities. Also, the damping coefficient k is regarded as a small quantity.

The well-known normal means, such as Harmonic Balance Method and Averaging Principle, leads to the following autonomous equation:

```
da/dτ = 1/(2ω²) [ -k ω a + (ω² - (n g)/ℓ r^{n-2}) b ] = f(a,b)
 db/dτ = 1/(2ω²) [ - (ω² - (n g)/ℓ r^{n-2}) a - k ω b - F ] = g(a,b)
 0 = -g/ℓ + Λ (11)
```

where

```
τ = ωt,   r² = a² + b²
 Λ = [ g/ℓ + n r^{n-2} (F a + ω² r²) ] / [ 1 + n² r^{2n-2} ] (12)
```

The autonomous equation (11) turns out to be two-dimensional with respect to a(t) and b(t), and the variable c(t) is determined by Eq. (5). The third equation in Eq. (11) comes from the third equation (3) under the above assumptions.

It is well known that a two-dimensional autonomous equation of a dissipative system has two types of steady states. One is equilibrium points and the other is limit cycles. However, Eq. (11) has only equilibrium points but not limit cycles. This matter originates from the Bendixson (Negative) Theorem on the existence of periodic solutions represented by closed curves.

This set of equations describes a pair of van der Pol oscillators with Huygens’ coupling. It has been found that this system shows chaotic behaviour for certain parameter values. Similar to the case of Duffing oscillators discussed before, the ratio m/m_h is a key parameter in the onset of chaotic behaviour. Note, however, that now the coupling bar is not driven.

Also here, in the chaotic regime, the coupled van der Pol oscillators may show synchronized behaviour provided that a control action is applied to one of them. Next, the particular cases of in-phase and anti-phase synchronization are discussed.

## 5.1. Case A: in-phase synchronization

By defining in-phase synchronization errors like (10), the corresponding error dynamics for system (27) can be written as

```
ë = 1/m [bė - ba(x_s² ẋ_s - x_m² ẋ_m - ẋ_h (x_s² - x_m²)) - k e + u_s]. (28)
```

In order to achieve in-phase synchronized motion in the chaotic oscillators, the following controller may be considered

```
u_s = -k_p e - k_v ė + η, (29)
```

where k_p > 0 and k_v > 0 and η can be chosen as

```
η = ba(x_s² ẋ_s - x_m² ẋ_m - ẋ_h (x_s² - x_m²)). (30)
```

Following the approach used in Proposition 27.1, it can be proven that the controller (29)–(30) yields global asymptotic stability for system (28), i.e. the coupled oscillators asymptotically synchronize in-phase, provided that k_v > b and k_p > 0.

## 5.2. Case B: anti-phase synchronization

If the two van der Pol oscillators with Huygens’ coupling show in-phase synchronized chaotic behaviour, the coupling bar will vibrate. However, when anti-phase synchronization is induced in the oscillators, the oscillations in the coupling bar will die out, i.e. x_h → 0 and the coupling bar comes to a standstill as in the original Huygens system, see also the third equation in (27). Therefore, if anti-phase is achieved, the originally chaotic motion

will change to periodic motion in both van der Pol oscillators, which are now uncoupled. To avoid this scenario and maintain chaotic motion for anti-phase synchronization, the external periodic excitation (9) is applied to the coupling bar.

The anti-phase synchronized motion in the chaotic oscillators is achieved by considering controller (29) with e = (x_m + x_s), ė = (ẋ_m + ẋ_s), and η(·) chosen as

```
η(·) = ba(x_s² ẋ_s + x_m² ẋ_m − ẋ_h (x_s² + x_m²)) − 2k x_h + 2b ẋ_h. (31)
```

As before, the control gains in (25) are chosen according to k_v > b and k_p > 0.

The synchronized chaotic motion of two van der Pol oscillators is validated by means of experiments. System (27) is implemented in the experimental setup with the following parameter values: b = 18.3135 [Ns/m], b_h = 3.2656 [Ns/m], k = 6.315 [N/m], k_h = 388.71 [N/m], a = 55·10^5 [1/m²], m = 4.210 [kg], and m_h = 5 [kg].

In a first experiment, the oscillators are synchronized in-phase by applying to one of them the control input (29)–(30) with k_p = 25 [N/m] and k_v = (b + 10) [Ns/m]. The controller is activated at t = 20 [s]. The oscillators are released from the initial conditions x_m(0) = 1 [mm] and x_s(0) = −1.5 [mm]. The remaining initial conditions are set to zero.

## 7. Conclusions and Recommendations

In this work, it has been shown that two Duffing and two van der Pol oscillators with Huygens’ coupling may experience chaotic behaviour. For the case of the Duffing oscillators, the coupling bar must be driven by a periodic force. The coupling strength—or rather the mass of the coupling bar—has been considered as the bifurcation parameter and for the case of the Duffing oscillators, a cascade of period-doubling bifurcations resulting ultimately in chaotic states has been found. In the chaotic regime, for the Duffing oscillators as well as the van der Pol oscillators, the oscillators do not synchronize spontaneously. This is not a surprise, because, although the oscillators are identical, there is extreme sensitivity to initial conditions. For this reason, a controller was added to one of the oscillators, i.e. the slave oscillator, in order to induce in-phase or anti-phase synchronization of chaotic motion. This

principle has been experimentally validated. When Huygens’ coupling is used to couple two arbitrary oscillators, the occurrence of spontaneous synchronization (in the same spirit of Huygens) depends on whether the oscillators have chaotic solutions or not.

For reasons mentioned in Sec. 6, it is interesting to perform a stability analysis of the (in-phase and/or anti-phase) synchronization error dynamics when the simple controller (32) is used.

Finally, it is worth saying that Huygens’ system is an exciting and challenging nonlinear dynamical system, which complex dynamical behaviour is far from being completely understood. Further studies of this system will lead to unveiling more details about the complex yet intriguing synchronization phenomenon.


An interesting area of study in nonlinear dynamics. Of particular interest in this connection is the effect of time delay in such systems. In this section, we will present some details on coupled piecewise linear time-delay systems and synchronizations exhibited by them.

## 8.1. *Synchronization transitions in coupled time-delay electronic circuits*

Now let us consider the following set of unidirectionally coupled first-order delay differential equations,

\[
\frac{dx}{dt}=-a_1x(t)+b_1F[x(t-\tau_1)], \tag{34a}
\]
\[
\frac{dy}{dt}=-a_2y(t)+b_2F[y(t-\tau_1)]+b_3F[x(t-\tau_2)], \tag{34b}
\]

where $a_1=a_2>0$ are positive constants, $b_1\ne b_2$ contributes to the parameter mismatch resulting in coupled non-identical systems, $b_3$ is the coupling strength, $\tau_1$ is the feedback delay and $\tau_2$ is the coupling delay [40]. The nonlinear function $F(x)$ is of the same form as in Eq. (32).

Now to analog simulate the coupled time-delay systems (Eqs. (34)) and to demonstrate experimentally the existence of different types of synchronizations, a unidirectionally coupled time-delay electronic circuit is constructed as shown in the block diagram of Fig. 20. One of the electronic oscillator circuits is used as the drive system, while the other structurally identical circuit is used as the response system with some parameter mismatches. The drive voltage ($U_1(t)$) after the delay line in the drive system is fed back to the nonlinear part ($ND_1$) of the drive system and a fixed $R_1C_1$ filter with time delay to generate chaotic/hyperchaotic oscillations.

Similarly, the response circuit with a nonlinear part ($ND_2$), a delay line and a fixed $R_2C_2$ filter is capable of generating chaotic/hyperchaotic oscillations. The signal after the nonlinear function of drive is used as the transmission signal, which is unidirectionally transmitted through the lowpass filter ($R_3C_3$), delay line and nonlinear part to the response circuit.

![](img/039e7d3c23390584a66737067db8b75b_312_0.png)

**Fig. 20.** Circuit block diagram of the coupled delayed feedback oscillator. Two delay oscillators are coupled through a nonlinear activation function ($ND$) but with a different time delay $T_{3d}$ in the coupling with a low pass first-order $R_3C_3$ filter. $U_1(t)$ and $U_2(t)$ are the voltages across the capacitances $C_1$ and $C_2$, respectively. $U(t-T_{1d})=U(t-T_{2d})$ are the voltages across the delay units of both the coupled oscillators.

Experimental observations reveal transitions from anticipatory to lag synchronization via complete synchronization, when excitatory couplings are a function of the coupling delay (time $\tau_2$) (see Fig. 21). Next, when we use an inhibitory coupling, $-b_3f[x(t-\tau_2)]$, in Eq. (34b) instead of the excitatory coupling $+b_3f[x(t-\tau_2)]$, we find the transition from inverse anticipatory to inverse lag synchronization via an inverse complete synchronization as a function of the coupling delay $\tau_2$ (see Fig. 22).

## 8.2. *Experimental confirmation of chaotic phase synchronization*

We now consider the possibility of chaotic phase synchronization exhibited by coupled time delay systems. For this purpose, we consider the normalized evolution equation of the coupled time-delay electronic circuits with error coupling,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x} &= -ax(t)+b_1F[x(t-\tau)],\\
\dot{y} &= -ay(t)+b_2F[y(t-\tau)]+\varepsilon(x(t)-y(t)),
\end{aligned} \tag{35}
\]

where $a$, $b_1$ and $b_2$ are positive parameters and $\tau$ is the time delay [36]. The function $F(x)$ is taken to be the symmetric piecewise linear function of Eq. (32).

The coupled electronic circuit investigated here is shown in Fig. 23 as a block diagram. The individual time-delay units have a ring structure and comprise of a diode based nonlinear device unit (ND), a variable time-delay unit (DELAY) along with an integrator (R₀C₀) unit. The block diagram also contains a differential amplifier circuit (A) with a gain ε = R_f/R used to find the difference between the two voltage signals U1 and U2. By changing the feedback resistance (R_f), the coupling parameter ε can be varied. We have fixed the same circuit parameter values as in the above studies and demonstrate here the existence of chaotic phase synchronization (CPS) as a function of the coupling strength ε in both chaotic and hyperchaotic regimes for suitable values of the delay time τ.

![](img/039e7d3c23390584a66737067db8b75b_314_0.png)

**Fig. 23.** Block diagram of the unidirectionally coupled time-delay electronic circuits with threshold nonlinearity.

Existence of CPS can now be demonstrated using the framework of localized sets in the chaotic regime. The sets obtained by sampling the time series of one of the systems whenever a maximum occurs in the other one are plotted along with the chaotic attractor of the same system for the delay time τ = 1.33 experimentally in Fig. 24(i). The sets distributed over the entire attractor of both the drive [Figs. 24(a)i] and the response [Figs. 24(b)i] systems for the coupling strength ε = 0.3 indicate that the time-delay systems evolve independently. The sets that are localized on the chaotic attractor of both the drive [Figs. 24(c)i] and the response [Figs. 24(d)i] systems for the coupling strength ε = 0.9 correspond to a perfect locking of the phases of both circuits, indicating CPS.

![](img/039e7d3c23390584a66737067db8b75b_314_1.png)

**Fig. 24.** Experimental characterization of CPS using the framework of localized sets in the chaotic regime (i) for τ = 1.33 and in the hyperchaotic regime (ii) for τ = 6.0. Sets in the drive and the response systems are distributed in (a) and (b) for ε = 0.3 indicating the asynchronous state and localized in (c) and (d) for ε = 0.9 indicating CPS, respectively.

## 8.3. Zero-lag synchronization in coupled time-delay systems

In this section we have considered the following three coupled piecewise linear time-delay systems with threshold nonlinear function,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x}_1(t) &= -\beta x_1(t) + \alpha F[x_1(t-\tau)] + C[x_2(t-\tau) - 2x_1(t) + x_3(t-\tau)],\\
\dot{x}_2(t) &= -\beta x_2(t) + \alpha F[x_2(t-\tau)] + C[x_1(t-\tau) - x_2(t)],\\
\dot{x}_3(t) &= -\beta x_3(t) + \alpha F[x_3(t-\tau)] + C[x_1(t-\tau) - x_3(t)], \tag{36}
\end{aligned}
\]

The parameter values are fixed as follows: β = 1.0, α = 1.2, τ = 6.0 and C is the coupling strength. The function F(x) is taken to be a symmetric piecewise linear function Eq. (32). We note that in contrast to the previous example (34) and (35), the above three equations are delay coupled to one another. In particular, the first oscillator (drive system) is coupled to both the second and third oscillators, while the second and third oscillators are coupled to the first oscillator only. This system can be studied experimentally and it is found to exhibit phase and anti-phase synchronizations even with the delay coupling.

In the presence of weak coupling the oscillators 2 and 3 are in transition from nonsynchronized state to a phase synchronized (PS) state. These two oscillators are synchronized in an anti-phase state with the oscillator 1. The experimental snapshots of the time series of both the response systems (2 and 3) as seen from the oscilloscope are shown in Fig. 25(a) in the chaotic regime for the delay time τ = 1.33 and the coupling strength C = 1.12 indicating the evolution of both systems in-phase with each other.

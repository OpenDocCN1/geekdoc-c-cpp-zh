# 1. Introduction

This chapter examines a variety of recent methods to model memristive systems using analog and micro-processor based circuits. Since Hewlett-Packard’s (HP) announcement in 2008 of the successful fabrication of a memristor [1], researchers have developed a number of circuits to observe memristive behavior either as a stand-alone phenomenon or in a practical application as a component.

The development of functional memristor models is important, if not essential, in the exploration of the properties and the development of

applications of this new component. Currently, memristors have not entered the market in any format. It is hoped, although there has been no indication of it happening, that at some time in the future the memristor will be available as a through-hole or surface mount two-terminal device. This will greatly benefit application development and prototyping. Few are prepared to wait for this to happen, and for now, all research into developing applications for memristive systems and knowledge of memristors must be done with circuit-based and mathematical models.

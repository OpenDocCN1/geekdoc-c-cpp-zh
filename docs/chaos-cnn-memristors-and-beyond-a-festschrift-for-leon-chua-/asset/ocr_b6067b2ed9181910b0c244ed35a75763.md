# 4. Conclusion

Summing up, we have presented a review of the delayed feedback control (DFC) methods, including experimental implementations, verifications for theoretical models and various modifications. We have also discussed the analytical theories developed for this method, which are connected with the bifurcations of target orbits. We stress that the research on the global properties of the DFC algorithm as

well as adaptive modifications are still weakly developed and require further attention of investigators. In Secs. 2.2 and 2.3, we have presented our recent achievements in this field.

Another way of application of a time-delay feedback is the forecasting chaotic dynamics of a master system via anticipating synchronization with a slave system. In Sec. 3, we have presented two algorithms based on a phase-lag compensating coupling (PLCC) and extended PLCC that allowed us to achieve a long term anticipation with the prediction time equal approximately to the characteristic period of chaotic oscillations. We hope that our findings in this field will stimulate the search for appropriate coupling laws in other problems of anticipating synchronization, e.g., to enhance the predictability of chaotic systems with unknown dynamical models [8] or excitable systems driven by random forcing [7, 9].

Although the DFC and anticipating synchronization algorithms are similar in the sense that both involve a time-delay feedback, however, the DFC is much more effective. In its usual form, it provides a stabilization of UPOs whose periods are comparable with the characteristic period of chaotic oscillations. In contrast, the usual anticipating synchronization algorithm is able to stabilize the anticipatory manifold only for small time-delays. To prolong this time we have designed a special phase-lag compensating coupling. The difference in efficacy of these two algorithms is due to different role of the delayed feedback in these algorithms. The DFC law (2) involves both the proportional feedback term $y(t)$ and the delay feedback term $y(t−\tau)$. The stabilization of UPOs is mainly caused by the proportional feedback. The delay term here is necessary to set to zero the control force on the desired UPO. In contrast, the feedback law $\mathbf K[\mathbf r_1−\mathbf r_2(t−\tau)]$ in the anticipating synchronization algorithm (10) does not contain the proportional feedback term. It involves only the delayed feedback term $\mathbf r_2(t−\tau)$ and only this term is responsible for the stabilization of the anticipatory manifold.

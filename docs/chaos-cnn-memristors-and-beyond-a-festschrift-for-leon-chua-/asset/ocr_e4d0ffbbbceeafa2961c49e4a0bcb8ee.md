while it is about few years for the DR one. Both results are related to the linear stability of the SM since the memory time of the response to external noise is maximum when the temporal integral of the largest stability exponent is close to zero, that gives a “precursor time” of order t₀ in both cases.

# 3.4. *Power law friction*

Finally it is worth questioning the relevance of this idea of transition for other mathematical models. In other terms how “generic” is this kind of transition? One can say first that it persists (as a way of going from slow to fast and conversely for the full dynamics) in the DR equations by changing the numerical values of the parameters. Another indication that it is a generic scenario is to check that it is still there in (slightly) modified equations.

We changed the logarithm ln(v) in the DR equations (38) and (39), by the function

$$g_\alpha(v)=\frac{v^\alpha-1}{\alpha}, \tag{66}$$

with α positive.

Solving the modified system of equations

$$\begin{cases}\dot{u}=v-1\\\dot{v}=-\gamma^2\left(u+\frac{1}{\xi}(\theta+g_\alpha(v))\right)\\\dot{\theta}=-v\left(\theta+(1+\epsilon)g_\alpha(v)\right),\end{cases} \tag{67}$$

we found stable periodic solutions, with slow and fast steps. More precisely this appears above a certain threshold (close to the Poincaré–Andronov bifurcation the limit cycle has ordinary behavior). The curves (a) and (b) of Fig. 14 display slow-fast

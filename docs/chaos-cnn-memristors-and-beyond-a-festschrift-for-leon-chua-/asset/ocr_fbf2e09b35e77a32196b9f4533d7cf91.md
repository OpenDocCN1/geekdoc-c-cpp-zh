Nonlinear dynamic circuits including memristors are considered. Graph-theoretical criteria are given as sufficient conditions for the existence of global state equations, with the capacitor and memristor charges as state variables (for convenience we do not consider inductors here). They are based on earlier work on circuits without memristors. Global state equations in these charges fail to exist when the charges are dependent or when singular points are present, so-called impasse points. The values of state variables at initial time uniquely determine a solution. Therefore, even for numerical circuit analysis that in general does not use state equations, the determination of a set of state variables is important, and to know whether, with the “canonical candidates” for states, global state equations exist, is the first and most important step.

The graph-theoretical criteria are easy to check directly for simple circuits. We give a few simple examples that illustrate the various cases. Larger circuits have to be examined by combinatorial algorithms.

# 1. Introduction

Typically, a circuit is described by a set of equations that are partly differential and partly nondifferential, i.e. static. Such systems are called *differential-algebraic equations*. Even though for the purpose of numerical analysis it is advantageous to solve algebra-differential systems directly, and most general purpose circuit analysis programs actually do so, the question whether or not it is possible to reduce them to a purely differential form, the *state equations*, is not only of theoretical interest.

In fact, differential-algebraic systems that cannot be reduced to a system of first order differential equations, not even locally, possess singular points, where the solution either does not exist (*impasse points*) or is not unique. Of course, this is only a property of the circuit model, and not of the circuit itself, but it indicates a singular behavior of the physical circuit also from a practical point of view, like e.g. jumps from a state to a distant one. Therefore, it is important to have computable criteria that allow excluding the potential presence of singular points.

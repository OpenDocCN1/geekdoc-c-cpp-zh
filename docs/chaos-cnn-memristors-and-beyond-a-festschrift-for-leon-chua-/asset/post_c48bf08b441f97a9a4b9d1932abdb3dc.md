
## 2.2. Dynamics of a neuron under synaptic action

Now we discuss basic properties of a neuron response to an excitatory synaptic action generated by the system of Eqs. (3)–(6). We consider the process of formation of the response on the example of Morris-Lecar neuron (the system of Eqs. (1)–(2)).

Under the action of the synaptic current I_syn(t) the system (1)–(2) describing the i-th neuron may be treated as non-autonomous and, therefore, it has 3-dimensional generalized phase space. The system (3)–(6) has a small parameter ε and therefore, the evolution of the s_i-variable is slow with respect to the evolution of v_i and n_i. Hence, in the first approximation s_i(t) may be considered as a parameter that is changing slowly. In Fig. 5(a), a fragment of the bifurcation diagram of the system (1)–(2) is presented. For s_i < s_d ≃ 0.724 the only equilibrium state P_1 exists. For s_i = s_d the saddle-node bifurcation of a limit cycle occurs, and two limit cycles — stable C^s and unstable C^u — appear on the phase plane. Further increase of s_i leads to the shrinking of C^u to the equilibrium state P_1, and for s_i = s_h ≃ 1.092 the equilibrium state P_1 changes its stability due to the subcritical Andronov-Hopf bifurcation.

In the generalized phase space the stable equilibrium states P_1(s_i) form a stable one-dimensional integral manifold M_st^s(0), and the stable limit cycles C^s form a stable two-dimensional integral manifold M_c^s(0). According to the theorem of persistence of integral manifolds (see, e.g. [9]), there are integral manifolds M_st^s(ε) and M_c^s(ε) close to M_st^s(0) and M_c^s(0) correspondingly. Since initially the i-th neuron is at the phase of rest, the representative point in the generalized phase space is situated on the manifold M_st^s(ε). Under a synaptic action the representative point starts moving along M_st^s(ε) until it reaches the boundary of M_st^s(ε). Due to a special choice of the parameter g_syn in the expression for the synaptic current, this piece of the itinerary of the representative point corresponds to the interval of increase of the variable s_i(t) until its maximal value. After that the representative point passes rapidly into a vicinity of M_c^s(ε), forming an oscillatory motion in the opposite direction (Fig. 5(a)). This piece of its itinerary corresponds to the decreasing part of the graph of s_i(t) from the maximal value (Fig. 3). The oscillating process is over when the representative point reaches the boundary of the manifold M_c^s(ε). As a result, a “transitive oscillating set” (see Fig. 5(b)) is formed in the generalized phase space. It corresponds to the generation of a collection of spikes in the i-th neuron. Because of our choice of parameters, the temporal intervals of the generation are relatively short with respect to the timescales of the synaptic response. Thus, in fact, the neuron can be in one of two states having different temporal scales: one of them corresponds to the slow monotonic change of the membrane potential v_i and another — to the fast generation of spikes (Fig. 5). In addition, these processes differ for the fast and slow synapses only by the duration of the slow phase of the change of v_i, and the spike oscillations in both cases are practically the same.

Then the trajectory Tr comes to a vicinity of the equilibrium point O_0 according to the dynamics of the system (3)–(6). It is accompanied by a “powerful” impulsive change of the variable r, i.e. an activation of the synaptic interaction. Here the evolution of the variable s has a form typical for the synaptic current (Fig. 2). If the representative point on the (r_i, s_i)-plane lies on the left side of W_1^s or L_u, the trajectory Tr again goes to O_0, but the relevant impulse response does not appear. In this case there is no activation of the synaptic coupling. The separatrix W_1^s and the unstable manifold L_u determine two thresholds of activation of the synaptic interaction. In order to go through the first threshold it is sufficient to have a comparatively weak presynaptic effect. On the other hand, for overcoming the second threshold the presynaptic action should be sufficiently strong. Moreover, overcoming of different thresholds leads to different efficiency of the activation of the synaptic interaction, i.e. characteristic times of the growth of the synaptic current. In the first case (weak presynaptic action) the growth of the synaptic current occurs slowly (slow synapses) (Fig. 2(b)). In the other case the strong presynaptic effect causes comparatively fast growth of the synaptic current (fast synapses) (Fig. 2(a)). Note that nonzero time which is necessary to get the representative point on the right side of W_1^s or L_u reflects the synaptic delay property.

Next, we will characterize fast and slow synapses by the length T of temporal interval that is equal to the time interval where s(t) increases from 0 to its maximal value. We consider a “resonance” case when the value T2 for the slow synapse and T1 for the fast one satisfy T2 ≃ 2T1.

## 3. Notes on Construction of Synaptic Cellular Automaton

Here we describe the construction for an arbitrary neural network consisting of N neurons coupled by excitatory synapses. Let a synapse of the network have a number of inputs from presynaptic neurons and one output to a postsynaptic one, i.e. the time course of synaptic conductance completely determines the dynamics of the postsynaptic neuron. Thus to analyze the dynamics of the whole neural network one can study only the dynamics of the synapse layer. Next we define graph of connections, introduce discrete states of a synapse, and define a synaptic cellular automaton determined on the graph of connections.

### 3.1. Definition of graph of connections

Let us define an oriented graph G = < Q_G, K_G >. It has N vertices Q_G = {Q_1, Q_2, ..., Q_N} which signify the synapses of the network. The set K_G = {K_1, K_2, ..., K_N} reflects the architecture of the connections in the network. Each K_i is a collection of indices such that j ∈ K_i if and only if there is a connection from the j-th synapse of the network to the i-th synapse through the j-th neuron.

### 3.2. Introducing discrete states of a synapse

Depending on the activity of presynaptic neurons and synaptic activation thresholds, there are two types of synaptic responses (waveforms of synaptic conductance s(t), see Fig. 2) — slow and fast ones which are distinguished by the characteristic rise times (T2 and T1) of the synaptic conductance. Then in the case of T2 = 2T1 the discrete states of a synapse can be introduced according to the behavior of the s-variable describing its dynamics. We say that the i-th synapse is in the state ψ_i(s_i) that is equal to

- P11 if s_i(t) undergoes the fast response and s_i'(t) > 0 (see Fig. 2(a));
- P21 if s_i(t) undergoes the response of the slow type, s_i'(t) > 0 and t_in ≤ t ≤ T1 + t_in, where s_i(t_in) = 0, s_i'(t_in) > 0, i.e. s_i(t) starts growing at the instant of time t_in (see Fig. 2(b));
- P22 if s_i(t) again undergoes the response of the slow type and s_i'(t) > 0, but T1 + t_in ≤ t ≤ 2T1 + t_in;
- P0 if it is not in one of the previous states, i.e. either it is in the equilibrium state s_i(t) = 0 or s_i'(t) < 0 in both slow and fast cases.

Let us emphasize that the exact resonance condition T2 = 2T1 is never realized precisely, but numerical experiments show that even the approximate validity of this condition is sufficient to construct the cellular automaton which adequately describes the spatio-temporal order of activation of synapses and of firing of neurons of the network.

### 3.3. Definition of CA rules

Let us assume that neurons and synapses of the network possess the following properties:

- when a synapse is active it is non-sensitive to the presynaptic stimulation, i.e. it is in refractory state;
- a neuron is at rest in the absence of synaptic current (the conductance s = 0);
- a neuron generates spikes when the synaptic conductance overcomes some threshold value which is close to its maximum;
- the temporal intervals of generation of spikes are relatively short with respect to the characteristic timescales of evolution of the synaptic conductance;

These properties allow one to formulate the rules of evolution in time of ψ_i, i.e. the rules of a CA:

- (i) If i-th synapse at the instant of time t is in the state P0 (ψ_i(s_i(t)) = P0), and there is j ∈ K_i such that ψ_j(s_j(t)) = P11 or ψ_j(s_j(t)) = P22, then depending on the values of synaptic activation thresholds (denote them by θ_ji) at the instant t + T1 either it becomes excited (i.e. ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) = P11 or ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) = P21) or it stays in the rest (i.e. ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) = P0).
- (ii) If i-th synapse at the instant of time t is in the state P21, i.e. ψ_i(s_i(t)) = P21, then ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) = P22 independently of other synapses. Moreover, a P21-synapse cannot excite another synapse.
- (iii) If ψ_i(s_i(t)) = P11 or ψ_i(s_i(t)) = P22, then ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) = P0 independently of the states of other synapses.
- (iv) Because of the existence of the refractory period, the synapses in the states P11, P21, P22 do not feel the action of other synapses.

These statements imply that for the fixed i the state ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) at the instant t + T1 is uniquely determined by

- the state ψ_i(s_i(t)) at the previous instant of time t;
- the states of the j-th synapse, j ∈ K_i, at the instant of time t;
- the values of the threshold constants θ_ji, j ∈ K_i.

| ψ_i | P0 | P11 | P21 | P22 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ψ_j, j ∈ K_i |  |  |  |  |

Hence, ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) = F_i(ψ_i(s_i(t)), {ψ_j(s_j(t))}, {θ_ji}, j ∈ K_i), where F_i is a function determined by the statements (i)–(iv). This relation defines a synaptic CA determined on the graph of connections G. In Table 1 we summarize the rules of the CA. In what follows we identify ψ_i(s_i(t)) with the symbol ψ_i and ψ_i(s_i(t + T1)) with the symbol ψ̄_i.

Finally, if we fix the values of thresholds θ_ji, the rules of the CA take the following form: ψ̄_i = F_i(ψ_i, {ψ_j}, j ∈ K_i), where ψ̄_i, ψ_i ∈ P.

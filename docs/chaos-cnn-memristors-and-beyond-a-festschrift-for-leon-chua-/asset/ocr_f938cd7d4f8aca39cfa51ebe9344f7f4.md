He concluded that the synchronized motion of the pendula was due to the imperceptible motion of the coupling wooden bar, see e.g. [7], and [17]. Note that the phenomenon discovered by Huygens is particularly remarkable because the synchronization of the pendula is not induced by a control action. Rather it is a consequence of the interaction between the pendula through the coupling, i.e. the wooden bar.

Other systems, where synchronization occurs spontaneously, are biological systems, where it has been found that two or more cells, independently of their nature, or functioning, can show sympathetic behaviour, i.e. they synchronize by using signaling messengers (as for example light) as the coupling signal [9].

In other cases, the synchronization phenomenon is artificially induced by for instance a control law. This kind of synchronization is called, for obvious reasons, controlled synchronization [3]. As an example, the reader may consider the synchronization of wireless network terminals, which are of vital importance in communication systems [22].

Synchronized behavior can also be observed in chaotic systems. Due to their extreme sensitivity to initial conditions, these systems are not expected to synchronize in a natural way. However, it is possible to find suitable couplings/controllers such that two (or more) chaotic systems may synchronize, see e.g. [1, 6, 14].

Nowadays, there exist several couplings/controllers used to synchronize two chaotic systems, see e.g. [4]. As a matter of fact, these couplings can be used even in the case that the oscillators are not chaotic. A different situation occurs when the oscillators are linked through Huygens’ coupling.

In this case, the limit synchronizing behaviour of the system is not always the same. In fact, whether the oscillators are chaotic or not, has an influence in the steady state (synchronized) behaviour, as will be discussed in this manuscript.

This work focuses on the synchronization problem of two chaotic oscillators with Huygens’ coupling. A simplified version of Huygens’ system of pendulum clocks is considered, where the clocks have been replaced by two chaotic oscillators. The purpose is to study the existence of (in-phase and anti-phase) synchronized chaotic motion in the oscillators. Two well known oscillators are used in the analysis, namely Duffing and van der Pol oscillators. The occurrence of synchronization in the oscillators is validated by means of experiments.

The outline of this chapter is as follows. In Sec. 2, a simplified Huygens’ model is presented and the experimental setup is briefly described. In Sec. 3, the synchronization of two Duffing oscillators with Huygens’ coupling is analyzed. Then, in Sec. 4, it is shown that by driving the coupling bar with a periodic signal, it is possible to induce chaotic behaviour in the oscillators. A nonlinear controller is designed to achieve (in-phase and anti-phase) synchronization in the oscillators when they behave in a chaotic fashion. This is experimentally validated. Next, in Sec. 5, a similar analysis is conducted for a pair of van der Pol oscillators. Finally, a discussion and some conclusions are presented in Secs. 6 and 7, respectively.

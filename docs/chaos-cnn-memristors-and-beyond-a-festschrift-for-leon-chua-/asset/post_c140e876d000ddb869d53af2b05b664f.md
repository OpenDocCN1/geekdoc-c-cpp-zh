## 6.2.3. *Delay-enhanced synchronization in a network of Hindmarsh-Rose neurons*

For the case of $y\to x$ coupled Hindmarsh-Rose neurons, $\lambda_{max}$ is depicted in Fig. 19(c). There exists a stable synchronized regime in the interval $\epsilon\in(0.28,1.23)$ for $\tau=0.0$, whereas for $\tau=0.8$ $\lambda_{max}$ indeed shows a large regime of a stable synchronized state in the range $\epsilon\in(0.036,2.31)$ extended for both smaller and larger values of $\epsilon$. It is a strong evidence that stable synchronization can occur even for smaller values of the coupling strength in the presence of the coupling delay, which is obtained only for sufficiently larger values of $\epsilon$ without delay in the same network. $N_{max}$ can also be increased proportionally as discussed above. Further, the isocline line corresponding to $\lambda_{max}=0$ as a function of $\epsilon\in(0,3)$ and $\tau\in(0,6)$ in Fig. 20(c) also confirms the extended regime of a stable synchronized state, compared to that for $\tau=0$, almost in the entire range of the coupling delay we have considered. Contours of $\lambda_{max}$ estimated from (18) for $y\to x$ coupled HR neurons depicted in Fig. 21(c) clearly delineate the stable synchronous regimes in the complex plane of the MSF for the coupling delay $\tau=4.0$.

## 6.2.4. *Delay-enhanced synchronization in a network of Chua’s circuit systems*

Figure 19(d) depicts $\lambda_{max}$ of $z\to x$ coupled network of Chua’s circuit systems illustrating stable synchronization in the interval $\epsilon\in(1.84,2.85)$ in the absence of delay in the coupling. On the other hand for appropriate delay in the coupling, that is for $\tau=0.3$, $\lambda_{max}$ in Fig. 19(d) confirms the phenomenon of delay enhanced synchrony by extending the stable synchronization to the interval $\epsilon\in(1.52,2.88)$. The contour for $\lambda_{max}=0$ in Fig. 20(d) confirms the above phenomenon. Further, the isocline line of $\lambda_{max}$ (see Fig. 21(d)) estimated from (18) for $z\to x$ coupled network of Chua’s circuit systems in the complex plane also corroborates the existence of stable synchronization.

Thus, we have demonstrated the existence of an extended regime of stable synchronized states at both smaller and larger values of the coupling strength in an arbitrary network induced by the coupling delay using the MSF. The generic nature of our results is also exhibited using three paradigmatic models that have been widely exploited in the literature for synchronization studies.

## 6.3. *Delay-induced synchronization in complex networks*

In this section, we demonstrate the phenomenon of delay-induced synchronization, that is inducing synchronization where there is no synchronization at all without time-delay, in an arbitrary network again using the master stability formalism. In order to demonstrate the phenomenon of delay-induced synchronization, we consider the different component coupling that fall under the class $\Gamma_0$, according to the classification by Huang *et al.* [57], which does not exhibit any synchronization at all. In the following we demonstrate delay-induced synchronization in an arbitrary network of $x\to y$ and $y\to x$ coupled Rössler systems and $z\to y$ coupled Chua’s circuit systems [67].

### 6.3.1. *Delay-induced synchronization in a network of Rössler systems*

Now, we consider the network of $x(t-\tau)\to y(t-\tau)$ coupled Rössler systems and the largest transverse Lyapunov exponent, estimated from the MSF (16), depicted in Fig. 22(a) for $\tau=0$ indicates that the network is in an asynchronous state as $\lambda_{max}>0$ in the entire range of $\epsilon$. In contrast, in the presence of a delay under the same coupling, the network of Rössler systems is entrained to synchronous oscillations as confirmed by the sign of $\lambda_{max}$ in Fig. 22(a) for $\tau=1.8$ attributing to the phenomenon of delay-induced synchronization in the network.

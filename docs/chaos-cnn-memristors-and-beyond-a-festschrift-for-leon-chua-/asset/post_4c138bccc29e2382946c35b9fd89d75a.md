He concluded that the synchronized motion of the pendula was due to the imperceptible motion of the coupling wooden bar, see e.g. [7], and [17]. Note that the phenomenon discovered by Huygens is particularly remarkable because the synchronization of the pendula is not induced by a control action. Other systems, where synchronization occurs spontaneously, are biological systems, where it has been found that two or more cells, independently of their nature, or functioning, can show sympathetic behaviour, i.e. they synchronize by using signaling messengers (as for example light) as the coupling signal [9].

In other cases, the synchronization phenomenon is artificially induced by for instance a control law. This kind of synchronization is called, for obvious reasons, controlled synchronization [3]. As an example, the reader may consider the synchronization of wireless network terminals, which are of vital importance in communication systems [22].

Synchronized behavior can also be observed in chaotic systems. Due to their extreme sensitivity to initial conditions, these systems are not expected to synchronize in a natural way. However, it is possible to find suitable couplings/controllers such that two (or more) chaotic systems may synchronize, see e.g. [1, 6, 14].

Nowadays, there exist several couplings/controllers used to synchronize two chaotic systems, see e.g. [4]. As a matter of fact, these couplings can be used even in the case that the oscillators are not chaotic. A different situation occurs when the oscillators are linked through Huygens’ coupling.

In this case, the limit synchronizing behaviour of the system is not always the same. In fact, whether the oscillators are chaotic or not, has an influence in the steady state (synchronized) behaviour, as will be discussed in this manuscript.

This work focuses on the synchronization problem of two chaotic oscillators with Huygens’ coupling. A simplified version of Huygens’ system of pendulum clocks is considered, where the clocks have been replaced by two chaotic oscillators. The purpose is to study the existence of (in-phase and anti-phase) synchronized chaotic motion in the oscillators. Two well known oscillators are used in the analysis, namely Duffing and van der Pol oscillators. The occurrence of synchronization in the oscillators is validated by means of experiments.

The outline of this chapter is as follows. In Sec. 2, a simplified Huygens’ model is presented and the experimental setup is briefly described. In Sec. 3, the synchronization of two Duffing oscillators with Huygens’ coupling is analyzed. Then, in Sec. 4, it is shown that by driving the coupling bar with a periodic signal, it is possible to induce chaotic behaviour in the oscillators. A nonlinear controller is designed to achieve (in-phase and anti-phase) synchronization in the oscillators when they behave in a chaotic fashion. This is experimentally validated. Next, in Sec. 5, a similar analysis is conducted for a pair of van der Pol oscillators. Finally, a discussion and some conclusions are presented in Secs. 6 and 7, respectively.

## 2. A Generalized Huygens’ Model

In the original setup of pendulum clocks used by Huygens in his discovery of synchronization, the coupling between the clocks was a wooden bar supported by two chairs, see Fig. 1(a). In its simplest form, the wooden bar at the top of two chairs can be modeled by a rigid bar of one dof elastically attached to the fixed world. The pendulum clocks can be replaced by two damped/driven pendula as depicted in Fig. 1(b). This simplified model is able to mimic the in-phase and anti-phase synchronized motion of the pendula observed by Huygens 300 years ago, see e.g. [2,12,13,19].

**Fig. 1.** (a) Original Huygens drawing cf. [7, 17]. (b) Simplified Huygens’ model.

A further simplification of Huygens' system of Fig. 1 is the schematic model depicted in Fig. 2. In this model, the pendulum clocks are now replaced by two (actuated) mass-spring-damper oscillators and the wooden bar at the top of two chairs is again modeled by a one dof suspended rigid bar. Note that in this model rotational angles are replaced by translational displacements. The control inputs \(u_1, u_2\) of the two oscillators can be used to guarantee steady-state oscillations and/or to modify the inherent dynamic properties of the system, like mass, stiffness, and damping, in a desired way.

![](img/039e7d3c23390584a66737067db8b75b_355_0.png)

**Fig. 2.** Two mass-spring-damper oscillators with Huygens' coupling.

As a matter of fact, the diagram of Fig. 2 schematically describes an experimental setup that has been constructed in order to study synchronization of coupled oscillators in general. By means of feedback control, it is possible to adjust the dynamical properties of the setup. Obviously, this opens the possibility of analyzing synchronous behaviour in a wide variety of dynamical systems. This experimental platform can be used to validate (robustness of) the theoretical findings. Moreover, the obtained experimental results may indicate new and interesting directions for research. For a detailed description of the setup, the reader is referred to [16].

The following questions are addressed in the remainder of this chapter: given the Huygens system (two pendulum clocks coupled through an elastic support), is it possible to replace the pendulum clocks by another type of second order nonlinear oscillator and still to observe the synchronized motion? In particular, what happens when the pendulum clocks are replaced by two chaotic oscillators? Since synchronization of the chaotic behaviour will not occur naturally, is it possible to induce the synchronized behaviour by means of a control action? The first question is addressed in the next section, whereas questions 2 and 3 are considered in Sec. 4 and Sec. 5.

## 3. Synchronization of Two Undriven/Undamped Duffing Oscillators with Huygens’ Coupling

In this section, it is shown that two undriven and undamped nonlinear oscillators with Huygens’ coupling can synchronize in a natural way. For the sake of illustration, a pair of Duffing oscillators is considered in the analysis. However, it should be stressed that at this point it is possible to consider any other pair of arbitrary second order nonlinear oscillators.

Consider the system depicted in Fig. 2 and assume that the undamped oscillators are identical, i.e. consider \(m=m_1=m_2\), \(k_1=k_2\), and \(b_1=b_2=0\). Furthermore, assume that the stiffness characteristic corresponding to both oscillators is given by:

\[
k_i(\Delta x_i)=k\,\Delta x_i+k_3\,\Delta x_i^3,\qquad i=1,2, \tag{1}
\]

where \(\Delta x_i=x_i-x_h\), \(k_3\in\mathbb{R}^+\), and \(k\) can be either positive or negative. As stated before, the oscillators are unforced, so \(u_1=u_2=0\). Also assume that the coupling bar is free to oscillate as in Huygens' case, i.e. \(u_h=0\). Then, using Newton's second law, the (idealized) equations of motion of system of Fig. 2 become

\[
\begin{aligned}
m\ddot{x}_1&=-k(x_1-x_h)-k_3(x_1-x_h)^3,\\
m\ddot{x}_2&=-k(x_2-x_h)-k_3(x_2-x_h)^3,\\
m_h\ddot{x}_h&=-m_1\ddot{x}_1-m_2\ddot{x}_2-k_h x_h-b_h\dot{x}_h.
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]

System (2) describes the dynamics of two undamped and unforced Duffing oscillators coupled through an elastically supported and damped rigid bar. In order to prove the existence of a synchronized regime, the total energy of system (2) is computed

\[
V_u=\frac{1}{2}m_h\dot{x}_h^2+\frac{1}{2}k_h x_h^2+\frac{m}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\dot{x}_i^2+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\Delta x_i}k_i(s)\,ds. \tag{3}
\]

Next, the time derivative of \(V\) is computed along the trajectories of (2). This yields

\[
\dot{V}_u=-b_h\dot{x}_h^2\leq 0. \tag{4}
\]

The analysis continues by using LaSalle’s invariance principle [8], from which it follows that all the system trajectories converge to a set where

\[
x_1=-x_2,\qquad \dot{x}_1=-\dot{x}_2,\qquad \dot{x}_h=x_h=0. \tag{5}
\]

This shows that two undamped, undriven Duffing oscillators with Huygens’ coupling will synchronize in anti-phase. Obviously, this result suggests that the coupling used by Huygens (a suspended bar) may be used to synchronize two arbitrary oscillators. In [21], an experimental analysis related to equation (2) has been presented. Other experimental results related to the phase synchronization of arbitrary oscillators with Huygens’ coupling are provided in [15] and [20].

## 4. Synchronization of Two Chaotic Duffing Oscillators with Huygens’ Coupling

The first objective of this section is to show that, by *driving* the coupling bar with an external periodic excitation and by *varying* its mass, it is possible to trigger the onset of chaos in two second order nonlinear oscillators coupled through a suspended rigid bar (i.e. with Huygens’ coupling). When the oscillators are in a chaotic state, the synchronization phenomenon discussed in Sec. 3 will not occur spontaneously. Consequently, the second objective is to show that by using the well-known master/slave configuration cf. [10] it is possible to achieve (controlled) synchronization, i.e. the slave system will be forced to follow the chaotic dynamics of the master oscillator. Figure 3 visualizes this. However, a mutual synchronization scheme can be considered as an alternative [18].

Consider again the mechanical system of Fig. 2 and assume that (1) holds. Subindices \(i=1,2\) are now replaced by the subindices \(i=m,s\), which refer to the master oscillator and slave oscillator, respectively. Then, the equations of motion of the system are:

\[
\ddot{x}_m=-\frac{b}{m}(\dot{x}_m-\dot{x}_h)-\frac{k}{m}(x_m-x_h)-\frac{k_3}{m}(x_m-x_h)^3. \tag{6}
\]

\[
\ddot{x}_s=-\frac{b}{m}(\dot{x}_s-\dot{x}_h)-\frac{k}{m}(x_s-x_h)-\frac{k_3}{m}(x_s-x_h)^3+\frac{1}{m}u_s. \tag{7}
\]

\[
\ddot{x}_h=-\frac{m}{m_h}(\ddot{x}_m+\ddot{x}_s)-\frac{k_h}{m_h}x_h-\frac{b_h}{m_h}\dot{x}_h+\frac{u_h}{m_h}. \tag{8}
\]

where \(u_s\) is the slave control input to be designed. This set of equations again describes a pair of two identical Duffing oscillators with Huygens’ coupling. Moreover, assume that the oscillators have negative nonlinear stiffness, i.e. \(k_3<0\). The rigid coupling bar is externally excited with the periodic force

\[
u_h=q\cos\omega t, \tag{9}
\]

where \(q\) is the amplitude of the force and \(\omega=2\pi f\) is the angular frequency of the external excitation.

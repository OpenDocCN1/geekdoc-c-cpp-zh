Spiral waves were initiated by a wave that propagates in a unidirectional fashion: waves blocked at a point near the site of initiation leading to unidirectional propagation and a wavefront free end. The free ends propagate back toward the initiation site, and, as more tissue is recruited into the wavefront, are able to pass through previously unexcitable regions leading to a spiral wave. The spiral wave’s period increased over time (by up to 80%), and eventually (after on average 30 rotations) the spiral wave terminates. Wave termination appears to be due to a loss of excitability of the cardiac substrate, leading to the wave tip to collide with the border of the monolayer.

Although in most cases bursting was associated with clearly visible reentrant waves in our experimental system, in some cases bursting target waves were observed [6]. Since our imaging system has relatively low spatial resolution, we could not rule out that reentry occurred at smaller (cellular) scale. As cardiac cells tended in our hands to be either quiescent or beat periodically, we primarily focused our attention on the contribution of reentry to bursting rhythms. However, recent results from other groups strongly suggest that bursting is an intrinsic property of cardiac cells. Harada [23] showed, using an elegant experimental system which captured long term recordings of single cells and small cell clusters at high magnification, that cardiac cells display bursts of activity, and can even spontaneously transition between regular periodic activity bursting rhythms. Tong and Holden [42] showed that a cardiac cell model displays bursting dynamics for certain parameters. Finally, Himel [24] showed that pharmacological agents can induce bursting rhythms in the form early and delayed after depolarizations in isolated neonatal cardiac cells. These results suggest that communication between cardiac cells may not be relevant to the genesis of bursts in this system, and that spiral waves are the result of, but do not cause, bursting. However, it is difficult to reconcile this interpretation with the presence of cohesive waves in culture, the increasing period of rotation during bursts, or the prevalence of non bursting target waves in more well connected monolayer systems. Further studies, such as the use of higher resolution imaging equipment and simulations with populations of bursting cardiac cells, are needed to resolve this issue.

# 3. Simulating Network Bursting with CAs

Simulating the dynamics observed in the isotropic cell culture experimental system posed several challenges. First, the monolayer culture is heterogeneous at microscopic space scales, with cell-free patches and dense tissue areas. We assume that these heterogeneities play a critical role as the unidirectional propagation observed at initiation sites doesn’t occur in homogeneous excitable systems. It’s possible to use PDE and ODE models to simulate cardiac propagation at microscopic scales, however, it would be difficult to draw conclusions from a single simulation run since heterogeneities in our system are distributed throughout the tissue and do not have a single representative geometry. Furthermore, simulations at microscopic scales typically use many subcompartments per cell [40] and are therefore computationally expensive. Up-scaling cell-level simulations to macroscopic scales while running a sufficient number of geometries to gain confidence in the generalizability of the results is therefore impractical. Our group [3, 41] and others [1] have used a more simplified approach, consisting of a uniform reaction diffusion system with randomly distributed defects at simulation grid points. However, the physical interpretation of a single grid point defect in this case is difficult: in general, the underlying lattice spacing should be an order of magnitude smaller than the heterogeneities being simulated in order to avoid artefacts. Since we are primarily interested in how network activity generates bursting, we opted to use CAs as cellular activity is abstracted to a small number of essential states. The computational efficiency of CAs allow parameter sweeps and randomly generated geometries to be quickly simulated, and the simplicity of the formulation facilitates analysis of wave speed and propagation failure from first principles [3, 5].

A simple cellular automata model was developed which demonstrates that the addition of a limited number of rules can result in bursting behaviour in groups of spontaneously active, excitable, but nonendogenously bursting cells. Spontaneous activity is simulated by giving all grid points a probability of firing when excitable. The slowing and eventual annihilation of the spiral is modelled by adding a fatigue term.

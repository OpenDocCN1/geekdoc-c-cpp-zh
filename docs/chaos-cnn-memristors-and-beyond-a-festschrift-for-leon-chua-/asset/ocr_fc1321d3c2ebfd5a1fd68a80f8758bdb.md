# 6.2.3. *Delay-enhanced synchronization in a network of Hindmarsh-Rose neurons*

For the case of $y\to x$ coupled Hindmarsh-Rose neurons, $\lambda_{max}$ is depicted in Fig. 19(c). There exists a stable synchronized regime in the interval $\epsilon\in(0.28,1.23)$ for $\tau=0.0$, whereas for $\tau=0.8$ $\lambda_{max}$ indeed shows a large regime of a stable synchronized state in the range $\epsilon\in(0.036,2.31)$ extended for both smaller and larger values of $\epsilon$. It is a strong evidence that stable synchronization can occur even for smaller values of the coupling strength in the presence of the coupling delay, which is obtained only for sufficiently larger values of $\epsilon$ without delay in the same network. $N_{max}$ can also be increased proportionally as discussed above. The generic nature of our results is also exhibited using three paradigmatic models that have been widely exploited in the literature for synchronization studies.

# 6.3. *Delay-induced synchronization in complex networks*

In this section, we demonstrate the phenomenon of delay-induced synchronization, that is inducing synchronization where there is no synchronization at all without time-delay, in an arbitrary network again using the master stability formalism. In order to demonstrate the phenomenon of delay-induced synchronization, we consider the different component coupling that fall under the class $\Gamma_0$, according to the classification by Huang *et al.* [57], which does not exhibit any synchronization at all. In the following we demonstrate delay-induced synchronization in an arbitrary network of $x\to y$ and $y\to x$ coupled Rössler systems and $z\to y$ coupled Chua’s circuit systems [67].

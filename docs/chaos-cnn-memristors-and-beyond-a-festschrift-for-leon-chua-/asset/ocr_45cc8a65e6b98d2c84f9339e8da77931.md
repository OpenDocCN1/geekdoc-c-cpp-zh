A periodic orbit may contain two, or more, states that are equivalent under rotation. Therefore, it is necessary to introduce a measure of the number of states between a state of the periodic orbit and any rotated version of it.

Definition 14.3. The period τ of a periodic orbit is the smallest integer for which ∃σ ∈ ℤ such that x^τ = S^σ(x⁰), where S it the right shift operator and x^τ = ℳ_N^τ(x⁰). This implies that x_i^τ = x_{i+σ}⁰

for any cell x_i⁰ of x⁰, where the subscript refers to the position of the cell within the array.

The value of |σ| ≠ 0 can be conveniently used to make a further distinction among periodic orbits: if |σ| ≠ 0 then it is called Bernoulli-σ_τ periodic orbit; otherwise, it is called period-τ periodic orbit. For period-τ orbits l = τ, ie, the length of the orbit is equal to its period; for Bernoulli-σ_τ orbits l > τ, ie, the length of the orbit is greater than its period.

Fig. 7. (a) Photo of a multi-chip implementation of disparity tuned neurons. Two AER retinas provide analogs of left and right eye inputs to the system. The insert board converts the AER address format used by the silicon retina to that used by the Gabor-type chips. The left and right eye inputs are then processed by orientation selective neurons tuned to vertical orientations. The outputs of the orientation tuned neurons of the left and right eyes are then combined to yield neurons tuned to binocular disparities. (b) Measured binocular tuning curves shown as images where the gray level of pixel encodes the response of the complex cell to a pair of spatial bar stimuli focused onto the left and right retina chips. Each position indicates the firing rate for an input presented at a particular position in the left and right eyes. Darker gray levels indicate higher firing rates. (A, D) The tuning curves of neurons tuned to negative disparities by phase and position encoding. (B) The regions of the maps corresponding to different disparities. (C, F) The RF maps of neurons tuned to positive disparities by phase and position encoding.

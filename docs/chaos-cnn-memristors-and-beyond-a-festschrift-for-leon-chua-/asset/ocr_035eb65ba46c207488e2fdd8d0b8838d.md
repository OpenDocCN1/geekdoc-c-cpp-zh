# 6.1. Master stability formalism for delay coupled networks

We consider an arbitrary delay coupled network with N identical chaotic units in accordance with the MSF [56] as follows:

```
ẋᵢ(t) = f[xᵢ(t)] − σ ∑_{j=1}^{N} gᵢⱼ h[xⱼ(t−τ)], (15)
```

where xᵢ ∈ ℝᵐ, i = 1,…,N, f is a nonlinear function describing the dynamics of the individual units, σ is the overall coupling strength, G = gᵢⱼ is a N × N matrix, which determines the topology of an arbitrary network, h is a coupling function and τ is the coupling delay. The invariant synchronization manifold is assured by the zero row sum, that is ∑_{j=1}^{N} gᵢⱼ = 0 ∀ i, of each row of the connectivity matrix G. At first, we consider the arbitrary network as undirected such that G is symmetric with real eigenvalues and then we extend our analysis to directed networks, in which case G is asymmetric leading to complex eigenvalues. It has also to be noted that our results remains unaffected in either cases, as the imaginary parts of the complex eigenvalues has only a rotational effect [59], as we will discuss below in detail. If G is diagonalizable, then the stability of the synchronization manifold is governed by the eigenvalues of G and the following block diagonalized variational equation for the transverse modes

```
ξ̇(t) = Df[x(t)]ξ(t) − ε Dh[x(t−τ)]ξ(t−τ). (16)
```

Here, x(t) is the dynamics within the synchronization manifold, ξ(t) is the perturbation transverse to the synchronization manifold, which could be complex, Df[x(t)] is the Jacobian evaluated on the synchronization manifold and Dh[x(t−τ)] is an m × m matrix that determines which components of the oscillator are coupled. The normalized coupling parameter ε = σ γₖ, where γₖ is an eigenvalue of the coupling matrix G, k = 0,1,2,…,N−1. It is to be noted that the eigenvalue γₖ is more

generic because of the arbitrary G and it could be the eigenvalue corresponding to any specific topology determined by G. Here, k = 0 corresponds to the synchronization manifold with γ₀ = 0, while all the other eigenvalues for k > 0 correspond to the transverse modes determining the stability of the synchronization manifold x(t). Thus, the synchronized state is stable for a given network topology, characterized by G, when all the eigenvalues of G for k > 0 are negative (or when all the transverse Lyapunov exponents of (16) are negative). Considering the fact that G could have complex eigenvalues arising from asymmetric couplings [59], the generic variational equation, that is the MSF, can be written as

```
ξ̇(t) = Df[x(t)]ξ(t) + (α + iβ) Dh[x(t−τ)]ξ(t−τ), (17)
```

where, α and β are the real and imaginary parts of the eigenvalue. Separating ξ(t) into the real part ξᵣ(t) and the imaginary part ξᵢ(t), we get

```
ξ̇ᵣ(t) = Df[x(t)]ξᵣ(t) + α Dh[x(t−τ)]ξᵣ(t−τ) − β Dh[x(t−τ)]ξᵢ(t−τ), (18a)
ξ̇ᵢ(t) = Df[x(t)]ξᵢ(t) + α Dh[x(t−τ)]ξᵢ(t−τ) + β Dh[x(t−τ)]ξᵣ(t−τ). (18b)
```

The maximal transverse Lyapunov exponent (λ_max) estimated from Eqs. (18) yields a complex surface in the (α, β) plane, namely the master stability surface of the considered network of oscillators. For a given value of the coupling strength σ and the delay time τ, one can locate a point in the complex surface and the sign of λ_max at that point determines the stability of the synchronization manifold.

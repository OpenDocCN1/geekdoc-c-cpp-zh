
Substituting
$x = v_1, \quad y = v_2, \quad z = -i, \quad w = \varphi, \quad \alpha - \frac{1}{C_1},$ 
$\beta = \frac{1}{L}, \quad \gamma = \frac{r}{L}, \quad \xi = G, \quad C_2 = 1, \quad R = 1$
the above equation can be rewritten as follows:

```
\begin{equation}
\frac{dx}{dt} = \alpha(y - x + \xi x - W(w)x),
\frac{dy}{dt} = x - y + z,
\frac{dz}{dt} = -\beta y - \gamma z,
\frac{dw}{dt} = x.
\end{equation}
```

In all considered types of circuits the nonlinear characteristic of the memristor was chosen as a three-segment piecewise linear one (Chua's diode type, comp. [15]). The slopes of the characteristic were chosen accordingly with the required property of local activity or passivity [15].

Bifurcation analysis of the five circuits were carried out numerically. Matlab environment was used with the following integration routine parameters: simulation time = [0 200], initial conditions ic = [0.01 0.01 0.01], numerical integration via the routine ode45 (with InitialStep = 0.1, RelTol = 1e-9, AbsTol = 1e-9, MaxStep = 0.01.

### 3.1. Bifurcation trees for Chua's circuit and oscillator

Chua's circuit and Chua's oscillator show very similar trajectory behavior (see Figs. 3 and 4) and what follows structure of bifurcation trees (compare Figs. 2-6). Bifurcations observed in Chua's circuit when parameter α was varied reveal two branches of the bifurcation corresponding to starting the experiments from two different (symmetric) initial conditions. Because of the perfect symmetry of the nonlinear characteristic we can observe coexisting symmetric trajectories in a wide range of system parameters — here we see periodic orbits of various length coming in pairs, each observed for an appropriate selection of initial conditions. Eventually the two attractors merge forming single

chaotic attractor of the double scroll type. It can be noticed that the branches of the bifurcation tree (still corresponding to coexisting trajectories symmetric in state space) are not symmetric due to the selection of the variable selected in the display of the bifurcation tree. However the bifurcation points and the regions of existence of various types of attractor coincide. The structure of periodic orbits is extremely interesting and very rich — some very long trajectories can be seen in periodic windows e.g. period-12, period-23, period-32. The magnification of the parameter range in which period doubling sequence can be observed (Fig. 6) reveals the existence of a standard type period-3 window characteristic for many kinds of chaotic regimes observed in physical systems. Standard period doubling route to chaos is observed.

### 3.2. Trajectories and bifurcations in Chua's memristive oscillators

When testing the nonlinear behavior of the memristive oscillators first various types of trajectories have been observed together with their changes when varying the selected circuit parameter. In all simulation experiments we used the normalized circuit equations. In the circuit no.1 when changing the parameter β one could observe a period-adding sequence leading to chaos as shown in Fig. 7.

As in all the considered circuits the nonlinear function was selected as a piece-wise linear one with symmetry against the origin, in many cases one could observe coexisting periodic or chaotic attractors symmetric against the origin for the same parameter value. Such attractors when the bifurcation parameter is being varied often change in size, collide in the state space and form one chaotic attractor as shown in Fig. 8.

In the second type of oscillator we could observe an interesting type of heart-shaped periodic trajectories which undergo standard period doubling sequence of bifurcations (see Figs. 9(a)-9(d). The two symmetric chaotic attractors eventually collide and form one chaotic attractor as depicted in Figs. 9(e)-9(h).

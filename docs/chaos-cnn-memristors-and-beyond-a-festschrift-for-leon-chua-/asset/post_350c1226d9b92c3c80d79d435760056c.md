## 1. Introduction

The first part of Leon Chua’s career, and the work that brought him to prominence within the international research community, saw him lay many of the foundations of nonlinear circuit theory [2,5]. This involved, among many other fundamental contributions, the definition of new circuit elements, most notably the memristor [3]. The explosion of interest in the memristor in recent years is a testament to the far-sighted and fundamental nature of Chua’s work.

Another aspect of this work employed the concept of the Lagrangian from classical mechanics [1,7] In Lagrangian mechanics, the Lagrangian is a function of a set of generalized coordinates ηi, velocities η̇i (and possibly time) from which the dynamical equations of the system can be derived.

The application of this concept in the context of circuit theory had been studied by a number of authors in the middle decades of the last century, and this work was significantly advanced by Chua in papers such as [4,8]. Chua presented an introduction to the properties of circuit elements characterized by a Lagrangian in his graduate courses in the University of California, Berkeley, and in a number of keynote addresses, but did not include it in any of his textbooks. The aim of this review paper is to capture that presentation and include it as a further record of his contributions in nonlinear circuits research and education.

## 3.3. Response to noise

In view of forecasting catastrophes we have studied the response of the DR system to an external white noise, as we did for the saddle-node bifurcation in the potential model [15]. We solved the set of stochastic equations

\begin{cases} \dot{u} = v - 1 + \eta_u f_u(t) \\ \dot{v} = -\gamma^2\left(u + \frac{1}{\xi}(\theta + \ln(v))\right) + \eta_v f_v(t) \\ \dot{\theta} = -v\left(\theta + (1 + \epsilon)\ln(v)\right) + \eta_\theta f_\theta(t), \end{cases} \tag{61}

where $f_u, f_v, f_\theta$ are three independent noise functions with short memory time (of order $\delta t_{eqk}$) and

$\eta_u, \eta_v, \eta_\theta$ are amplitudes smaller than $\gamma^2$. We have calculated the correlation functions of $x(t)$, $x$ standing for any function $u$, $v$, or $\theta$,

\begin{align*} \Gamma_x(t, t - t') &= \langle x(t) x(t - t') \rangle \\ &\quad - \langle x(t) \rangle \langle x(t - t') \rangle, \tag{62} \end{align*}

which are functions depending on the delay $t'$ but also on the time $t$, and the standard deviations

\sigma_x(t) = \Gamma_x(t, t). \tag{63}

The results are very different from those of the potential model. First because here the standard deviation $\sigma_v$ of the response dramatically increases with time before the event, much more than the velocity $v(t)$ itself, that could be seen as a precursor. This contrasts with the saddle-node case, where the standard deviation of the response grows nearly like $v(t)$ and therefore cannot be used as precursor. The two functions $\sigma_v(t)$ and $v(t)$, are drawn in Fig. 12(a) in Log scale, showing that the growth of $\sigma_v$ is three

orders of magnitude larger than the growth of v(t), on the time interval (tf − t0, tf), where the final time tf = tc − 0.1 corresponds to about two months before the event for typical earthquake case.

Secondly the correlation time τx (defined as the the width of correlation function) decreases very strongly during the large time interval ∼ 2t0 before the event, see Fig. 12(b). This could be understood as a sort of speed-up, contrary to what happens in the potential model where the correlation time becomes maximum shortly before tc, in agreement with the well-known critical slowing-down phenomenon (or critical opalescence in spatial systems).

This striking result appears when we compare Figs. 13 and 4(b) which display the correlation time

τx of the fluctuating part of the displacement for the DR and saddle-node model respectively. For the saddle-node model τx increases slowly before it get enhanced (critical slowing down) at time ∼ tc − t0 (t0 being the intermediate time scale in this system[15]). For the DR system we find that the correlation time of the response to noise decreases very slowly until the singularity (speed-up). The duration of the slow decrease of τx is of order few t0, that is a noticeable part of the total period for the DR model. In summary, while the maximum of τx occurs before the catastrophe in both cases, the time interval is very large for the DR model, while is short for the saddle-node model, because we have

```
t0/δt_eqk ∼ γ, (64)
```

and

```
t0/δt_eqk ∼ a^{-1/3}, (65)
```

respectively. Therefore the effects (rapid increase of τx /slowing-down for the saddle-node model versus slow decrease of τx/ speed-up for the DR model) seem opposite while they both result from the fact that the memory time of the response shifted from tc by a time interval of order t0.

The two relations (64) and (65) explain why the response to noise is changed much earlier in the DR model than in the saddle-node model. Using parameters pertinent for earthquake, the precursor time t0 is about few hours for the saddle-node model,

Both results are related to the linear stability of the SM since the memory time of the response to external noise is maximum when the temporal integral of the largest stability exponent is close to zero, that gives a “precursor time” of order t₀ in both cases.

## 4. Discussion and Perspectives

We have presented two models of “relaxation dynamics” with similar looking behavior: long periods of “quiet” slow motion are interrupted by fast transitions, followed again by slow phases, etc. However the two models are deeply different, both because they show different mechanisms of slow to fast and reverse transitions, but also because they are objectively different in their response to an external source of noise. The saddle-node like model shows the standard “critical slowing down” with time correlations becoming more and more long ranged before the transition, something well-known in many areas. Here the correlation time reaches a maximum within a short time interval before the transition, of order $t_0$. On the contrary the DR-inspired model shows a very slow squeezing of the correlation time of the response as the transition approaches, the opposite of the dynamical saddle-node transition. In both cases the maximum of the correlation time occurs at time $t_c-t_0$, which is delayed from the catastrophe time $t_c$ by the time scale $t_0$, but in the DR model the intermediate time $t_0$ is much longer than in the saddle-node model, relations (64)–(65). Therefore the response to noise of the two models can be summarized as follows: a slowing down which persist practically until $t_c$ for the saddle-node model, and a very slow “speed-up” behavior until $t_c$ for the DR-model. The sharp difference between the two models could perhaps be shown in real life conditions, and could be a way of proving (or disproving) the validity of some models of stick-slip behavior with laboratory experiments of solid friction.

We note that the geophysics literature describes many phenomena arising during the days or hours preceding strong earthquakes, that seems better in favor of the saddle-node model involving the short intermediate time. These phenomena have been attributed [17] to the emergence of electrical currents at the earth surface, arising from hard crystalline rocks near dip-slip faults, and caused by charge carriers, stress activated in the rocks deep below.

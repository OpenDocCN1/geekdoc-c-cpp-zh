# 6.3.2. Delay-induced synchronization in a network of Chua’s circuit systems

Fig. 23(a), where λ_max is shown as a surface in the (τ, ε) plane. An increase in the color intensity corresponds to a decrease in λ_max and the isoclinic lines encompasses the stable synchronous regime corresponding to λ_max < 0. It is evident from Fig. 23(a) that without delay in the coupling the network of Rössler systems does not exhibit any synchronous oscillation. On the other hand, as τ is increased the network attains synchronization after an appreciable delay and the range of stable synchronization increases with it up to a certain threshold. Further increase in the delay results in a decrease in the spread of stable synchronization and finally reaches asynchronization for further larger delays. The corresponding master stability surface, i.e. the surface of λ_max in the complex (α, β) plane, estimated from the MSF (18) is illustrated in Fig. 24(a) for the coupling delay τ = 1.8, in which the stable regimes are displayed by the contours of λ_max < 0.

A similar result is also observed for another choice of the delayed coupling, namely y(t − τ) → x(t − τ). The sign of λ_max (see Fig. 22(b)) as a function of ε for both with and without delay in the coupling asserts the phenomenon of delay-induced synchronization in the network of Rössler systems. The surface of λ_max in Fig. 23(b) and its contours for λ_max < 0 clearly illustrate the above phenomenon. Further, the complex (α, β) plane of the MSF in Fig. 24(b) for τ = 4.9 with λ_max < 0 confirms the same.

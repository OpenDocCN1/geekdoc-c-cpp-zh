New design methods for computationally intensive 2D topographic problems had been presented in [114]. An efficient wave-like solution of a wave problem was demonstrated in [115].

Implementing non-topographic problems on topographic architectures is another major algorithmic challenge. In [116] such a problem was solved for particle filters. A general problem is the geometric partition of program control, since it might not be possible for a global control. Such a problem was solved via local data driven methods [117].

Indeed, the new world of algorithm on topographic physical architectures is just opening for the computer science and computer engineering communities.

## 10. Conclusions and Major New Challenges

Motivated and sometimes required by the physical constraints and possibilities of semiconductor technology and new discoveries in neurobiology, respectively, the very notion of computers and computing has gradually changed during the last 20 years. Major new aspects are

- the need for the introduction and key role of a geometric or topographic address of a processor or a memory,
- the need for using continuous valued basic processing building blocks, and
- to use a new kind of algorithmic quality, the alpha recursive functions where elementary instructions are spatial-temporal dynamic processes

Nanoscale technologies, both CMOS and non-CMOS, both digital or analog-and-logic computing models need the spatial-temporal dynamic properties of algorithms. This fact leads to a new kind of computer engineering and computer science that will help to design a realm of new algorithms for kilo- and mega-processor chip-based computers. In addition, the non-CMOS nanoscale technologies require this kind of theory and design methods due to the use of new building blocks and interactions defined by physical processes.

Recent discoveries and related quantitative spatial-temporal nonlinear models in neurobiology also provide for new ideas, among others the role of synchronization in detecting events.


## 4. Cellular Automata as Space-time Dynamical Systems

Here we wish to understand the system under the joint action of the time dynamics (i.e. the map $f$) and the shift $\sigma$. The joint action of the shift and the CA-map, $\sigma^m f^n$, $m\in\mathbb{Z}, n\in\mathbb{N}$, is much more complex than the simple nonlinear dynamics of the CA-map. What are the consequences of the invariance of CA-dynamics under the spatial translation: are the chaotic properties invariant under translation? Is there a family of CA-maps for which the Lyapunov exponents and the entropy are also invariant under translation? Considering the CA-map action in a moving reference frame, is there an influence of the velocity of the frame on the growth of local perturbations? These are some reasons to compute those quantities under the maps $\sigma^m f^n$. It is quite possible to define the Lyapunov exponents and the entropy of $\sigma^m f^n$, $\lambda^\pm(\sigma^m f^n)$, $h(\sigma^m f^n)$, as above for any CA. They represent the entropy for an observer moving on the axis $Ox$ with a velocity $v=\frac{m}{n}$. Lyapunov exponents for a moving observer have been considered independently in the theory of coupled maps on lattice dynamical systems [10] in a different setting, in relation to open-flow fluids.

It is also possible to extend the above definitions from $(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}$ to continuous parameters $(x,t)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}^+$ allowing to consider the entropy in the continuous space-time, for an arbitrary velocity $v=\frac{x}{t}$ [6, 7]. We shall not go into these details. For the Wolfram elementary CA the computation of this entropy denoted $h(x,t)$ (or $h_\mu(x,t)$ in the metric case) is still an open question. Only in the permutative case we have partial answers in some cones of the space-time. The entropy is fully known in the bipermutative case. For the uniform Bernoulli measure we have the following results:

$$h_\mu(x,t)=\begin{cases}(x+t)\log k & t\le x,\ x>0 \quad (I),\\ 2t\log k & -t\le x\le t \quad (II),\\ (t+|x|)\log k & t\le -x,\ x<0 \quad (III).\end{cases}$$

![](img/039e7d3c23390584a66737067db8b75b_118_0.png)

It is interesting to note that this entropy is a continuous function of $(x,t)$, symmetric under the reflection $x\longrightarrow -x$, independent on the form of the bipermutative map and also invariant under the spatial translation as soon as $v=\left|\frac{x}{t}\right|\le 1$. Only when $v>1$, the spatial entropy start to change linearly in terms of $x$. It is possible to express the directional entropy in terms of the velocity $v$ by using the homogeneity property of the entropy: $h_\mu(cx,ct)=ch_\mu(x,t)$, for $c>0$. Then, $h_\mu(x,t)=th_\mu(v,1)$. The directional entropy by time unit as a function of the reference frame velocity $v$, $h_\mu(v,1)$, is then constant as the velocity has not reached the threshold of the interaction range, it increases linearly in terms of $v$ as this threshold is exceeded. It would be interesting to compute the curves of $h_\mu(v,1)$, in all Wolfram CA to see exhaustively the influence of the dynamics on the directional entropy behaviour.

The directional Lyapunov exponents in the bipermutative maps have been also computed as follows:

$$\lambda^+_{(x,t)}=\begin{cases}0 & \text{in }(I),\\ t-x & \text{in }(II),\\ t-x & \text{in }(III),\end{cases}\qquad \lambda^-_{(x,t)}=\begin{cases}x+t & \text{in }(I),\\ x+t & \text{in }(II),\\ 0 & \text{in }(III).\end{cases}$$

Here, we can also make the same observations about the dependence of the directional Lyapunov exponents on the velocity since they have the same homogeneity property. A computation of the directional entropy in other example is given in [18]. We have the following equality for the bipermutative rules:

$$h_\mu(x,t)=h_\mu(\sigma)\left(\lambda^+_{(x,t)}+\lambda^-_{(x,t)}\right).$$

In general, for any invariant measure $\mu$ that is ergodic with respect to $\sigma$ we have the Shereshevsky inequality [7]

$$h_\mu(x,t)\le h_\mu(\sigma)\left(\lambda^+_{(x,t)}+\lambda^-_{(x,t)}\right).$$

Several upper bounds have been obtained for the CA, they show a privileged role of the permutative rules. Among the 256 Wolfram rules, the maximal entropy and the maximal Lyapunov exponents are reached by the bipermutative ones. This means that these cellular automata maps are the most chaotic. However they are simple since they are surjective.

A detailed study of the space-time chaotic quantities may bring new surprises and especially concerning the existence of CA-maps for which they are translation invariant.

Also included in the envelope was a personal letter inviting me to work with him at NOEL on CNN. I was excited and humbled by the personal attention he showed to a newly minted university graduate. Leon was the only professor who had contacted me and I promptly accepted his offer. So I arrived in September 1990 at UC Berkeley without knowing much about how to do scientific research.

# 2. NOEL

During my time there, NOEL was located on the mezzanine of the second floor in Cory Hall, the Electrical Engineering and Computer Sciences building of the University of California at Berkeley. It is a happy coincidence that NOEL is LEON spelled backwardsᶜ since Leon Chua’s leadership and vision define NOEL and in every part of the lab I see his involvement.

Several of the bookcases were filled with reprints and research reports documenting the work that was done in the lab since the 1970’s. It was quite interesting to see the evolution of research in nonlinear circuit theory, chaotic dynamics and Cellular Neural Networks by perusing these reports. There were several desks in the lab assigned to students and visitors. When I arrived at NOEL there was no desk available for me, and I was given a desk in the next office (i.e. the other “half” of the split room). Since all that separated my desk from NOEL were bookcases, I could talk to my colleagues in the lab simply by shouting. After a year or two I had my own desk in the lab proper, and I promptly covered it with books and papers (Fig. 2). The main area of the lab had an eclectic assortment of equipments. There were oscilloscopes, logic analyzers, power supplies and standard circuit building tools such as soldering irons, breadboards, wire wrap tools, and discrete components.

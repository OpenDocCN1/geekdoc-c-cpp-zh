
There is a relation between the existence and the uniqueness of the solution of a single nonlinear resistive circuit and the existence and uniqueness of the solutions of an infinity of linear resistive circuits. These linear resistive circuits have in fact all the same linear resistive circuit structure, or they have one among a well-defined finite number of such structures.

Thus, we have to specify, which linear resistive circuit structures are associated with a nonlinear resistive circuit. This association can be partially understood by noting that the difference between any two solutions of the given nonlinear resistive circuit is the solution of a certain linear resistive circuit. Note that we do not perform any linearization, the two solutions can be far apart. Furthermore, in any two solutions, the voltage source voltages and the current source currents must be identical. Therefore, in the linear resistive circuit the sources must be zero. Now, if the linear resistive circuit has exactly one solution, it must be the trivial one, where all currents and voltages vanish. This implies that the two solutions of the nonlinear resistive circuit must be identical. As the two solutions were arbitrarily chosen, the solution of the nonlinear resistive circuit must be unique.

Since we postulate an arbitrary pair of solutions for the nonlinear circuit the linear circuit can have all kinds of resistor values. Therefore, we must consider simultaneously a whole set of resistive circuits. They have all the same linear resistive circuit structure, or slightly different ones. These are the linear resistive circuit structures associated with the nonlinear resistive circuit. They are obtained by taking the circuit graph and by replacing on each edge the circuit element with a label of Fig. 6. On most edges there is only one possible label. Sometimes, two different labels are possible. If there are m such edges, 2^m resistive circuit structures have to be analyzed, in principle. In practice, much less effort is sufficient.

Finally, according to Sec. 3, in order to establish the global existence of state equations, the existence and uniqueness of the solution of the associated nonlinear resistive circuit has to be proved. We can combine this association with the association of the nonlinear resistive circuit with linear resistive circuit structures. We limit this to our basic set of circuit elements plus charge controlled passive memristors. The extension to other circuit elements is rather obvious.

## Definition 42.2.

A pair of conjugate trees (t1, t2) in a linear resistive circuit structure is defined by the following conditions:
- t1 contains all norator, no nullator, all voltage source, no current source and some resistor edges.
- t2 contains no norator, all nullator, all voltage source, no current source and the same resistor edges as t1.

A uniform partial orientation of the resistor edges is an orientation of part of the resistor edges, such that the following two conditions are satisfied:
- Each orientated resistor edge belongs to at least one uniform loop composed of orientated resistor edges, voltage source edges and norator edges.
- Each orientated resistor edge belongs to at least one uniform cutset composed of orientated resistor edges, current source edges and norator edges.

A loop is uniform if all orientated edges of the loop are directed in the same way around the loop. A cutset is uniform, if all orientated edges of the cutset are orientated in the same way across the cutset. A partial orientation of the edges is trivial if no edge is orientated.

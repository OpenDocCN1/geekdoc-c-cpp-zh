As in quantum optics and in skyrmion research [18], we can get very far using semiclassical methods alone.

Second, these solitons could be used as a kind of alternative quark model, based on magnetic and electric charge rather than the more complex properties assumed today, as proposed years ago by Julian Schwinger [34]. This would fit with the startling empirical findings of Macgregor [35] of Lawrence Livermore, who found evidence that simple electromagnetic coupling (alpha) seems to be decisive in determining the masses and lifetimes of all the known elementary particles. Though I strongly disagree with Comay’s proposed Lagrangian for electromagnetism [36], his concept of the nucleon as a bound state of four objects, core and quarks, all defined my electric and magnetic charge and mass rather than the usual properties, and some of the associated empirical arguments, may be worth thinking about further, in conjunction with the other sources cited in [34].

Finally, the unification of such classical field models with general relativity would be quite straightforward, using the recipe given by Moshe Carmelli [37]. This should also be true for the alternative model of gravity by Moffat, which currently appears more promising in view of empirical data in 2012 suggesting a low density of dark matter.

Of course, this would still leave open the mysteries related to superweak interactions (CP) violation, dark energy, dark matter, and perhaps some other interesting phenomena beyond today’s standard model of physics — but it would be a great step forwards in any case.

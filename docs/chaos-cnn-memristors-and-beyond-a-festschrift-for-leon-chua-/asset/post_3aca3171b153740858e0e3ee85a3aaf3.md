### 4.1. Case A: in-phase synchronization

When the synchronized motion of the oscillators is in-phase, the following equations should be satisfied:

e = x_s − x_m = 0 and ė = ẋ_s − ẋ_m = 0.  (10)

Writing down the error dynamics for system (6)–(8) yields:

ë = 1/m [ − b ė − k e − k_3 e³ − 3k_3 (x_s x_m − x_h (x_s + x_m) + x_h²) e + u_s ].  (11)

Similar to [11], the control input u_s is defined:

u_s = −k_p e − k_v ė + η,  (12)

where k_p > 0, k_v > 0, and η is a compensation term to be designed. Substitution of this control in (11) yields the following closed-loop error dynamics:

ë = 1/m [ −(b + k_v) ė − (k + k_p) e − k_3 e³ − f(x_s, x_m, x_h) e + η ],  (13)

where f(x_s, x_m, x_h) = 3k_3 (x_s x_m − x_h (x_s + x_m) + x_h²).

**Proposition 1.** If the gains k_p, k_v satisfy

k_p > −k,  k_v > −b,  (14)

and the compensation term η is designed such that

η = 3k_3 (x_s x_m − x_h (x_s + x_m) + x_h²) e,  (15)

then, system (13) is asymptotically stable, i.e.

lim_{t→∞} e = 0,  lim_{t→∞} ė = 0.  (16)

**Proof.** The closed-loop (11)–(12), with η as given in (15), is described by

ë = 1/m [ −(b + k_v) ė − (k + k_p) e − k_3 e³ ].  (17)

Now, consider the candidate Lyapunov function

V = 1/2 (ė + α e)² + 1/2 [ ((k + k_p)/m) + α ( (b + k_v)/m ) − α² ] e² + k_3/(4m) e⁴,  (18)

where α ∈ ℝ⁺ is a constant. Function (18) is positive definite if α satisfies

α < (b + k_v)/m.  (19)

The time derivative of (18) along the solutions of system (17) is given by

V̇ = −( (b + k_v)/m − α ) ė² − α(k + k_p)/m e² − α k_3 / m e⁴.  (20)

Next, assume that α satisfies (19). If the gains of the controller (12) are chosen according to (14) then the closed-loop system (17) is asymptotically stable and in consequence

lim_{t→∞} (x_s − x_m) = 0,  and  lim_{t→∞} (ẋ_s − ẋ_m) = 0.  (21)

### 4.2. Case B: anti-phase synchronization

It has been shown in Sec. 3 that when the coupling bar is free to oscillate, i.e. u_h = 0, the undamped oscillators will synchronize in anti-phase and as a consequence x_h ⟶ 0. However, in the present case the coupling bar is being excited with a periodic force and anti-phase synchronization in the same spirit of Huygens will not occur. Therefore, a control input is designed (in analogy with the in-phase synchronization case) to realize anti-phase synchronization of the slave oscillator with respect to the chaotic motion of the master oscillator.

The anti-phase synchronization errors are defined by

e_1 = x_s + x_m = 0  and  e_2 = ė_1 = ẋ_s + ẋ_m = 0.  (22)

Consequently, the error dynamics of the coupled Duffing oscillators (6)–(7) are

ė_1 = e_2,
ė_2 = 1/m [ −b e_2 − k e_1 − k_3 e_1^3 + γ(x_m, x_s, e_1) + u_s ].  (23)

where

γ(·) = −k_3 [ 3 x_m x_s e_1 − 3 x_h (x_m^2 + x_s^2) + 3 x_h^2 e_1 − 2 x_h^3 ] + 2k x_h + 2b ẋ_h.  (24)

As before, the control input u_s is defined as:

u_s = −γ(x_m, x_s, e_1) − k_p e_1 − k_v e_2.  (25)

Note that the closed-loop dynamics (23)–(25) coincides with the closed-loop dynamics (17) with e = e_1 = (x_s + x_m) and ė = e_2 = (ẋ_s + ẋ_m). Then, by following the same reasoning as used in Proposition 1, it is possible to show that system (23)–(25) with k_p and k_v satisfying (14) is asymptotically stable, i.e. the oscillators will asymptotically synchronize in anti-phase.

### 4.3. Experimental validation

The synchronized motion of the chaotic Duffing oscillators with Huygens’ coupling is experimentally verified in this section. As a testbed, the electromechanical setup schematically depicted in Fig. 2 is used. For a detailed explanation about this setup, the reader is referred to [16]. By means of feedback control, the inherent mechanical properties are adjusted such that the dynamical behaviour of the setup is described by the set of equations (6)–(8). In all experiments, the following parameter values are considered: b = 0.0840 [Ns/m], k = −0.2310 [N/m], k_3 = 1.6·10^5 [N/m^3], ω = 1.2 [rad/s], q = 0.225 [N], k_h = 388.71 [N/m], b_h = 3.2656 [Ns/m], m = 0.210 [kg], and m_h = 42 [kg].

In the first experiment, the in-phase synchronized motion of oscillators (6)–(7) is investigated. The nonlinear feedback controller (12) is implemented for the slave oscillator (7). For this experiment, the oscillators are released from the initial conditions x_m(0) = 5 [mm], x_s(0) = −3 [mm], and x_h(0) = ẋ_h(0) = ẋ_m(0) = ẋ_s(0) = 0. The values of the gains in the controller are k_p = 1.3 [N/m] and k_v = 0.5 [Ns/m]. With this choice, (14) is satisfied.

The corresponding experimental results are depicted in Fig. 5. The transient responses for the displacements, the synchronization error, and the control input are shown in Figs. 5(a), 5(c), and 5(e) respectively.

For t ∈ [0,20), the controller is not activated and the behaviour of the oscillators is uncorrelated as depicted in Fig. 5(a). At t = 20 [s], the controller is activated and as a consequence, the slave oscillator synchronizes in-phase with the chaotic trajectory of the master. This can be seen in Fig. 5(b), where the complete time series corresponding to the displacements of the oscillators is depicted and synchronized chaotic steady-state behaviour is achieved.

The synchronization error e = x_s − x_m obviously is large when the control action is not applied yet, but once the controller is activated, this error becomes practically zero as illustrated in Figs. 5(c) and 5(d) respectively. In theory, this error should become exactly zero. However, in practice the oscillators are not exactly equal and consequently in the experimental result there is a small error. Fig. 5(e) shows that the control effort stays very small once in-phase synchronization is realized. Fig. 5(f) presents a projection of the displacements corresponding to the oscillators onto the plane (x_m, x_s). The light grey part of the curve corresponds to the transient behaviour, whereas the dark grey part of the curve indicates the synchronized steady-state behaviour.

## 5. Synchronization of Two Chaotic van der Pol Oscillators

In the previous section, a (slightly) modified version of Huygens’ system was considered, where the pendulum clocks were replaced by two Duffing oscillators. This section is in the same spirit, in the sense that now the pendula are replaced by two van der Pol oscillators. In order to achieve (in-phase or anti-phase) synchronization, again a nonlinear control action is included to one of the oscillators.

Consider again the system depicted in Fig. 2 and assume identical oscillators, i.e. m = m_1 = m_2, k = k_1 = k_2, and b = b_1 = b_2. Furthermore, assume that the damping characteristic corresponding to both oscillators is given by

β_i(x_i, Δẋ_i) = -b(1 - a x_i^2) Δẋ_i,    i = m, s,    (26)

where Δẋ_i = (ẋ_i - ẋ_h). Note that subindices i = 1,2 in Fig. 2 have been replaced by subindices m, s respectively.

For now, consider the case u_m = u_h = 0. Then, the equations of motion of the system are

ẍ_m = (b/m)(1 - a x_m^2)(ẋ_m - ẋ_h) - (k/m)(x_m - x_h)

ẍ_s = (b/m)(1 - a x_s^2)(ẋ_s - ẋ_h) - (k/m)(x_s - x_h) + (1/m) u_s

ẍ_h = - (m/m_h)(ẍ_m + ẍ_s) - (k_h/m_h) x_h - (b_h/m_h) ẋ_h.    (27)

We now determine the dynamics of the delay by the following equation:
\[\frac{d\tau}{dt} = -\beta \frac{d\Delta}{d\tau}.\tag{8}\]

Here Δ is the same as Δ̄ but after a substitution (approximation) τ(t′) → τ(t). We choose the value of the parameter β in such a way as to satisfy 1/β ≫ 1/ν, i.e. the delay time τ evolves much slower than the time of averaging. The form of Eq. (8) suggests that the functional Δ may be regarded as a potential in the space of delay τ. Since the dependence Δ = Δ(τ) has a form of parabolas in the neighborhoods of periods of UPOs, one may expect that the nearby solution of varied delay will converge towards the minima, τ → Tk, where Tk is the period of the desired UPO.

After denoting the gradient as G(t) ≡ dΔ/dτ, and differentiating it with respect to time, we can collect the above results into one system:
\dot{\mathbf{X}} = \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{X}, K[s(t) - s(t - \tau(t))]), \tag{9a}
\dot{\tau} = -\beta G, \tag{9b}
\dot{u} = \gamma[s(t - \tau(t)) - u], \tag{9c}
\dot{G} = -\nu G + 2\gamma[s(t) - s(t - \tau(t))][s(t - \tau(t)) - u]. \tag{9d}

Equation (9c) is introduced for an approximate estimation of the time-derivative, i.e. we set \dot{s}(t - τ(t)) ≈ γ[s(t - τ(t)) - u] in Eq. (9d). Equations (9b)–9(d)) enable us to find the dynamics of the variable delay τ(t). They are valid if the parameters satisfy the following inequalities: 1/γ ≪ 1/ν ≪ 1/β.

In Fig. 4 we show the results of numerical simulations for the period-one UPO of the Rössler system, when the state variable X = (x, y, z) satisfies Eqs. 1. We see that the adaptive algorithm forces the variable delay to converge towards the period of UPO, although the initial delay τ0 = 3 differs considerably from the period T ≈ 5.88 of the UPO.

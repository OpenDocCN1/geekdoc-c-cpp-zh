# 5. Fun and Games

It was not always just work at NOEL. As children of the video game generation, many of the students unwound by playing computer games. The World Wide Web was just in its infancy, and somehow I became the person responsible for downloading games demos from an FTP server onto our computers for our enjoyment. Maciej Ogorzałek, who is a frequent visitor, would copy these games demos from the PC for his young sons. Since Maciej spent some time “testing” these games out in the lab, we were always wondering whether he was downloading the games for himself to play.

This was also long before Massive Multiplayer Online Games (MMOG) such as World of Warcraft existed. One popular game we played was Xtrek, a (primitive by today’s standard) MMOG that ran on Unix workstations. On one occasion we sneaked into a conference room to play Xtrek by projecting it onto the big screen, making us feel like Captain Kirk on the bridge of the starship Enterprise. One time we were playing Xtrek late at night on the computers in NOEL when Leon walked in to tell us about some ideas he had. Luckily he was so excited to tell us what he was thinking about that he did not notice all the moving spaceships on our computer screens. How I wish I had a boss key¹ at the time although a spreadsheet would not have made much sense either for the research that we were doing.

We did many other activities together, including Hong Kong movie nightsᵐ and The Simpsons marathon. Bert Shi and I were both part of the UC Berkeley Wushu team. I participated in the annual UC Berkeley Wushu competition one year and placed third in the beginner category. I should mention that there were a total of 3 entrants in that category. One year we had a lab picnic at the Berkeley marina and Philippe Deregel volunteered to prepare crêpes for all of us. Since Philippe is French, he pronounced crêpe like a Frenchman would, which sounded to my unsophisticated ears very much like a word in English that is much less palatable.

When Arnold Schwarzenegger came to the UC Berkeley campus to film “Junior”, Ann Chen and

I went to see the movie being made and found out just how tedious movie making is. We sat there for several hours only to see a few takes being shot over and over again. Reading countless papers and writing pages of code don’t seem so boring after that.

One highlight over the years was being invited to Leon’s house for fine cheese, dinner and drinks. In addition to walls of bookcases filled with books on a diverse set of topics, Leon’s house also contained many interesting artifacts from around the world that he has collected over the years. Fig. 6 shows me attempting to blow a horn (albeit unsuccessfully). Leon is a fine wine connoisseurᵉ and his wine cellar is always a delight to visit. He would show us his extensive French wine collection including bottles of wine of size Nebuchadnezzar (i.e. 15 liters!) and his homemade earthquake-resistant wine holders.

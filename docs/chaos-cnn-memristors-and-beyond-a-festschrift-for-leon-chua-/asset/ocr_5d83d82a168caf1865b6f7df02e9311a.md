where \(\nu=\sqrt{1/LC}\), \(Z=\sqrt{L/C}\), and \(\alpha\) is an arbitrary function. With the new variables

\[\beta(\tau)=\alpha(2l\tau/\nu),\qquad \tau=(\nu t/l-1)/2,\tag{6}\]

the combination of (5) and the second condition (2) yields the continuous argument difference equation

\[\beta(\tau+1)=f(\beta(\tau)),\qquad \tau\ge -1,\tag{7}\]

with the nonlinearity giving by the piecewise linear (single-valued or multivalent) map

\[f:z\mapsto A_sz-B_s\quad\text{for }z\in I_s,\ s=0,\pm1,\tag{8}\]

where

\[A_s=-1+c_s,\qquad B_s=\frac{c_s}{2}\left[E+s\left(1-\frac{m_0}{m_s}\right)\right],\]
\[c_s=\frac{2Z}{1/m_s+R+Z},\qquad m_{-1}=m_1,\]
\[I_0=\left\{z:\left|z-\frac E2\right|\le |\delta|\right\},\]
\[I_{\pm1}=\left\{z:\pm\left(z-\frac E2\right)>\delta\right\},\qquad \delta=\frac{m_0Z}{c_0}.\]

The initial states \(v_0(x)\) and \(i_0(x)\) determine the initial condition for Eq. (7):

\[\left.\beta(\tau)\right|_{\tau\in[-1,0]}=\phi(\tau),\]

\[
\phi(\tau)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\phi(-\theta)+Z\psi(-\theta)}{2}, & -1\le \tau<-\dfrac12,\\[4pt]
\dfrac{-\phi(\theta)+Z\psi(\theta)}{2}, & -\dfrac12\le \tau<0,
\end{cases}
\qquad \theta=l(1+2\tau),\tag{9}
\]

therewith \(\phi(\tau)\) has the property: \(\phi(0)=f(\phi(-1))\), which is a result of the consistency of the initial and boundary conditions for the BVP.

Equation (7) can be considered as the continuous family of the same maps (oscillators) \(z_n\mapsto z_{n+1}=f(z_n),\ z_0=\phi(\tau)\), “placed” at all points of the interval \([-1,0]\). Thus the dynamics of the lossless transmission line is reduced to

the dynamics of a continuous family (continuous “lattice”) of uncoupled oscillators.* Cumulative vibrations of these oscillators are described by the (infinite-dimensional) dynamical system

\[S:\phi(t)\mapsto f(\phi(t)),\tag{10}\]

generated by Eq. (7) on the space of continuous piecewise smooth initial functions \(\phi:[-1,0]\to\mathbb R\) such that \(\phi(0)=f(\phi(-1))\).

# 3.2. Managing map

It follows from the previous section that the behavior of solutions for the BVP (1), (2) is determined by the one-dimensional (1D) map \(f\), which we will refer to as the managing map. As is shown in [22] the dynamics of \(f\) can prove to be intricate only in the following two cases:

\[(*)\quad -m_0Z\le E<1+m_0R;\]
\[(**)\quad -m_0R-\frac{m_0}{m_1}<E<-m_0Z.\]

We restrict ourselves to the case (*). Then \(f\) is single-valued and has an invariant interval \(I\) that “takes up” all the points \(z\in\mathbb R\): for any \(z\in\mathbb R\), there exists an \(n\ge0\) such that \(f^n(z)\in I\). Moreover, \(f\) is equivalent on \(I\) to the two-segment map

\[
g(z)=
\begin{cases}
p(z-a)+1, & z\in[0,a],\\
q(1-z), & z\in(a,1],
\end{cases}
\qquad 0<p<1,\quad q>1,\quad a=1-1/q,\tag{11}
\]

with

\[p=\frac{-1+(Z-R)m_1}{1+(Z+R)m_1},\qquad q=\frac{1-(Z-R)m_0}{1+(Z+R)m_0}.\]

The two-parameter family of maps (11) has been studied closely enough, in particular, its main properties were given in the above-mentioned our works 90s. Here we discuss in more detail those needed for further.

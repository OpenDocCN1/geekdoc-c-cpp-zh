Although showing many relevant neural characteristics, Izhikevich neurons are less biological plausible, but not so time consuming and can be implemented in dedicated digital hardware [69] with the advantage of behavior flexibility as a function of the parameters used. The single layer CNN standard cells have the advantage of a straightforward and already existing analog hardware implementation [35]: this guarantees high performance in terms of computation time. On the other hand, CNNs have widely been demonstrated to be a paradigm for the generation and also learning of complex spatial-temporal dynamics [106], some of them derived by biological signals [96], even in single layer cells [83]. Moreover, complex spatial-temporal dynamics were shown to take place also in digital hardware implementations [46]. For the proposed systems, we studied how the sequence of active states is sensitive to the incoming stimuli and independent from initial conditions, underlining the high capacity of the networks. We also present a compact code representing each different sequence in order to extract from the spatial-temporal pattern a compact information as a code for the class associated with the current stimulus.

Finally, in [16], we proposed an application demonstrating the possibility to use a WLC network in an artificial cognitive architecture in the challenging task of autonomous robot navigation where the ability to correctly interpret the incoming stimuli is crucial in presence of unpredictable and potentially hostile situations. In particular, the WLC network plays the role of perceptual core within an already developed control architecture discussed in the previous section, which models the sensing-perception-action loop, where, instead of using an RD-CNN, we use a WLC network, to generate the perceptual states, and consider, in place of the emerging Turing patterns, the spatiotemporal patterns coded by the sequence of activated neurons.

Also in this case the sequence constitutes a compact representation of the surrounding environment and is associated with an action by a selection network. To perform a given task, the robot is provided with no a priori knowledge about the

action to be associated with each pattern and learns by trial and error. Learning is implemented by two mechanisms: an unsupervised learning acts at the sensing block allowing the system to modulate the “basins of attraction” of the sequences, while a simple reward-based reinforcement learning builds associations between spatiotemporal sequences and actions. All the details on the implementation and results are reported in [16].

# 5. Conclusions

CNNs are for sure a paradigm for complexity. Under this framework this chapter aims to survey a line of research focussed at using the CNN paradigm for the representation of spatial temporal neural dynamics in Biorobotics and Brain science. In particular the former aspects were dealt with referring to the capability of CNNs to act as central pattern generators for the locomotion control in legged and swimming robots. The design of the CNN was performed starting from the definition of the dynamic behavior of the single motor neuron (CNN cell) to the design of the proper templates to obtain different locomotion pattern. The suitability of the approach was tested in the most diverse situation in really working robot prototypes. The second part of the chapter focuses on how to add to these moving machines additional perceptual capabilities. Once again the capability of CNNs to represent and reproduce complex dynamics is exploited. Perception is here conceived as a complex phenomenon, in which environmental condition are considered and used to extract a compact and concise representation of the state of the surroundings, useful for taking on the action which increases a performance index, defined according to the robot mission. The architecture used as the site for the emergence of these perceptual states is once again a RD-CNN showing Turing Patterns. The hidden metaphor here is that, somewhere in the brain, the collection of all the information from the environment leads to the emergence of a perceptual state similar to the generation and evolution of a form in morphogenesis. More interestingly the same neuron used for the Central Pattern Generator implementation was used within the Turing pattern CNN generator, so as to indicate that very different dynamics can be obtained using the same basic dynamics (CNN neuron model) and the same paradigm (the RD-CNN).

Substituting (34) into (33) and recognizing that $I_M = -I_L$, one can show that the three-element chaotic circuit is described by three ordinary differential equations:

$$\frac{dV_C}{dt} = \frac{1}{C} I_L \tag{37}$$

$$\frac{dI_L}{dt} = -\frac{1}{L} (V_C + \beta (X^2 - 1) I_L) \tag{38}$$

$$\frac{dX}{dt} = -I_L - \alpha X + I_L X \tag{39}$$

## 6. Summary

In this chapter, we have described six-, five-, four- and three-element chaotic circuits. Chua’s original five-element design contained two linear capacitors, a linear inductor, a linear resistor, and a symmetric piecewise-linear voltage-controlled nonlinear resistor $N_R$. The circuit was first simulated by Matsumoto [21] and then realized experimentally by Zhong and Ayrom [27]. Chua et al. established the universality of the circuit by showing that the augmented six-element Chua’s oscillator can reproduce all know dynamical behavior reported for third-order autonomous systems [7,8].

Elwakil and Kennedy increased the frequency of operation of Chua’s circuit by a factor of 100 by replacing the voltage feedback op amps by current feedback op amps in the two-NIC realization of the Chua diode [11]. Kennedy also reduced the frequency of operation by replacing the inductor with a gyrator and capacitor; this is the slowest reported Chua's circuit. Cruz and Chua used the same gyrator-based structure in the first integrated circuit implementation of Chua's circuit [9].

In addition to the piecewise-linear Chua diode, Matsumoto et al. also simulated the circuit with a cubic nonlinearity [22]. A Chua's circuit with a cubic nonlinearity was first implemented by Zhong [26], and later simplified by O'Donoghue et al. to produce the fastest reported Chua's circuit [25]. Kennedy showed that the Colpitts oscillator with a piecewise-linear nonlinearity is equivalent to Chua's circuit with an asymmetric Chua diode [18].

Returning to a circuit topology first proposed by Chua, Barboza and Chua reported chaos in a simplified four-element version of Chua's circuit [2]; this contains two linear capacitors, a linear inductor, and a Chua diode. Muthuswamy and Chua showed that chaos could be produced in an autonomous circuit containing just three circuit elements: a linear capacitor, a linear inductor, and a memristor [24].

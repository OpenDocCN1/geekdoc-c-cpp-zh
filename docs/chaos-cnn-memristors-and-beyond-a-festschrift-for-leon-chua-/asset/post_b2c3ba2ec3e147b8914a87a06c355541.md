In spite of the fact that Turing presented here a kind of a prototype case study only (later it turned out that his cells were locally active), the model showed a dramatically different kind of computation: using analog dynamics in spatially distributed processing cells with local interaction, and a spatial-temporal wave was the key computing mechanism.

John von Neumann, using a prototype by Stanislaw Ulam, designed a spatially distributed array of identical processing digital cells with local interactions to make self-reproducing automata [2]. Though all state variables and interactions were digital, the operational mechanism was spatial-temporal, a kind of waves. Again, the topographic, geometric address of a processing cell was important. Through a sequence of steps, due to the refractory period of his cells (reflecting the very simplified model of a neuron, understood at that time), the Cellular Automaton was able to reproduce a static pattern.

Interestingly, the need and practical possibility to use arrays of processors was far away in computer engineering for a while.

In 1973, an important principle, extending Maxwell’s result for linear resistive networks to nonlinear resistive networks, the stationary principle [12], paved the way for a new way of thinking about computation as the optimum of a multivariable energy function. Namely, in any nonlinear resistive network, the stationary operating point is the minimum of a potential energy, the so called co-content function, as a sum of co-content functions of each resistor, actually being an integral of the two variables, the port current and voltage.

In the 1980’s, several attempts had been made to use simple analog array processing cells (actually, dynamic circuits) for computing. The name “neural networks” was coined. A simple “neuron” model, as an amplifier with one time constant and output bipolar saturation nonlinearity, as well as a sum of weighted threshold function, has been considered. The typical network was a many input many output model, having all neuron outputs with weighted feedback connections to each other neuron input. The generic inputs were also connected with weights to all neuron inputs. Hence, having n neurons and n generic inputs, 2n² weights defined the network. Placing inputs to the network, the settled outputs

coded the input event. This, so called Hopfield network [13], had a learning rule to train the weights to perform some functionality, basically leading to some optimization of a cost function, typically a quadratic function of the state variables. Since this publication, 1984, many solutions have been proposed to solve various problems. In 1987, it was shown that, indeed, this optimization is nothing else than an application of the stationary principle for a nonlinear memoryless network. The name of the game was: solving a many-variable problem by a single circuit dynamics (with many analog processors/amplifiers).

The problem with these fully connected architectures was that practical implementations were feasible only in very limited size, and the full feedback led to instabilities (the stability criterion of symmetry cannot be ensured). The many learning algorithms though proved to be practical and useful, even if running on a single digital processor emulating the many processor/neuron network dynamics. Moreover, the neuromorphic character was mainly missing, and being one single network, its capability very limited. The term “neural” became an exaggeration in view of the great variety and complex behavior of real neurons.

## 3. The Standard CNN (Cellular Neural/Nonlinear Network) as the Practically Feasible Prototype Solution and Related Stability Issues

The seminal publication of the Cellular Neural/nonlinear Network (CNN) architecture in 1988 provided a different scenario [4]. The topographic array of simple cells (“neurons”) with their important geometric address, as well as the precedence of locality (the cellular character) proved to be extremely important. In this architecture the robustness and stability is not restricted to the symmetry of interconnections [14].

This architecture provided for the physical, silicon and optical, implementation of kilo-cell/processor computing arrays. Note that due to the local feedback the indirect global interconnection is also achieved. That nature can be exploited in implementing not only local operators, however, global wave operators as well. In this

way, complex PDEs, wave equations, even Navier Stokes equations for fluid dynamics can be represented by CNN operators in discretized space [15, 20, 21].

The cellular locally connected character is the basis for many neuromorphic models, literally converting real neural anatomy, their receptive fields, into cloning templates (local interaction patterns) [16,17]. This neuromorphic property can be used to generate CNN models for many sensory organs for vision, hearing, touching, etc. The key step is to convert the receptive field organization into a local interaction pattern defined by the cloning template of CNN, an obvious strength of this prototype architecture.

During the first few years of the 1990s, about a hundred various templates had been developed for different purposes, including visual, logic, PDE operators, as well as mathematical morphology, etc. [18].

Non-standard CNNs became important soon, by introducing nonlinear and delay templates, as well as space varying and adaptive templates [19].

Stability studies were important right from the beginning. Moreover, it turned out soon that analytic properties of CNN dynamics, depending on the templates, will be a vital condition to design stored programmable spatial-temporal computers. The issue is that under various initial and boundary conditions the feedback template defines the analytic properties, including stability and the maximal value of state variables.

The first challenge was to study various non-reciprocal CNN Templates, in particular when the feedback template (A template) is not centrally symmetric. The first result [14] showed that sign symmetry and positivity of the off diagonal elements in the template or the system matrix might lead to completely stable systems. This result was later generalized to many equivalent conditions [22]. Several other important results and analytic tools were published in [23–28].

A few non-stationary state patterns were soon discovered by some other template classes. A two cell chaotic behavior, and more general chaotic attractors were identified [29, 30]. An oscillating behavior was used to detect some patterns (e.g. edges) by the onset of oscillating cells [31], and an array of Chua’s circuits were formed by a special template [32] and elementary spatial/temporal interaction, inspired by ant colonies was generated [33].

## 4. Inventing the Stored Programmable Spatial-temporal Computer: The CNN Universal Machine (CNN-UM) and the Cellular Wave Computer

The lesson learned from the practical limitations of any fixed program array processors with well defined cells and interconnections, as well as the need of the precedence of locality to make the architecture silicon-friendly, became soon clear. The role of stored programmability or algorithmic programmability in the digital computer was also clear from John von Neumann. To overcome the lack of stored programmability, another way of thinking was applied [5], combing analog/arithmetic array dynamics and logic. The new stored programmable spatial-temporal Machine, the CNN Universal Machine [6] became a fruitful paradigm. The basic idea is independent of technology, though silicon technology was considered as the main target platform.

The core of the cell array is a CNN architecture, each cell is extended by local analog/arithmetic and logic memories and a local communication and control unit connected to a Global Analog-and-logic Control Unit of the Global Analog-and-logic Program Unit. The latter contains also 3 registers for storing (and sending) the templates, the local logic operators, and the switch configurations defining and changing the local extended cell configuration (operating mode). Herewith, elementary instructions defined by spatial-temporal CNN dynamics can be used in a stored program algorithm. The optional local logic unit and local analog/arithmetic unit takes the values from the local memories, and places the results to them, respectively.

This architecture is a minimal architecture to make universal machine, universal in the logic universe, as a Turing Machine, and also as a nonlinear operator on topographic image flows. The algorithms running on this cellular wave computer can be defined formally as the so called alfa-recursive functions, as a counterpart of the mu-recursive functions for digital computers.

This chapter is structured as follows: in Sec. 2 we discuss the main features of elementary Cellular Automata, which are the CA model used by Chua; in Sec. 3, we study the dynamics of the single Cellular Automaton cell and prove that it can be used to define rigorously a complexity index for elementary CA rules; in Sec. 4, we focus on the global dynamics of the Cellular Automaton and present the original classification of elementary CA rules proposed by Chua; in Sec. 5, we formalize the link between Cellular Automata and Nonlinear Dynamics; finally, in Sec. 6 we give some final remarks on Chua’s contribution and discuss how it can be fruitfully employed in the future.

In the most general definition, a Cellular Automaton is a lattice of coupled dynamical systems, called cells, whose states update synchronously according to a given rule N. In general, each cell interacts only with a limited number of other cells included in its radius of neighborhood. If the state space of the Cellular Automaton is denoted by Σ, then any CA rule N induces a map:

M_N : Σ → Σ (1)

In general, the mapping of the state x^n onto the state x^{n+1}, where the superscript refers to the iteration of the discrete-time system, performed by rule N can be summarized by the notation x^{n+1} = M_N(x^n); similarly, x^{n+2} = M_N^2(x^n) = M_N(M_N(x^n)) etc. Throughout this paper, italics and bold font will be used for the states of the cells and the Cellular Automaton, respectively.

There are countless models of Cellular Automata depending on the setting of its parameters (dimension of the lattice, number of states of the cells, radius of neighborhood etc.) Following the same approach as the majority of the authors, Chua studied the so-called elementary Cellular Automata that consist of a one-dimensional array of L binary cells, each interacting only with the two nearest neighbors. Therefore, the state of the cell in position i at iteration n + 1 depends on the states of the cells in positions i − 1, i, and i + 1 at iteration n: x_i^{n+1} = M_N(x_{i-1}^n, x_i^n, x_{i+1}^n). In this paper, we assume that the two cells in position 0 and L − 1 are neighbors, thus x_0^{n+1} = M_N(x_{L-1}^n, x_0^n, x_1^n) and x_{L-1}^{n+1} = M_N(x_{L-2}^n, x_{L-1}^n, x_0^n). In other words, the state of an elementary Cellular Automaton is actually a ring because the first and the last cell of the one-dimensional array are ‘glued together’. Such boundary conditions are called periodic and they are widely used in the scientific literature.

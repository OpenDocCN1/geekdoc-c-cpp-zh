# 2.2. Periodic and almost-periodic solutions

Early analysis of solutions to nonlinear dynamical systems developed by Chua and co-workers were based on frequency domain analysis using, for instance, Volterra functional series [25]. Then the approach turned to use the Hopf theorem [26] for studying almost sinusoidal oscillations [27]. Electronic circuits were considered as dynamical systems and in 1990, Chua published a survey of the qualitative behavior of dynamic nonlinear (RLC) electronic circuits [28]. The motivation was to better understand the sometimes “bizarre” solutions observed for particular initial conditions. Among others, some solutions were investigated using “one-dimensional invariant sub-manifold”, that is, using nullclines. Some relevant contributions to nonlinear dynamics with many applications in electronic circuits were quoted such as, for instance, Minorski’s book [29] and Hayashi’s book [30]. There is also one paper published by Gollub and co-workers about chaos in a system of coupled tunnel diode relaxation oscillators [31] where they remarked that “the nature of the coupling is much more important than the number of degrees of freedom in generating chaos”. The qualification of “transitional” by Ueda may leave the impression that chaos was only an “artefact”. Although that Ueda was not followed in his presentation of chaotic behaviors as “transitional phenomena”, many others complained too that no proof for the chaotic nature of the solution to the Lorenz and the Rössler systems was provided [34–37]. Chua questioned himself too whether Lorenz’s results [38] were actually asymptotic behaviors and not artefact. He was therefore pushed to develop some algorithms to be ensured that the numerical solutions he obtained were not artefact as we will see below.

Chua and Ushida therefore started by checking that the asymptotic behavior was actually reached when they were observing some quasi-periodic solutions [39].

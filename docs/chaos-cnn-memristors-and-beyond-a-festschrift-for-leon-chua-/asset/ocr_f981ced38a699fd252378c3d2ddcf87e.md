# 4. Traveling Waves in Locally Connected Chaotic Neural Networks and Vector-valued Chaotic Neural Networks

The dynamics of a chaotic neural network heavily depends on the network connectivity. We have already considered the case of a sparse, globally connected network in Sec. 3. On the other hand, the cellular neural network [2] and other models [33,34] use a locally connected network architecture in which each neuron is connected only to its neighborhood.

A locally connected chaotic neural network can be constructed as follows [35]. Let us assume that the neurons are arranged in a two-dimensional lattice, and let $(l_i, m_i)$ denote the position of neuron $i$ with $l_i, m_i \in \boldsymbol{N}$. Its neighborhood $N_i$ is defined as $N_i = \{(l, m) | \max(|l - l_i|, |m - m_i|) \le d$ for $l, m \in \boldsymbol{N}\}$. Within the area $N_i$, we select $L$ neurons at random and connect them to neuron $i$. All other connections are removed by setting the synaptic weights of these connections to 0.

The network shows rich spatio-temporal dynamics characterized by traveling waves. Starting from a random state, the network is initially divided into many small clusters of different memory patterns. These clusters merge to become bigger ones over time, and then the boundaries between the clusters start moving, as shown in Fig. 7. Various kinds of traveling waves such as plane waves, target waves, rotating waves, and spiral waves can be generated locally in space and temporally. The network state continues to change until it eventually converges to a globally coherent state such as a global plane wave with a constant period.

The waves discussed above correspond to moving boundaries of local cell assemblies. Here, cell assemblies refer to particular patterns of active and inactive neurons; these cell assemblies are distinguished by their different colors in Fig. 7. Movement of the boundaries between the assemblies results in the alternation of local activity patterns. It should be noted that the waves disappear if the neurons have no refractoriness.

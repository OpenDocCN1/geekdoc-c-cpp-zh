The parameter τ(r), defined in Eq. (4) and representing the average recurrence time, is now analyzed. This is shown in Fig. 4 with respect to different values of r and for different values of α. It can be noticed that the trend of τ(r) is similar for the three chaotic attractors, and as r → 0, the average recurrence time τ(r) of a chaotic state tends to infinity, while obviously this does not occur for limit cycle attractors. Therefore, when r → 0, chaos allows to obtain a quantitative representation of the uniqueness of the states according to Bergson’s view: the time to return back to the same state (or in other terms the duration of time) in fact becomes not measurable.

The same analysis was repeated for experimental data. Data were obtained by acquiring the three state variables of the Chua’s circuit in Fig. 1 by using a data acquisition board (National Instruments AT-MIO 1620) with a sampling frequency of fₛ = 100kHz. They have been then analyzed with the same routines used for the numerically integrated trajectories. Three qualitatively different dynamical behaviors have been obtained by varying the value of the variable resistor R₆. In particular, for R₆=488Ω and R₆=427Ω two limit cycles approximatively corresponding to those obtained for α=7.2 and α=8.1 are generated. For R₆=397Ω (approximatively corresponding to α=8.6) the

For chaotic attractors, τ(r) increases as r decreases, thus confirming the analogy between Bergson’s time and what can be defined as the Chua’s time, i.e., the recurrence time of the states of the Chua’s circuit.

# 5. Conclusions

This Chapter starts from a series of considerations made by the philosopher Henri Bergson on the concept of time. Bergson’s view on time is that the real duration of time, qualitative, heterogeneous, irreversible, is registered by the consciousness. The consciousness is in fact always influenced by the whole past, so that, even if the external circumstances may be the same, the person which experiences them is not the same and so are their thoughts and actions, since the evolution of his/her consciousness is continuous and irreversible. The consequence of this is that the consciousness cannot pass two times through the same identical state, and that each point of the continuous flow of the consciousness is unique.

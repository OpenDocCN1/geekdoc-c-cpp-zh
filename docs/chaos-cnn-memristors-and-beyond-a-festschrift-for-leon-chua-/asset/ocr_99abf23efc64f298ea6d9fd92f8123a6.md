Such causes are typically associated with irreversible rate-dependent electro- or thermo-dynamic changes that are contingent on both the present as well as the past environment. Particularly in the case of nanoscale memristors, this inertia can be associated with the displacement of mobile ions or oxygen vacancies [5, 6], the formation and rupture of conductive filaments [7, 8] or even the phase-transitions of an active core [9,10]. And despite the fact that these mechanisms have a more substantial effect in lamella devices, similar attributes can also be supported by considerably larger ionic systems [11], contingent upon the extend of the stimulating cause, the nature of the pertinent ions and the barrier medium that governs their kinetics.

Essentially, this signature memristor property is quite characteristic for all devices in support of a discharge phenomenon, no matter what the conveying medium is. For example, naturally occurring ionic systems, such as biological ion-channels [12,13], allow the passive displacement of ions due to an electrochemical gradient along the cells membrane. The extend of this electrochemical gradient sets the probability of an ion-channel being in an open or closed state and in consequence governs the amount of ions that are conveyed through the channel. The intra- and extra-cellular current conduction per channel is governed by distinct rate-limiting dynamics and therefore a pinched-hysteresis loop should inherently appear when an appropriate periodic signal is applied [14]. Such hysteretic effects have been deliberately omitted from past publications as being errors provoked by the employed instrumentation set-ups, while very early investigations only resolved the static characteristics of such systems. Recent studies however, have identified that ion-channels can genuinely undergo a hysteresis in their voltage dependence during normal gating. This is clearly illustrated in Fig. 1 for hyperpolarization-activated, cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) ion-channels [15], attributed to the rate-limiting conformational changes of the channels pore during gating charge movement [16].

Chemical diffusion and ion migration are considered as the facilitating mechanisms for most functional properties of organic and inorganic systems; both of them being in essence distinct forms of ionic discharge. # 3. Discharge Tubes can be Classified as Memristors

To further prove this, our quest for discovering the earliest artificially made memristor is innately focused on historical devices that support ionic-discharge, particularly when luminous effects accompany this discharge. Over the past century, discharge tubes and low-pressure lamps have been extensively scrutinized, facilitating the first experimental studies on the behavior of atoms and electrons that have been efficiently translated into a multitude of innovations, particularly in lighting. Discharge lamps are recognized as being rather dynamic since the time required for ionization and deionization to take place, depends not only upon the instantaneous current flow, but also upon the current, which has previously flowed through it as well as the rate of change of current. When a potential difference (PD) is applied across a gas-discharge tube that is sufficiently larger than the static ignition potential of the tube, the current flow increases rapidly. However, the deionization time is observed to be substantially larger than the time that it takes to decrease this current flow abruptly, rendering an increase in the tubes resistance as deionization occurs. This dynamic response was especially observed for two types of glow discharge tubes, as depicted in Fig. 2 by Reich and Depp [23] in 1938.

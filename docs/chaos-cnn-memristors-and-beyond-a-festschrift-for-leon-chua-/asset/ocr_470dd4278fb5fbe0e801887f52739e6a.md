Generally speaking, the stick-slip observed in the friction between solids makes it a priori a good example of relaxation dynamics. It is ubiquitous in real world phenomena like in geophysics and in engineering and the class of model proposed by Dieterich [8] and by Ruina [9] was designed to represent this physics. They use a “state” variable, denoted as θ later on, which depends on the physical state of the surfaces facing each other and describe memory effects of the response. This state variable has its own dynamics, in agreement with the observation of time dependent solid/solid friction.

Numerical solutions of this family of models (we shall refer to them as the DR model below) show well relaxation-like oscillations with two widely separated time scales. However we show that, in this family of models, the transition from slow to fast and conversely does not occur by an instability of the slow manifold (SM) similar to the one existing in the strongly nonlinear regime of the van der Pol equation. The stick-slip behavior in the DR model makes a new class of relaxation oscillations: here the slow-fast transition occurs because the slow dynamics becomes singular in a finite time, i.e. by a runaway of the slow dynamics, not by transverse instability of the SM.

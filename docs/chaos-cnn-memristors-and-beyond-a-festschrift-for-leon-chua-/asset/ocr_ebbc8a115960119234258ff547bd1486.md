The symmetric attractor shown in Fig. 18(d) looks like a Burke and Shaw attractor [75, 76]. Such an attractor is common in Lorenz-like systems [77,78]. In fact, there is also a Lorenz-like attractor [Fig. 18(e)] solution to the Chua circuit (13) but it has an inversion symmetry and not a rotation symmetry like the original Lorenz attractor. It thus corresponds to an embedding of the Lorenz attractor using variable x or y of the Lorenz system since, in that case, an inversion symmetry is recovered [79]. It is also possible to observe in the Chua circuit (13) two attractors obtained after a period-doubling cascade as observed in the Lorenz system for large R values [80]. These attractors have a Logistic-like first-return map to a Poincaré section. Nevertheless, these attractors are related by an inversion symmetry and not by a rotation symmetry as observed in the Lorenz system.

A “strange” attractor with a complex topology has also been observed [Fig. 18(g)]. Its complexity comes from the central zone, between the two scrolls. It will be shown below that such a structure is related to a toroidal structure. An attractor [Fig. 18(h)] similar to an attractor solution to a simple model for a pulsating star [81] was also found in the Chua circuit (13). This attractor has a very different structure from all the others already discussed. The topology of the similar

attractor produced by the pulsating star model was investigated in Ref. [82]. The double scroll shown in Fig. 18(i) is interesting due to its central zone. The two scrolls are quite similar to the two scrolls of the common double scroll attractor but the way in which they are linked is rather unusual. It looks like a Lorenz attractor whose central part was mirrored. Note that such a special topology seems to be only observed in a system with an inversion symmetry.

The last three attractors [Figs. 18(j), 18(k), and 18(l)] are all related to the Rössler spiral attractor. The first ones [Fig. 18(j)] are two symmetry-related spiral attractors. They may become a single symmetric chaotic attractors through a merging attractor crisis. The case of the attractors shown in Fig. 18(k) looks like a two-fold cover of a spiral Rössler attractor [79]. It is thus a symmetric Rössler attractor with two foldings, one being the symmetric of the other under an inversion symmetry. A second example of such a two-fold cover was also obtained [Fig. 18(l)], but with a different orientation in the phase space compared to the attractor shown in Fig. 18(k). In spite of this, the last two attractors are topologically equivalent. The richness of the dynamics produced by the Chua circuit could result from the fact that it is characterized by an universal template as shown by Ghrist and Holmes [69].

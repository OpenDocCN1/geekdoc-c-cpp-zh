A homoclinic trajectory can be formed when the outer equilibrium points (P₋ and P₊) have a stable real eigenvalue and an unstable complex conjugate pair of eigenvalues and the equilibrium point (0) at the origin has an unstable real eigenvalue and a stable complex conjugate pair. A trajectory starting from an initial state X₀ on the eigenplane Eᶜ(P₋) near P₋ spirals away from the equilibrium point along the eigenplane Eᶜ(P₋) until it enters the D₀ region, where it is folded back into D₋₁ and returns to the unstable eigenplane Eᶜ(P₋) close to P₋. In this way, trajectories are repeatedly stretched apart (in D₋₁) and folded (in D₀) to produce chaos [15].

Figure 4 shows the structure of a double scroll chaotic attractor in Chua’s circuit. A trajectory starting from an initial state X₀ on the eigenplane Eᶜ(P₋) near P₋ spirals away from the equilibrium point along the eigenplane Eᶜ(P₋) until it enters the D₀ region. If it enters the D₀ region below the eigenplane Eᶜ(0), it is reinjected into D₋₁. However, if it enters the D₀ region above Eᶜ(0), it is injected into the D₁ region, where it spirals around P₊ with increasing amplitude until it returns to the D₀ region. In this way, a symmetric double scroll chaotic attractor is formed.

Prof. Chua’s doctoral student, Farhad Ayrom, had been working on synthesis techniques for piecewise-linear nonlinear resistors [5].

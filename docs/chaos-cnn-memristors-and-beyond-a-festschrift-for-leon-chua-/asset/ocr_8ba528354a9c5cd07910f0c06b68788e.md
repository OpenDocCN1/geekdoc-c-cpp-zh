At any given time, there were researchers in NOEL proving theorems, writing code, running software simulations, designing integrated circuits, building electronic systems, running physical experiments and writing papers. The lab was always full of people and was a fertile ground for sprouting technical discussions and research projects among physicists, mathematicians, circuit designers, post-docs, students and professors.

I started my Ph.D. research working on CNN, particular on the stability of various types of CNNs [9–11]. I also created a maze-solving CNN that uses the dynamics of the network to find a solution to a maze. When Bert Shi was the Teaching Assistant (TA) for Leon’s circuit theory class, he used this problem as an exercise problem for the class. As I will describe later on, this has profound consequences (for me). Sometime in the middle of my stay at NOEL, I changed my research focus to concentrate more on chaotic dynamics[12] and synchronization of chaos[13]. Even though this appeared to be a drastic change in research focus, the mathematical tools used (linear algebra, dynamical systems) are similar and the underlying theme of my research both in CNN and in chaotic dynamics was complex yet orderly behavior in a collection of interconnected systems. The mathematics

department at UC Berkeley, located in Evans Hall not far from Cory Hall, is well known for its research faculty and their contributions to nonlinear and chaotic dynamics, with world renowned mathematicians such as Stephen Smale, Morris Hirsch and Charles Pugh on its staff. Being able to talk to them face to face was a boon to our research. In particular, Moe Hirsch’s work on cooperative systems[14–16] shows that state trajectories of cooperative systems are eventually monotone, i.e. they preserve the natural partial ordering of the states over time and we used these results extensively in our work on CNN stability. I also had many interesting discussions with Charles Pugh who was my advisor for my Master’s thesis in mathematics. The following coincidence is worth mentioning. On my first day of work at IBM I went to the security office to get my badge. I was quite surprised to see Charles Pugh standing in line at the same office, also waiting for his badge. It turned out he was visiting Mike Shub, who was my manager at IBM at the time, and Charles and I both arrived at IBM on the same day and went to the security office at around the same time!

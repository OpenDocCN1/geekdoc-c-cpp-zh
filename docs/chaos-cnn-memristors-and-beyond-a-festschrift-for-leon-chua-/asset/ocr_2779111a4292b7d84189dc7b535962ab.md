Pershin and Ventra [3] have pointed out that the ADC, microprocessor and digital potentiometer can all be upgraded to provide greater frequency handling capability and stepping resolution, and that the components chosen for the demonstration are good enough to provide proof of theory. The advantages of their method may become more evident when further experimental data describing the operational characteristics and parameters of actual memristors are obtained and published. At that time it is a relatively simple process to upgrade the basic system and alter the coding to suit the data as it is revealed. Furthermore, there will not be just one type of memristors, and in the same sense there are a variety of capacitors in terms of construction materials, capacitance value and performance ratings. It can thus be expected that a number of different memristors and memristive systems will be developed in the near future. Pershin and Ventra’s circuit is an adaptable design that can be modified to effectively emulate the many variations in memristor types that are expected to be available.

# 2.3. Plug-and-play MMC

A third MMC has been presented by Sodhi and Gandhi [6], as shown in Fig. 3, which is primarily intended for educational purposes rather than as a means to develop memristor based applications. Nevertheless, the circuit operates effectively up to at least 100 Hz and shows V-I characteristics simulating that of the TiO₂ memristor. The circuit is derived from careful analysis of the equations describing the TiO₂ memristor in the paper by Strukov et al. [1], i.e.,

```
v(t) = (R_ON w(t)/D + R_OFF (1 - w(t)/D)) i(t), (3)
```

```
w(t) = μ_v w(t)/D q(t), (4)
```

```
M(q) = R_OFF (1 - μ R_ON / D² q(t)), (5)
```

where D is the total width of the TiO₂ film, w(t) is the width of the region of high dopant concentration on the TiO₂ film, R_OFF and R_ON are the limit values of the memristor resistance for w(t) = 0 and w(t) = D, and μ_v is the dopant mobility. Here, (5) describes the memristance of the circuit, obtained from equations (3) and (4) and simplified by assuming R_ON ≪ R_OFF [1].

Designed with the same goal as the Valsa et al.’s circuit [2], this circuit serves to observe memristive behavior rather than as a circuit for practical implementation. The circuit shows good correspondence with SPICE results, displaying regular pinched hysteretic loops in response to a sinusoidal input.

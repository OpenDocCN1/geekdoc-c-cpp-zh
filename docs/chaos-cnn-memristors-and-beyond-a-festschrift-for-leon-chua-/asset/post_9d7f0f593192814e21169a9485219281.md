All these functions work concurrently and, although neuron main communication tools are spikes, their intrinsic dynamics is inherently analog, whereas the overall neural processing is peculiarly parallel and asynchronous. The somatotopic organization of sensory motor maps is an archetype of how living beings interact with outer world through proprioceptive and exteroceptive mechanisms. This Chapter will deal with how CNN largely contributed to a new generation of locomotion and perception systems embedded in Biologically inspired artificial machines.

CNNs can guarantee to a robotic system embedded solutions with parallel computation capabilities. These peculiar characteristics are extremely important in bio-robotics and in particular when multi-legged platforms are considered. The possibility to face with multiple degrees of freedoms in a robust and parallel way is a key point of CNNs and represents a significant contribution in this field. Locomotion in legged robots is by itself an hard task: under this perspective, analog CNN implementations of feedforward strategies based on Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) have been proposed and successfully applied to hexapod robots, crab-like structures and lamprey-like solutions. However CPGs can be enriched with sensory feedbacks coming from the environment to allow basic feedback control mechanisms like step-reflexes, steering control, searching reflexes, attitude control and others. A CNN-based CPG allows a distributed computation of the sensory information that can act at the level of single cells of the neural structure used as motor neurons for the robotic system. An implementation of these solutions was realized in a six-legged insect-like robot by using Reaction Diffusion (RD) Cellular Neural Networks [3]. Subsequently a new approach to the CPG implementation, based on the principle that the same group of neural cells can be reorganized by changing the synaptic connections, was proposed. Its corresponding CNN implementation is realized by using space variant templates and the mechanism was called Multi-template approach [4]. Additionally, such template values could be learned, in an unsupervised way, in order to provide a really flexible, environmentally mediated locomotion adaptation via a weighted combination of the fixed templates [7].

In the last years of research in using CNNs for Biorobotics, a question arose: is it possible to extend the CNNs paradigm to model higher level functionalities of the brain? Following this hypothesis a bottom-up approach was proposed to unravel the potentialities of CNNs in Brain Science. Particular attention was devoted to insects due to their simple brain that however allows the emergence of their complex adaptive behaviours.

Growing up from locomotion to cognition, a new general purpose perceptual control architecture, based on nonlinear neural lattices, was presented and applied to solve robot navigation tasks. Insects show the ability to react to certain stimuli with simple reflexes, using direct sensory-motor pathways, which can be considered as basic behaviors, inherited and pre-wired. Relevant brain centres, known as Mushroom Bodies (MB) and Central Complex (CX) were recently identified in insects: though their functional details are not yet fully understood, it is known that they provide secondary pathways allowing the emergence of cognitive behaviors [23, 104]. These are gained through the coordination of the basic abilities to satisfy the insect’s needs. Taking inspiration from this idea, the proposed architecture modulates, through reinforcement learning mechanisms, a set of competitive and concurrent basic behaviors in order to complete the task assigned through a reward function. The core of the architecture is constituted by the so-called Representation layer, used to create a concise picture of the current environment situation, fusing together different stimuli for the emergence of perceptual states. These perceptual states are steady state solutions of lattices of Reaction-Diffusion Cellular Nonlinear Networks (RD-CNN), designed to show Turing patterns. The exploitation of the dynamics of the multiple equilibria of the network is emphasized through the adaptive shaping of the basins of attraction for each emerged pattern. Experimental campaigns on standard robotic platforms were performed to demonstrate the potentiality and the effectiveness of the approach. The robot, endowed with a Turing Pattern CNN based generator as perceptual controller was experimentally found able to cope with new situations, not encountered in the learning phase, which in any case had been performed in an unsupervised, reinforcement based way. The additional interesting aspect is that the same CNN cell, which is a standard two-layer Piecewise linear cell, can generate Turing Patterns or spiking-like dynamics only changing its parameters. The overall architecture is therefore built around the same functional CNN cell, which, like the neuron in a brain structure, can give rise to completely different self organizing emergent dynamics if embedded in different neural lattices and if its parameters are slightly modulated. The main message to be drawn by CNN processing in Biorobotics is their analog, spatially distributed way of computation. In locomotion control, the whole set of actuators which concurrently move in the environment constitute the visible solution of the hidden spatial-temporal PDE-like processing performed by the CNN controller. Perception is also seen as the site where environment representation is incrementally formed, just like a morphogenetic pattern grows, under the control of endogenous and environmental stimuli. Moreover there is a topographically organized projection between motor neuron cell bodies and specific regions of the vertebrate spinal cord and higher brain centers, and this organization is maintained through a lot of living species. A wide variety of living beings reveal an extremely organised neural architectures CNN-based visual processors take inspiration from the retinotopic ganglion cells organization which is maintained within the appropriate area in the midbrain: this allowed to design and realize analog, digitally programmable VLSI chips able to implement extremely fast visual dynamics. Within the same perspective it was drawn by Neurobiologists.

A final, alternative way to face with the perception process using CNNs will be briefly addressed in the final part of the chapter. It will refer to the mathematical paradigm of the WinnerLess Competition (WLC), which was efficiently used to model olfactory processing in insects and mammalian. The sequence of saddle points crossed in the phase space of the multidimensional nonlinear system was used as a code for embedding the perceptual state of the robot. The large memory capacity shown by the WLC-CNN is able to contribute to the new perceptual framework for autonomous artificial agents, where the association between stimuli and sequences is learned through the experience.

The proposed examples lead to demonstrate that CNNs can be considered as an unifying framework for studying and implementing bio-inspired solution in robotics and for understanding basic brain functions.

## 2. CNN-based CPGs for Locomotion Control in Bio-robots

### 2.1. Basis of locomotion

It is often surprising how even simple beings possess a high level of adaptability while moving into various environments. Studies on how the control of locomotor activity is realized in a large category of animals reveal a hierarchical organization of the neural networks devoted to the locomotion control task. The key feature of these networks is that the pattern of neural activity is mapped into the pattern of locomotor activity. The generation of the rhythmic patterns of muscles activity takes place at the neural level called Central Pattern Generator (CPG). This structure has been revealed both in vertebrates (located in the spinal cord) and in invertebrates (located in relevant ganglia).

The CPG includes subnetworks of Command Neurons (CNs) and Local Pattern Generating Neurons (LPGNs). The former ones act as decisional centers: they receive stimuli from the sensorial organs or from higher level neurons. Therefore, at this level some important decisions such as escape reactions or the choice of the right locomotion pattern (i.e. swimming if the being is on the water or walking if it is on the terrain) take place. The LPGNs are responsible of coordination of the organs devoted to locomotion (cilia, legs). The neurons of this subsystem are called also motor-neurons, because they constitute the last neural level before movement actuators. In many biological cases this functional subdivision does not correspond to different neural networks: the same networks may act at different levels of the structure. It is also not so clearly established which are the mechanisms used to reorganize the LPGNs configuration in order to switch among locomotion patterns. Two mechanisms involved in this operation are focused by neurobiologists. In some cases it is necessary only to change the parameters of the synapses, while in other cases a strong reorganization of the network is required. In this latter case it is not rare that some synaptic connections are inhibited and other ones are excited towards neurons not yet involved in the former pattern generation.

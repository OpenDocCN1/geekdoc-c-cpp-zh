This augmented six-element circuit, shown in Fig. 7, is canonical in the sense that every continuous three-dimensional odd-symmetric piecewise-linear vector field may be mapped onto the circuit [7,8]. Consequently, the circuit can exhibit every dynamical behavior that is known to be possible in a system described by a continuous odd-symmetric three-region piecewise-linear vector field. With the appropriate choice of parameters, the circuit can be made to follow the classic period-doubling, intermittency, and torus breakdown routes to chaos [7,8]. For a comprehensive bibliography of papers on Chua's circuit, we refer the reader to Chua's historical review paper [4].

The original five-element Chua's circuit quickly established itself as the first provably chaotic electronic circuit. As a result, it attracted huge interest in the electrical engineering community. With a solid underpinning theory and a robust practical implementation, Chua's circuit played an important role in the 1990s in the development of applications of chaos including chaos communications [10], cryptography, and chaos control [12]. When applications demanded high speed operation and integrated circuit implementation, appropriate variants were developed [9,25]. In 2008, a simplified four-element Chua was proposed [2]. Today, we have a three-element chaotic circuit which uses a memristor to realize simultaneously nonlinearity, local activity and memory [24].

In this chapter, we will discuss the six-, five-, four- and three-element chaotic circuits which have been inspired by Chua's original invention.

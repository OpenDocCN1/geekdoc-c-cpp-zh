Fig. 4. Chua comes out of the laboratory, walks and sits on the immortal stone, thinking about the failed experiment. He writes the equations of Lorenz and Roessler in the sand. Then he stands up and goes away.

Professor Chua is thinking in his office at the University of Waseda. Some months after Matsumoto’s simulations of the Chua circuit, Chua is dissatisfied. In spite of his numerous suggestions, Matsumoto has not managed to build Chua’s circuit, since his research group has never synthesized a prescribed non-monotonic v – i characteristic.

Chua leaves for Berkeley and finds out that his circuit works. Ayrom and Zhong, Chua’s collaborators at Berkeley University, have built the simplest electronic circuit exhibiting chaos, or rather, the phenomenon of bifurcation chaos. It will become a system of great importance, a paradigm in the study of complex phenomena in nonlinear circuits. Furthermore, if compared to Lorenz’s attractor, Chua’s circuit shows a much greater variety of

complex phenomena. In this way, Zhong and Ayrom become the first researchers to experimentally document and observe the chaotic phenomena created by Chua’s circuit (Fig. 7).

Garden of Waseda University, Tokyo. The story ends where it started, with the same characters commenting on Chua’s discovery.

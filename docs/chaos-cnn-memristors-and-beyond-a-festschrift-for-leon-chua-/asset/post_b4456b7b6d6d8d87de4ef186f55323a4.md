## 5. Effect of Noise on Synchronization in Time-delay Systems

Effects of noise on synchronization have also been studied extensively [3, 4, 36]. The phenomenon of “bubbling”, i.e., noise-induced desynchronization due to local instability of the synchronization manifold [37] is an example of a non-constructive effect of noise. Nevertheless, noise can also induce and enhance synchronization in coupled/uncoupled oscillators [38–40]. The influence of stochastic forces is also of great importance in various applications [41,42]. For instance, stochastic process in the central nervous system is essential for the expression of spatiotemporal patterns [43]. Hence investigating noise effects on CPS of delay dynamical systems will provide a better understanding on the stochastic process mediating various natural phenomena including the information processing in the central nervous system. In this section, we will illustrate the phenomenon of noise-enhanced phase synchronization in coupled time-delay systems with threshold nonlinearity. Further, we will show that a common Gaussian noise can drive two independent time-delay systems to evolve in complete synchrony without establishing any explicit coupling between them.

### 5.1. Noise-enhanced phase synchronization in time-delayed systems

Here, we will investigate the phenomenon of noise-enhanced PS in coupled time-delay systems along with its mechanism. As PS is the coincidence of characteristic time scales of interacting dynamical systems, it is crucial and interesting to understand the interplay of noise and the multiple time scales in achieving PS in such systems for various applications. In particular, we will consider the piecewise linear time-delay system with a threshold nonlinearity with parameter mismatches and demonstrate that both an independent and a common additive noise can enhance PS. Interestingly, multiple time scales of a time-delay system are clearly identified from the return times of the flow to a Poincaré section. We find noise-enhanced PS in both basic coupling configurations: unidirectional and bidirectional. On the one hand the response system adjust its time scales to that of the drive in achieving PS in an unidirectional coupling configuration. On the other hand, both subsystems adjust their rhythms to the main time scale of the uncoupled system to achieve PS in bidirectionally coupled systems. Further, we will show that phase slips are accompanied by small and large return times, which are not present in the noise-free system, due to the unlocked unstable periodic orbits present in the coupled system.

We consider the following coupled piecewise linear time-delay system

```
ẋ = ωx(−x(t) + b f[x(t − τ)])
    + εx(y(t) − x(t)) + σ ξx(t),
ẏ = ωy(−y(t) + b f[y(t − τ)])
    + εy(x(t) − y(t)) + σ ξy(t),    (10)
```

where ωx = 1.0 and ωy = 1.1 contributes to the frequency mismatch, b = 1.2 is the nonlinear parameter, τ = 1.33 the delay, εx,y the coupling strength, ξx,y(t) an added Gaussian noise with zero mean and unit variance, and σ the noise intensity. When ε = εx = εy, then the systems are bidirectionally coupled, whereas εx = 0 corresponds to a unidirectional coupling configuration. When ξx = ξy, the Gaussian noise is common otherwise it is independent. The function f(x(t − τ)) is taken to be a symmetric piecewise linear function defined by

```
f(x) = Ax,y f* − B x.    (11a)
```

Here

```
f* = { -x*    x < -x*, x    -x* ≤ x ≤ x*, x*    x > x* , (11b)}
```

where x* is a controllable threshold value, A_x,y and B are parameters. The parameter values are fixed in accordance with the values of the circuit elements [26].

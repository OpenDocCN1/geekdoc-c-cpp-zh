Fig. 2. Leading Floquet multipliers of unstable periodic orbits arising from different bifurcations: (a) period doubling, (b) tangent or subcritical Hopf, and (c) Nejmars-Sacker (discrete Hopf) bifurcations. The unit circle defines the region of stability.

The theory of DFC is difficult because the delayed feedback induces an infinite number of degrees of freedom. Even linear analysis of such systems is complicated due to the infinite number of Floquet exponents (FEs) characterizing the stability of controlled orbits. Nevertheless, some analytical approaches have been developed in vicinity to various bifurcations of periodic orbits, such as the period doubling bifurcation [18,35], the subcritical Hopf bifurcation [34,37,38] and the Nejmars-Sacker (discrete Hopf) bifurcation [44]. The leading Floquet multipliers (FMs) of UPOs arising from these bifurcations are shown in Fig. 2. Note that all three types of orbits have different topological properties. The orbits with the negative real multiplier flip their neighborhood during one turn. The orbits with the complex conjugate pair of the multipliers have a finite torsion, and the orbits with the positive real multiplier have no torsion. Below we discuss separately the theoretical treatments of the DFC algorithm for these three different types of UPOs.

The orbits with the negative real multiplier [Fig. 2(a)] arise from a period-doubling bifurcation and are typical, for example, for the Rössler system. The mechanism of stabilization of such orbits by delayed feedback is well understood [18,35]. In [35] there has been derived the relationship between the FEs of periodic orbits controlled by proportional and delayed feedback algorithms. Then it is enough to know the dependence of the Floquet exponents on the control gain for the proportional feedback control algorithm. Similar dependence for the EDFC is obtained from a transcendental equation and we do not need to solve the delay differential equations.

An analytical treatment of the delayed feedback controlled UPOs with the complex conjugate pair of leading FMs [Fig. 2(c)] has been developed in [44]. Such orbits arise from a Nejmars-Sacker

(discrete Hopf) bifurcation and cannot appear in low-dimensional chaotic attractors. However, such orbits may appear in low-dimensional non-chaotic systems, e.g. in a weakly nonlinear van der Pol oscillator subjected to a periodic force and delayed feedback control. For this system, we have derived a simplified averaged equation and obtained analytical expressions defining the region of stabilization of the controlled system as well as an optimal value of the control gain.

The orbits with the positive real multiplier [Fig. 2(b)] come, for example, from a tangent or a subcritical Hopf bifurcation and are typical, for instance, for the Lorenz system. They satisfy the so-called odd number limitation, which states that any UPOs with an odd number of real Floquet multipliers greater than unity can never be stabilized by any DFC technique. During the last fifteen years, this limitation has been intensively discussed in the literature. The history of the limitation is dramatic. This statement was first proven by Ushio [52] for discrete time systems. [18] and [27] proved the same limitation for the continuous time DFC, and then this proof was extended for the EDFC [29]. It was commonly accepted that it is impossible to overcome this inherent limitation. However, [11] have shown by a simple example that this limitation does not hold in general for autonomous systems. Nevertheless, it remains valid for non-autonomous systems.

To overcome the odd number limitation a counterintuitive idea based on an unstable controller has been proposed [34,37,38]. By including an additional unstable mode into the control loop one artificially enlarges the set of real multipliers greater than unity to an even number. The performance of such an unstable controller has been numerically demonstrated for the Lorenz system [34] as a representative of systems with torsion-free UPOs. Later on, it has been realized that the unstable DFC allows an analytical treatment if a system is close to a subcritical Hopf bifurcation. First, such a treatment has been performed for a simple nonlinear second order electronic circuit modeling a subcritical Hopf bifurcation [38]. Then this approach has been extended for wider class of dynamical systems with an arbitrary large phase space dimension [37].

Furthermore, in real experimental situation, the period of actual UPO may evolve due to the evolution of the system parameters under the effects of exogenous, unpredictable factors. In this context, adaptive control techniques with an automatic adjustment of the delay time are desired.

The problem of estimating the period of a target UPO from observed experiential data has been considered in several publications. In the original paper [32], it has been shown that the amplitude of DFC perturbation has a resonance-type dependence on the delay time, and the periods of UPOs can be extracted from the minima of this dependence. More sophisticated theoretical foundation for obtaining the UPO period from the observed control signal has been developed in [19]. The first adaptive technique employing online variation of the delay time has been proposed [20]. Here the delay time is adjusted in a discrete way according

to the distance between successive maxima of the output signal. Another adaptive discrete-time technique based on the gradient-descent method has been proposed in [5].

Here we briefly outline our recently proposed [43] adaptive continuous-time DFC algorithm with a state-dependent delay, which is based on resonance dependence of the DFC perturbation on the delay time pointed out in [32]. We implement an adaptive strategy similar to that proposed in [49] to maintain a synchronous state in time-evolving complex networks. Note that in the last years the systems described by state dependent delay differential equations have become of particular interest. Recently, [13,26] have demonstrated that the variation of the delay time can enhance the stability properties of the DFC.

Consider a general case of DFC controlled dynamical system:

```
Ẋ(t) = F[X(t), KΔs(t)]. (6)
```

The first argument in the function F shows the dependence of the vector field on internal degrees of freedom, while the second argument denotes the dependence on control force KΔs(t). Here K is the feedback gain, and the delayed difference is defined as Δs(t) = s(t) − s(t − τ) = g[X(t)] − g[X(t − τ)], where s(t) = g[X(t)] is a measurable scalar signal that is a function of state vector variable X(t). Assume that the free system (K = 0) has an unstable periodic solution X(t) = ξ(t) = ξ(t − T) that we intend to stabilize by control perturbation KΔs(t) with time-dependent delay time τ(t). Our aim is to construct an algorithm, which provides an asymptotic convergence of the delay τ(t) towards the period of desired UPO and stabilizes this UPO.

As a helpful tool for this purpose, we construct the following functional:

```
Δ̄(t) = ∫₀ᵗ exp[−ν(t − t′)][s(t′) − s(t′ − τ(t′))]² dt′. (7)
```

This functional describes the running time-average of the quantity [s(t) − s(t − τ(t))]². It estimates the averaged value of this quantity in the time interval [t − 1/ν, t]. The parameter ν defines the window of averaging.

If we chose τ as a constant of time, we could scan the values of τ, and plot the dependence of the functional Δ̄ vs. τ. At the periods of UPOs it is exactly zero, i.e. we have resonances for τ = T_k (here T_k is the period of the k-th UPO).

Chua and Ushida investigated Duffing’s equation

```
ẍ + 0.1 ẋ + 2x + x³ = 0.4 cos t + 0.4 cos 0.35 t
+ 0.4 cos 0.155 t. (3)
```

This motivation was that, although describing a symmetrical mechanical pendulum, the Duffing system was often studied by Hayashi who used an electronic analog computer to make simulations [30]. It was also often quoted in books dealing with electronic circuits. Although deeply impressed by the Lorenz paper [38], Chua did not investigate the Lorenz system because it did not correspond to an electronic circuit by these times. In fact, the Lorenz system was only introduced in IEEE Journal in 1983 by Colin Sparrow [40]. The Duffing equation as investigated by Chua and Ushida can be rewritten as a forced second order differential equation

```
{
ẋ = y
ẏ = −0.1 y − 2x − x³
+ A(cos ω₁ t + cos ω₂ t + cos ω₃ t)
}(4)
```

where A = 0.4 and ω₁ = 1, ω₂ = 0.35 and ω₃ = 0.155. The system is here forced with multiple input frequences [Fig. 4(a)]. Only an excerpt of a time series was proposed by Chua and his co-workers [39]. With all the conditions specified in the paper

we obtained a period-211 limit cycle as exhibited by computing 50,000 points in the Poincaré section after the transient regime [Fig. 4(b)]. Thus, in spite of a phase portrait which could lead to conclude to a chaotic solution, the asymptotic behavior is periodic as stated in Chua and Ushida’s paper.

# 2.2.1. Early investigations of chaotic solutions

The first occurrence of a chaotic attractor in Chua’s works was the result of a collaboration with scientists involved in numerical studies, namely Jacques Neirynck (1931-) who spent few years in the Philips Laboratories and was in charge of Computer-Aided Design, and Philippe Verburgh (1957-) who was in the Circuit Theory Group of the Swiss Federeal Institute of Technology (Lausanne). One of their motivations was to demonstate that all properties for a chaotic motion was indeed observed, that is, sensitivity to initial conditions, hyperbolicity, existence of a strange attractor and a broadband frequency spectrum. This study was performed “through a long series of piecewise linear analysis using exact analytical formulas” [41].

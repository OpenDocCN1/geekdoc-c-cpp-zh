Let us emphasize that exact resonance condition T2 = 2T1 is never realized precisely but numerical experiments show that even the approximate validity of this condition is sufficient to construct the cellular automaton which adequately describes the spatio-temporal order of activation of synapses and of firing of neurons of the network.

# 3.3. Definition of CA rules

Let us assume that neurons and synapses of the network possess the following properties:

+   - when a synapse is active it is non-sensitive to the presynaptic stimulation, i.e. it is in refractory state;
+   - a neuron is at rest in the absence of synaptic current (the conductance s = 0);
+   - a neuron generates spikes when the synaptic conductance overcomes some threshold value which is close to its maximum;
+   - the temporal intervals of generation of spikes are relatively short with respect to the characteristic timescales of evolution of the synaptic conductance;

These properties allow one to formulate the rules of evolution in time of ψi, i.e the rules of a CA:

+   (i) If i-th synapse at the instant of time t is in the state P0 (ψi(si(t)) = P0), and there is j ∈ Ki such that ψj(sj(t)) = P11 or ψj(sj(t)) = P22 then depending on the values of synaptic activation thresholds (denote they by θji) at the instant t + T1 either it becomes excited (i.e. ψi(si(t + T1)) = P11 or ψi(si(t + T1)) = P21) or it stays in the rest (i.e. ψi(si(t + T1)) = P0).
+   (ii) If i-th synapse at the instant of time t is in the state P21, i.e. ψi(si(t)) = P21, then ψi(si(t + T1)) = P22 independently of other synapses. Moreover, P21-synapse cannot make excited another synapse.
+   (iii) If ψi(si(t)) = P11 or ψi(si(t)) = P22, then ψi(si(t + T1)) = P0 independently of the states of other synapses.
+   (iv) Because of the existence of the refractory period, the synapses in the states P11, P21, P22 do not feel the action of other synapses.

These statements imply that for the fixed i the state ψi(si(t + T1)) at the instant t + T1 is uniquely determined by

+   - the state ψi(si(t)) at the previous instant of time t;

Hence, ψi(si(t + T1)) = Fi(ψi(si(t)), {ψj(sj(t))}, {θji}, j ∈ Ki), where Fi is a function determined by the statements (i)–(iv). This relation defines a synaptic CA determined on the graph of connections G. In the Table 1 we summarize the rules of the CA. In what follows we identify ψi(si(t)) with the symbol ψi and ψi(si(t + T1)) with the symbol ψ̄i.

Finally if we fix the values of thresholds θji the rules of the CA takes the following form: ψ̄i = Fi(ψi, {ψj}, j ∈ Ki), where ψ̄i, ψi ∈ P.

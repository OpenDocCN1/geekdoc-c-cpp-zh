**Table 1.** The *n*-th order models of basic circuit elements.

| Circuit element | Replacement by *n*-th order model | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Independent voltage source, $V(t)$ is given [10] | $n=1$: $V(t)$; $n>1$: short circuit | first order: no change; higher orders: short circuit |
| Independent current source, $I(t)$ is given [10] | $n=1$: $I(t)$; $n>1$: open circuit | first order: no change; higher orders: open circuit |
| Nonlinear resistor, $V(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}R_kI^k(t)$ [10] | $n=1$: $R_1$; $n>1$: voltage source $V_{En}(t)$ in series with $R_1$ | $V_{E1}(t)=0$; $V_{E2}(t)=R_2I_1^2(t)$; $V_{E3}(t)=2R_2I_1(t)I_2(t)+R_3I_1^3(t)$ |
| Nonlinear capacitor, $V(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}S_kQ^k(t)$ [10] | $n=1$: $C=1/S_1$; $n>1$: voltage source $V_{En}(t)$ in series with $C$ | $V_{E1}(t)=0$; $V_{E2}(t)=S_2Q_1^2(t)$; $V_{E3}(t)=2S_2Q_1(t)Q_2(t)+S_3Q_1^3(t)$ |
| Nonlinear inductor, $V(t)=\dot{\Phi}(t)$, $\Phi(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}L_kI^k(t)$ [10] | $n=1$: $L_1$; $n>1$: voltage source $V_{En}(t)$ in series with $L_1$ | $V_{E1}(t)=0$; $V_{E2}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}L_2I_1^2(t)$; $V_{E3}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}(2L_2I_1(t)I_2(t)+L_3I_1^3(t))$ |
| Memristor, $V(t)=M(Q(t))I(t)$, $M(Q)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M_kQ^k$ (Section 3.2) | $n=1$: $M_0$; $n>1$: voltage source $V_{En}(t)$ in series with $M_0$ | $V_{E1}(t)=0$; $V_{E2}(t)=M_1Q_1(t)I_1(t)$; $V_{E3}(t)=M_1Q_2(t)I_1(t)+M_2Q_1^2(t)I_1(t)+M_1Q_1(t)I_2(t)$ |

For the first three orders $n$, we get

$V_1(t)=M_0I_1(t),$

$V_2(t)=M_0I_2(t)+(M_1Q_1(t)I_1(t)),$

$V_3(t)=M_0I_3(t)+(M_1Q_2(t)I_1(t)+M_2Q_1^2(t)I_1(t)+M_1Q_1(t)I_2(t)).$

It can be seen that $V_n(t)$ is the sum of $M_0$ times the *n*-th order current and a term in parentheses, which does not depend on any quantity of order $\ge n$. The *n*-th order memristor-model therefore is a linear resistor $M_0$, connected in series with a time-dependent voltage source (see Table 1).

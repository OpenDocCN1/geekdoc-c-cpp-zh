In similar fashion, the functional property of the electric arc depends upon a discharge phenomenon that has been mainly observed for carbon electrodes placed a few millimetres apart with a relatively large PD being applied across them. Quoting De La Rue [31] the stratified discharge in a vacuum tube is simply a magnified form of the electric arc that is in essence a modification of the same phenomenon.

Hertha Ayrton was one of the first eminent female engineers (first female MIEE) that have extensively studied the electric arc [32]. As the electric arc was employed commercially since 1879,ᵇ Ayrtons work complemented the work of Swan and Edison towards addressing the effects of the electrodes degeneration due to oxygen coming into contact with the carbon electrodes that caused the hissing of the arc, as depicted in Fig. 5. She proved that if air was excluded from the arc, or when nitrogen was introduced in isolation, the hissing did not occur; eventually leading to the development of gas-discharge tubes. Most importantly, she was one of the first noticing that the characteristic curve of an alternating current (AC) arc shows lower voltages when the current is decreasing than when it is increasing that is dependent upon the supply current, the length of the arc, the thermal conductivity of the electrodes as well as the pressure of the surrounding gas. # 4. Discharge at Different Length Scales

The larger the distance between the electrodes, i.e. the barrier across which the PD is applied, the larger the PD must be in order to establish discharge. On the other hand, when the separation gap is miniscule, a few volts are sufficient for mobilizing ions across this barrier, which is also the case in nanoscale implementations of memristors. And one should be able to steadily sustain this effect as the barrier width is enlarged, provided that the PD across the device is enhanced appropriately. In fact the effect on the barrier width was thoroughly studied for a predecessor of discharge tubes, also known as the electric arc [30].

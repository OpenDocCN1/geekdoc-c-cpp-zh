Circuits are by definition connections of circuit elements. The connections are realized by ports that carry two variables, a current and a voltage. While the sum of the currents of connected ports must be zero, the voltages must be identical. The circuit elements themselves are described by equations involving their port variables, and possibly additional, internal variables such as charges and fluxes. They are called the constitutive relations of the elements. The constitutive relations may be linear or nonlinear and the corresponding elements carry the attribute linear or nonlinear, accordingly.

The electronics literature is full of different circuit elements, the most classical being the resistor, the capacitor, the inductor, the transistor, the operational amplifier, controlled sources, etc. This list of elements is redundant in the sense that a large part of them can be obtained by connection of only a few of them, the basic circuit elements. Different sets of basic circuit elements can be chosen. Here we favor the set composed of nonlinear locally passive resistors, independent current and voltage sources and linear capacitors as 1-ports, and the ideal operational amplifier as 2-port.

In addition we find it useful to decompose the ideal operational amplifier into two juxtaposed singular 1-ports, the nullator and the norator (Fig. 2). In this way, all basic elements are 1-ports. Inductors, transistors, controlled sources, etc., can all be obtained by connecting these basic elements. Even nonlinear resistors with non-monotonic characteristics, and nonlinear capacitors and inductors have equivalent circuits composed exclusively of the basic circuit elements.

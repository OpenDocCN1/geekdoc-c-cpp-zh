The most important new frontier for practical applications of memristors today is the greater use of learning in order to make greater use (and find greater application) of all kinds of massively parallel hardware [1]. Traditional systems for neural networks and machine learning had difficulties in meeting the real needs of applications with a high degree of complexity, or with a mix of continuous and discrete variables; however, we now understand the principles which allow us to overcome those limitations [2, 3].

There is a clear four step roadmap [3] for developing more powerful general-purpose learning systems by Eq. (1) developing more general systems for “vector intelligence” by better integrating fundamental principles for optimal decision-making and prediction; Eq. (2) moving up a level by injecting new approaches to handling complexity in space (as a few systems now begin to do, such as some breakthrough systems which have recently broken world records in image recognition [4, 5]); Eq. (3) handling skill learning and complexity over time in a more principled way (as no working systems really do quite yet); and finally Eq. (4) adding capabilities for brain-like stochastic search [6] which exist at the highest levels of the mammal brain.

Even the fulfillment of this roadmap in technology will not end up replicating the fullest capabilities of the human mind [7], but it will enable many important technological capabilities. For example, the current White House policy statement on the smart grid [8] states that “NSF is currently supporting research to develop a ‘4th generation intelligent grid’ that would use intelligent system-wide optimization to allow up to 80% of electricity to come from renewable sources and 80% of cars to be pluggable electric vehicles (PEV) without compromising reliability, and at minimum cost to the Nation (Werbos 2011).” [9]. In paying for electricity, the world now pays on the order of 10 cents per kilowatt hour (kwh) for 20,000 terawatt hours per year (IEA/OECD data on total electricity in 2008). That amounts to $2 trillion per year. The most cost-effective sources of solar power and wind can supply electricity at about that price, or less, in quantity in the US, and at lower cost in most other places. (By contrast, the latest estimates I hear from industry sources say that the total cost for new nuclear power plants, exploiting the new expedited safety reviews, will still end up costing 13.5 cents per kwh, and present greater problems outside the US.)

This would seem to say that we can become independent of fossil fuel, avoid military conflicts over fossil fuel, and avoid the large-scale environmental threats associated with fossil fuel or nuclear proliferation, simply by shifting over to wind and solar, at a small cost or small profit in the end. But in the real world, the ten cents cost which gets paid to generators is not the full cost of using wind and solar. Use of wind or solar on the real world power grid involves huge new costs in stabilizing the power grid (an operation conducted in real time), and in backup and storage. Yet wind and solar farms contain a lot of switchable power electronics, which could convert these energy sources into assets rather than liabilities, if only we could generate the right control signals to make use of this control authority in real time (every two second rather than every 5 to 15 minutes). At present, the most advanced system for optimal power flow in practical use is the system recently developed by Marija Ilic of Carnegie Mellon, which makes approximate calculations every 15 minutes, and lacks full measures to anticipate areas of future power scarcity, but has shown major savings over older systems in the areas where it has been tested. But with cellular types of neural networks and new cellular hardware, Liang et al. [10] have illustrated how it is possible to perform the same kind of task, with a system designed for anticipatory optimization [2], in real time. The sheer speed due to new computing hardware is a crucial factor here. If solar farms and wind farms can be converted from being a net cost to a net asset in stabilizing the grid, the benefits could clearly be on the order of trillions of dollars per year — and perhaps large enough to overcome the obstacles which block today’s efforts to solve the urgent problems of the world in that sector.

Of course, the development of new computer hardware to exploit memristor capability and learning together, to provide ever greater computational horsepower, is another important frontier being funded by NSF, a frontier which needs to be advanced in lockstep with the advancement of learning architectures [1]. This frontier requires the use of very fundamental dynamical systems and quantum physics [11] to exploit the utmost in what we can do with hardware. There are many possible physical platforms for memristors, as other chapters in this book will probably review more systematically.

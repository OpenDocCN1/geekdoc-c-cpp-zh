# 3. Instability, Invariant Measures and Entropy

We saw in the previous section how the Lyapunov exponents measure the instability of the initial conditions. A topological classification of the attractors of CA has been investigated by Kurka and others [1] on the infinite lattice ℤ. In the finite case, the topology of an attractor may be complex as shown by Chua and al. [1], especially as the length of the lattice L ⟶ ∞. In this case, on account of instability of the initial conditions and complexity of attractors, only statistical knowledge may be reasonable for an understanding of the system.

Two statistical approaches to CA are possible. The first one corresponds to the description of long time behaviour of the CA-dynamics, when we start from a set of random initial conditions. Such a statistical description is reminiscent to the equilibrium micro-canonical ensemble in statistical mechanics. The second one corresponds to convergence to equilibrium under the CA-map of non-invariant measures. Concerning this problem, the only results to our knowledge in this respect are given by [4, 14] for some special CA-rule.

Concerning the first problem, there may be different stationary probability measures, invariant under the CA-map f and the shift σ, corresponding to disjoint attractors eventually. As in statistical mechanics, one considers a class of observables, we may conveniently identify them with the continuous functions, Φ, on X. Given an initial configuration x, a probability measure on the set X of all configurations is given by the equality of time averages to ensemble averages

∀Φ lim_{n→∞} 1/n Σ_{k=0}^{n-1} Φ(f^n x) = ∫_X Φ(x) dμ(x).

It it exists, this measure μ depends on the initial condition x. We do not go here into a full discussion of the above ergodic property of the CA which may be very complicated, and not yet totally understood. It is therefore more practical, in studying the statistical property of a given CA-map f, to consider a family of shift invariant measures and to try to extract from them a subfamily of f-invariant measures.

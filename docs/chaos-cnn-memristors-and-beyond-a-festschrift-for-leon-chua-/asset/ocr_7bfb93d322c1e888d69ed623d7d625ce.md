Gas is normally a poor conductor of electricity, however at sufficiently high electric fields the molecules in the vicinity of the electrodes are ionized and are set in motion, allowing the conduction of small currents. These ionized molecules predominantly support the establishment of current percolation paths in a rather similar manner to nanoionic systems [24] that are classified as memristors, including resistive switching elements [25,26], quantum-point contact switches [27] and polymeric devices [11]. Despite the fact that the intrinsic

mechanism differs substantially in the aforementioned systems, they all exhibit a rather similar dynamic response that is often exemplified by a pinched-hysteresis loop [25].

An excellent analysis of the dynamic characteristics of devices exhibiting discharge phenomena was presented by V.J. Francis [28], where alike dynamic characteristics were observed for tungsten filaments, high-pressure mercury-vapour lamps, low-pressure mercury tubes, discharge-tubes and sodium tubes as illustrated in Fig. 3 respectively. The operation of these devices relies upon distinct configurations, with the discharge phenomenon being expressed in rather dissimilar environments.

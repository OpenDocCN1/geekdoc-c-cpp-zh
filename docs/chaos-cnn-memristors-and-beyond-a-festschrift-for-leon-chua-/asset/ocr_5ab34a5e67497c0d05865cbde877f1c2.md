# 2.1. The mechanism of formation of synaptic responses

Let us briefly discuss the process of formation of synaptic responses. Consider the ith synaptic coupling starting from the case when vj < θji holds for all presynaptic neurons. In this case χi = 0 and

the system (3)–(6) has the phase portrait shown on Fig. 3(a). Point O0 is globally stable. Note that s-coordinate of O0 equals 0. Hence ζi = 1 and the synapse can be activated. If at some instant of time the potentials of one or several neurons vj exceed the threshold values θji, then χi takes quickly some positive integer value. Numerical simulation of (3)–(6) shows that for 1 ≤ χi < χ* the system has a phase portrait shown on the Fig. 3(b) while for χi ≥ χ* it has phase portrait shown on Fig. 3(c). In both cases the only attractor of the system is the limit cycle — either C1 or C2, which is established independently of initial conditions. Thus the dynamics of the synapse is governed by the alternating action of the trajectories from two phase planes.

If the exceeding of potentials vj over values of θji is multiple then a glued trajectory Tr (Fig. 4) appears on the (ri, si)-plane. The dotted lines correspond to the pieces of trajectories of the Fig. 3(b) or Fig. 3(c) while continuous ones to trajectories of the Fig. 3(a). The process of formation of the glued trajectory Tr is ended when the inequalities vj(t) < θji hold for all j. The further dynamics is fully determined by the system (3)–(6) with the phase plane presented on Fig. 3(a). Two possibilities can occur.

# 5. Three-element Chaotic Circuit

We stated at the outset that, in order to exhibit chaos, an autonomous circuit consisting of resistors, capacitors, and inductors must contain

+   - at least one nonlinear element,
+   - at least one locally active resistor, and
+   - at least three energy storage elements [15].

The four-element Chua's circuit contains three energy storage elements and a locally active nonlinear resistor. A memristor can exhibit simultaneously the characteristics of a nonlinear element, a locally active resistor, and an energy storage element. Therefore, it should be possible to build a chaotic circuit using just three elements: a memristor and two energy storage elements. Muthuswamy and Chua reported this in 2010 [24]. Their “simplest chaotic circuit” is shown in Fig. 36.

This circuit is described by two ordinary differential equations:

```
dV_C/dt = 1/C I_L  (32)
dI_L/dt = -1/L (V_C - V_M)  (33)
```

where V_M is the voltage across the memristor M, which is defined by:

```
V_M = β(X² - 1) I_M  (34)
dX/dt = I_M - α X - I_M X.  (35)
```

The memristance R(X) is given by:

```
R(X) = β(X² - 1).  (36)
```

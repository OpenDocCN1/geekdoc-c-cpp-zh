All these functions work concurrently and, although neuron main communication tools are spikes, their intrinsic dynamics is inherently analog, whereas the overall neural processing is peculiarly parallel and asynchronous. The somatotopic organization of sensory motor maps is an archetype of how living beings interact with outer world through proprioceptive and exteroceptive mechanisms. This Chapter will deal on how CNN largely contributed to a new generation of locomotion and perception systems embedded in Biologically inspired artificial machines.

CNNs can guarantee to a robotic system embedded solutions with parallel computation capabilities. These peculiar characteristics are extremely important in bio-robotics and in particular when multi-legged platforms are considered. The possibility to face with multiple degrees of freedoms in a robust and parallel way is a key point of CNNs and represents a significant contribution in this field. Locomotion in legged robots is by itself an hard task: under this perspective, analog CNN implementations of feedforward strategies based on Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) have been proposed and successfully applied to hexapod robots, crab-like structures and lamprey-like solutions. However CPGs can be enriched with sensory feedbacks coming from the environment to allow basic feedback control mechanisms like step-reflexes, steering control, searching reflexes, attitude control and others. A CNN-based CPG allows a distributed computation of the sensory information that can act at the level of single cells of the neural structure used as motor neurons for the robotic system. An implementation of these solutions was realized in a six-legged insect-like robot by using Reaction Diffusion (RD) Cellular Neural Networks [3]. Subsequently a new approach to the CPG implementation, based on the principle that the same group of neural cells can be reorganized by changing the synaptic connections, was proposed. Its corresponding CNN implementation is realized by using space variant templates and the mechanism was called Multi-template approach [4]. Additionally, such template values could be learned, in an unsupervised way, in order to provide a really flexible, environmentally mediated locomotion adaptation via a weighted combination of the fixed templates [7].

In the last years of research in using CNNs for Biorobotics, a question arose: is it possible to extend the CNNs paradigm to model higher level functionalities of the brain? Following this hypothesis a bottom-up approach was proposed to unravel the potentialities of CNNs in Brain Science. Particular attention was devoted to insects due to their simple brain that however allows the emergence of their complex adaptive behaviours.

Growing up from locomotion to cognition, a new general purpose perceptual control architecture, based on nonlinear neural lattices, was presented and applied to solve robot navigation tasks. Insects show the ability to react to certain stimuli with simple reflexes, using direct sensory-motor pathways, which can be considered as basic behaviors, inherited and pre-wired. Relevant brain centres, known as Mushroom Bodies (MB) and Central Complex (CX) were recently identified in insects: though their functional details are not yet fully understood, it is known that they provide secondary pathways allowing the emergence of cognitive behaviors [23, 104]. These are gained through the coordination of the basic abilities to satisfy the insect’s needs. Taking inspiration from this idea, the proposed architecture modulates, through reinforcement learning mechanisms, a set of competitive and concurrent basic behaviors in order to complete the task assigned through a reward function. The core of the architecture is constituted by the so-called Representation layer, used to create a concise picture of the current environment situation, fusing together different stimuli for the emergence of perceptual states. These perceptual states are steady state solutions of lattices of Reaction-Diffusion Cellular Nonlinear Networks (RD-CNN), designed to show Turing patterns. The exploitation of the dynamics of the multiple equilibria of the network is emphasized through the adaptive shaping of the basins of attraction for each emerged pattern. Experimental campaigns on standard robotic platforms were performed to demonstrate the potentiality and the effectiveness of the approach. The robot, endowed with a Turing Pattern CNN based generator as perceptual controller was experimentally found able to cope with new situations, not encountered in the learning phase, which in any case had been performed in an unsupervised, reinforcement based way.

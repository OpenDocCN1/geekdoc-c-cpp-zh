In 1905, the German physicist Hermann Theodor Simon (1870–1918) highlighted this effect that he called “Lichtbogenhysteresis”, i.e. “arc hysteresis” [35, p. 305]. When the voltage and current are varied very rapidly (as it is the case when the oscillatory circuit LC is connected), the curve is called “dynamic characteristic” and the rising voltage curve is different from the falling one (see Fig. 7).

So, when the arc is functioning its “static characteristic” is no longer valid and must be replaced by its “dynamic characteristic”.

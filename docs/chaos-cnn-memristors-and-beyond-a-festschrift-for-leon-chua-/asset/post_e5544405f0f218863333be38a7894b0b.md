
# 1. Introduction

Professor Leon Chua’s multifaceted research interests and contributions have been a great source of inspiration for researchers in nonlinear dynamics and circuit theory around the world for the past five decades or so. Study of the nonlinear dynamics of piecewise-linear systems and circuits are essentially the outcome of the invention of Chua’s circuit by Leon Chua in 1983 [4, 6, 21]. The nonlinear dynamics community has been indeed greatly influenced by Chua. Our group (specializing in theoretical physics) at Tiruchirappalli, a remote southern Indian town, was greatly motivated by a series of suggestions and encouragement by Leon Chua since early 1990 to supplement our theoretical analysis of nonlinear systems by taking up experimental works based on nonlinear electronic circuits. The present article essentially reviews our efforts to understand the behaviour of nonlinear dynamical systems through piecewise-linear systems, circuits, coupled systems and arrays, starting from MLC circuits, as a grateful appreciation to Professor Chua on his seventy fifth birthday.

In the field of nonlinear dynamics, the study of nonlinear electronic circuits is a convenient framework to undertake systematic exploration of fundamental mechanisms underlying the onset of bifurcations and chaos [12, 21, 24, 31]. Over the past three decades, piecewise linear circuits, either of autonomous or of nonautonomous nature, have become simple yet powerful models for studying various bifurcation structures and chaos phenomenon. The piecewise linear circuits usually consist of at least one circuit element whose transfer characteristics is piecewise linear in nature. These circuits are natural extensions of their linear counterparts, yet they exhibit a repertoire of nonlinear dynamical phenomena. From a practical point of view, these circuits are quite easy to construct, analyze, observe and even to prove the admittance of various bifurcations and chaos therein [6].

A variety of nonlinear electronic circuits consisting of piecewise linear circuit elements which exhibit chaos have been investigated in the recent literature [20]. Among them, the most prominent one is the autonomous, classic, Chua’s circuit [6], which is represented by a system of three autonomous first order coupled ODEs. The system admits a variety of dynamical phenomena, including limit cycles, homoclinic and heteroclinic orbits, strange attractors, etc. Further, its higher dimensional version, namely the driven Chua’s circuit [26],

which is fourth order in nature, has been investigated as a black box exhibiting a rich variety of bifurcations and chaos phenomenon, ranging from period-doubling route to chaos to quasiperiodicity, intermittency, period adding sequences and so on.

Besides the above, even simpler chaotic circuits, namely the Murali-Lakshmanan-Chua (MLC) circuit and the variant MLC circuits, consisting of the piecewise linear resistor (Chua’s diode) as the only nonlinear element, have been suggested to exhibit a wide variety of bifurcations and chaos phenomenon. In recent times several other piecewise linear circuits have also been suggested to study quasiperiodic orbits, strange nonchaotic attractors, hyperchaotic attractors and different kinds of synchronisations. These include the forced series LCR circuit with negative conductance [45], Inaba-Mori circuit [13, 14] and piecewise linear time delay systems [33]. Coupled arrays of such systems are also of considerable interest from the point of view of various types of synchronizations.

In this article, we will review briefly the bifurcations, chaos and synchronization phenomena exhibited by some of the above piecewise linear circuits using exact solutions, numerical analysis and experimental results. A preliminary version of these results has appeared earlier in Ref. [22]. Here we present a more upto date review of the progress. To start with, in Sec. 2, we will present the nonautonomous MLC circuit as a system of two coupled first order ordinary differential equations with a piecewise linearity representing the current-voltage characteristic of the nonlinear resistor, namely, the Chua’s diode. This set of equations can also be rewritten as a single second order nonautonomous linear differential equation with discontinuous right hand side. Obtaining the exact solutions sector wise, and matching the solutions across the boundaries, we present the bifurcation and chaos scenario by varying the control parameters. The results are corroborated by direct numerical integration and experiments. In Sec. 3, the dynamics of a variant of the MLC circuit which belongs to a family of MLC circuit classification is discussed. Next, in Sec. 4, the spatio-temporal dynamics of coupled MLC circuits has been considered. Following this, in Sec. 5, nonlinear dynamics of certain simple electronic circuits consisting memristor, in particular memristor based MLC circuit, has been studied. Then, in Sec. 6, we point out how quasiperiodically forced piecewise linear systems are natural candidates to exhibit strange nonchaotic attractors of various

types. Next, the bifurcation and chaos scenario in a scalar piecewise linear time delayed system is briefly described in Sec. 7. In Sec. 8, the mechanism of chaotic synchronization of coupled time-delay circuits and systems has been considered. Finally, in Sec. 9, we summarize our results.

## 2. The Basic MLC Circuit and its Chaotic Dynamics

The basic Murali-Lakshmanan-Chua (MLC) circuit [27], shown schematically in Fig. 1(a), consists of a capacitance (C), an inductance (L), a resistor (R), all of which are linear circuit elements, an external periodic forcing f(t) = F sin ωt and only one nonlinear element, namely, the Chua’s diode (N_R) which satisfies the piecewise linear current-voltage characteristic [Fig. 1(c)],

$$i_R=h(v_R), \tag{1}$$

where

$$h(v_R)=\begin{cases}(G_bv_R+(G_b-G_a)B_p), & v_R\le -B_p\\G_av_R, & -B_p\le v_R\le B_p\\G_bv_R+(G_a-G_b)B_p, & v_R\ge B_p.\end{cases} \tag{2}$$

Essentially the circuit in Fig. 1(a) is a standard forced LCR circuit to which a nonlinear resistor, N_R, is connected in parallel. A current sensing resistor R_s is also included in the circuit for measurement purpose. Applying Kirchhoff’s laws for currents and voltages to the circuit in Fig. 1(a), one can write down the state equations as a system of two coupled first order piecewise linear differential equations,

$$C\frac{dv_R}{dt}=i_L-h(v_R), \tag{3a}$$
$$L\frac{di_L}{dt}=-Ri_L-R_si_L-v_R+F\sin\omega t. \tag{3b}$$

Here i_L is the current along the inductor L and v_R = v is the voltage across the capacitor C, while h(v_R) is the piecewise linear function defined by equation (2).

Introducing the scaled variables

$$x=v/B_p,\quad y=i_L/GB_p,\quad t'=tG/C,$$
$$\omega'=\omega C/G,\quad G=1/R. \tag{4}$$

Eq. (3) can be rewritten (after dropping the primes) as

$$\dot{x}=y-h(x),\quad (\cdot)=\frac{d}{dt}, \tag{5a}$$
$$\dot{y}=-\beta(1+\nu)y-\beta x+f\sin(\omega t). \tag{5b}$$


The parameters are a = Gₐ/G, b = G_b/G. Now the dynamics of (5) depends on the six parameters ν, β, a, b, ω and f.

### 2.1. Explicit analytic solutions and period doubling route to chaos

The dynamical equations (5) can be equivalently written as a second order linear differential equation with discontinuous right hand side:

```
ẍ + β(1 + ν)ẋ + βx - f sin ωt = Δᵢ(t, x, ẋ), i = 1, 2, 3,  (7)
```

where Δ₁ = -bẋ - βb(1 + ν)x - β(1 + ν)(a - b), in D₀{x > +1}  (8a)

given as x(t) = C₀,±¹ exp(α₁t) + C₀,±² exp(α₂t) + E₁ + E₂ sin ωt + E₃ cos ωt,  (10)

where C₀,± are arbitrary constants in the corresponding regions D₀, D±₁. The other parameters are given by α₁ = [-A + √(A² - 4B)]/2, α₂ = [-A - √(A² - 4B)]/2,  (11a)

where A = β(1 + ν) + μ, B = β(1 + μ(1 + ν)), μ = a in D₀ and μ = b in D±₁  (11b)

and E₁ = 0 in D₀, E₁ = ∓β(1 + ν)(a - b)/B in D±₁, E₂ = [f(B + ω²)/(α₁² + ω²)(α₂² + ω²)], E₃ = [fAω/(α₁² + ω²)(α₂² + ω²)].  (11c)

Knowing x(t), y(t) can be evaluated from Eq. (5) as y(t) = ẋ + h(x).  (12)

Starting from the given initial conditions x(0) and y(0) in a given region, say D₀, the arbitrary constants C₀¹ and C₀² can be fixed. Then as the system evolves, the variable x(t) may cross one of the boundaries, say x = +1 and enter into the region D₊₁ at a time t = T₁, provided ẋ(T₁) > 0.

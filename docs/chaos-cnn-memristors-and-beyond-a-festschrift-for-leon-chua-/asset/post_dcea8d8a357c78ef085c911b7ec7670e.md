## 3. Mutators

The mutator is another highly original and fundamental creation of Chua [3] for synthesizing and building any 2-terminal nonlinear inductor and nonlinear capacitor from 2-terminal nonlinear resistors [3]. It has since been generalized to synthesize any memristor [4] as well as any α − β circuit elements [12]. The basic concept of the mutator is first presented in Section II of [2], which we reproduce below:

The mutator is an active, two-port linear network for transforming one type of nonlinear network element into another type. There are three classes of mutators-an L-R mutator, a C-R mutator, and an L-C mutator. An L − R mutator is characterized by the property that if a nonlinear resistor with a v − i curve Γ is connected across port two of this element, the resulting one-port network (seen across port one) becomes a nonlinear inductor in the sense that it can be characterized by a φ − i curve, or by an i − φ curve, which is identical to the original v − i curve Γ. In the case where the φ − i curve is identical to Γ, the element is called a type 1 L-R mutator. If the i − φ curve is identical to Γ, the element is called a type 2 L − R mutator. Conversely, if a nonlinear inductor with a φ − i curve Γ (or i − φ curve Γ), is connected across port one of a type 1 (or type 2) L − R mutator, the resulting one-port network (seen across port two) becomes a nonlinear resistor whose v − i curve is identical to Γ. In view of this two-way mutation property, the mutator is said to be a reversible two-port.

We will now express the above characterizations in the following precise mathematical terms:

### Type I L-R Mutator: This two-port is defined by

```
v1 = dv2/dt
 i1 = -i2
(24)
```

In other words, the characteristic curve of the resulting inductor is simply obtained by relabeling the voltage and current variables of the v − i curve Γ by the flux-linkage and current variables for a type 1 L − R mutator, or by the current and flux-linkage variables for a type 2 L − R mutator.

In the frequency domain, (24) is equivalent to the transmission (ABCD) matrix [25]

```
T_{LR_1}(p) = \begin{bmatrix} p & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad (25)
```

where p is the complex frequency variable.

### Type 2 L-R Mutator:

This two-port is defined by

```
v_1 = -\frac{di_2}{dt}

i_1 = v_2 \quad (26)
```

The above equations are easily obtained from the respective transformations and relationships i = -i_2, and v = v_2, where i and v are the current and voltage of the nonlinear resistor i-v curve. ## 4. Other Chua Creations

While the above concepts of impasse points and mutators are concerned with specific applications, there is a lesser known aspect of Chua's research on nonlinear circuits which involves very general theories that formed the mathematical foundations nonlinear circuit theory. In particular, instead of inventing a new circuit with a specific purpose, Chua asked what circuit-theoretic and dynamical properties does certain broad classes of nonlinear circuit exhibit. These are “qualitative properties”. For example, how does one characterize and generalize the circuit-theoretic properties and passivity and losslessness for nonlinear n-ports [1,7,9,13].

Even more fundamental are a series of what are now regarded by mathematicians as of a foundational nature; namely, what class of dynamical nonlinear circuits can exhibit only a unique steady response for any input signal [8]? What class of autonomous dynamical nonlinear circuits have multiple equilibrium points [8]?

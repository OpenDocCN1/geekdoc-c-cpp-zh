breakpoints are determined by the positive and negative saturation levels (E_sat^+ and E_sat^-) of the op amps. In a 741 op amp with power rails that are symmetrically placed about zero, the saturation levels are not symmetric. In particular, E_sat^- < E_sat^+; this makes the “bottom” lobe of the attractor smaller than the top.

The breakpoints in the op amp-based two-NIC Chua diode are determined by the saturation levels the op amps, which in turn depend on the internal structure of the op amp and the power supplies. If the saturation levels are asymmetric about zero, so are the breakpoints and the resulting attractors, as we have seen. Matsumoto et al. proposed an alternative implementation of the Chua diode [22] where an op amp-based NIC provides the underlying nonlinear resistance Ga and the outer breakpoints required by eventual passivity.

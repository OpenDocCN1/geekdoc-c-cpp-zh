Sharkovsky[19]ⁱ showed that the least periods of periodic orbits on a continuous map on an interval follow a specific ordering of the integers, now known as the Sharkovsky ordering. A consequence of Sharkovsky’s theorem is that any continuous map on the unit interval with a period 3 orbit contains orbits of any period. We developed the first chaotic communication system that is inherently error-free[23] and built an implementation using Chua’s circuit as the chaos generator. In this setup, the chaotic signal is modulated by the information signal by embedding the information source into the dynamics of the chaotic circuit. The modulated chaotic signal is then sent to the receiver. At the receiver, an identical chaotic

circuit demodulates the signal to recover the information signal. At the 1993 NOLTA conference in Hawaii, I was supposed to demo this at the end of Leon’s talk. We built two identical Chua’s circuits, one embedded in a receiver circuit and one embedded in a transmitter circuit. A low-amplitude music signal comprising of a selection from Mozart’s Eine Kleine Nachtmusik was fed into the transmitter and is completely masked by the chaotic signal. This combined signal is then sent via a coax cable of several feet to the receiver circuit to be decoded. I have checked all the setup at NOEL and also right before the talk. Of course Murphy’s Law kicked in and when I turned on the system in an auditorium full of people, the sound that came out of the receiver’s speakers is … absolute silence. Since Chua’s circuit contains an active nonlinear resistor,ᵏ it consumes power and this was delivered via a battery. It took several minutes of profuse sweating to realize that somehow I must have unplugged the battery to conserve power and neglected to plug it back in for the demo. Luckily the crowd was patient enough and eventually I was able to demo the system successfully. Ever since then, whenever something goes wrong, I would always check the power switch or power supply, even when I am writing software code.

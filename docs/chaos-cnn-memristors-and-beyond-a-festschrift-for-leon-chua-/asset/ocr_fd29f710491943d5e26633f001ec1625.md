# 2. The Basic MLC Circuit and its Chaotic Dynamics

The basic Murali-Lakshmanan-Chua (MLC) circuit [27], shown schematically in Fig. 1(a), consists of a capacitance (C), an inductance (L), a resistor (R), all of which are linear circuit elements, an external periodic forcing f(t) = F sin ωt and only one nonlinear element, namely, the Chua’s diode (N_R) which satisfies the piecewise linear current-voltage characteristic [Fig. 1(c)],

$$i_R=h(v_R), \tag{1}$$

where

$$h(v_R)=\begin{cases}(G_bv_R+(G_b-G_a)B_p), & v_R\le -B_p\\G_av_R, & -B_p\le v_R\le B_p\\G_bv_R+(G_a-G_b)B_p, & v_R\ge B_p.\end{cases} \tag{2}$$

Essentially the circuit in Fig. 1(a) is a standard forced LCR circuit to which a nonlinear resistor, N_R, is connected in parallel. A current sensing resistor R_s is also included in the circuit for measurement purpose. Applying Kirchhoff’s laws for currents and voltages to the circuit in Fig. 1(a), one can write down the state equations as a system of two coupled first order piecewise linear differential equations,

$$C\frac{dv_R}{dt}=i_L-h(v_R), \tag{3a}$$
$$L\frac{di_L}{dt}=-Ri_L-R_si_L-v_R+F\sin\omega t. \tag{3b}$$

Here i_L is the current along the inductor L and v_R = v is the voltage across the capacitor C, while h(v_R) is the piecewise linear function defined by equation (2).

Introducing the scaled variables

$$x=v/B_p,\quad y=i_L/GB_p,\quad t'=tG/C,$$
$$\omega'=\omega C/G,\quad G=1/R. \tag{4}$$

Eq. (3) can be rewritten (after dropping the primes) as

$$\dot{x}=y-h(x),\quad (\cdot)=\frac{d}{dt}, \tag{5a}$$
$$\dot{y}=-\beta(1+\nu)y-\beta x+f\sin(\omega t). \tag{5b}$$

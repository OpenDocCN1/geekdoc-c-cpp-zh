is obtained:
$$y''_m = \frac{y_{m+1} - 2y_m + y_{m-1}}{h^2} + O(h^2), \tag{12.101}$$

which is $O(h^2)$ due to the final division by $h^2$.

The application of central-difference schemes for discretized linear two-point problems leads to linear systems with symmetric band matrices, which positively impacts on the stability of the subsequent solution methods. By using the $O(h^2)$ central-difference formulas 12.100 and 12.101 for discretizing ODE 12.93 and Formulas 12.99 for approximating the boundary conditions 12.94, we obtain the following linear system:

```
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle \frac{y_{m+1} - 2y_m + y_{m-1}}{h^2} = p_m \frac{y_{m+1} - y_{m-1}}{2h} + q_m y_m + r_m, \\
m = 2,3,\dots,M-1, \\
\alpha_1 y_1 + \beta_1 \frac{y_2 - y_1}{h} = 1, \\
\alpha_2 y_M + \beta_2 \frac{y_M - y_{M-1}}{h} = 1.
\end{cases}
```

Regrouping the unknown solution values $y_m$, the discretized linear system corresponding to the bilocal boundary-value problem 12.93–12.94 takes the form:

```
\begin{cases}
(h\alpha_1 - \beta_1) y_1 + \beta_1 y_2 = h, \\
-\left(2 + h p_m\right) y_{m-1} + \left(4 + 2h^2 q_m\right) y_m - \left(2 - h p_m\right) y_{m+1} = -2h^2 r_m, \\
m = 2,3,\dots,M-1, \\
-\beta_2 y_{M-1} + (h\alpha_2 + \beta_2) y_M = h.
\end{cases} \tag{12.102}
```

It is noticeable that this system has a tridiagonal matrix and can be represented finally as

```
\begin{bmatrix}
b_1 & c_1 & & & \\
a_2 & b_2 & c_2 & & 0 \\
 & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \\
 & & a_m & b_m & c_m \\
 & & & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots \\
0 & & & a_{M-1} & b_{M-1} & c_{M-1} \\
 & & & & a_M & b_M
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ \vdots \\ y_m \\ \vdots \\ y_{M-1} \\ y_M \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} d_1 \\ d_2 \\ \vdots \\ d_m \\ \vdots \\ d_{M-1} \\ d_M \end{bmatrix}, \tag{12.103}
```

with the nonzero elements having the expressions:

```
\begin{aligned}
b_1 = h\alpha_1 - \beta_1, \quad c_1 = \beta_1, \quad d_1 = h, \\
\begin{cases}
a_m = -\left(2 + h p_m\right), & m = 2,3,\dots,M-1, \\
b_m = 4 + 2h^2 q_m, \\
c_m = -\left(2 - h p_m\right), \\
d_m = -2h^2 r_m,
\end{cases} \tag{12.104} \\
a_M = -\beta_2, \quad b_M = h\alpha_2 + \beta_2, \quad d_M = h.
\end{aligned}
```

# 12.3.2 What was Not Covered

This book concentrated on the AVR architecture mainly from a programmer's perspective. One aspect of the architecture that was not covered was the implementation of the AVR in hardware. This information is actually proprietary to Microchip. Although they give a programmer's view of the architecture, they do not give away how the AVR core is implemented in hardware. Even if they did, studying the actual hardware implementation would take another whole book.

This book did not cover all of the on-chip peripherals and features of the ATtiny microcontrollers used. It was considered sufficient to cover how some of the peripherals are accessed using assembly instructions. A detailed study of AVR on-chip peripherals and other features is the subject for another book.

# 12.3.3 Other AVR Microcontrollers

Although we studied ATtiny AVR microcontrollers in this book, the core of other AVR microcontrollers is the same. It is easy to transition onto other ATtiny parts, as well as parts from the ATmega range, now that the fundamentals of the AVR architecture have been covered. As we saw, other AVR devices may implement additional, or fewer instructions. On devices with more on-chip peripherals with control registers that fall outside the address range of instructions such as IN and OUT, alternate instructions must be used. You now have the skills to study these other microcontrollers on your own. Refer to the datasheets of other AVR microcontrollers and use the debugger and simulator to analyze any additional features, such as memory and on-chip peripherals.

# 12.3.4 Assembly Language Resources

To continue studying AVR assembly language, refer to application notes and datasheets that use assembly language. Look at the assembly language that the C tools generate. Write your own programs and test them using the debugger. Of course the internet is another source to find AVR assembly language code to study. One site worth looking at is www.avrfreaks.net which is a forum for AVR enthusiasts. Use the search box to search for topics beginning with [ASM] to find articles and discussions about various aspects of AVR assembly language.

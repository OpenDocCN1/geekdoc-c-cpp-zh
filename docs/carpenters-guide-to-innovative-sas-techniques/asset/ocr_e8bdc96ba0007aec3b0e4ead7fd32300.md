# # EMDR Therapy for PTSD

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) is a psychotherapy treatment that was originally designed to alleviate the distress associated with traumatic memories. EMDR therapy facilitates the accessing and processing of traumatic memories and other adverse life experience to bring these to an adaptive resolution.

# ## How Does EMDR Work?

EMDR therapy uses a structured eight-phase approach that includes:

+   - History-taking and treatment planning
+   - Preparation
+   - Assessment
+   - Desensitization
+   - Installation
+   - Body scan
+   - Closure
+   - Reevaluation

During the desensitization phase, the client focuses on the traumatic memory while experiencing bilateral stimulation (typically eye movements). This is thought to help the brain process the memory in a way similar to what occurs during REM sleep.

> > "EMDR does not erase the memory, but it transforms the way it is stored in the brain, reducing its emotional charge."

# ## Effectiveness of EMDR

EMDR is recognized as an effective treatment for PTSD by the WHO, APA, and SAMHSA. Research shows significant symptom reduction in many patients after 6–12 sessions.

```
cmpq %rdi, %rsi
jb ..B3.4
```

To generate the assembly, the compiler is invoked with the
-S -fno-verbose-asm

options, in addition to the compilation options, as in the pattern rule in section 2.3.4.
The assembly of the leibniz.cpp program, which contains the leibniz() function of page 160, is more than 1,300 lines. Locating the inner loop within that assembly is an easy skill. One has to search for “leibniz” or its mangled name _Z7leibniz (which may be found as explained in section 2.1), and the beginning of the function definition will be.

Within the function definition, paying a little attention to the jump statements helps identify the inner loop. In our listing, a jb (jump if below) instruction, and it is jumping to the instruction labeled ..B3.4, which is the top of the loop. In between, we have two jump instructions. The je (jump if equal) jumps to the case where the term of the Leibniz series is negative and must be subtracted. The jmp (unconditional jump) instruction follows the addition of a positive term of the Leibniz series. It jumps to ..B3.7, where the loop termination condition is verified before properly jumping to the top of the loop.

Even in this snippet of assembly code, there are many unfamiliar instructions. Because our purpose is only to understand the quality of the code generated by the compiler, we do not need to understand many of these. The crucial part for us the first

```
..B3.5:
dvdisd %xmm3, %xmm2, %xmm3
vaddsd %xmm3, %xmm0, %xmm0
```

In this assembly snippet, the first instruction divides the %xmm2 register by the %xmm3 register and stores the result in the %xmm2. The source are %xmm3 and %xmm2, and the destination register is %xmm0. The %xmm2 has 4.0 and %xmm3 has 2i+1 (as a double). So the effect is to compute the 4/(2i+1) term of the Leibniz series. In this case, i is even. Therefore, the second instruction is adding the computed answer to %xmm0. The answer is accumulated in %xmm0, and the approximation of π will be found in this register at the end of the loop. The second snippet tackles the case where i is even.

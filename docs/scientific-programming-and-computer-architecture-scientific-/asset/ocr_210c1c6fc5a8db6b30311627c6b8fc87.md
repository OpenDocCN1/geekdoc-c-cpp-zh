# 6.3 MPI examples

In this section, we get more deeply into MPI syntax. Our account of MPI syntax is not extensive. However, every bit of syntax introduced is related to computer architecture. MPI can make programming a lot more challenging. Yet users take the trouble to write MPI programs to make programmers faster and solve bigger problems. MPI syntax can look a bit monochromatic, and by looking at MPI syntax (or semantics), one cannot gain any sense of how effective MPI facilities may be in speeding up programs. The discussion of Infiniband network architecture in the previous section will help us learn MPI with a double focus on syntax and its effectiveness.

We introduce standard optimizations such as load balancing and overlapping processor with network activity. At a conceptual level, these optimizations are no more than common sense. At a practical level, implementing such optimizations effectively be quite challenging.

Section 6.3.1 is about sending and receiving of messages. Three versions of send and receive are discussed. In persistent send/receive, the sender and the receiver preallocate memory using the MPI library. All the sends and receives are from to the preallocated memory. In nonblocking send/receive, the send/receive returns quickly, and it is up to the user to use functions such as `MPI_Wait()` and `MPI_Test()` to confirm that the sending and receiving has indeed completed. Persistent send/receives are nonblocking. We also discuss blocking send/receive and explain why they are slower than the nonblocking versions.

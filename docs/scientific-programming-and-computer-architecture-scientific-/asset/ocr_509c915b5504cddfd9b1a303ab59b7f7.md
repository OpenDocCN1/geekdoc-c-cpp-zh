In this program, the Leibniz series are grouped into 10 sets, and each iteration of the loop updates the sum over each set. The statements inside the loop body are independent of each other and may be executed in parallel with the packed double instructions. Loop unrolling is one of the most popular and effective loop transformations.

In the assembly of the loop bodies of leibnizX(), in fact four sets of packed double instructions operate on YMM registers. Because each YMM register is wide enough for doubles, the increment of the loop counter has been unrolled by a factor of 16. It is if the i=2 increment of the loop counter in each for-loop has been replaced by i+32 and the loop body expanded to treat 16 terms in parallel.

As shown in table 3.2, the program with loops (with the unrolling either manually or by the optimizing compiler) takes seven cycles per term, which is half of the leibniz() function, which branched the loop body.

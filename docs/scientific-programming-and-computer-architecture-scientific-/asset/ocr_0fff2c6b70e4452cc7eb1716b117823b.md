# 1.4 A little Fortran

In this section, we show a bit of Fortran syntax. The syntax is deliberately 77 and not the newer varieties. When the need to use old Fortran codes arises, it is often of Fortran. We do not recommend programming in Fortran.$^{20}$ The language is rigid and does not allow for dynamic data structures as linked lists, trees, and graphs in its 77 version. Such structures are increasingly used in scientific computing and are indispensable to computer science.

> $^{19}$See chapter 11 of Kunth (1996); originally published as Babylonian Algorithms in Communications of the ACM, vol. 15 (1972), with errata in vol. 19 (1976). Inabit-Anu, priest of Abu and Antub around 300 BC, determined 105 of the 144 sexagesimal numbers with terminating reciprocals. Inabit-Anu's table gave the reciprocal of $60^2-3 \times 60^{-3} + 45$ as $59 \times 60^{-3} + 15 \times 60^{-4} + 33 \times 60^{-5} + 20 \times 60^{-6}$.

> $^{20}$Ritchie (1996) has noted that the inadequacy of Fortran programming became apparent as Ken Thompson attempted to write Unix in Fortran in 1969. Fortran was an amazing advance in compiler technology and technology for its time but has been forgotten in the world of systems programming for more than four decades.

# Multiple dispatch ports and register read stalls

The mulpd instructions are dispatched for execution on port 0 and the addpd instru-
spatched on port 1 (see figure 3.4, where the units are numbered). Because
the dispatch ports are separate, it is possible to dispatch a mulpd and an addpd in the
cycle. In fact, even on the most recent AVX/AVX2 architectures, at most one
mulpd can be simultaneously dispatched with an addpd. So much of the discussion
remains valid.

Our first attempt to observe this type is the following program:

```
for(i=0; i < nitns; i++){
    addmm("%xmm8", "%xmm0");
    addmm("%xmm9", "%xmm1");
    addmm("%xmm10", "%xmm2");
    mulmm("%xmm11", "%xmm3");
    mulmm("%xmm12", "%xmm4");
    mulmm("%xmm13", "%xmm5");
    mulmm("%xmm14", "%xmm6");
    mulmm("%xmm15", "%xmm7");
}
```

In each block of five cycles, we may expect the mulpd instructions and three addpd
structions in the loop body to be scheduled. There is certainly enough parallelism
the loop body to permit such a schedule. Thus, we expect 5 cycles per iteration.
The observed number is 8 cycles per iteration and is greater than our expectation.
The initial observation is in fact slightly more than 9 cycles per iteration. By unrolling
t loop, we get close to 8 cycles per iteration.
It is as if the addpd and mulpd instructions cannot be dispatched in the same cycle
even though they have separate execution ports. Why is that happening? Because of
register read stalls.²

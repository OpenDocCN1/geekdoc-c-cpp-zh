# 7.3 Two examples: FFT and matrix multiplication

Section 7.3.1 $n looks at the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) implemented inside the MKL library. For small $n$, the FFT on the Phi/MIC device is twice as fast as it is on the AVX. For large $n$ such as $n=2^{26}$, the FFT does not appear to be well optimized and is slower on the Phi/MIC device by more than a factor of 8. The fact that even FFT reaches only 15% of the peak floating point bandwidth shows how misleading metric can be.

Section 7.3.2 is a look at the Phi instruction pipeline in the context of matrix multiplication. We write a microkernel in assembly that reaches 50% of the peak floating point bandwidth. The discussion is carried far enough to show the principles of instruction pipeline optimization, explained in depth in chapter 3, apply to Phi/MIC devices. Not all the techniques pertinent to matrix multiplication and developed in earlier chapters are reviewed. To approach the peak bandwidth more closely, as would require an application of the full range of techniques.

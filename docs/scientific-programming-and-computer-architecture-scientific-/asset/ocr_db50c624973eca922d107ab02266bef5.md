The FFT is a method to effect the discrete transform (2.1) or its inverse (2.2) using $\mathcal{O}(N \log_2 N)$ arithmetic operations, which is a considerable improvement over the $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ arithmetic operations required by direct matrix-vector multiplication.$16$ The improvement in operation count is vital to making the FFT fast but is not the full story. The implementation can make a difference of more than a factor of 10 to the program speed. We present the structure of the 2 FFT but but omit mathematical details.

Suppose $N = 2^n$. We assume the data to be $N$ complex numbers. The first step in the power of 2 FFT is to separate the data into even and odd parts as follows:

$f_0, f_2, \dots, f_{N-2} \quad f_1, f_3, \dots, f_{N-1}$.

An $N/2$ FFT is applied separately to even and odd parts. The odd part is multiplied by the twiddle factors $1, \omega, \dots, \omega^{N/2-1}$. The FFT of size $N$ is generated by adding and subtracting points in the even and odd parts.

Because $N/2$ is also a power of 2, the FFTs of size $N/2$ are used using the same strategy. Thus, the even and odd part are once again separated into even and odd parts to obtain four lists of numbers. Repeated separation into even and odd parts leads to the bit-reversed permutation, which is perhaps the most important element in an efficient implementation of the FFT.

The $N$ data items are indexed using binary numbers with $n$ bits. The bit-reversed permutation is obtained by reversing indices in binary and reordering the data. The last bit moves to the first position, and it is immediately evident that the bit reversal separates the data into even and odd parts. If $N=8$, the bit-reversed permutation is given by

$f_0, f_2, f_4, f_6, f_1, f_3, f_5, f_7$.

Once the data is bit-reversed, the FFT begins by operating on successive pairs of numbers.

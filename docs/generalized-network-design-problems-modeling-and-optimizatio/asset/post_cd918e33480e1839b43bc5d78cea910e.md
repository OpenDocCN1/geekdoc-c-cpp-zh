
which implies

$$w(\delta^-(S)) \ge \sum_{i \in S} w(\delta^-(i)) - z(S) + z_i$$
$$= \sum_{i \in S} \left[ 1 - \sum_{l \in V_k \setminus \{i\}} w(\delta^-(l)) \right] - z(S) + z_i$$
$$\ge \sum_{i \in S} \left[ 1 - \sum_{l \in V_k \setminus \{i\}} z_l \right] - z(S) + z_i = z(S) - z(S) + z_i = z_i.$$

## Directed cluster subpacking formulation (Feremans [37])

The directed counterpart of the cluster subpacking formulation of the GMSTP is given by:

$$\min \sum_{e \in E} c_e x_e$$
$$\text{s.t. } z(V_k) = 1, \quad \forall k \in K = \{1, \dots, m\}$$
$$x(E) = m - 1$$
$$w(A(S)) \le z(S) - 1, \quad \forall S \subset V, 2 \le |S| \le n - 1, |\{k : V_k \subseteq S\}| \neq 0$$
$$w(\delta^-(j)) = z_j \quad \forall j \in V \setminus V_1$$
$$w_{ij} + w_{ji} = x_e. \quad \forall e = (i, j) \in E$$
$$x, z, w \in \{0, 1\}.$$

Let \( P_{dspack} \) denote the projection of the feasible set of the linear programming relaxation of this model into the \( (x, z) \)-space. Obviously, \( P_{dspack} \subseteq P_{spack} \) and \( P_{spack} = P_{dspack} \) (see [37]). This means that, even if there are fewer variables, a simple, undirected formulation can be as tight as a directed formulation. This is also the case for the MST problem and Steiner Tree Problem.

## 2.3.3 Flow based formulations

All the formulations described so far have an exponential number of constraints. The formulations that we will consider next have only a polynomial number of constraints but an additional number of variables. In order to provide compact formulations of the GMSTP, one possibility is to introduce 'auxiliary' flow variables beyond the natural binary edge and node variables.

We wish to send a flow between the nodes of the network and regard the edge variable \( x_e \) as indicating whether the edge \( e \in E \) is able to carry any flow or not. We consider four such flow formulations: a single commodity model, a multicommodity model, a bidirectional flow model and a flow cut formulation. In each of these models, the flow variables will be directed, although the edges are undirected. That is, for each edge \(\{i, j\} \in E\), we will have flow in both directions \(i\) to \(j\) and \(j\) to \(i\).

### Single commodity flow formulation Pop [142]

In the *single commodity model*, the source cluster \(V_1\) sends one unit of flow to every other cluster. Let \(f_{ij}\) denote the flow on edge \(e = \{i, j\}\) in the direction \(i\) to \(j\). This leads to the following formulation:

```
\(\min \sum_{e \in E} c_e x_e\)
\(\text{s.t. } z(V_k) = 1, \quad \forall k \in K = \{1, \dots, m\}\)
\(x(E) = m - 1\)
\(\sum_{e \in \delta^+(i)} f_e - \sum_{e \in \delta^-(i)} f_e = \begin{cases} (m-1)z_i & \text{for } i \in V_1 \\ -z_i & \text{for } i \in V \setminus V_1 \end{cases} \quad (2.17)\)
\(f_{ij} \le (m-1)x_e, \quad \forall e = \{i, j\} \in E \quad (2.18)\)
\(f_{ji} \le (m-1)x_e, \quad \forall e = \{i, j\} \in E \quad (2.19)\)
\(f_{ij}, f_{ji} \ge 0, \quad \forall e = \{i, j\} \in E \quad (2.20)\)
\(x, z \in \{0, 1\}.\)
```

In this model, the mass balance equations (2.17) imply that the network defined by any solution \((x, z)\) must be connected. Since the constraints (2.1) and (2.3) state that the network defined by any solution contains \(m - 1\) edges and one node from every cluster, every feasible solution must be a generalized spanning tree. Therefore, when projected into the space of the \((x, z)\) variables, this formulation correctly models the GMSTP. We let \(P_{\text{flow}}\) denote the projection of the feasible set of the linear programming relaxation of this model into the \((x, z)\)-space.

### Multicommodity flow formulation (Myung et al. [118])

A stronger relaxation is obtained by considering multicommodity flows. This *directed multicommodity flow model* was introduced by Myung et al. in [128]. In this model, every node set \(k \in K_1\) defines a commodity. One unit of commodity \(k\) originates from \(V_1\) and must be delivered to node set \(V_k\). Letting \(f^k_{ij}\) be the flow of commodity \(k\) in arc \((i, j)\), we obtain the following formulation:

```
\(\min \sum_{e \in E} c_e x_e\)
\(\text{s.t. } z(V_k) = 1, \quad \forall k \in K = \{1, \dots, m\}\)
\(x(E) = m - 1\)
\(\sum_{a \in \delta^+(i)} f_a^k - \sum_{a \in \delta^-(i)} f_a^k = \begin{cases} z_i, & i \in V_1 \\ -z_i, & i \in V_k, k \in K_1 \\ 0, & i \notin V_1 \cup V_k \end{cases} \quad (2.21)\)
\(f_{ij}^k \le w_{ij}, \quad \forall a = (i, j) \in A, k \in K_1 \quad (2.22)\)
\(w_{ij} + w_{ji} = x_e, \quad \forall e = \{i, j\} \in E \quad (2.23)\)
\(f_a^k \ge 0, \quad x, z \in \{0, 1\}.\)
```

In [128], Myung et al. presented a branch and bound procedure for solving the GMSTP. The computational efficiency of such a procedure greatly depends upon how quickly it generates good lower and upper bounds. Their lower bounding procedure was based on the directed multicommodity flow model, since its linear programming relaxation not only provides a tight lower bound, but also has a nice structure, on the basis of which an efficient dual ascent algorithm can be constructed. They also developed a heuristic algorithm, which finds a feasible solution for the GMSTP by using the obtained dual solution.

We let \(P_{mflow}\) denote the projection of the feasible set of the linear programming relaxation of this model into the \((x, z)\)-space. Pop [142] showed the following relation between the feasible sets of the linear programming relaxation of the single commodity flow model and the multicommodity flow model:

### **Proposition 2.5.** \(P_{mflow} \le P_{sflow}\).

*Proof.* Let \((w, x, z, f) \in P_{mflow}\), then

\(0 \le \sum_{k \in K_1} f_{ij}^k \le |K_1| w_{ij} \le (m-1) x_e.\)

With \(f_{ij} = \sum_{k \in K_1} f_{ij}^k\) for every \(e = \{i, j\} \in E\), we find

\(\sum_{a \in \delta^+(i)} f_a^k - \sum_{a \in \delta^-(i)} f_a^k = \sum_{k \in K_1} (\sum_{a \in \delta^+(i)} f_a^k - \sum_{a \in \delta^-(i)} f_a^k)\)

\(= \begin{cases} (m-1)z_i & \text{for } i \in V_1 \\ -z_i & \text{for } i \in V \setminus V_1. \end{cases} \quad \square\)

### **Bidirectional flow formulation** (Pop [142])

We obtain a closely related formulation by eliminating the variables \(w_{ij}\). The resulting formulation consists of the constraints (2.1), (2.3), (2.21), (2.23) plus

\(f_{ij}^h + f_{ij}^k \le x_e, \quad \forall h, k \in K_1 \text{ and } \forall e \in E. \quad (2.24)\)

We refer to this model as the bidirectional flow formulation of the GMSTP and let \( P_{bidflow} \) denote its set of feasible solutions in \( (x, z) \)-space. Note that, since we have eliminated the variables \( w_e \) when constructing the bidirectional flow formulation, this model is defined on the undirected graph \( G = (V, E) \), even though for each commodity \( k \) we permit flows \( f_{ij}^k \) and \( f_{ji}^k \) in both directions on edge \( e = \{i, j\} \).

In the bidirectional flow formulation, the constraints (2.24), which are called the bidirectional flow inequalities, link the flow of different commodities flowing in different directions on the edge \(\{i, j\}\). These constraints model the fact that in any feasible generalized spanning tree, if we eliminate edge \(\{i, j\}\) and divide the nodes in two sets, any commodity whose associated node is in the same set as the root node, does not flow on edge \(\{i, j\}\). Additionally, any two commodities whose associated nodes are both in the set without the root, both flow on edge \(\{i, j\}\) in the same direction. So, whenever two commodities \( h \) and \( k \) both flow on edge \(\{i, j\}\), they both flow in the same direction and therefore one of \( f_{ij}^h \) and \( f_{ij}^k \) equals zero.

### **Proposition 2.6** (Pop [142]). \( P_{mcflow} = P_{bidflow} \).

Proof. If \( (w, x, z, f) \in P_{mcflow} \) then, using the constraints (2.11) we have

```
\( f_{ij}^h + f_{ji}^k \le w_{ij} + w_{ji} = x_e, \quad \forall e = \{i, j\} \in E \text{ and } \forall h, k \in K_1. \)
```

On the other hand, assume that \( (x, z, f) \in P_{bidflow} \). From the constraints (2.24), we get

```
\( \max_h f_{ij}^h + \max_k f_{ji}^k \le x_e \quad \forall e = \{i, j\} \in E. \)
```

Hence, we can choose \( w \) such that \( \max_h f_{ij}^h \le w_{ij} \) and \( x_e = w_{ij} + w_{ji} \) for all \( e = \{i, j\} \in E \). Take for example

```
\( w_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}(x_e + \max_h f_{ij}^h - \max_k f_{ji}^k). \)
```

Clearly, \( (w, x, z, f) \in P_{mcflow} \). \(\square\)

We obtain another formulation, referred to as the undirected multicommodity flow model, by replacing the inequalities (2.22) with the weaker constraints:

```
\( f_{ij}^k \le x_e \text{ for every } k \in K_1 \text{ and } e \in E. \)
```

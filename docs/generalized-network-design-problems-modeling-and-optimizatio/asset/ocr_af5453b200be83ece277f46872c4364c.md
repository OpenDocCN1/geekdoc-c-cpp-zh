Analyzing the computational results we can observe that the branch-cut-and-price algorithm can find optimal solutions of the GFCND problem for test instances with up to 10 clusters and 300 nodes, 20 clusters and 150 nodes, and 30 clusters and 50 nodes. It is also clear that most of the computational time is spent on solving the root-LP problem.

Using the advantages of the local-global formulation of the GFCND problem, its compact form and the reduced number of variables and constraints, we solved the problem with CPLEX as a MIP solver. Due to the fact that the authors Thomadsen and Stidsen [200] did not make their instances available, we generated the test instances as in the case of the GMSTP with 40–200 nodes, in steps of 20 nodes and 10–30 clusters in steps of 10 clusters.

The tests were performed on an Intel Core 2 quad with 4GB memory. The computational results when solving the MIP local-global model of the GFCND problem with CPLEX are reported in Table 6.2 and are compared with the results obtained by Thomadsen and Stidsen [200].

In the following, we conduct a statistical analysis. The Expected Utility Approach [56] technique was employed to determine the most accurate heuristic. Let x be the percentage deviation of the heuristic solution from the best known solution of a particular heuristic for a given problem:

\( x = \frac{\text{heuristic solution} - \text{best known solution}}{\text{best known solution}} \times 100. \)

The expected utility function may be: \( \gamma - \beta(1 - \overline{b}\overline{t})^{-\overline{x}} \), where \( \gamma = 500, \beta = 100 \) and \( t = 0.05 \). \( \overline{b} \) and \( \overline{\tau} \) are the estimated parameters of the Gamma function. Because four problems were used for the testing, the following notations are used in Table 3.8:

\( \overline{x} = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{j=1}^4 x_j, s^2 = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{j=1}^4 (x_j - \overline{x})^2, \overline{b} = \frac{s^2}{\overline{x}}, \overline{\tau} = \left(\frac{\overline{x}}{s}\right)^2. \)

The last column provides the rankings 1 to 5 of the entries. As indicated in the Table 3.8, the SRM ranks first for being the most accurate algorithm among the compared algorithms.

The results presented in Table 3.8 indicate that the novel SRM algorithm outperforms the other considered heuristics.

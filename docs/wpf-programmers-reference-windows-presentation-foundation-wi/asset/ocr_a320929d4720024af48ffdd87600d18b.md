# VIEWPORTS AND VIEWBOXES

Two of the most confusing concepts behind tiled brushes are viewports and viewboxes. Briefly, the viewbox defines the part of the brush's defined content that is used to fill the area. The viewport defines the part of the output area that should be filled with one tile of the brush.

The ViewportsAndViewboxes example program shown in Figure G-5 demonstrates viewports and viewboxes.

In the image on the left, the lighter area shows the viewbox defined by the property value Viewbox = 0.5,0.0,0.5,0.5. These numbers represent the left, top, width, and height of the brush's content that is used to draw the Brush. In this example, the coordinates are relative to the upper-left corner is at (0, 0) and the lower-right corner is at (1, 1).

![](bbox=[0.575, 0.195, 0.865, 0.355])

The middle of Figure G-5 shows a Rectangle with the viewport 0.0,0.25,0.25 filled with white. These values also give the left, top, width, and height of area and are relative coordinates.

The right of Figure G-5 shows the result. The area defined by the viewbox on the left is stretched to fill the viewport defined in the middle. That result is tiled to fill the Rectangle.

The following code shows how the program draws the final rectangle:

```
<Rectangle Width="100" Height="75" Stroke="Black">
  <ImageBrush ImageSource="Smiley.bmp" Stretch="Fill" TileMode="Tile"
    AlignmentX="Left" AlignmentY="Top"
    Viewbox="0.5,0.0,0.5,0.5" ViewboxUnits="RelativeToBoundingBox"
    Viewport="0.0,0.25,0.25,0.5" ViewportUnits="RelativeToBoundingBox"/>
</Rectangle.Fill>
</Rectangle>
```


- Dayhoff Margaret – a physical chemist and pioneer of bioinformatics, known for developing the first substitution matrices (the PAM matrices) and the amino acid single-letter code; she also initiated the first collection of protein sequences, which she published in the Atlas of Protein Sequence and Structure, which ultimately led to the creation of the PIR Protein Sequence Database (PIR-PSD).

- DDBJ – the DNA Bank of Japan, based at the National Institute of Genetics and Informatics of the NSDC.

- DEC VT100 – a video terminal, first manufactured by Digital Equipment (DEC) in 1978, whose graphical attributes led it to become the standard for terminal emulation.

- deoxyribose 2-deoxy-erythrose-pentose, a monosaccharide derived from the sugar ribose by loss of an oxygen atom.

- deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) – see DNA.

- dereferencing – the act of locating an object referred to by an identifier.

- design pattern – a documented solution to a common design problem that is independent of any specific implementation, which outlines the pros and cons of different approaches.

- diagnostic signature – a mathematical abstraction of a conserved region or motif, or a set of motifs derived from a multiple sequence alignment (e.g., as a consensus expression, fingerprint, profile or HMM), used to search each individual sequence or a full database for the occurrence of that same, or similar, region or motifs.

- difference engine – an automatic, mechanical calculator designed to tabulate polynomials.

- diffraction pattern – the pattern of intensities that occurs at a receiver having the interference of waves of electromagnetic radiation (e.g., light, X-rays, electrons) that interacted with, or passed through, diffracting objects, such as closely-spaced slits or atoms in a crystal.

- Digital Object Identifier (DOI) – see DOI.

- dihedral angle – torsion angle, the angle between two planes.

- nucleotide – a molecule consisting of two nucleotides in 3',5'-phosphter linkage.

- peptide – a molecule consisting of amino acids joined by a single peptide bond.

- disease – an abnormality or impairment of the normal functioning of an organism, or any of its parts, other than injury; may arise as a result of environmental factors (bacteria, toxins, etc.), or as a combination of genetic factors.

- distributed system – a set of multiple computers that collaborate via a computer network to achieve a common goal.

- distribution – in the context of pharmacology, the process by which a substance is carried by the bloodstream to effector sites, such as muscle and organs.

- disulfide bond – a covalent bond between two atoms; usually formed by the oxidation of sulfhydryl groups in cysteine.

- divergent evolution – the process of acquiring similar traits via an evolutionary path from a common ancestor.

- divide and conquer – the splitting of a large problem into smaller, and therefore tractable, problems.

## Glossary

- DNA – deoxyribonucleic acid, one of two main types of nucleic acid, consisting of a long unbranched polymer of nucleotides, each of which may be single-stranded or double-stranded.

- DNA Bank of Japan – see DDBJ.

- docking – the binding of a molecule to its recognition site on another molecule (molecular structure); used to predict the orientation of drugs to their target.

- DOI – Digital Object Identifier, an international standard used for identifying digital documents.

- domain – a compact, distinct region within a protein structure formed from contiguous regions; domains often have structural and functional roles – they may exchange contacts with neighbours.

- Doittle – a biochem known for his work on the structure and evolution of proteins; he co-developed the hydrophropathy index and was part of the team that determined the dotplot – a visual method for comparing pairs of sequences in which sequences occupy the vertical and horizontal axes of a scoring matrix, whose cells are shaded when residues match; diagonal lines provide an estimate of sequence similarity.

- Down Cell Adhesion Molecule (Dscam) – see Dscam.

- draft sequence – in the context of genome sequencing, a DNA sequence, parts of which may be missing, oriented incorrectly, or here less accurate than finished sequences.

- drug – a naturally occurring or synthetic substance that, when absorbed in the body, alters normal function; used in the prevention and treatment of disease.

- drug discovery – the process by which candidate pharmaceuticals are identified, using high-throughput methods, such as combinatorial chemistry and high-throughput screening.

- drug target – usually a protein or a gene that interacts with, and whose function is modulated by, particular compound.

- Dscam – Down Cell Adhesion Molecule, a gene belonging to ‘Down syndrome critical region’ of humans; in Drosophila, the gene has thousands of alternatively spliced forms.

- duodenum – the first section of the small intestine of mammals, reptiles and birds.

- dynamic programming – a programming approach that breaks down large, often intractable, problems into solvable sub-problems, combining the results to give an overall solution.

## E

- E – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for glutamic acid.

- EBI – the European Bioinformatics Institute, the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) primarily dedicated to the provision of bioinformatics services to the European community, based on the Wellcome Trust Campus, Hinxton, UK.

- EBV – see Epstein-Barr Virus.

- EC – see European Commission.

- Ecoyc – a database for the bacterium Escherichia coli K-12 MG655, including handcrafted, literature-based annotation of its entire genome, transcriptional regulation, transporters and metabolic pathways.

- E. coli – a Gram-negative bacterium, and probably the most widely used organism in cell and molecular biology, biochemistry and genetics.

- EC system – the Enzyme systematic classification scheme for the chemical reactions catalysed by enzymes; EC numbers are four-digit codes that designate the enzyme class (hydrolase, lyase, ligase, etc.) and progressively detail the nature of the reaction and substrate (e.g., 3.5.1.2 are hydrolases, specifically glutases).

- Edman degradation – a peptide sequencing method in which amino acids are sequentially removed from the terminus.

- efficacy – the ability to produce an effect; used especially in the context of the effectiveness of a drug to produce intended result under ideal conditions.

- EGF – Epidermal Growth Factor, a low molecular, polypeptide hormone that stimulates growth.

- electromagnetic radiation – energy transmitted and absorbed by charged particles that is propagated through space in the form of a wave.

- electromagnetic spectrum – the complete frequency range of electromagnetic radiation.

- electronic engineering – a discipline within the field of engineering that’s concerned with the design of electronic circuits, devices and systems, using applications, principles and algorithms from fields such as solid-state physics, computer engineering, signal processing, robotics, etc.

- electron microscopy – an imaging technique that exploits high-energy electrons to produce up to 10⁷–fold magnifications; electron microscopes achieve greater resolution than optical microscopes because the wavelength of electrons is significantly shorter than that of light.

- electrostatic force – the force of interaction between two charges; positive forces are repulsive and negative forces attract (like charges repel, unlike attract).

- ELIXIR – a large inter-governmental project that aims to produce a pan-European research infrastructure for biological information.

- email – e-mail or electronic mail, the method for exchanging digital messages across the Internet or other computer network (its history goes back to the ARPANET).

- EMBL – the European Molecular Biology Laboratory, an inter-governmental organisation funded by its member states; EMBL operates across five sites, the main one being at Heidelberg, with four others, at Genoble, Hamburg, Monterterodo and Hinxton.

- EMBL–Bank or EMBL data library, the European collection of nucleotide sequences maintained by the EMBL, first released in June 1982; it is a member of the INSDC.

- EMBL data library (EMBL–Bank) – see EMBL-Bank.

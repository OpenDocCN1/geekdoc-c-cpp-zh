Finally, the software extracts text- and content-mining Web services to identify objects of interest within the body of the document (e.g., like gene or protein names), linking these automatically to definitions in online databases.

UD helps by allowing editors and readers to annotate terms with definitions from online resources (e.g., by using SPARQL to query RDF representations of resources like Wikipedia/DBedia, UniProtKB and PDB, which part of the (see Sections 7.4 and 7.5)); moreover, it gives readers the flexibility to find themselves, and add their annotations and comments (which are set in RDF using the Annotation Ontology (Cicarese, 2011)).

Aside from facilitating look-up, UD is able to transform static tables and figures, in situ, into dynamic, interactive objects. To communicate the results of an experiment, or of sets of experiments, as easily (and digestibly) as possible, figures and tables are generally just snapshots of larger, more complex datasets (which are nowadays held elsewhere online); a table, might focus on a pertinent fragment of a vast experimental data set; or a figure may highlight part of a protein structure, to underscore a specific feature described in the text. The ability to link tables and figures to their associated data gives readers richer, interactive experiences, providing opportunities for further exploration and much deeper investigation of a paper’s principal claims.

Finally, for many readers, a given article is often a springboard to other studies cited by the authors, and diligent researchers will spend considerable amounts of time looking to find out more. We take for granted that current articles will be available in electronic form; moreover, most publishers have made their back-catalogues electronically accessible. Navigating the multitude of online repositories and bibliographic tools can however, be a complex and daunting task. UD simplifies the process of identifying related articles: where readers have legitimate access to papers, either they are Open Access or are available by institutional license, the software automatically links to their digital online versions.

UD offers a range of functionality not afforded by many PDF readers. What makes it unique is not only that the PDF itself (that’s left unchanged, so that readers are confident that they’re seeing the ‘uncontaminated’ original), but how it layers and superimposes the additional tools and data on top of the PDF.

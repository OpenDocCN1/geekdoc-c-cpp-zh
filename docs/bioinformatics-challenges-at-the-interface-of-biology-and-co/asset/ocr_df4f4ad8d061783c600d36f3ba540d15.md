# 3.10 InterPro

The development of TreMBL was spearheaded by the EBI by Rolf Apweiler in collaboration with Bairoch at the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics (SIB). In 1997, Apweiler, Bairoch and Attwood discussed the feasibility of uniting PROSITE and PRINTS. This time, the plans were much more ambitious: the goal was to create an instrument to analyse and annotate the growing numbers of uncharacterized sequences, essentially developing a tool to annotate TreMBL. The case for this proposal was much, especially as there were also ProDom to bring into place, and the first release of Pfam had been announced. A new proposal was submitted to the European Commission, and the vision of an integrated protein database was finally founded.

A release of the unified resource was made in October 1999, characterising 2,423 families and domains in SwissProt 38.0 and TreMBL 11.0 – which were also InterPro (Apweiler et al., 2001). By that time, PROSITE and the Features Database had undergone major changes: PROSITE had grown threefold to 1,370 entries, while the Features Database had grown fortyfold to 1,157 entries, and had been re-named ‘PRINTS’ (Attwood et al., 1994). InterPro's first release combined the contents of PROSITE 16.0 and PRINTS 23.1; it also included 241 descriptors from the Profiles database and 1,465 entries from Pfam 4.0.

Although ProDom was part of the original InterPro content (as shown in Figure 3.9), it wasn't included in the first release, initially because there was no obvious way of doing so. ProDom is built automatically by clustering Swiss-Prot. But it is not a true signature database, it doesn't involve the creation and refinement of diagnostic discriminators; moreover, its sequence clusters not have precise biological correlations, but change between database releases. This is because, as Swiss-Prot releases, the composition of the database fluctuates, for example because release may include more data from a particular sequencing project, or projects, relative to – such compositional necessarily yield different results when the particular releases are clustered. Assign accession numbers to ProDom was therefore impossible, and this issue had to be addressed before including InterPro.

Other factors rendered a step-wise approach to the development of InterPro desirable. The scale of aggregating PROSITE, PRINTS and Pfam was immense. Trying to sensibly merge what appeared to be equivalent database entries that, in fact, defined specific families, or domains within those families, or repeat within those domains, presented enormous challenges.

# Box 3.6 Organism-specific databases and Ensembl

The development of high-throughput technologies made that, producing the first bacterial genome in the mid-1990s. The sequencing frequency followed in 1995 and the first draft of the human genome in 2001, has continued unabated and has created in its numerous organism-specific databases. Some of the databases includes FlyBase⁵ (the repository of Drosophila genes) and WormBase⁶ (the database of mapping, sequencing and phenotypic information to C. elegans and nematodes), TAIR⁷ (a database of the biological and genetics data of yeast cerevisiae), molecular biology data for the higher plant Arabidopsis thaliana), and Ensembl⁸ (the system housing data from the Human Genome Project (HGP)).
The Ensembl project (a joint operation of the EBI and the Well Trust Sanger Institute in the UK) began in 1999, before the human genome became available in draft form. Early on, it was obvious that manual annotation of the information contained in the sequence's three billion bps wouldn't be practical. The Ensembl system was thereafter conceived as a means of storing and automatically annot the genome, augmented with data from other sources. It was first launched on the Web July 2000.

However, the reality is modern bioinformatics has become a discipline of its own right — bigger, broader and more complex than this seemingly trivial overlap might suggest, and bringing with it many new challenges of its own. The reality, then, tends to look more like Figure 0.2.

A common difficulty for bioinformatics courses is that they often have to cater for mixed audiences with diverse backgrounds in biology and computer science. As a result, generally isn't enough time to cover all of the basic biology and all of the basic informatics, and even less time to deal with some of the emerging issues in computer science and how these relate to bioinformatics. It's hard to do justice to each of these disciplines on its own, let alone to tackle the problems that emerge when they're brought together.

For teachers and students, this book is therefore an attempt to bridge the interdisciplinary gap, to address some of the challenges for bioinformatics at the interface of biology and computer science. Compared to other textbooks, it offers rather different perspectives on the challenges, and on the relationship between these disciplines necessary for the science of bioinformatics in the future. It will appeal, we hope, to 'how to write that program'; it will speak to students who want to look beyond 'how to use this tool' or discover why the things we want to do in bioinformatics are not straightforward as they should be; it will resonate with those who understand how we can use computer science to make such tasks straightforward than they currently are; and it will challenge the thoughtful to reflect on where the limits are in terms of what we want computers to be able to do and what's actually doable.

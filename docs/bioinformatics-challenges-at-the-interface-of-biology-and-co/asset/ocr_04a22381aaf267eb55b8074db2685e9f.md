Let's now explore identifiers that appear in the field of bioinformatics.

LSIDs

Life Science Identifiers (LSIDs) are URIs that use the urn: scheme, with a namespace identifier of LSID. With a complementary resolution protocol, they were invented as a way of unifying all the identifiers used by biological databases around the globe, in an attempt to tackle some of the issues we've been discussing in this chapter. The general format of an LSID, with an example from the International Plant Names Index (IPNI):

```
urn:lsid:<AU> <NS>: <LSID>: <VE>
urn:lsid:ipni.names:1234-1
```

As we can see, LSIDs include an authority (AU), a namespace (NS) within that namespace, and an optional version code (VE). They provide a single identifier format that could take over the job of all existing identifiers and a single point of resolution for them all. Unfortu-n haven't been as successful as we might hope, because lately uptake has been slow, partly through simple inertia.

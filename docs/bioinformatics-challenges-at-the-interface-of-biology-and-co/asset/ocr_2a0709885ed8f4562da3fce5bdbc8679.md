# Glossary

# A

A – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for alanine, or the purine base 6-aminopurine.
Å – the symbol for Ångström.

α-carbon (alpha-carbon) – the central tetrahedral carbon atom of an amino acid.
α-helix (alpha-helix) – a basic element of protein secondary structure with helical con-formation, characterised by 3.6 amino acid residues per helix.
α-solenoid (alpha-solenoid) – a protein fold generalised by α-helices arranged in an elongated curved structure.

abacus – a counting tool, with beads/tokens on wires/pillars within a frame of some sort.

ABC transporter – a membrane protein that contains an ATP-Binding Cassette (ABC), a domain found in a large superfamily of proteins that exploit ATP hydrolysis to transport a variety of small molecules across membranes.
ab – the process by which one substance is taken up by another, such as a drug taken into the bloodstream in the context of pharmacology.

Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion and Toxicity (ADME-Tox) – see ADME-Tox genetic data.

ACDB – a c. elegans DataBase, originally created to display and annotate genetic data.
accession number – a unique (and theoretically invariant) computer-readable number or code to identify an entry in a particular database.
accoustic modem – a modem that is mechanically connected to a telephone via its hand-set (also modem).

Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) – see AIDS.
actin – a multi-functional, globular protein found in almost all eukaryotic cells, it is a major component of the cytoskeleton, with roles in muscle contraction, cell mobility and cell division.

adenine – 6-aminopurine, a purine derivative that’s one of the principal bases of nucleic acids.\adenosine 5’-triphosphate (ATP) – see ATP.
adhesion receptor – name given to a class of membrane proteins, some of which have roles in adhesion, that belongs to the GPCR class.

ADME-Tox – in pharmacology, the principal factors that influence the performance and pharmacological activity of a drug: Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion and Toxicity.

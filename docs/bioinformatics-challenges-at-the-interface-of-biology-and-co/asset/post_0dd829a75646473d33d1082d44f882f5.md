
# Glossary

endoplasmic reticulum – an organelle found in eukaryotic cells; different types of endoplasmic reticulum (rough, smooth, sarcoplasmic) have roles in the synthesis of proteins, lipids and metabolism of carbohydrates, regulation of calcium concentration and detoxification of drugs and poisons.

empirical – relates to the acquisition or verification of information through the act of observation or experimentation rather than theory.

ENA – the European Nucleotide Archive, which collates all publicly nucleotide sequence data; it has three main sections: the Sequence Read Archive (SRA), the Trace Archive and EBI-Bank.

Ensembl – a software system that creates and maintains automatic annotation for selected eukaryotic genomes, originally launched in 1999 in preparation for the human genome project; it is a joint project between the EBI and Wellcome Trust Institute in the UK.

enzyme – a biological catalyst; increases the rate of biochemical reactions by acting on specific substrates – the reactions they catalyse by the EC system (e.g., whether they are hydrolases, lyases, ligases, etc.).

Enzyme Classification (EC) System – see EC system.

EPD – Eukaryotic Promoter Database, an annotated, non-redundant database of eukaryotic RNA polymerase II promoters, for which the transcription sites have been experimentally verified.

Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF) – see EGF.

Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) – or human herpesvirus (HHV-4), is one of the most prevalent viruses in humans, transmitted via saliva; it is associated with infectious mononucleosis and contains forms of cancer, and may be related to increased risk of developing autoimmune diseases.

erythrocruorin – a large member of the globin family that functions as an oxygen carrier in invertebrates.

Escherichia coli (E. coli) – see E. coli.

EST – Expressed Sequence Tag, fragment of a cDNA sequence that results from one-shot sequencing of a cloned mRNA; ESTs, which are short (500-800 nucleotides) and of relatively poor quality, represent regions of expressed genes and are used in gene identification.

EU – see European Union.

eukaryote – an organism whose cells are bounded by membranes and contain a complex mass of organelle cytoplasm; most notably, eukaryotic cells contain a nucleus, or nuclear envelope, hosting the genetic material.

Eukaryotic Promoter Database (EPD) – see EPD.

European Bioinformatics Institute (EBI) – see EBI.

European Commission (EC) – the executive body of the European Union, responsible for its daily running, for proposing legislation, implementing decisions and upholding its treaties.

European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) – see EMBL.

European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) – see ENA.

European Union (EU) – an economic and political confederation of primarily European member states; the EU developed a free market for goods and services by adopting standardized laws in all its member states, most of whom are also partners in the Schengen Agreement, which allows free movement of people (without passport control).

Europe PubMed Central – a mirror of the NIH PubMed Central archive hosted by the European Bioinformatics Institute, and augmented with extra features, as text-mining results.

e-value – in a database search, the number of matches with scores greater than equal to that of the retrieved match that are expected to occur by a database of the same size and composition, using the same scoring system (the closer to 0, the more significant the score).

evolution – the process by which inherited characteristics of biological systems across generations give rise to diversity, from the molecular level (DNA, protein), all the way up to individual organisms, populations and species.

excretion – the process by which waste materials and products of metabolism are eliminated from a body or organism, as the removal of drugs and their products by the kidney, liver and lungs.

exon – an expressed region of a nucleotide sequence; in eukaryotic DNA, exons are the fragments of sequence from non-coding introns that are ligated to the mRNA coding sequence – hence, the term can refer to the DNA sequence or to its RNA transcript, depending on context.

Expressed Sequence Tag (EST) – see EST.

extensible Markup Language (XML) – see XML.

# F

F – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for phenylalanine.

false negative – in the context of database searching, a true member of a data-set fails to meet the conditions of a search for that data-set.

false-positive – in the context of database searching, a result that matches the conditions of a particular data-set, but is not a true member of the data-set.

FASTA – a computer program for searching nucleotide or protein databases with a query sequence, developed in 1988; or a sequence format suitable for database searches or with sequence analysis tools.

feature table – a table within a biomolecular sequence database that lists known or predicted structural or functional characteristics of the sequence (e.g., locations of the CDS, the translation product, etc., in nucleotide sequences, or the locations of transmembrane domains, metal-binding sites, PTMs, etc., in protein sequences); members of the INDSC share a common feature-table-definition, in order to facilitate data exchange.

fingerprint – in the context of protein classification, a group of ungapped, aligned sequence motifs that, taken together, form a characteristic signature of a particular protein family; the concept of a fingerprint embodies not only the number of motifs it contains, but the order in which the motifs appear and the distances between them.

flat-file – a plain-text data-file that has not structured inter-relationships; a convenient format for data interchange or analysis, flat-files may be created from relational databases, in a format suitable for upload to other databases or for access by analysis or query software.

floating-point – a representation that stores the significant digits of a real number separate from its radix point.

FlyBase – a database of Drosophila genes and genomes, the primary repository for genetic and molecular data.

# Glossary

fMet – the IUPAC abbreviation for N-formyl-methionine.

fMet initiator – a derivative of methionine to which a formyl group has been added, used specifically to initiate protein synthesis.

fold-classification database – a database that classifies tertiary structures (including domains within folds) according to architectural, topological and/or evolutionary similarities.

formal methods – mathematical methods of investigating and verifying the behaviour of both software and hardware.

formyl group – the acyl group (H-CO-) derived from formic acid.

Forsythe, George – mathematician credited with helping establish computer science as an academic discipline in its own right.

Franklin, Rosalind – a biophysicist best known for her work on X-ray diffraction of DNA, which led directly to Watson and Crick’s proposal for DNA’s double-helix structure; also known for her pioneering work on the tobacco mosaic and polio viruses.

rizz receptor – given to a class of membrane proteins that function as receptors in the Wnt signalling pathway, and belong to the GPCR clan.

functional programming – the programming paradigm that treats computations as series of side-less functions, the execution of which provides the solution to the problem at hand.

# G

G – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for glycine, or the purine base guanine.

gap – ‘space’ character introduced into amino acid or nucleotide sequences during the process of alignment to bring equivalent parts of the sequences into register.

G-content – the proportion of bases in a nucleotide sequence that are guanine and cytosine; regions of high GC-content relative to the background GC-content for the entire genome are often taken as markers for the presence of genes.

GDB – Human Genome Database, a multi-annotated database of human genetic and genomic data, including high-quality mapping data; GDB is now longer maintained.

G – guanosine diph, a metabolic precursor of triphosphate.

3Gen – third-generation sequencing, single-molecule sequencing, that observes single molecules of DNA polymerase synthesising single molecules of DNA; that thread single molecules of DNA through nanopores, and that image DNA molecules are read directly using microscopy techniques – 3Gen technologies aim to reduce the cost of sequencing a single human genome to $1,000.

GenBank – the nucleotide database maintained at the NCBI, first released in December 1982; it is a molecular unit of heredity, broadly corresponding to a unit of DNA or RNA that encodes a polypeptide or functional RNA.

gene family – see protein family.

Gene Ontology (GO) – see GO.

Gene3D – a collection of pre-calculated structural assignments for protein sequences whose structures have not been determined, based on structural models defined in the CATH database.

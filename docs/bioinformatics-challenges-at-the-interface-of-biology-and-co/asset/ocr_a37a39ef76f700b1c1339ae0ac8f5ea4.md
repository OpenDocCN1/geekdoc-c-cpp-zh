quaternary structure – the fourth order of complexity of structural organisation in multi-subunit macromolecules, referring to the arrangement in space of the individual subunits, and the ensemble of the inter-subunit contacts and interactions (irrerespective of their internal geometry); *e.g.*, such as the arrangement of polypeptide chains in a multi-subunit protein molecule.

**random access** – also known as direct access, the ability to directly access elements in a sequence (or values from memory) given their coordinates.
**RCSB** – Research Collaborative for Structural Biology, a research group hosted at Rutgers, and of the Protein Data Bank (PDB).

**reasoner** – a computer program used to check the validity of an ontology, or to infer new axioms from ones.
**recall** – sensitivity.
**reconstruction** – an approach to understanding complex entities by reducing them simpler, more fundamental and more tractable components.
**regular expression** – a regular language that allows us to match patterns of text, including and optional matching, but not recursive matching.
**relative database** – a database in which data and its attributes are stored in formally described databases from which information can be readily retrieved; they are the predominant choice for data storage.
**release factor** – a protein that participates in the release of a newly formed polypeptide chain from a ribosome, recognising the stop codon in an mRNA and halting translation.
**Remote Procedure Call (RPC)** – *RPC*.
**Research Collaborative for Structural Biology (RCSB)** – *RCSB*.
**resolution** – the extent to which an imaging system can distinguish juxtaposed objects as entities; the ability of the imaging system to discern detail in the being imaged.
**resolving** – in the context of computer science, the act of finding an object referred by an identifier. In the case of opaque identifiers, this requires the object/identifier pairings to be looked up in some form of database; for non-opaque identifiers this can be achieved algebraically.
**Resource Description Framework (RDF)** – *RDF*.
**reusability** – in the context of computer science, the degree to which a callable piece of code – such as a function, or a Web service – can be used in multiple contexts, reducing the amount of new code required to solve a given problem; usability is one of the 12 (original) properties of a Research Object.
**rhopsin** – a light-sensitive visual pigment in the rod-shaped photoreceptor cells of the retina of vertebrates; it is an achromatic receptor, mediating vision in dim light, and has a characteristic TM architecture – rhodopsin is a member of the rhodopsin-like GPCR superfamily.
**rhodopsin-like receptor** – name given to a widespread superfamily of membrane proteins that includes hormone, neurotransmitter and light receptors; it is the largest group belonging to the GPCR clan.
**ribonuclease** – a nuclease enzyme that catalyses the degradation of RNA by phosphodiester bonds.

# Glossary

DNA – deoxyribonucleic acid, one of two main types of nucleic acid, consisting of a long, unbranched polymer of 3',5'-deoxyribonucleotides, each of which 3'-phosphate groups are joined in 3',5'-phosphodiester linkage to the 5'-hydroxyl group of the next; may be single-stranded or double-stranded.

DNA Data Bank of Japan – see DBDJ.

docking – the binding of a molecule to its recognition site on another molecule (molecular) structure; used to predict the specific binding orientation of drugs to their target proteins.

DOI – Digital Object Identifier, an international standard primarily used for identifying digital documents.

domain – a compact, distinct region within a protein structure that may or may not be formed from contiguous regions of an amino acid sequence; domains may be discrete entities, joined only by a flexible linking region of the chain; they may have extensive interfaces, sharing many close contacts; they may exchange chains with neighbours. Domains often have discrete structural and functional roles – those formed from contiguous regions of sequence may be excised from sequence alignments and used to format diagnostic signatures for the same domain family.

Doittle – a biochem known for his work on the structure and evolution of proteins; he co-developed the hydrophropathy index and was part of the team that determined the structure of fibrinogen.

dotplot – a visual method for comparing pairs of sequences in which sequences occupy the vertical and horizontal axes of a scoring matrix, whose cells are shaded when residues match; regions that are identical or similar (depending the underlying scoring matrix) appear as diagonal lines across the plot, the length and number of which provide an estimate of the sequence similarity.

Down Syndrome Cell Adhesion Molecule (Dscam) – see Dscam.
draft sequence – in the context of genome sequencing, a DNA sequence, parts of which may be missing, in the wrong order, or oriented incorrectly; draft sequences are here less accurate than finished sequences.

drug – a naturally occurring or synthetic substance that, when absorbed in the body of an organism, alters normal function; used in the prevention and treatment of disease.

drug discovery – the process by which candidate pharmaceuticals are identified, using high-throughput methods, such as combinatorial chemistry and high-throughput screening.

drug target – usually, a protein or a gene that interacts with, and whose function is modulated by, particular particular compound.

Dscam – Down Syndrome Cell Adhesion Molecule, a gene belonging to the ‘Down syndrome critical region’ of 21 humans; in Drosophila, the gene has thousands of alternatively spliced forms.

duodenum – the first section of the small intestine of mammals, reptiles and birds.

dynamic programming – a programming approach that breaks down large, often intractable, problems into solvable sub-problems, combining the results to give an overall solution.

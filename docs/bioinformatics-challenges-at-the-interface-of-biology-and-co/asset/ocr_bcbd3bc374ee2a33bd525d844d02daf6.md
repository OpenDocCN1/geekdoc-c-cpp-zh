The annotation of Q23293_CAFE could have changed for several reasons: i) TrEMBL's analysis pipeline might have changed, in terms of the tools it uses for annotation, or of the parameters and priorities given to the tools in the pipeline; ii) the sequencing database will have changed (i.e., it will be substantially bigger than it was in 1996), and other sequences are now more similar; and iii) the source data (ENA) may have changed (e.g., the C. elegans genome has been revised). Any one or all of these could have influenced the annotation protein during the 20 years since it was published in TrEMBL.

The DMSR annotation doesn't obviously differ from the protein family databases, which all seem to suggest that the sequence may be a member of the GPCR (although there is some ambiguity about its first appearance in which it belongs). 'DMSR' seems to have made its appearance in the UniProtKB description in March 2012 (version 76). Within that entry, the DMSR annotation can be tracked by ENA's Sequence Version Archive (SVA) sequence F0081573, version 4, Release 11,5 January 2012, which identifies the gene as Q304.11, protein product DMSR-11 four days later, version 5 identifies the gene as dms-11 (two other genes also bear the annotator). At this point, it is not clear what DMSR-11 means, or why the gene and product name have changed. If we follow the WormBase entry we get closer to the annotation source – the, gene is identified as DroMySuppression Receptor related, dms-11. Exploring the Gene class link reveals that the designating label was that of Oliver Hubert, of Columbia University, but not how the annotation was. We were left to deduce that the gene must have been identical to the Drosophila myosuppressor protein following a BLAST search.

This exercise reveals some of the ambiguity and of computationally derived annotations, and it highlights the fact that different analytical processes are used to annotate DNA and protein sequences, and these processes are not synchronised. We had to work hard to find out what DMSR means. Note that in December 2015, the detective work has a different starting point, as the ENBLANK entry is BY284605 other than F0081573. Within entry DMSR-11 is one of the genes bearing the DroMySuppression-related annotation.

# ORCIDs

Perhaps more than identifying digital objects like Web pages and database records, or inmates like books, is that of uniquely identifying people – not just authors of articles, but also those who’ve contributed to the creation of data, or who need to be credited for one reason or another for a scientific result. There are many more people than are publications; and more institutions with international levels of politics than humans have much more ambiguous names than do scientific articles (Spruce, 2007). It's estimated that around two-thirds of the six million or others in ONLINE share a family name and a first initial with least one other author (see Box 7.2), and then, on average, any particularly ambiguous name refers to either (Torvik and Smalheiser, 2009). Other authors also encountered this thorny problem in some (e.g., Farkas, 2008; Torvik et al., 2003; Aronson and Lang, 2010; Jonallagada and Topham, 2010).

Several attempts have been made to tackle this. In November 2010, the National Library of Medicine announced its intention to establish PubMed ORCID IDs; and the Dutch ‘Author Identifier’ has been used with some service in the Netherlands. However, domain-specific or national-identifiers have obvious limitations in an increasingly connected research environment. A large number of publishers launched identifiers of their own, such as the ‘ResearcherID’ from Thomson-Reuters and Elsevier’s ‘Scopus Author ID’, but the idea of trusting control such an important global enterprise has met with understandable resistance from the community, and ID systems have seen take-up outside their respective environments.

The perhaps most moving has been the creation of a not-for-profit organisation to manage the development of the Open Researcher and Contributor ID (ORCID). Formally, ORCID IDs are URIs with the ‘path’ component being a 16-character identifier consisting of nine-digits, separated in groups of four by hyphens (for example, Amos Bairoch, of many renowned bioinformatics databases, has http://orcid.org/0000-0003-2826-6444). ORCID IDs are often provided by publishers to ORCID: 0000-0000-xxxx. Technically, ORCID IDs are subsets of the International Standard Name Identifier’ scheme used of academic publishing to identify contributors to television programs, songs and newspapers, etc. Not only does ORCID have a pleasing acronym, but it appears to be attracting the kind of cross-publisher and international critical mass necessary to turn an initiative into a practical reality.

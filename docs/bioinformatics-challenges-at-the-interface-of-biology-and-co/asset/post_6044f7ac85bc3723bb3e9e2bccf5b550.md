motif – in the context of protein alignment, a contiguous, conserved region, typically 10–20 amino acids in length, often denoting a key structural or functional feature of its parent proteins; motifs are sometimes exploited by pattern-recognition techniques to decode sequences that share the same features.

MRC – Medical Research Council, the government agency responsible for coordinating and funding medical research in the UK

mRNA – messenger RNA, a class of RNA molecules that carry the genetic information within genes to ribosomes, the sites of protein synthesis, where the chemical message embedded in the mRNAs is translated into a polypeptide by means of tRNA.

Muirhead, Hilary – a crystallographer, known for her work with Max Perutz in determining the 3D structure of hemoglobin; she was jointly responsible for establishing crystallography at Bristol, where she went to determine the structures of a glycolysis enzymes.

Multain – a multiple sequence alignment tool based on dynamic programming and clustering.
multiple alignment – the process of, or the product of the process of, comparing (aligning) more than two sequences of proteins or nucleic acids, necessitating the insertion of gaps to bring equivalent parts of the sequences into vertical register; alignments help to illustrate which sequences are most highly conserved, often scoring functions to help quantify their degree of relatedness.

mutation – the process by which genetic material undergoes a detectable, heritable structural change, such as a base change in a DNA sequence, a structural change in a chromosome, addition or loss of a chromosome, and so on.

Mycoplasma genitalium – a small parasitic bacterium that lives on the ciliated cells of respiratory and genital tracts; it is the smallest genome that can constitute a cell, and is hence used as a model organism.

myoglobin – iron-containing, oxygen-carrying protein of the muscle tissue of vertebrates, a homologue of haem.

# Glossary

NSF – National Science Foundation, a United States government agency responsible for supporting research and education in science fields.

N terminus or amino-terminus, the end of a peptide chain that terminates with free amino group (-NH2).

Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) – see NMR.

nuclear membrane – or nucleus, the membrane that envelopes the nucleus, acting as a physical barrier to separate the contents of the cytoplasm.

nucleic acid – a single- or double-stranded polynucleotide, which may comprise deoxyribonucleic acids (DNA) or ribonucle acids (RNA); concerned with the storage, transmission and transfer of genetic information, nucleic acids are essential for all known forms of life.

nucleus – the genetic material organized as linear DNA molecules complexed with proteins; it is the site of DNA replication and RNA synthesis.

nucleotide base – or base, the nitrogen-containing component of nucleotides, the fundamental blocks of nucleic acids; the principal bases are adenine, cytosine, thymine, and uracil, whose base-pairing structures in characteristic nucleic acid – adenine and guanine are double-ring structures termed purines, while thymine and cytosine are single-ring structures termed pyrimidines.

nucleus – a membrane-bound organelle of eukaryotic cells that contains most of the genetic material, organized as chromosomes.

-oriented database – a database-management system in which data are represented as objects, as used in object-oriented programming.

object-oriented programming – the programming paradigm that encapsulates into self-contained objects both the program’s data and the methods of management.

Object Request Broker (ORB) – see ORB.

OBO – Open Biomedical Ontologies, an initiative to create interoperable controlled vocabularies in the biomedical domain, or the organisation associated with this.

oligonucleotide – a molecular sequence that contains a small number of nucleotide units (typically, up to 50), often synthesized in a sequence-specific manner; used as primers, allowing DNA polymerases to extend the sequence and the complementary strand.

oligosaccharide – a polysaccharide containing a small number of simple sugars, or monosaccharides (typically, 2-10); oligosaccharides have a variety of functions, including roles in cell-cell recognition.

ontology in computer science, the formal representation of knowledge as a set of concepts within a field of knowledge, or the relationships between these concepts; ontologies may be used to describe a domain and to reason about the entities within the domain.

ontology language – a computer language used to specify an ontology.

opaque identifiers – identifiers that reveal nothing about the object they identify without being dereferenced. Such identifiers often appear to be random characters.

Open Biomedical Ontologies (OBO) – see OBO.

Open Reading Frame (ORF) – see ORF.

Open Researcher and Contributor Identifier (ORCID) – see ORCID.

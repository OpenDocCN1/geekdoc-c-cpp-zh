Automated, high-throughput DNA sequencing technologies in the mid-1980s made genome-sequencing feasible in realistic time-frames;

High-throughput technologies have created a gulf between the accumulating mass of sequence data and the amount of data that can be annotated by;

High-throughput technologies have also widened the gap between the number of available protein sequences and their deduced 3D structures;

InterPro is the world’s principal unbiased protein family resource, offering comprehensive hand-crafted annotation;

The founding partners of InterPro (PROSITE, PRINTS, Profiles, ProDom and Pfam) make use of different diagnostic techniques;

The main diagnostic methods encode conserved sequences of regions as individual motifs (consensus expressions/patterns), groups of motifs

ENSA consists of three databases: the Sequence Read Archive (SRA), the Ensembl Trace Archive (ETA) and EMBL-Bank;

3Gen technologies are being developed to bring the speed and cost of sequencing human genomes down to $1,000;

The ultimate goal of 3Gen sequencing is to be able to easily deploy sequencing in clinical settings.

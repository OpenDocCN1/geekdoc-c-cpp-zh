# Glossary

SRA – Sequence Read Archive, a database of short reads generated from high-throughput sequencing projects, maintained collaboratively by members of the INSDC.
Src-like kinase – a member of the Src family of protein tyrosine kinases (enzymes that transfer phosphate groups from ATP to cellular proteins); Src kinases demonstrate a wide variety of functions, including roles in signal transduction pathways.
5S rRNA – a type of rRNA found in the large ribosomal subunits of prokaryotes and eukaryotes.

start codon – the first codon of an mRNA transcript to be translated by the ribosome, most common AUG – in eukaryotes, it encodes methionine; in prokaryotes, it encodes N-formylmethionine.
Star Trek – a science fiction TV series that charted the intergalactic fortunes of the captain (James. Kirk) and crew of a 23rd-century exploration vessel, the Starship Enterprise.

stateless – a system or function that does not retain any state between invocations, to previous history does not change future behaviour.
statistics – broadly, the study of the collection, analysis and interpretation of numerical data.
stop codon – an mRNA codon (which is UAG, UAA or UGA) that specifically signifies the end of translation; unlike the start codon, it doesn't specify an amino acid residue.

Structural Classification Of Proteins (SCOP) – see SCOP.
ubiquitin – a broad-specificity bacterial serine protease that hydrolyzes peptide bonds.
sugar – a generalised name for carbohydrate; sugars exist as monosaccharides, disaccharides, oligosaccharides and polysaccharides.
computer – a (usually custom-built) computer that boosts computational speed, memory and storage space close to the limits of what is technologically possible.

SUPERFAMILY – a database that uses HHMs to classify protein sequences into structural domains

Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics (SIB) – see SIB.
Swiss-Prot – a non-redundant collection of (largely) manually-annotated protein sequences, with annotation from the scientific literature and curated evaluation sequence.
sndrome – a set of concurrent symptoms, signs, phenomena or characteristics that are indicative of a particular pathological state.
syntax – the rules that govern the structure of sentences or statements in natural or computer languages.
systems biology – an interdisciplinary field of biology that adopts holistic rather than reductionist approaches, embracing complex interactions within complete biological systems.

# T

TAIR – The Arabidopsis Information Resource, host of the database of genetic and molecular biology data for the higher plant, Arabidopsis thaliana.

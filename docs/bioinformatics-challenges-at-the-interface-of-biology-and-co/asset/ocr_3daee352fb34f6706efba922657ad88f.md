This is important, because it means that, for the same set of sequences, the patterns of consensus and gap insertion are different, the results of downstream analysis based on these outputs will also be different (for example, see Markov-Raina and Petrov, 2011).

Given that tools give different results, how can we judge which is the 'best', the 'true' or 'right' alignment, especially when their outputs are so difficult to compare? Some stakeholders try to justify why one particular tool or algorithm is better than others, or some types of alignment are more meaningful than others – and the opposing views sometimes collide, generating substantial amounts of heat in the process.

The problem, perhaps, is funding infrastructure to 'keep the lights on' is a much harder and infinitely less sexy idea to those who hold the purse strings than 'curing cancer', even if one of them is a prerequisite for the other. As a result of, the world's scientific cyberinfrastructure is built on the back of projects whose primary goal is fundamental research, and any infrastructures they create tend to be of incidental. This issue was neatly summarised by Geoffrey Bilder, when he said that

If we funded and ran physical infrastructure like we fund and run scholarly cyberinfrastructure, and water would go out every grant we would have architects bidding to build Quonets /sic/ with the promise that the client would get railways and electrical grids as side-effects (Bilder, 2014).

As part of his scathing indictment of all attitudes towards building and maintaining a viable scholarly cyberinfrastructure, Bilder asked bluntly, 'Why are we so at infrastructure? How can we get it right for a change?' The sobering truth is that we almost certainly know the answer to these questions; however, the harsh reality is that implementing the solutions requires drastic changes in the mind-sets of academics and funding-bodies alike, and the recognition that modern scientific breakthroughs are not just the result of standing on the shoulders of giants, but also of creating the kind of cyberfrastructural bedrock upon which our scientists need to reach their achievements. Deadly though this might say, when high-level strategising is essential if research infrastructures are to be fully recognised for, and supported, the vital role they play in the underpinning of knowledge and technology. ES ES project of relevance to ELIXIR, a multi-million euro, pan-European, intergovernmental initiative that's being created to help manage and safeguard, in the long-term the vast quantities of biological data being generated by publicly funded life-science projects. Following a five-year preparatory phase, ELIXIR went into its implementation phase at the beginning of 2013. Although still in its infancy, the project is not short of ambition, as a brief visit to its website shows:

For the first time, ELIXIR is creating an infrastructure — that kind of highway system — integrates research data from all corners of Europe and ensures a seamless service that is accessible to all. In this way, open access to these rapidly expanding and critical datasets will facilitate discoveries that benefit humankind.

Science and technology change very quickly, exploiting these advances can be a challenge. ELIXIR partners are building an intelligent, responsive and sustainable system that will deliver the fruits of these advances to the scientists upon whom many hopes are pinned, and where curiosity is the very cornerstone of progress.

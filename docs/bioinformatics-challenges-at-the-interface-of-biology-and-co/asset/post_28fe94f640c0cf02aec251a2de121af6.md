
β-turn (beta-turn) – a basic element of protein secondary structure resembling a hairpin, the polypeptide chain turns back on itself, reversing its direction; often, there is a hydrogen bond between the first and four residues of turn.

Babbage, Charles – mathematician and engineer who conceived the idea of a programmable computer, and is credited with inventing the first mechanical computer.

Babylonians – inhabitants of Babylon, an ancient nation in central-southern Mesopotamia (Iraq); the Babylonians are known for developing methods to predict the motions of the planets, and for using a sexagesimal system of mathematics (a modified form of which provides the basis of our systems for measuring angles, geographic coordinates and time).

back-up – a physical storage device for copying data, particularly to allow restoration of a computer system (data, files, etc.) following a crash, data loss or data corruption.

bacteria – ubiquitous source of prokaryotic microorganisms as single cells or in clusters of single cells; bacteria consist of three kingdoms of life.

bacteriophage – a bacterial virus whose genetic material (circular or single-double-stranded RNA or DNA) is enclosed in a protein capsid.

bacteriophage ϕ174 – a small, single-stranded DNA bacteriophage, the first DNA-genome to be sequenced, by Fred Sanger in 1977.

bacteriophage λ – a small, double-stranded DNA bacteriophage that infects Escherichia coli.

bacterioropsin – a light-driven proton pump used by archaea to traffic protons across membranes, creating chemical energy from the resulting proton gradient.

Bairoch, Amos – a biochemist and person of bioinformatics, responsible for developing and co-developing a range of databases, using Swiss-Prot, PROSITE, ENZYME, SeqAlRef, InterProt, UniProt and nextProt, and analysis software, PC/Gene; he is the co-founder of the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics, and of the GeneBio and GeneProt products.

base – nucleotide base.

base-calling – see Phred base-calling.
base pair (bp) – pairing between bases in opposing strands of nucleotide sequences (DNA or RNA): adenine with thymine in DNA, or uracil in RNA; guanine with cytosine.

Basic Alignment Search Tool – see BLAST.
basic pancreatic trypsin inhibitor – an inhibitor that reduces the availability of biologically active trypsin in a range of animals, including humans; it weakly inhibits chotry.

baud – in telecommunications and electronics, the number of symbols or pulses per second.

BBN – Bolt, Baranek and Newman, a high-technology company, best known for its work contributing to the development of ARPANET and the Internet.

BCE – Before Common Era, a neutral designation for the calendar era traditionally identified as Before Christ (BC).

beta-barrel – see β-barrel.
beta-hairpin – see β-hairpin.
beta-propeller – see β-propeller.
beta-sheet – see β-sheet.
beta-strand – see β-strand.
beta-turn – see β-turn.

# Glossary

binary notation – a positional-based system that represents numerical values using the symbols 0 and 1; this system is used in the logic gates of the digital electronic circuitry of computers.

biochemical pathway – a series of biochemical reactions that occur in a biological system, usually catalysed by enzymes and requiring a variety of cofactors to function correctly.

bioinformatics – a computer-based discipline at the interface of biology and computer science that deals, among others, with the processes of capturing, storing, retrieving, analysing and simulating biological data and systems.

biological sequence – a linearly ordered set of monomeric units, covalently linked to create a polymer; includes the sequence of amino acids in the primary structures of proteins, linked via planar peptide bonds; and the sequence of nucleotides in DNA, linked via a sugar-phosphate backbone.

biophysics – a discipline at the interface of biology and physics that uses a range of physical techniques and methods to analyse biological systems at all levels of organization, from the molecular scale to whole organisms and ecosystems.

biotechnology – a discipline at the interface of biology and engineering that explores living systems (organs, tissues, cells, etc.) in the development of products and services (e.g., in the production of medicines) within a living organism.

biotic system – a unit of storage that has two values: 1 or 0.

Bitcoin – an entirely virtual currency used by various companies and institutes around the world; Bitcoin relies on high security and a global consensus to ensure the validity of its currency.

BLAST – Basic Local Alignment Search Tool, a computer program for searching nucleotide or protein sequences with a query sequence, developed at the NCBI in 1990.

block – an ungapped local alignment derived from a conserved region of a protein sequence alignment – equivalent to a motif – consisting of sequence segments that are clustered by their degree of shared similarity and to build a diagnostic signature.

Blocks database – a database of blocks created at the Fred Hutchinson Research Centre; maintenance of the database created in 2007.

BLOSUM matrix – BLOSUM Substitution matrix, a matrix to score protein sequence alignments, with scores for all possible amino acid substitutions based on empirical substitution frequencies observed in the Blocks database; different BLOSUM matrices relate to data-sets with different levels of sequence identity (e.g., BLOSUM 62, BLOSUM 80).

bluish ops – an ops with characteristic short-wavelength sensitivity, responsible for perception in the blue region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

BNL – see Brookhaven National Laboratory.

Bolt, Baranek and Newman – see BBN.

bovine – of, or relating to, an ox.

bp – symbol for base pair.

Brahmagupta – a mathematician, the first to use zero as a number and to provide rules for computing with zero and negative numbers.

Brookhaven National Laboratory – a national laboratory in the USA, based in New York, originally owned by the Atomic Energy Commission and now owned by the US Department of Energy, and the former home of the Protein Data Bank (PDB).

byte – a collection of eight contiguous bits that can encode 256 different values; bytes are normally the smallest portion of storage addressable by software.

# C

C – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for cysteine, and the pyrimidine base cytosine.
Cα – symbol for the amino alpha-carbon.

cadherin – a member of a family of calcium-dependent cell-adhesion proteins that function to bind cells together within tissues.

calcium – a chemical element essential for living organisms, involved in cell signalling, and in bone and shell mineralisation.

C-alpha – alternative name for the amino alpha-carbon.

cAMP – cyclic adenosine monophosphate, important for intracellular signal transduction in many different organs.

cancer – a malignant growth of tissue (a neoplasm) resulting from unregulated cell proliferation and growth; cancers may be classified as carcinomas (derived from epithelial tissue) or sarcomas (derived from connective tissue).

Caenorhabditis elegans – a nematode that inhabits soil environments, used extensively as a model organism for understanding genetic control of physiology and development; C. elegans was the first multicellular organism whose genome was completely sequenced.

calcitonin – or thyroconin, a linear peptide hormone secreted by the thyroid glands, which act in concert with parathyroid hormone to regulate calcitonin concentration in the body.

calcitonin-related peptide – a member of the calcitonin family of polypeptides created by alternative splicing of the calcitonin gene, found in parts of the nervous system and other organs, which (among others) may play a role in pain perception.

Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre – CCDC.

Cambridge Structural Database – CSD.
camel – an even-toed ungulate of the genus Camelus, noted for its characteristic fatty deposits, or humps, on its back.

carbohydrate – or sugar, an organic compound based on the general formula Cn(H2O)n (exceptions exclude deoxyribose); carbohydrates exist as monosaccharides, disaccharides, oligosaccharides and polysaccharides.

carboxyl – an chemical group in an organic molecule consisting of carbon and hydroxyl constituents, universally expressed as –COOH or –CO2H.

carboxylic acid – an organic acid that contains one or more carboxyl groups.

caspase – a protease enzyme that cleaves, or hydrolyses, the peptide bond of an amino acid at its C-terminal.

catalyst – a substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction but remains unchanged at the end of the reaction.

Catch-22 – a situation or dilemma made inextricable by mutually contradictory rules or constraints.

CATH – Class, Architecture, Topology, Homology, a protein-fold classification database maintained at University College London, UK, a highly automated classification of protein domains.

CCDC – Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre a UK organisation whose primary role is to compile and maintain the Cambridge Structural Database (CSD) of small molecule crystal structures.


# Operating System (OS) – OS.

**opsin** – or photopsin, a light-sensitive visual pigment found in the retinas of most vertebrates; members of the GPCR superfamily, with characteristic 7TM architectures, opsins mediate colour vision in daylight – their different wavelengths (long, medium, short) allow perception in the red, green and blue regions of the electromagnetic spectrum.

ORB – Object Request Broker, part of a distributed system that matches requests for remote calls from clients to appropriate servers, providing a degree of location transparency between components.

**ORCID** – Open Researcher and Contributor Identifier, a method of identifying people, primarily in the context of academic authorship.

**ORF** – Open Reading Frame, part of a nucleotide sequence starting a start codon through to a stop codon that represents a putative or known gene; ORFs are mostly used to assist gene finding, ORFs helping to identify coding regions in DNA sequences.

**organ** – part of the body of a multicellular organism specialised for one or more vital functions.

**organelle** – a discrete component of a cell that is specialised for the performance of one or more vital functions, usually bounded within a lipid bilayer.

**orthologs** – homologous proteins that perform the same function in different species (i.e., they arise following speciation events).

OS – Operating System, an essential component of the software of a computer, the OS is software that manages computer hardware resources and provides common services for computer programs.

**ovine** – of, or relating to a sheep.

**OWL** – Web Ontology, a widely-used family of ontology languages.

# P

**P** – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for proline.

**P2P** – Peer to Peer, an architecture for a distributed system in which all components behave as equals and co-operate to solve an overall problem.

**pairwise alignment** – the process of, or the product of, comparing (aligning) two linear sequences of proteins or nucleic acids, usually necessitating the insertion of gaps to bring equivalent parts of the sequences into vertical register; pairwise alignment algorithms underpin database search programs, such as BLAST and FASTA.

**PAM matrix** – to score protein sequence alignments, whose scores represent probabilities for all possible amino acid substitutions; each matrix in the PAM series is based on the original 1 PAM (Point Mutation) matrix, which gives substitution probabilities for sequences in which there has one point mutation per 100 amino acids.

**PANTHER** – a database that employs HMMs to classify protein sequences into families and families.

**paralogs** – homologous proteins that perform differently but nevertheless related functions in the same species (i.e., they arise following gene-duplication events).

**parallel execution** – the simultaneous execution of more than one task; this requires the tasks to be independent of each other's output.

**parathyroid hormone** – or parathyr, a polypeptide hormone secreted by the parathyroid glands, which acts in concert with calcitonin to regulate calcium concentration in the blood.

pathogenic process – a process that has the capacity to cause disease.

**pattern recognition** – the process of identifying characteristic patterns of conservation in biomolecular structures and using some of pre-calculated or trained signal or discriminator (e.g., a consensus expression, fingerprint, profile or HMM); the process of discovering patterns in large data-sets (e.g., using principal-component-analysis, neural-network or partial-least-squares methods).

Pauling, Linus – a chemist and biochemist, known principally for his research into the nature of the chemical bond, for which he won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1954; Pauling also won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1962.

PDB – 3D structural data of biological macromolecules including proteins, nucleic acids, carbohydrates and protein-nucleic acid complexes, largely developed by means of X-ray diffraction and NMR spectroscopy or electron microscopy, and maintained by the RCSB; PDB is overseen by the wwPDB.

PDBe – Data Bank, the European repository of biological macromolecular structures, maintained by EMBL-EBI; PDBE is overseen by the wwPDB.

PDBj – Data Bank Japan, the Japanese repository of biological macromolecular structures, maintained by the National Science Database Center of Japan and Osaka University; PDBj is overseen by the wwPDB.

PDF – Portable Document Format, a file format that specifies document layout including text, functions and graphics and so on, in a way that’s software/hardware and operating-system independent.

PDZ domain – a structural domain in signalling proteins that help anchor TM proteins to the cytoskeleton and hold signalling complexes together by binding to specific, short C-terminal regions of their target proteins.

Peer to Peer (P2P) – P2P.

**pepsin** – a digestive enzyme, derived from pepsinogen; found in the gastric juice of vertebrates, it cleaves peptide bonds preferably linked to aromatic amino acids, tryptophan, tyrosine and phenylalanine.

**peptide** – a compound containing two or more amino acid residues linked via peptide bonds, formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next, with the elimination of water.

**peptide bond** – a covalent bond (-C(O)NH-) formed between the carboxyl group of one molecular entity and the amino group of the next, with the elimination of water; the peptide bond has double-bond character, rendering it planar – chains of amino acid residues joined by peptide bonds, the torsional rotations about which lend proteins determine their characteristic and tertiary structures.

Perl – a general-purpose scripting language with in-built regular-expression functionality. personalised therapy – personalised medicine.

**personalised medicine** – a proposed customised approach to health-care to individual patients, according to their genetic, proteomic and metabolic profiles.

Perutz, Max – a molecular biologist known for his pioneering work in determining the first structures of proteins (notably, hemoglobin) at atomic resolution, for which he was jointly awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John Kendrew in 1962.

Pfam – a database that explores HMMs to classify protein sequences into families and domains.

pH – a measure of the activity of solvent hydrogen ions; the pH scale, which relates to an agreed set of solutions, allows relative measurement of solution pH: pure water has a pH of ~7 at 25°C; solutions with pH < 7 are acidic; those with pH > 7 are basic.

pharmacology – the study of the characteristics, biological interactions and effects on normal or abnormal biochemical function of drugs or chemicals, and their modes of action.

Phe – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for phenylalanine.

phenotype – the full complement of observable functional and structural characteristics of an organism determined by the interaction of its genotype with the environment in which it exists.

phenylalanine – 2-amino-3-phenylpropionic acid, a chiral acid found in proteins.

pheromone – a substance that, when released by an organism, stimulates social responses in other organisms (usually of the same or similar species).

phosphate – the trivalent anion, PO₄³⁻ derived from phosphoric acid.

photosynthesis – the synthesis by plants, algae and bacteria of organic compounds (particularly carbohydrates) from carbon dioxide using energy derived from the sun.

Phred base-calling – the process of identifying a nucleotide base, from a fluorescence trace generated by an automated DNA sequencer, by means of the Phred base-calling software.

phylogenetic tree – a graphical diagram, in the form of a branching tree, that represents evolutionary relationships among organisms, species or other entities according to similarities and differences in their physical or genetic characteristics; trees are constructed from multiple sequence alignments.

physicochemical property – a property that relates to the physical chemistry of a molecule, such as its mass, volume, pH, hydrography, surface charge, etc.

pH – a solution in which a molecular entity has zero mobility in an electric field; which has no net charge.

Pingala – an Indian mathematician who presented the first description of a binary system in his Sanskrit treatise called Chandsasastra.

PIR – Protein Information Resource, an integrated bioinformatics resource that reports genomic and proteomic research, including databases such as the PIR-PSD and PIRSE.

PIR-Protein Sequence Database (PIR-PSD) – see PIR-PSD.

PIR-PSD – Protein Sequence Database, a database of protein sequences that grow out of the Atlas of Protein Sequence and Structure, maintained collaboratively by PIR-International, a consortium including the NBRF, MIPS and JIPID.

PIRSF – a database that explores clustering and HMM-based approaches to classify protein sequences into families and subfamilies.

point mutation – a mutation that involves the exchange of a single nucleotide base for another within DNA or RNA.

poly(A) tail – a sequence of (40–250) adenine bases located at the 3’ terminus of eukaryotic mRNA; polyadenylation is the process that’s responsible for the production of matter mRNA.

peptide – a peptide molecule containing 10–20 amino acid residues joined by peptide bonds; proteins may contain one or more polypeptide chains (as many proteins contain only a single polypeptide chain, the term is sometimes used as a synonym for protein).

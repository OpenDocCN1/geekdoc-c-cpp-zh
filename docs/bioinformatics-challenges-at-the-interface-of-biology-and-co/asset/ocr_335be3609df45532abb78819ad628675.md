# Glossary

fMet – the IUPAC abbreviation for N-formyl-L-methionine.
fMet initiator – a derivative of methionine to which a formyl group has been added to the amino group, used specifically to initiate protein synthesis.

fold-classification database – a database that classifies tertiary structures (including domains within tertiary folds) according to architectural, topological and/or evolutionary similarities.

formal methods – mathematical methods of investigating and verifying the behaviour of both software and hardware.

formyl group – the acyl group (H-CO-) derived from formic acid.

Forsythe, George – mathematician credited with helping establish computer science as an academic discipline in its own right.

Franklin, Rosalind – a biophysicist best known for her work on the X-ray diffraction of DNA, which led directly to Watson and Crick’s opportunistic proposal for DNA’s double-helix structure; also known for her pioneering work on the tobacco mosaic and polio viruses.

rizz receptor – given to a class of membrane proteins that function as receptors in the Wnt signalling pathway, and belong to the GPCR clan.

functional programming – the programming paradigm that treats computations as series of side-effectless functions, the execution of which provides the solution to the problem at hand.

# G

G – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for glycine, or the purine base guanine.
gap – ‘space’ character introduced into amino acid or nucleotide sequences during the process of alignment to bring equivalent parts of the sequences into register.
G-content – the proportion of bases in a nucleotide sequence that are guanine and cytosine; regions of high GC-content relative to the background GC-content for the entire genome are often taken as markers for the presence of genes.

GDB – Human Genome Database, a multi-annotated database of human genetic and genomic data, including high-quality mapping data; GDB is now longer maintained.
G – guanosine diph, a metabolic precursor of triphosphate.
3Gen – third-generation sequencing, single-molecule sequencing, that observes single molecules of DNA polymerase synthesising single molecules of DNA; that thread single molecules of DNA through nanopores, and that image DNA molecules are read directly using microscopy techniques – 3Gen technologies aim to reduce the cost of sequencing a single human genome to $1,000.
GenBank – the nucleotide database maintained at the NCBI, first released in December 1982; it is a molecular unit of heredity, broadly corresponding to a unit of DNA or RNA that encodes a polypeptide or functional RNA.

gene family – see protein family.
Gene Ontology (GO) – see GO.
Gene3D – a collection of pre-calculated structural assignments for protein sequences whose structures have not been determined, based on structural models defined in the CATH database.

pathogenic process – a process that has the capacity to cause disease.

**pattern recognition** – the process of identifying characteristic patterns of conservation in biomolecular structures and using some of pre-calculated or trained signal or discriminator (e.g., a consensus expression, fingerprint, profile or HMM); the process of discovering patterns in large data-sets (e.g., using principal-component-analysis, neural-network or partial-least-squares methods).

Pauling, Linus – a chemist and biochemist, known principally for his research into the nature of the chemical bond, for which he won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1954; Pauling also won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1962.

PDB – 3D structural data of biological macromolecules including proteins, nucleic acids, carbohydrates and protein-nucleic acid complexes, largely developed by means of X-ray diffraction and NMR spectroscopy or electron microscopy, and maintained by the RCSB; PDB is overseen by the wwPDB.

PDBe – Data Bank, the European repository of biological macromolecular structures, maintained by EMBL-EBI; PDBE is overseen by the wwPDB.

PDBj – Data Bank Japan, the Japanese repository of biological macromolecular structures, maintained by the National Science Database Center of Japan and Osaka University; PDBj is overseen by the wwPDB.

PDF – Portable Document Format, a file format that specifies document layout including text, functions and graphics and so on, in a way that’s software/hardware and operating-system independent

PDZ domain – a structural domain in signalling proteins that help anchor TM proteins to the cytoskeleton and hold signalling complexes together by binding to specific, short C-terminal regions of their target proteins.

Peer to Peer (P2P) – P2P.

**pepsin** – a digestive enzyme, derived from pepsinogen; found in the gastric juice of vertebrates, it cleaves peptide bonds preferably linked to aromatic amino acids, tryptophan, tyrosine and phenylalanine.

**peptide** – a compound containing two or more amino acid residues linked via peptide bonds, formed between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amino group of the next, with the elimination of water.

**peptide bond** – a covalent bond (-C(O)NH-) formed between the carboxyl group of one molecular entity and the amino group of the next, with the elimination of water; the peptide bond has double-bond character, rendering it planar –chains of amino acid residues joined by peptide bonds, the torsional rotations about which lend proteins determine their characteristic and tertiary structures.

Perl – a general-purpose scripting language with in-built regular-expression functionality. personalised therapy – personalised medicine.

**personalised medicine** – a proposed customised approach to health-care to individual patients, according to their genetic, proteomic and metabolic profiles.

Perutz, Max – a molecular biologist known for his pioneering work in determining the first structures of proteins (notably, hemoglobin) at atomic resolution, for which he was jointly awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John Kendrew in 1962.

Pfam – a database that explores HMMs to classify protein sequences into families and domains.

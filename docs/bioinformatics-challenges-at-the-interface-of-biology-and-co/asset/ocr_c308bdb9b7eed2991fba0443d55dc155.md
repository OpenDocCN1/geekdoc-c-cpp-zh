# Glossary

archaea – single-celled microorganisms that constitute one of three primary kingdoms of life.

architecture – in the CATH database, the structural similarity between proteins where there is no evidence for homology.

Arg – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for arginine.

arginine – 2-amino-5-guanidinopentenoic acid, an amino acid found in proteins.

aromatic – refers to organic compounds in which the atoms form one or more unsaturated rings; the term was coined to differentiate fragrant compounds from aliphatic compounds.

ARPANET – Advanced Research Project Agency NETWORK, the world’s first packet-switching network and one of the generators of the Internet.

arrestrin – a member of a family of proteins that play an important role in regulating signal transduction

ASCII – American Standard Code for Information Interchange, a scheme to encode characters (originally from the English alphabet, plus numerical digits and punctuation) into 7 bits.

Asn – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for asparagine.

Asp – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for aspartic acid.

asparagine – 2-amino-3-carbamoylpropananoic acid, a chiral amino acid found in proteins.

aspartic – 2-aminobutanedic acid, a chiral amino acid found in proteins.

asymmetric – the smallest unit which a crystal is divided, usually used in crystallographic structure determinations of small molecules or proteins.

Atlas of Protein Sequence and Structure – a published collection of amino acid sequences, organised by gene families, first compiled and edited by Margaret Dayhoff in 1965; the Atlas is the progenitor of the PIR-Protein Sequence Database (PIR-PSD).

ATP – adenosine triphosphate, a coenzyme and enzyme regulator used by cells to transport chemical energy, which may be used in active transport, converted to mechanical, electrical or light energy, or released as heat.

automated reasoning – the process of using a reasoner to check the validity of an ontology or infer new axioms.

axiom – a fundamental rule or statement about a system that can be derived from other rules or statements.

# B

β-barrel (beta-barrel) – a protein supersecondary fold, or super-fold, formed from a β-sheet with twists and coils to form a closed structure via hydrogen-bonding between its first and last turns.

β-hairpin – β–turn.
β-propeller (beta-propeller) – a protein generalised by a series of blade-shaped β-sheets arranged toroid around a central axis; each component sheet to have four anti-parallel β-strands twisted in a way that the first and fourth almost perpendicular to each other.

β-sheet (beta-sheet) – a basic element of protein structure with an extended and usually twisted conformation, formed by hydrogen bonding between backbone atoms in adjacent β-strands (strands may run in parallel or anti-parallel directions).

β-strand (beta-strand) – region of a polypeptide chain with an almost extended conformation.

taxonomy – a controlled vocabulary with particular semantics associated with the terms, arranged in a hierarchy.

T-Coffee – Tree-based Consistency Objective Function for Alignment Evaluation, a multiple sequence alignment tool based on a progressive alignment algorithm.

template strand – or non-coding strand, in the context of DNA transcription, the strand of DNA that’s used as a template for RNA synthesis; it has a base sequence that is complementary to the RNA transcript (but with thymine, not uracil); its complement is the nontemplate or coding strand.

tertiary structure – the third order of structural organisation in macromolecules, referring to the arrangement in the space of the molecule as a result of its intramolecular contacts and interactions – in multi-subunit macromolecules, such as proteins (e.g., hemoglobin), the tertiary structure refers to the 3D fold of each individual polypeptide chain.

text mining – broadly, by which specific information is retrieved and extracted computationally from unstructured, natural-language text.

The Arabidopsis Information Resource (TAIR) – see TAIR.

The Atomic City – a suspense-filled film from 1952, set in Los Alamos, New Mexico, involving a terrorist plot to acquire the formula for the hydrogen bomb.

theory of computation – the branch of mathematics and computer science that deals with algorithmic problem-solving.

third-generation sequencing (3Gen) – see 3Gen.

Thr – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for threonine.

reonine – (2R*,3S*)-2-amino-3-hydroxybut acid, a chiral amino acid found in proteins.

thrombin – a serine protease formed from prothrombin that plays a key role in blood coagulation.

thromboxane – a member of a group of lipid compounds that plays a role in clot formation.

thymine – 2,4-dihydroxy-5-methylpyrimidine, a pyrimidine derivative that is one of the five principal bases of nucleic acids.

TIGERs – a collection of diagnostic protein signatures that use HMMs to identify functionally-related proteins.

time complexity – how an algorithm behaves in terms of the time it takes as the amount of data it must process.

tinker – one who adapts and adjusts something in the course of making repairs or improvements.

TM domain – transmembrane membrane, an α-helical span of around 20 amino acids crosses a membrane; transmembrane domains are usually hydrophobic in character.

topology – in the CATATH database, the fold in the core of a protein domain, taking into account the overall shape and connectivity of its secondary structures.

torsion angle – the degree to which a substance is poison or harmful to parts of an organism (cells, organs, etc.) or to a whole organism; toxic effects are generally dose-dependent.

transcription – the process of converting or transcribing the chemical code of one nucleic acid into the complementary of another, such as in the synthesis of RNA from a DNA template, or DNA from an RNA template; in transcription of DNA to RNA, the RNA complement incorporates uracil in all locations where thymine would have occurred in a DNA complement.

Transcription Factor database (TRANSFAC) – see TRANSFAC.

transcriptomics – the study of transcripts, and in particular how patterns of transcription are influenced by development, disease, environmental factors, etc.

TRANSFAC – Transcription Factor database, an annotated database of eukaryotic transcription factors, genomic their binding sites and DNA-binding profiles.

transfer RNA (tRNA) – see tRNA.

transistor – a basic building-block of electronic devices; transistors are semiconductor devices that are used to amplify and switch electronic signals and electronic power.

translation – the process of converting or translating the chemical code of nucleic acid molecules (i.e. the specific sequence of codons in mRNA) into the chemical code of polypeptides (i.e. the corresponding sequence of amino acids in a protein) in the third stage of protein synthesis.

Transmembrane domain (TM domain) – see TM domain.

Telling Salesman Problem (TSP) – see TSP.

Tree-based Objective Function for alignment evaluation (T-Coffee) – see T-Coffee.

TrEMBL – a database that contains computer-annotated protein sequences created by translation of coding sequences in EMBL-Bank; TrEMBL was created to provide swift access to genomic data that could be achieved using manual methods.

tRNA – transfer RNA, a small adapter RNA molecule that mediates the synthesis of proteins, by carrying specific amino acids to the appropriate codon specified by an mRNA template.

Trp – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for tryptophan.

true negative – in the context of database searching, an entity that does not belong to the data-set.

true-positive – in the context of database searching, a match to the conditions of a search is a true member of the data-set.

trypsin – a digestive enzyme, serine protease, formed from inactive trypsinogen in the pancreatic duct of vertebrates, in the smallest intestine to hydrolyse protein.

tryptophan – 2-amino-3-(1-H-indol-3-yl)propanoic acid, a chiral acid found in proteins.

TSP – Travelling Salesman Problem, the problem faced by a travelling salesman who wishes to visit each of a set of cities connected by roads of different lengths; the TSP aims to find the shortest path that visits every city node.

Tubby – a protein involved in cell signalling, common to multi-cellular eukaryotes, mutations in which have been shown to give delayed-onset obesity.

Twilight Zone – a region of sequence identity (~15% to 25%) in which alignment scores are not statistically significant; in the absence of statistically significant similarity, homology inferences depend on collateral structural and ' functional information.

Tyr – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for tyrosine.

tyrosine – 2-amino-3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)propanoic acid, a chiral acid found in proteins.

knowledge representation – an aspect of artificial intelligence research concerning the means by which knowledge can be represented in the form of symbols in order to be able to infer new knowledge.

# L

L – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for leucine.

lactate dehydrogenase – an enzyme present in a variety of organisms that catalyses the interconversion of lactate and pyruvate; lactate dehydrogenases are cytochrome c-dependent or NAD(P)-dependent.

LAG – Laminin-G, a globular domain found in different numbers in a variety of lysosomes, glycoproteins that are integral components of the structural scaffolding in almost all tissues, with diverse roles including migration and adhesion.

lambda function – an unnamed function that can be passed through a system and executed on arbitrary data by components.

Laminin (G) – laminin (G) domain, see LAG.

LANL – Los Alamos National Laboratory, a laboratory in the USA that undertakes work on the design of nuclear weapons; LANL was home to the Los Alamos Sequence Database, which evolved into GenBank in 1982 – it remained the home of GenBank until the database was moved to the NCBI in 1992.

laptop – a personal computer designed for mobile by combining the components of a desktop computer into a single, portable unit.

Large Hadron Collider – the largest, most powerful particle accelerator in the world, comprising a 17-mile ring of superconducting magnets, with additional accelerating units to boost the energy of particles in route.

Latin Script – also known as Roman Script, the alphabet used in most Western and European languages.

lectin – a sugar-binding protein that mediates biological recognition phenomena involving cells and proteins.

hemoglobin – a member of the family that carries nitrogen or oxygen, found in the root nodes of legitimate plants.

Leu – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for leucine.

leucine – 2-amino-4methylpentanoic, a chiral amino acid found in proteins.

lexical analysis – the process of converting a sequence of characters (typically readable by humans) into discrete tokens that have computational meaning. Often the first step in parsing the source code of programs.

Life Science Identifier (LSID) – see LSID.

ligand – usually, a small molecule that complexes with a biomolecule in order to affect a particular biological function.

Linked Data – a method of publishing data and resources using HTTP, RDF and URIs.

lipid bilayer – a flat, lamellar structure comprising two layers of amphipathic lipid molecules; the polar components are oriented towards the polar environment and the non-polar tails towards the centre of the bilayer; the lipid bilayer forms a continuous protective barrier around cells and organelles.

logarithm – the power to which a fixed value (the base) must be raised in order to produce a specific number; formally, if $a=b^n$, then $n$ is the logarithm of $a$ to base $b$, or $n=\log_b(a)$.

logic – in mathematics and computer science, the study of modes of reasoning and the forms that may follow.

logic programming – the paradigm of programming that concentrates solely on the logic of what is possible on the given model.

Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL) – see LANL.

LSID – Life Science Identifier, a mechanism based on URNs, for identifying life-science resources on the Web. Now largely deprecated in favor of HTTP URIs.

Lys – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for lysine.

lysine – 2,6-diaminoanoic acid, a chiral amino acid found in proteins.

# M

M – the IUPAC single-letter abbreviation for methionine.

machine learning – a discipline of computer science concerned with the design and development of algorithms that permit adaptive behaviours on empirical data; central to the approach is being able to automatically recognise complex patterns, and to guide decision-making based on the data.

MAFFT – a multiple sequence alignment tool based on a progressive alignment

mammoth – a species of the extinct genus Mammuth, and member of the Elephantidae, covered in long hair and possessing substantial, curved tusks.

mass spectrometry – an analytical technique for measuring the mass-to-charge ratio of charged particles, used to determine the relative masses and abundances of components of a beam of ionised molecules or molecular fragments, produced from a sample in high vacuum, which ultimately reveals the chemical structures of such samples (e.g., peptides or other chemical compounds) to be deduced.

mathematics – a discipline that evolved from counting, calculation and measurement into the use of abstraction and logical reasoning in the formal study of quantity, space, motion, structure, etc.

M-Coffee – an implementation of T-coffee that allows the outputs of a multiple sequence alignment algorithms (e.g., MUSCLE, ClustW, MAFFT, ProCons) to be combined.

Medical Research Council (MRC) – see MRC.

MEDLINE – an online bibliographic database that includes information on articles published in journals spanning the biological and life sciences (including medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, biology, molecular evolution, etc.); the resource is searchable via PubMed Search.

membrane – see lipid bilayer.

memory – in computing, the physical devices used to store programs or data temporarily for use in a computer or other digital electronic device.

messenger RNA (mRNA) – see mRNA.

metabolic pathway – a series of interconnected biochemical reactions occurring in a biological system, usually catalysed by enzymes and requiring a variety of cofactors to function correctly.

metabolism – in the context of pharmacology, the chemical reactions and processes undergone by a substance, or class of substances, in a living organism; new substances created by the process of metabolism are metabolites.

metabolomics – the systematic, quantitative analysis of the chemical fingerprints of cellular processes – the study of a cell's small-molecule metabolite profile.

metaphor – a linguistic device, figure of speech, that describes an entity in expressive or symbolic way by equating it with some other, unrelated entity (e.g., the dawn of bioinformatics, the roller-coaster of modern biology).

methemoglobin – an oxidation product of haem, with the haemo in the Fe3+ (ferric) state rather than the Fe2+ (ferrous) state of normal hemoglobin.

Met – the IUPAC three-letter abbreviation for methionine.

Methanocococcus jannaschii – a thermophilic methanogenic archaeon of the class Methanococci, the first archaeon to have its genome sequenced.

methionine – 2-amino-4-(methylthio)butanoic acid, a chiral amino acid found in proteins.

Midnight Zone – a region of identity (0–15 per cent) in which sequence comparison techniques fail to detect structural relationships; it denotes the theoretical limit to sequence analysis techniques.

MIPS – Munich Information Center for Proteins, a research centre hosted by the Institute for Bioinformatics, Neuberg, Germany, focusing on genome-oriented bioinformatics; MIPS maintained the MIPS sequence database, part of PIR-International.

mirror – an exact copy of a data-set or server, made at a different location, that facilitates access to the data-set or serving traffic load.

mitochondria – semi-autonomous, replicating, membrane-bound organelles found in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells; they are considered the ‘power plants’ of cells – the mitochondrion is the site of tissue respiration.

mobile device – in computing, a small hand-held device that has an operating system capable of running various types of application software.

modem – a device that modifies an analogue signal to encode digital information and demodulates a signal to decode the transmitted information.

modelling – the task of describing a real-world situation in an abstract format; this is often done using mathematics, graphical diagrams or programming languages.

model organism – an organism used to investigate particular phenomena with the expectation that insights gained will be applicable to or provide insights into the functions and behaviours of other organisms.

module – an autonomous functioning unit to have arisen as a result of genetic shuffling mechanisms (e.g., kringle domain, apple domain); modules are contiguous in sequence and are often used as building-blocks to confer a variety of functions on a parent protein, either via one module, or by combinations of different modules to form protein mosaics.

molecular clock – the principle that the relationship between evolutionary time and distance (as determined from the distance of molecular sequences) is approximately linear; it is used to estimate events when speciation occurred.

molecular evolution – a scientific discipline, embracing molecular biology, evolutionary biology and genetics, that concerns the study of the processes by which changes in DNA or RNA accumulate over time giving rise to changes in genes, gene products, gene expression, gene frequency, and so on.

So, far, in considering the various diagnostic models that have been developed to recognize members of the superfamily, we noted 242 full fingerprint matches (with 51 partial matches) to the $\alpha$ hemoglobin protein, 1,152 matches to the globin profile, and 17,947 matches to the globo HMM. It is important to understand what these numbers relate to each other. Crucially, they refer to different sets of sequences in different versions of the underlying sequence database: the fingerprint encodes only the $\alpha$ hemoglobin subsets in Swiss-Prot/TremBL in 1999, which is the last this fingerprint was updated; profile to the wider family of globins in UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot in November 2015; and the HMM relates to globins in UniProtKB. The numbers were based on the July 2014 release of UniProtKB. The numbers are not directly comparable.

We also saw that there is no consensus expression PROSITE for the globins, because especially members of the superfamily are too divergent for a pattern to be noticeably useful; for the same reason, there is no fingerprint in PRINTS, but instead there are family-specific fingerprints for hemoglobin $\alpha$ and $eta$ chains, for myosin, erythroidoxins, plant proteins, and so on, which the other methods do not provide; by contrast, the profile and HMM offer superfamily signatures, which methods are identified by encoding members. Here, we get a sense of the completeness of single-motif, multi-motif and domain-based approaches, and the different diagnostic opportunities they afford. We can perhaps begin to understand the importance of their integration within InterPro, so that users can analyze both sequences using a range of different, complementary, tools and compare the results meaningfully.


# Bioinformatics challenges at the interface of biology and computer science

- 5 Amongst the most important tags for linking information within and between databases are those of an entry's identifier (ID) and accession number (AC);
- Information-retrieval software can exploit tags to allow information within flat-file databases to be indexed, interconnected and interrogated;
- To quantify the similarity between sequences, scoring or substitution matrices as PAM and BLOSUM series are used;
- Dotplots are simple ways of assessing the similarity of pairs of sequences - plots 'clean' when unitary scores are used, but only when similarity matrices are used;
- FASTA and BLAST are pairwise comparison tools that have been optimised for database searching - the (mathematical) significance of matches which can be returned assessed by p-values or e-values;
- Matches that have significant p- or e-values may not be biologically relevant - more sensitive diagnostic tools may be needed to verify their biological significance;
- There are many different tools for multiple sequence alignment – most are based on a type of progressive algorithm and use heuristics to return good (but not necessarily optimal) alignments in real-time;
- Multiple alignments underpin the main sequence-based methods used to diagnose protein family membership;
- The method employ simple motifs (consensus expressions or patterns), multiple motifs (fingerprints) and complete domains (profiles, HMMs);
- These methods have different strengths and weaknesses: identifying small functional sites and closely related protein families perform well at diagnosing protein families and subfamilies; profiles and HMMs have greatest strength in diagnosing members of protein superfamilies and domain families;
- InterPro manages a unique range of diagnostic tools, providing the world's most comprehensive package for sequence annotation database.

Many enhancements over the years have led to programs that produce alignments with better quality, and sometimes with reduced computational costs, ClustW is probably still the most popular, owing to ease-of-use and portability across different systems. In 1997, a new version was released (ClustX), wrapped in a graphical user interface, which allowed users to visualise alignments more effectively and to select, automatically re-align, and hence refine, parts of the alignment to be of lower quality (Thompson et al., 1997).

The need to correct errors and automatically-generated alignments like Clustal (Higgins and Sharp, 1988), were made available. This resulted in a proliferation of many alignment editors, such as SOMAP (Perry-Smith and Attwood, 1991), SEVIEW (Galtier et al., 1996), CINEMA (Perry-Smith et al., 1998; Sinnott et al., 2004; Pettier et al., 2009), ANTHEROP (Deleage et al., 2001), and JalView (Clamp et al., 2004); many of these, and other editors, are still used, and continue to play an important role while heuristic algorithms produce good, but not optimal, sequence alignments.

# 4.3.5 Alignment-based analysis methods

At the start of this chapter, we discussed the importance of adding annotation to uncharacterised sequences in order to give meaning and to make them usable. In the second part, we reviewed some of the fundamental tools that are routinely used to practise and quantify the similarity between pairs and groups of sequences, in order to explore and understand the relationships between them. This final section brings the story full circle, as we explore some of the principal ways by which the information concealed in groups of sequences can be used for sequence annotation. It's important to understand the nature of this information, because protein families are hierarchical, and the functional insights we may gain from exploring them at different levels of the hierarchy – an overview of this complexity is given in Box 4.5.

All of these approaches use alignments as their starting points; they differ both with respect to how they use regions of similarity and difference within alignments to diagnose structural, functional and evolutionary relationships, and in terms of diagnostic strengths and weaknesses.

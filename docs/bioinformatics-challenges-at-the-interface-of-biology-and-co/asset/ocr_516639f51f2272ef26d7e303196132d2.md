Up to this point, we've discussed the kinds of information and annotation that can be attached to raw sequences in order to make them meaningful. Before considering how we derive such information, there is one further aspect of sequence formats that we should briefly introduce. Figure 4.10 shows the EMBL flat-format database format; it is too bloated for most forms of computational processing. Sequence databases thereof usually provide options to download their sequence data in a more streamlined format, as illustrated in Figure 4.11.

The examples in Figure 4.11(a) and (b) depict what is known as FASTA-format, which may be the 'standard' text-based format for representing nucleotide and amino acid sequences, owing to its simplicity.

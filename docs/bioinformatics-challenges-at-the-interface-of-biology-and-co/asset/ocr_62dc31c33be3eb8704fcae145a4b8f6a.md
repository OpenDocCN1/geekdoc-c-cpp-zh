By working out a division of labor by the EMBL and newer Japanese database efforts, by involving the authors and journal editors, GenBank and the EMBL databases are currently keeping pace with the literature.

Today, the buoy is much on the other foot, as the literature can no longer keep pace with the data: GenBank contained >188 million entries, presenting insurmountable management and annotation hurdles (note that this total appears smaller than the size of the EMBL data library because it doesn't contain sequences from Whole Genome' (WGS) projects). GenBank and EMBL are now so large that they're split into divisions, to help manage their data and make them more efficient. For example, GenBank has 12 taxonomic divisions (including primate, rodent, invertebrate, bacteria); five high-throughput data divisions (Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs), Genome Surveys (GSS), etc.); a patient division recorded by supplied offices; and a WGS division containing sequences from WGS projects.

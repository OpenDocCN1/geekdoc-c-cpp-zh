The PAM matrices saw wide-take in the bioinformatics community, and as mentioned, PAM 250 became a kind of standard for many sequence analysis tasks. Nevertheless, less sequence data became available during the 1980s, researchers saw opportunities to derive more sensitive and accurate models of amino substitution.

Theoretically, there are two main weaknesses of the PAM model of evolutionary change. First, substitution rates were derived from alignments of sequences where at least 85 percent identical. For most practical purposes, however, sequence tasks are likely to involve detection of more distant relationships. The problem is, such relationships are only inferred from the model, matrices corresponding to larger evolutionary distances are derived by multiplication of the 1 PAM matrix.

A number of scientists noticed this; among them were Steve and Jord Henikoff. They endeavoured to represent distant relationships more explicitly by exploring information latent in aligned sequences held in Blocks database (Blocks is no longer maintained, but in November 2015, was still accessible from the Fitchson Research Center). Similar in concept to the PRINTS fingerprint database (see Box 3.4), Blocks contain groups of ungapped motifs – blocks –that characterize particular protein families. The set of BLOCKS Substitution Matrices they developed are referred to as the BLOSUM series (Henikoff and Henikoff, 1992).

The Kyle and Doittle scale estimates the differences in free energy (in kcal/mol) of amino acid buried in the hydrophobic protein interior and when it is in solvent water. Positive values denote the residue. By contrast, the Engel-scale, for membrane proteins (where **lipid bilayer interior is hydrophobic**) estimates the free energy cost for removal of an amino acid from the bilayer to water. Another important property is the surface area of an amino acid that, accessible in an unfolded state, formed when the chains fold. The table shows the accessible surface area of residues when they occur in a Gly-X-Gly tripeptide (*Miller et al.*, 1987; Creighton, 1993). A different perspective was taken by *Rose et al.* (1985), who calculated the average fraction of the accessible surface area's buried in the interior of known crystal structures. The hydrophobic residues support that the hydrophobic effect is a major driver of protein folding.

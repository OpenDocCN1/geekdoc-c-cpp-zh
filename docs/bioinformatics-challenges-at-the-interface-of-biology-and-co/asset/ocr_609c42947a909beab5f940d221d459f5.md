# Bioinformatics at the interface of biology and computer science

Abstract
Yeast two-hybrid screening was conducted using a human cDNA library to search for a novel binding protein transforming growth factor-beta stimulated clone-222. The selected protein fortlin has been characterized as a nuclear anti-apoptotic protein. Overexpression of fortlin in ovarian carcinoma cells TSC-22 mediated apoptosis, and the inhibition of fortlin expression via interfering RNA resulted in an increase in the apoptosis of ovarian carcinoma cells. Moreover, fortlin overexpression promoted the degradation of TSC-22. Thus, an interaction between fortlin and TSC-2 prevents the destabilization of TSC-22 in ovarian carcinoma cells.

The stated formulation makes fairly uncomfortable, but is necessary if the facts described in the paper are to be rendered computer-accessible. Inevitably, most of the encoded relationships in the paper to MINT entries because the pilot project was a collaboration between the Journal and the MINT curators. Nevertheless, this was to design the system that could be sufficiently robust to readily generalise other biological relationships, and would be sufficiently easy to use wide take-up.

In spite of the alleged simplicity of the approach, the experience of processing a handful of manuscripts was encouraging (Ceol et al., 2008). Only five authors agreed to participate, and although most of these needed very little to complete their spreadsheets, they needed significant assistance from the MINT curators. After completion of the pilot, take-up was slow, and in the following 10 months, 90 FEBS Letters were published with SDAs (Shutton et al., 2009). Ultimately, were they were to have judged a success, SDAs would become an integral part of MEDLINE abstracts. However, this hasn't happened, and most FEBS Letters articles still don't contain SDAs (to give an idea, of the 536 articles published in the 10 months to 2 May 2014, 77 SDAs).

Comparing the contents against the fold-classification databases reduces the number further, because SCOP and CATH use structure-comparison rather than sequence-comparison approaches (and structure is more conserved than sequence).

Nonidentical databases may be larger than non-redundant composite databases because they receive only identical sequences, and retain that are highly similar (i.e., that are near identical – sequences, for example, with only one amino acid difference, such as a leading methionine).

UniProt and NRDB are widely used composite protein databases: strictly, NRDB is non-identical, while UniProt is non-redundant – specifically, it provides Ref: in UniRef90 and UniRef50, no pair of sequences in the representation has >90 percent or >50 cent mutual identity; in UniRef100, identical sequences and their sub-fractions are reduced to a single entry.

Both databases include errors: these are sequencing errors, human errors or systematic errors. NRDB is particularly error-prone because it is retained in a simpler manner; with a result of polymorphisms or sequencing errors; errors that have been corrected in Swiss-Prot are re-introduced when sequences are re-translated from nucleotide sequences in GenBank; and numerous sequences are duplicates of fragments.

InterProt is an integrated protein family database. The main diagnostic methods are based on the use of motifs (e.g., consensus expressions, which underpin PROSITE), multiple motifs (e.g., fingerprints, which underpin PRINTS) or complete domains (e.g., profiles, which underpin the Protein library, Pfam, PANTHER, etc.).

Some of the integration challenges faced in InterProt include: the different perspectives on protein families held by each of sources (which are motif-based, domain-based, etc.), which give different views on membership; the volume of data contained in some of the sources (e.g., the size of PANTHER renders rapid processing impossible); the complexity of data contained in some of the sources (e.g., structure-based data in Gene3D); the transient nature of automatically generated clusters (e.g., as found in ProDom, which can make it hard to assign stable accession to entries between successive database releases).

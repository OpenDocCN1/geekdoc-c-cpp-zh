We want to be sure of the extent to which InterPro's implementation of the partner database models also affects the overall numbers. The discrepancy between InterPro entries ‘G protein-coupled receptor, rhodopsin-like’ (IPR000276) and ‘GPCR, 7TM’ is perhaps more than the first matching 107,165 and the latter 144,321 sequences. The question is, why database entries with similar names different numbers of sequences? The answer lies in the difference between the PROSITE pattern and profile designed (according to their IDs, G_PROTEIN_RECEPT_F1_1 and G_PROTEIN_RECEPT_F1_2) to identify members of the ‘G-coupled receptor families’. As we expect, the profile is more permissive – pernicious than the latter. Consequently, in order to ‘maintain’ rhodopsin-like GPCRs, it also matched 41 taste receptors. These were not included in the reported 2,443 true-positive set, but were annotated in the database as ‘unknowns’. According to Pfam, the taste receptors belong to a separate family within the GPCR_A clan, designated TAS2R (PF052967) – a list of Pfam’s GPCR_A clans is given in Table 5.3.

What we have observed is that because the InterPro profile matched sequences not matched by Pfam or PRINTS, the InterPro curators (rather than attempting to resolve the discrepancy) simply made a separate entry, with highly similar name but different accession number. This is unfortunate. The result is especially confusing, as in InterPro release 50, IPR017452 had –48 percent overlap with IPR000276, the set of sequences containing –99.9 per of the set. The additional sequences matched by IPR017452 include the taste receptors matched by the profile, and, in addition, a variety of vomeronasal receptors, proteohome IX farnyltransferases, uncharacterised proteins and other curiosities – but not note, that, overall, this is a much cleaner result compared to the version of this entry formerly found in InterPro release 48.0, which was confounded by bacterhodopsins, heat-shock sensory proteins, rhodopsins, halorhodopsins, archaea-hodopsins, and even chromonasal c oxidase subunit II.

# 3.11 UniProt

The year 2004 marked a turning point for the way in which protein-sequence data were to be collected and disseminated globally. As we saw in Section 3.6, the PIR-PSD had been available since 1986, and Swiss-Prot became available in the same year. In the years that followed, problems arose how to maintain such, disparate and independent resources when, world-wide funds for database maintenance were dwindling. The situation was exacerbated by the appearance of TrEMBL in 1996. For PIR, some of the difficulties were mitigated, in the early years, by charging for copies of their databases and online access to their software; while the funding changed, as the international cooperation with MIPS and JIPID, supported by National Science Foundation (NSF) and European grants, helped to sustain the resource.

Meanwhile, despite its huge popularity, Swiss-Prot was having a rocky road and had to be rescued from the brink of collapse. The database had fallen into a ‘Catch-22’ scenario: the Swiss government had declined to provide further financial support unless the database also gained additional or matching funds from a European Union (EU) grant, because they now viewed Swiss-Prot as an international resource; however, a joint proposal with the EBI for an EU infrastructure grant had been declined because Swiss-Prot was not supported by the Swiss government! In the negotiations that followed, it was clear that a more stable vehicle for long-term funding of Swiss-Prot was needed; in the ensuing debate, draft plans emerged to establish what was to become the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics (Bairoch, 2000).

Several major developments were to emerge from the work of this industrious student, especially as by-products of his PC/Gene software package. To maximise the usefulness of the software, he needed to bundle with each a nucleotide- and a protein sequence database. In 1983, he acquired a computer tape containing 811 sequences in version 2 of the EMBL library; for his peptide database, he initially used sequences he'd typed in for his Masters' project project. However, the following year, he received the first electronic copy of the *Atlas*, and was quick to appreciate the relative merits of the different database formats. The main strength of the PIR-PSD was its protein annotations, while the strength of the EMBL library was its semi-structured format. Coupling the two, he realised, would give him access to a powerful and resourceful. His innovation was to convert the PSD's manually annotated data into something like EMBL's semi-structured format, producing a resource with the strengths of both – called this PIR+, and released side-by-side with PC/Gene, by which time it had commercialised through *IntelliGenics* (Bairoch, 2000).

Although many researchers welcomed these advances, the use of public PIR data-set with this caused problems. Setting aside commercial and proprietary issues, there were other hurdles: it was difficult to parse PIR-PSD files to extract specific information (e.g., relating to post-translational modifications (PTMs), etc.); some of the new database entries lacked functional annotations; protein sequences lacked cross-references to their parent DNA; and so on. Nevertheless, his pleas went unceded. With increasing demands for easier access to PIR/Gene, he decided to make the database freely available to the entire research community. The new, public version of the resource was released on 21 July 1986 and contained around 3,900 sequences (the precise number is unknown as Bairoch lost the original floppy disks!) This was *Swiss-Prot* (Bairoch and Boeckmann, 1991), which was to become the world's premier-manually annotated protein-sequence database.

But here note that, we, are hedging when we say that this is *possibly the most famous example of hedging*: what really means is that this is the most example that readily springs to our minds, but that there may be others who we don't know — this strategy is designed to pre-empt those readers who might have been tempted to write in to correct our ignorance, as we asserted, out that is the most example of scientific hedging! Such linguistic tricks have been tightly interwoven into the fabric of scientific discourse since researchers began committing their results to others to read, and we have become accustomed to reading between the lines; but for computers, it's extremely difficult to distinguish genuine scientific conditions from these more strategies and affordables of writing.

By contrast, while reluctance to make statements to soften scientific claims, the act of citing previous work can lead to claim amplification.

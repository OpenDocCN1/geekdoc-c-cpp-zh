# Chapter 8 Linking data and scientific literature

We don't know how Dayoff searched the literat, let's assume she had access to a range of journals and skimmed them for articles that mentioned 'protein sequence' in the title. PubMed indicates that a single article was published during the 15-year period to 1965. On closer inspection, we find that the article was about 'Acid to protein sequence determination', in the Journal of Theoretical Biology by no other than Margaret Dayoff (1965). So that wasn't fruitful; but we may guess that Dayoff was also interested in describing 'crystal structures'. In this case, she could find three papers published in Nature, Science and the Journal of Ultrastructure Research, respectively, in 1951, 1957 and 1959. For articles mentioning 'nucleotide sequence' in the title, Dayoff identified as of 22 articles, published in 10 different journals (some of them Nature and Japanese), and it's highly unlikely that Dayoff would have had access to translate them); and even if she is, however, from 1950 to 1965, the 26 articles she included the terms 'protein sequence', 'protein crystal structure' and 'nucleotide sequence'. Widening the PubMed search to 'Title and abstract', that number rose to 31 (see Table 8.1). Realistically Dayoff probably had access to 20 articles about half-a dozen journals; sifting through those articles would have been time-consuming, but nevertheless manageable.

Repeating these searches for articles published in the 15 years between 2000 and 2015, inevitably, gives a rather different picture. PubMed identified 2,791 articles that included those in the title, and also both title and abstract fields; 31,312 articles and for all articles published since 1950, 11,737 identified those keywords in the title, and 63,579 across both title and abstract fields. Arguably we could say that if we use these methods to make the task all hand seem it is difficult – few researchers would scrutinise all articles published on a given topic since 1950; the last five years would seem like a more realistic target. To see how much the task takes, then, let's refine the above searches to the period from 2010 to 2015. In this, PubMed identified 815 articles that included those query terms in the title, and across abstract, 8,863 – which is still far too many articles to process by hand in sensible time-scales, and orders of magnitude more than Dayoff had to grapple with.

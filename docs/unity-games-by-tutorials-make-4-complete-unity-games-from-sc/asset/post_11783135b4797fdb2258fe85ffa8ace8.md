Chapter 16: Texturing With Blender

## The Material tab

Open your scorpion model or you can open the scorpion starter project included with the Resources folder. Make sure you are in Object mode.

- 1. Click the body of your model to select it. In the Properties panel on the right, click the Material tab, then click the New button.
- 2. It's good to get into the habit of naming your material. Do that here.
- 3. Scroll down to the Options section, and select UV Project.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_447_0.png)

## The Texture tab

- 1. In the Properties panel, click the Texture tab, then click the New button. More options will appear.
- 2. Ensure that the Type pop-up is set to Image or Movie.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_447_1.png)

- 3. Scroll down to Image and click Open. Select the PNG you just created.
- 4. Scroll down to Mapping, set the Coordinates pop-up to UV.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_447_2.png)

## Configuring lighting in your scene

In the **3D View**, switch the Viewport Shading to **Material**. This will let you see the material colors you apply, right in the Main view.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_448_0.png)

Not much changes, because you haven't mapped your model yet. But notice that with the default lighting in a Blender scene, it can be hard to see your model from all angles.

Orbit around to the bottom of the model and it looks almost completely black. When adding color to your model, you want to be able to see it from any angle.

Select the light in your scene. It looks like this:

![](img/fd5c2ca2ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_448_1.png)

> **Note:** You can also select it by clicking the **Lamp** object in the Outliner, at the top-right of your screen.

In the **Properties** panel, click the **Object Data** tab and in the **Lamp** section, change it from **Point** to **Sun**.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_449_0.png)

This removes falloff, where the light gets dimmer as you get farther from it, and makes the light “parallel,” where everything in your scene will be lit the same amount and from the same angle.

> Note: The position of a Sun light is irrelevant. Only the rotation angle matters, which is indicated by the dotted line coming out of the light.

Your model is now much brighter, but it’s still dark on one side. To solve this, create a fill light:

- 1. Press I to switch to Front view. Duplicate the light (Shift-D) and move the copy down and to the left. I know I just said the position doesn't matter, but they will be easier to select if they are not in exactly the same place.
- 2. Press R and rotate the angle of the light so that it is pointing in the opposite direction as the first light.
- 3. In the Properties panel, change the Energy to 0.6.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_449_1.png)

Orbit around your model, and you'll see that it's nicely lit up to no matter which angle you're looking from.

## UV mapping your model

Now that you've got your material, texture and lighting set up, it's time to map the texture to the model!

## Setting up your workspace

At the top of the Blender window, Switch to the UV Editing workspace.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_450_0.png)

The screen layout now shows two panels, the UV/Image editor on the left, and the 3D View on the right.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_450_1.png)

> **Tip:** Move your cursor over the 3D View and press T to toggle the Tools panel. With your cursor over the UV/Image editor, press N to toggle the Properties panel. That gives you a bit more real estate to work with.

The Viewport Shading mode in the UV editing workspace is controlled independently to the Default workspace, so switch this to Material also.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_451_0.png)

Remember, this will let you see the material colors applied to your model.

## Opening 2D texture

At the bottom of the UV Image editor panel on the left, click the **Open** button. Open the PNG you made.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_451_1.png)

Click the **Pin** button at the bottom to prevent Blender from switching away from this image when you select a different object in **3D View**. You only have one texture for all objects, so you only want to swap that texture.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_451_2.png)

## The UV Mapping tool

Now you're ready to “unwrap” your model! Models are 3D, but each polygon that makes it is flat. UV mapping allows you to “unfold” your model and lay it on 2D texture. If you were doing a hand-painted texture, you'd arrange the faces of your model onto your texture choice. However, this model will be "flat," wherein each face has a single flat color. This greatly simplifies things for your UV map.

- 1. In the 3D View, select the Body. Press Tab to switch to Edit mode. Make sure you are in Face Select mode (Ctrl-Tab).
- 2. Start by selecting the faces that make up the body. Press A for Select All doesn't work since you don't want the spike included. Press A to Select None, then select one face on the body.
- 3. Press L to Select Linked. This selects everything's attached to the current selection. When you have multiple meshes combined into a single object, this is an essential shortcut for selecting them.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_452_1.png)
- 4. Press 1, then 5 to switch to Front view, no perspective. Press U and select Project From View. Imagine the mesh getting squashed flat. The flattened mesh is projected onto the image in the UV Editor panel on the left. Move your cursor over the UV/Image Editor panel and use the same shortcuts for moving and scaling as 3D View: G to move and S to scale. Scale down so that the entire thing fits into one of the colored squares, then move it onto the darkest square.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_453_0.png)

You've got some color on your mesh. But it's a little plain. You can use some of the shaded color variations you made to liven up a bit. With your cursor in the 3D View, press 7 to switch to Top view. To make the separate segments a slightly different color, you need to select a loop of connected faces, encircle the body. To do this, hold the Alt key and click the edge shown directly under the 3D Cursor, here.

Alt-click performs a Loop Select operation. When in Face Select mode, it selects the quads on either side of the edge and all connected faces all the way around the model (as long as they are quads). Orbit around your model to verify which faces are selected.

Press 7 to return to Top view. Shift-Alt-click the segment just forward of the one you have selected, to add it to the selection.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_453_1.png)

The faces you select in the 3D View are now selected in the UV/Image Editor panel. No need to scale them this time! With your cursor in the UV Editor panel, just press G to grab them and move them over one square, to the next-lightest shade of green.

- 11. Repeat the process for each set of two segments, leaving the smaller “folded inward” parts of the model dark. When finished, press L to select the whole body again. Your UV map should look something like this:

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_454_0.png)

Now the body color has a bit of variation, with some definition along the ridges in each segment.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_454_1.png)

## Coloring to the head

- 1. Orbit around to the front, and select the frontmost faces.
- 2. Rather than manually selecting each face for the head, use the Select More ▸ Less feature. Press Ctrl + (plus) to “select more”. The faces attached to the current selection are now selected.

By the way, you used to have to buy a paid Unity license to access these features — but they are free for all to use.

+   - Play Awake: Plays the sound as soon as the GameObject is activated. Note that this is checked, which is great: It means the background music will start playing automatically as soon as the game begins.

+   - Sound Sliders: Adjusts the current sound's properties, such as volume and pan location.

+   - 3D Sound Settings: Changes how a sound plays in 3D space. For instance, you can adjust the doppler level to create the pitch and frequency of the sound for a fast-moving object, as a bullet whizzing past your player.

It's important to understand that Unity doesn't factor in level geometry when playing sounds. Here's a case in which level geometry matters: Imagine two players, each with a large ray gun, stand on opposite sides of a two-meter thick wall. If player one fires, in Unity player would hear the sound as if player one were right next to him (rather than being muffled, as you might expect).

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_47_2.png)

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_47_3.png)

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_47_4.png)

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_47_5.png)

The transform tool, new to Unity 2018, combines the move, rotate, and scale tool into one swiss army knife. This tool is your one stop shop for providing all your object transformations.

# Introducing GameObjects

In Unity, scenes contain objects with a name that you'll guess: GameObjects.

Just as the Project Browser contains all assets, the Hierarchy contains a list of GameObjects in your scene. To see this, you'll need to have your project open; if it isn't open, do so.

> Note: Unity's way of opening projects is a bit strange. You can navigate your file system and look for the scene file. Double-click the scene, then Unity will open. From that screen, you can select the project you want to open.

Or, if you start Unity or click **File>Open Project**, you'll see a project list. Select the desired project and Unity will take care of the rest.

If your project is not listed, click the **Open** button to bring up a dialog box. Instead of searching for a particular file, try navigating to the top-level directory of your project and click **Select Folder**. The engine will detect the project within the folder and open it.

When you open an older Unity project or even a project from this book, you may get a warning from Unity that the project was created with an older version. All this means that Unity may re-import all your assets. That is, it will create new metadata for all your assets. Needless to say, for large games, this re-import could take a long time. Also, since Unity is updating your project, you should ALWAYS make a backup.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_51_0.png)

When your project is open, take a look at your Hierarchy and count the GameObjects.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_51_1.png)

Your first thought may be three because you added three GameObjects in Chapter 1: Arena, space marine body and space marine head.

However, there are two other GameObjects: **Main Camera** and **Directional Light**. Remember how Unity creates them by default? Yes, these are also GameObject.

Yet, there are even more GameObjects. You'll notice that there are disclosure triangles to the left of the GameObjects that you imported.

Holding down the **Alt button** on PC, or **Option** on Mac, **click** each **disclosure triangle**.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_52_0.png)

As you can see, you have so many GameObjects:

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_52_1.png)

Three important points to remember:

- GameObjects can contain other GameObjects. On a base level, this useful behavior allows organizing and parenting of GameObjects that are related to each other. More importantly, changes to the parent GameObjects may affect their children — more on this in just a moment.
- GameObjects are converted into Prefabs. Unity creates Prefabs for the various pieces of your model that you can alter anywhere.
- Everything contained in the Hierarchy is a GameObject. Even things such as cameras and lights are GameObjects. If it's in the Hierarchy, it's a GameObject subject to your command.

Our hero is so bold that he's picking his nose with his gun. You need to get him moving but, first, you need to reposition your GameObjects.

# Moving GameObjects

Before starting, collapse all the GameObject trees by clicking the disclosure triangles.

Select BobbleArena in the Hierarchy and take a moment to observe the Inspector, which provides information about the selected GameObject.

GameObjects contain a number of components, which you can think of as small units of functionality. The one that all GameObjects contain: The Transform component.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_53_0.png)

The Transform component contains the position, rotation and scale of the GameObject. Using the inspector, you can set these to specific numbers instead of having to rely upon your eye. When hovering the mouse over the axis name, you will see arrows next to the pointer.

Press the left mouse button and drag the mouse left or right to adjust those numbers. This trick is an easy way to adjust the values by small increments.

## iOS

Publishing your app to the iOS App store is an easy process in some respects, but the actual app submission process can be very complicated. The process requires you to have a Mac, the latest version of Xcode (version 9 at the time of this writing) and an Apple Developer Account. The developer account costs $99 per year, but you’ll be making millions off your game soon...right?

To enroll in the Apple Developer Program, head to https://developer.apple.com/programs/

In Unity, open the Build Settings window, select iOS and click Switch Platform.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_614_0.png)

One of the nice things about switching to the iOS platform is that the Game will update with all the device sizes. This makes it easy to test on a device screen size without pushing to device.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_614_1.png)

Open the Player Settings and make sure iOS is selected. First, you must choose a company name and a product name. The product name is what the game will be called on the device.

| Company Name | RazeWare |
| --- | --- |
| Product Name | Runestrife |
| Default Icon | |

At this point, you can change specific iOS settings. Here's the breakdown:

- **Icon**: The icons used in the app. Unity populates the icons based on your default icon, but you can override the icon for certain devices.
- **Resolution and Presentation**: Lets you to set the starting orientation, whether you'd like the status bar to display, and full screen support.
- **Splash Image**: The first image the user sees when the game is launched. Ideally, this should be a static screenshot of the first screen in your game.
- **Debugging and Crash Reporting**: Lets you turn profiling on and determine how the app will behave when it encounters an unhandled exception.
- **Other Settings**: Configures build settings and specific device settings.
- **XR Settings**: According to Unity documentation, XR is an umbrella term that encompasses Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR), and Mixed Reality (MR). At the moment, it has only one setting which is to indicate your game supports VR.

Expand the Resolution and Presentation and set the Default Orientation to Auto Rotation. You will see a series of orientations for your game. Uncheck the Portrait and Portrait Upside Down.

Now to customize the launching of your game in iOS. In iOS, Apple recommends you provide a screenshot of your first screen. When the user taps your launch icon, iOS shows this screenshot, which gives the feeling that the app launches right away even though it is still loading.

Expand the Splash Image section and you will see that you have a lot of options.

![](img/fd5c2ca20ea33da76d2b61297cc57f6c_616_0.png)

If you are using Unity Personal, your games will show a Made with Unity screen. If you have the Pro version of Unity, you can disable this splash screen or customize it. You can even display a list of logos to show as your game loads and designate the team between them.

The launch screen options are where you'll put your image of your launch icon. This section allows you to use custom XIBs, Storyboards, or you can use the legacy launch images. You'll need to get the dimensions of each device and make a corresponding image.

For more information about the screen sizes, please see Unity's documentation: https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-PlayerSettingsiOS.html).

Finally, expand the Other Settings section and change the Bundle Identifier to match your company name. This is very important, as every app must have a unique identifier. The format of this string is: com.[DEVELOPER].[GAME NAME]. When configuring your app in iTunes Connect, you'll need to use this identifier.

Now to make your build. Open File > Build Settings and click Build. You will be prompted for a location to save the build. Select your Desktop, call it Runestrifle, and click Save.

Unity will take a bit of time to generate the Xcode project, which project contains everything necessary to build and test your game on the device. Apple will permit you to test the game on a device without a developer account, but you must have a developer account to submit to the App Store.

To learn more about creating a developer account, running your app on a device and submitting your game to the App Store, check out our tutorial How to Submit an App to Apple: From No Account to Store.

+   5. \( f_n(M) = S^n \). Using this, we can find the solution to knapsack(1, n, m).
+   6. If the last pair in \( S^n \) is \( (p, w) \), then
+      (a) set \( x_n = 0 \) if \( (p, w) \in S^{n-1} \);
+      (b) set \( x_n = 1 \) if \( (p, w) \in S^{n-1} \), and compute \( p = p - x_n \) and \( w = w - w_n \).
+   7. Repeat step 6 for \( x = n, ..., 1 \).

Let us solve a few examples based on this concept.

**EXAMPLE 16.5** Generate the sets \( S^i, 0 \le i \le 3 \), for the following knapsack instance: \( n = 3, (w_1, w_2, w_3) = (2, 3, 4), (p_1, p_2, p_3) = (1, 2, 5) \), and \( M = 6 \). In addition, find an optimal solution.

# Solution

\( S^0 = \{(0, 0)\} \)
\( S^0_1 \) is obtained by adding \( (p_1, w_1) = (1, 2) \) to each pair of \( S^0 \).
\( S^0_1 = \{(1, 2)\} \)
\( S^1 \) is obtained by merging and purging \( S^0 \) and \( S^0_1 \).
\( S^1 = \{(0, 0), (1, 2)\} \)
\( S^1_1 \) is obtained by adding \( (p_2, w_2) = (2, 3) \) to each pair of \( S^1 \).
\( S^1_1 = \{(2, 3), (3, 5)\} \)
\( S^2 \) is obtained by merging and purging \( S^1 \) and \( S^1_1 \).
\( S^2 = \{(0, 0), (1, 2), (2, 3), (3, 5)\} \)
\( S^2_1 \) is obtained by adding \( (p_3, w_3) = (5, 4) \) to each pair of \( S^2 \).
\( S^2_1 = \{(5, 4), (6, 6), (7, 8), (8, 9)\} \)
\( S^3 \) is obtained by merging and purging \( S^2 \) and \( S^2_1 \).
\( S^3 = \{(0, 0), (1, 2), \{(2, 3), (5, 4), (6, 6)\} \)

The pair (3, 5) gets purged here by dominance rule. In addition, the pairs (7, 7) and (8, 9) get purged because \( w > M \).

The last pair in \( S^3 \) is \( (p, w) = (6, 6) \in S^3 \); hence, \( x_3 = 1 \). However, \( (p_3, w_3) = (5, 4) \). Hence, \( (p, w) = (6 - 5, 6 - 4) = (1, 2) \). Since \( (1, 2) \in S^2 \) and \( (1, 2) \in S^1 \), set \( x_2 = 0 \). Since \( (1, 2) \in S^2 \), set \( x_1 = 1 \). Hence, an optimal solution for the given knapsack problem is \( (x_1, x_2, x_3) = (1, 0, 1) \).

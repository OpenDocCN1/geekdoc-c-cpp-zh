Therefore, we create a variable called PROVTYPE that is this substring of the PROVIDER variable, starting at the third position and ending with the sixth position (inclusive). Then, we output a set UTLIP_2010 FNL_SS, keeping only those records with values of PROVTYPE that identifies IPSF and critical access hospitals. We output this data to a permanent library because we will use it in Chapter 8 when we measure Medicare payments for inpatient services (i.e., we will measure payments only for those services that occurred in acute short-stay hospitals).

In addition to keeping only inpatient patients that occurred in acute short-stay hospitals, our code in Step 7 also uses the Medicare payment amount (PMT_AMT) to remove inpatient with negative payment amounts. There should be few such claims. Generally, these claims are excluded because the Medicare payment amount is seen as an indicator that information on the claim may not be reliable. In some instances, investigators do not use claims with negative payments for payment analysis, but do these claims for utilization and quality analyses. For example, in studying quality of care, we may choose to "err on the side of the provider" and look for evidence of a service that indicates high care (a retinal exam) regardless of the value of payment. In our example research project, we will keep it and exclude these records from the inpatient data for the remainder of our work. Therefore, from this point forward, we will use the output of Step 7, called UTLIP_2010 FNL_SS, for all work requiring inpatient claims.

In Step 7, we use the UTLIP_2010 FNL_SS data set that contains only claims for acute short-stay hospitals to calculate the length of stay (IP_POS), measured in days for each beneficiary's hospital stay. In simple terms, the length of stay is the discharge date (DISCHGRDT) less the admission date (ADMS_DT), plus one day to account for (without adding one day, the result would be a day of zero days). In some cases, you may consider writing although for cases where the admission and discharge dates fall on the same day, or stays longer than a single day. We also perform a frequency distribution of the length of stay calculations. We output a set called STAY_UTIL, which contains beneficiary and provider identifiers, the start and end days of the hospitalization, and the calculated length of stay. We also perform a frequency distribution of IP_POS, using SAS' Output Delivery System to create our results.

```
* STEP 7.6: CALCULATE LOS, ASSUMING EVERY INPATIENT CLAIM IS A STAY /
data stay_util(keep=bene_id admn_dt dischgrdt provider ip_pos);
  set utlip_2010 fnl_ss, admn_dt=1;
run;
ods html file="Users\Gillingham\Desktop\BookFINAL_DATA_OUTPUT\Gillingham_fig_6.html"
  image_dpi=300 style=GrayscalePrinter;
ods graphics on / imageft=png;
  title "FREQ OF INPATIENT LENGTH OF STAY";
proc freq data=stay_util(where=(1<=ip_pos<=10));
  tables ip_pos;
run;
```

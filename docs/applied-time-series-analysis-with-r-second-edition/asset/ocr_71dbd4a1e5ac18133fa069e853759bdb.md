$$X(t)=C\cos(\lambda t+\Upsilon), \tag{1.31}$$

where, as before, $C$ and $\lambda$ are real-valued constants, and we examine the special case in which $\gamma\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,2\pi]$. We recall from Example 1.3 that $\mu=0$ and $\gamma(h)=(C^2/2)\cos(\lambda h)$. Thus, $\gamma(h)$ is a periodic function of period $2\pi/\lambda$ or said another way, $\gamma(h)$ has a frequency $\lambda/(2\pi)$. The spectrum as defined in Equation 1.23 is

$$P_X(f)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi ifh}\gamma(h)\,dh,$$

$$P_X(f)=\frac{C^2}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi ifh}\cos(\lambda h)\,dh. \tag{1.32}$$

$$P_X(f)=\frac{C^2}{4}\left\{\delta\left(f-\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\right)+\delta\left(f+\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\right)\right\}. \tag{1.33}$$

where $\delta$ denotes the *Dirac delta function*.

**Remark:** The integral in Equation 1.32 is not convergent if one considers it as an improper Riemann integral. Moreover, the $\delta$-function in Equation 1.33 is not really a function but is instead a functional. Nevertheless, the results of Equations 1.32 and 1.33 can be made rigorous by interpreting the integral in Equation 1.32 as the limit of a sequence of integrals and by interpreting $\delta$ as a “generalized function.”

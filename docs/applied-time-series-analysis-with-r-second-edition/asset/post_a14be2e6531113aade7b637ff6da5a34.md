which can be written in factored form as $(1 + 0.95B)(1 - 1.6B + 0.9B^2)X_t = a_t$. The factored form of the model in Equation 3.75 has the same second-order factor that was in model (3.73) with the difference being that the characteristic equation associated with Equation 3.75 has a negative real root $r_1 = 1/(-0.95) = -1.05$. As mentioned in the discussion of **Equation 3.73**, the AR(2) model based on the second-order factor $1 - 1.6B + 0.9B^2$ will have: (a) realizations that are pseudo-periodic in nature with period about 11, (b) autocorrelations that are a damped sinusoidal with a period of about 11 lags, and (c) a spectral density with a peak near $f_0 = 0.09$. ## 3.2.10 AR(1) and AR(2) Building Blocks of an AR(p) Model

In Examples 3.8 and 3.9, we were able to determine the characteristics of the AR(3) models by examining the first- and second-order factors of the model. These examples suggest that the key to understanding AR(p) models is to recognize that the AR(1) and AR(2) models serve as “building blocks.” The pth-order characteristic equation $\phi(z) = 1 - \phi_1z - \cdots - \phi_pz^p = 0$ will have real roots, complex roots (which occur as complex conjugates), or a combination of real and complex roots. As a consequence, $\phi(z)$ can be factored as

$$\phi(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\alpha_{1j}z-\alpha_{2j}z^2)$$  
(3.76)

where there are two types of factors.

- 1. *Factors associated with real roots (linear factors):* Factors of **Equation 3.76** associated with real roots are linear factors, that is, $\alpha_{2j}=0$. The absolute value of the reciprocal of the associated root is $|r_j^{-1}|=|\alpha_{1j}|$, and, thus, it is necessary that $|\alpha_{1j}|<1$ in order for the associated root to be outside the unit circle. “System frequencies,” $f_0$, for these real roots are assigned as

$$
f_0=0 \quad \text{if } \alpha_{1j}>0
$$

$$
\phantom{f_0}=0.5 \quad \text{if } \alpha_{1j}<0.
$$

- 2. *Factors associated with complex roots (quadratic factors):* In this case the factor $1-\alpha_{1j}z-\alpha_{2j}z^2$ is an irreducible quadratic factor, that is, $\alpha_{1j}^2+4\alpha_{2j}<0$, and the two roots associated with this factor are complex conjugate pairs. For measuring the proximity of a root to the unit circle, it is useful to note that $|r_j^{-1}|=\sqrt{-\alpha_{2j}}$. Recall that $|r_j^{-1}|<1$ for a stationary process, and the closer $|r_j^{-1}|$ is to 1 the closer these roots are to the nonstationary region. The system frequency associated with the

quadratic factor $1 - \alpha_{1j}z - \alpha_{2j}z^2$ is

$$f_{0j} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{a_{1j}}{2\sqrt{-a_{2j}}}\right).$$

Thus, an AR(p) model is fundamentally a combination of first and second-order components. Specifically, $\rho_k$ for an AR(p) process behaves as a mixture of damped exponentials and/or damped sinusoids. Each $j = 1, ..., m$ in Equation 3.76 corresponds to either a real root or a complex conjugate pair of roots. Without loss of generality, we let $j = 1, ..., m_r$ correspond to the real roots and $j = m_r + 1, ..., m$ to the pairs of complex conjugate roots. A useful reformulation of the general expression in Equation 3.49 for the autocorrelation of an AR(p) (with no repeated roots of the characteristic equation) can be rewritten as

$$\rho_k = \sum_{j=1}^{m_r} C_j (r_j^{-1})^k + \sum_{j=m_r+1}^{m} \left(C_{j1}(r_j^{-1})^k + C_{j2}(r_j^{*-1})^k\right) \tag{3.77}$$

$$= \sum_{j=1}^{m_r} C_j \alpha_j^k + \sum_{j=m_r+1}^{m} D_j (\sqrt{-\alpha_{2j}})^k \sin(2\pi f_{0j} k + \psi_j),$$

where

$$f_{0j} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{a_{1j}}{2\sqrt{-a_{2j}}}\right), \tag{3.78}$$

and where $C_j$, $j = 1, ..., m_r$ and $D_j$ and $\psi_j$, $j = m_r + 1, ..., m$ are real constants. Equation 3.77 shows that $\rho_k$ for an AR(p) process behaves like a mixture of damped exponentials and/or damped sinusoids. Thus, the factors of $\phi(z)$ determine the correlation structure of an AR(p) as well as the behavior of its realizations. The understanding of these factors is often critical to understanding the underlying physical phenomenon.

## EXAMPLE 3.10: AUTOCORRELATION FUNCTION FOR EXAMPLES 3.8 AND 3.9

Based on Equation 3.77, the autocorrelation function for the AR(3) model of Example 3.8 is

$$\rho_k = 0.66(0.95)^k + 0.3518(0.95)^k \sin(2\pi f_0 k + 1.31), \tag{3.79}$$

where $f_0 = 0.09$. From Equation 3.79, it can be seen that the expression for the autocorrelation function is a mixture of a damped exponential and a damped sinusoidal component which is consistent with our observations concerning Figure 3.16b.

The autocorrelation function for the AR(3) model in Example 3.9, which has a negative real root and the same second-order factor as the model in Example 3.8, is given by

$\rho_k = 0.139(-0.95)^k + 0.862(0.95)^k \sin(2\pi f_0 k + 1.5), \qquad (3.80)$

where again $f_0 = 0.09$. Note that the coefficient (0.139) of the oscillating damped exponential term in Equation 3.80 is relatively small causing the damped oscillating exponential behavior to not be very visible in a plot of the autocorrelations as was noted in the discussion of Figure 3.17b.

## 3.2.11 Factor Tables

It is useful to summarize information regarding the first and second-order factors of an AR($p$) model, in a factor table. For each first-order or irreducible second-order factor of the model the factor table displays the

- 1. Roots of the characteristic equation
- 2. Proximity of the roots to the unit circle (by tabling the absolute reciprocal of the roots)
- 3. System frequencies $f_{0j}$

Factors in the factor table are listed in decreasing order of the proximity of the associated roots to the unit circle since the closer to the unit circle, the stronger the autocorrelation effect. See Woodward et al. (2009).

While there is no volatility in the noise sequence, the realization in Figure 4.14b shows oscillation in amplitudes, which is consistent with realizations from AR(2) models with complex roots near the unit circle. Figure 4.14c shows a realization from an ARCH(1) model with α0 = 0.1 and α1 = 0.9, and Figure 4.14d shows a realization from the AR(2) model in Equation 4.32, where a_t is the ARCH(1) noise realization in Figure 4.14c. The ARCH (1) realization shows fairly isolated spikes at about t = 700 and t = 1150, which initiate an abrupt increase in amplitude of the corresponding output process in Figure 4.14d, followed by a relatively slow decay. This behavior is similar to that seen in Figure 4.7 in the DOW returns data in the 1930s. In Figure 4.14e, we show a realization from the GARCH(1, 1) model in Equation 4.31, and, in Figure 4.14f, we show a realization from the AR(2) model in Equation 4.32 using the GARCH(1, 1) realization as the driving noise sequence. The shocks in the GARCH(1, 1) sequence tend to be longer lasting but smaller in amplitude than those of the ARCH(1) sequence. Correspondingly, the AR(2) realization in Figure 4.14f shows more subtle response to regions of volatility in the noise.

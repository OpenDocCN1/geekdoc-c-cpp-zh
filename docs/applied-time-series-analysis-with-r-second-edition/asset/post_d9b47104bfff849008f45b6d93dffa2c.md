ARMA spectral estimators based on AR(p) fits are often called *AR spectral estimators*.

The ARMA spectral density estimator has an inherent smoothness. By increasing the orders p and q of a model fit, the corresponding spectral density becomes less smooth, similar to the manner in which increasing the truncation point M in the spectral estimates in Chapter 1 produces spectral estimators that are less smooth. Thus, choosing orders p and q that are too low may produce estimators that lose important detail, while using orders that are too high can produce spectral estimators with spurious peaks. Thus, the choice of p and q (to be discussed in Chapter 8) is an important step in the modeling procedure.

**Using R:** In tswge function `true.arma.spec.wge` you can specify a model and the function outputs a plot of the true (log) spectral density along with 251 values of the spectral density and associated frequencies as part of the output. In order to obtain ARMA spectral estimates, you can use this function by entering the estimated coefficients as the “true model.” Function `plotts.true.wge` also outputs and plots the spectral density values.

## 7.8 ARUMA Spectral Estimation

ARUMA(p,d,q) models, such as

$(1 - B)(1 + B^2)(1 - B + 0.8B^2)X_t = a_t,$
(7.75)

have spectral densities with infinite spikes, in this case, at f = 0 and f = 0.25. In practice, if an ARUMA model such as in Equation 7.75 is fit to a set of data, then we recommend plotting the ARUMA spectral density estimates by plotting ARMA spectral density estimates of related near-nonstationary models. In this case, we could plot the true spectral density associated with

$(1 - 0.995B)(1 + 0.99B^2)(1 - B + 0.8B^2)X_t = a_t.$
(7.76)

Note that the coefficients were selected so that all three near-nonstationary roots are equally close to the unit circle. In this case, the roots are 1.005 and ±1.005i. Figure 7.4 shows an approximate ARUMA(2,3,0) spectral density estimate associated with the model fit in Equation 7.75. The sharp peaks at f = 0 and f = 0.25 represent the infinite peaks at those frequencies.

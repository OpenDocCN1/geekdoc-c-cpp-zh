- 1. *Forecasting:* Assume that $X(t)$ is observed at equally spaced points and that forecasts are desired for $X(k+1), X(k+2), \ldots, X(k+h)$ for some forecast origin $k$. - 2. *Spectral analysis:* It has been mentioned that the $G(p; \lambda)$ spectral density can be a very useful tool for summarizing frequency information based on a stationarizing time transformation. However, the frequency scale in the dual space is difficult to relate back to frequencies in the original timescale. For this reason, it is often desirable to transform the frequency information in the $G(p; \lambda)$ spectral density back into the original time and frequency domains using the *Instantaneous Spectrum*. Appendix 13A provides details concerning the definition of IF and the instantaneous spectrum. By referring back to Figure 13.3, it is seen that the peak in the dual spectrum in Figure 13.3d is associated with an IF of about $f=0.2$ at $t=19$, of about $0.09$ at $t=80$, and about $0.06$ at $t=152$. The instantaneous spectrum is a time–frequency plot (similar to the Wigner–Ville plot, Gabor transform, and wavelet spectrograms) that transforms the information in the $G(p; \lambda)$ spectral density back into the original dimensions of time and frequency to display the manner in which frequency changes with time.

**Using R:** (i) Forecasts using a $G(p; \lambda)$ model using the outline discussed above can be obtained using the tswge function `fore.g.lambda.wge`. (ii) The Parzen or AR spectral density estimates can be transformed into instantaneous spectra using the tswge functions `is.parzen.wge` and `is.ar.wge`. See Appendix 13B for more details.

Figure 13.11c shows the AR spectral density estimator of the original data based on fitting an (unsatisfactory) AR(20) model to the original bat data. This spectral density estimate (which is the AR(11) spectral density estimate of the dual data) shows a peak at $f = 0$ along with three other distinct peaks. It is clear that a much better understanding of the signal is obtained by making the time transformation.

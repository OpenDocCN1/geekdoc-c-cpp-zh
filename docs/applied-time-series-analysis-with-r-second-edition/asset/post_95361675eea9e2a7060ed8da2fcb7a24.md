
where χ²(p) denotes a chi-square distribution with p degrees of freedom, and where σ̂ₐ² and Γ̂ denote data-based estimates of σₐ² and Γ (see Brockwell and Davis, 1991). From Equation 7.57, an approximate (1 − α) × 100% confidence ellipsoid for φ is given by

(\hat{φ} - φ)' \hat{Γ} (\hat{φ} - φ) ≤ \frac{\hat{σ}_a^2}{n} χ²_{1-α}(p),  (7.58)

where χ²_β denotes the β × 100th percentile of the chi-square distribution with p degrees of freedom. If confidence intervals are desired for an individual parameter, φ_j, then Equation 7.58 can be modified to yield

\hat{φ}_j ± z_{(1-α)/2} \frac{\hat{σ}_a^2 \hat{Γ}_{jj}^{1/2}}{√n},  (7.59)

where z_β denotes the β × 100th percentile of the standard normal distribution, and \hat{Γ}_{jj} denotes the jth diagonal element in \hat{Γ}^{-1}.

Before continuing to the ARMA(p,q) case, a few comments can be made concerning the estimation of σₐ² and Γ. First, for the AR(1) and AR(2) cases, σₐ² \hat{Γ}^{-1} can be estimated directly by inserting parameter estimates into Equations 7.53 and 7.56, respectively. For higher order models, σₐ² can be estimated by \hat{σ}_a², the estimated white noise variance associated with the particular estimation procedure employed using backcasting as discussed in Section 7.4. Then, the matrix \hat{Γ} can be obtained by estimating the elements γ_k using \hat{γ}_k in Equation 7.5.

## 7.5.2 ARMA(p,q) Case

In this section, we briefly discuss the asymptotic distribution of \hat{ρ} = (\hat{φ}_1, ..., \hat{φ}_p, \hat{θ}_1, ..., \hat{θ}_q) for the more general ARMA(p,q) process, φ(B)X_t = θ(B)a_t,  (7.60) where the parameter estimates are obtained using ML or least squares (conditional or unconditional). This asymptotic distribution is given by

$n^{1/2}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta}) \Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_a^2\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{-1})$, (7.61)

where $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ denotes the vector of true parameter values. In order to obtain the matrix $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ in this case, we consider the two AR processes $\mathcal{U}$ and $V_t$ defined by

$\phi(B)\mathcal{U}_t = a_t$, (7.62)

$\theta(B)V_t = a_t$, (7.63)

where $\phi(B)$ and $\theta(B)$ are as in Equation 7.60. We use the notation $\gamma_U(k)=E[U_t U_{t+k}]$, $\gamma_V(k)=E[V_t V_{t+k}]$ and $\gamma_{UV}(k)=E[U_t V_{t+k}]$. Then, $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ is the $(p+q)\times(p+q)$ matrix defined by

$\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{pp}&\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{pq}\\ \boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{qp}&\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{qq}\end{bmatrix}$, (7.64)

where

- $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{pp}$ is the $p\times p$ matrix whose $ij$th element is $\gamma_U(i-j)$
- $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{qq}$ is the $q\times q$ matrix with $ij$th element $\gamma_V(i-j)$

The matrix $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{pq}$ is the $p\times q$ matrix whose $ij$th element is $\gamma_{UV}(i-j)$, while $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_{qp}$ is the $q\times p$ matrix with $ij$th element given by $\gamma_{UV}(j-i)$. It follows from Equation 7.64 that the $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ matrix for the MA$(q)$ model, $X_t=\theta(B)a_t$ is identical to that for the AR$(q)$ model $\theta(B)X_t=a_t$. Using this fact, in Example 7.6, we find the asymptotic distribution associated with an MA(1) and an MA(2) model, and, in Example 7.7, we find the asymptotic distribution for an ARMA(1,1). For the derivation of the asymptotic distribution in the ARMA$(p,q)$ case, see Box et al. (2008) and Brockwell and Davis (1991).

### EXAMPLE 7.6: ASYMPTOTIC DISTRIBUTIONS FOR MA(1) AND MA(2) MODELS

1. MA(1): It follows from the earlier discussion that for an MA(1) model, $\boldsymbol{\Gamma}$ is the $1\times1$ matrix whose element is given by $\gamma_0=\sigma_a^2/(1-\theta_1^2)$. Thus, $\sigma_a^2\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{-1}=1-\theta_1^2$, and $\hat{\theta}_1$ is asymptotically normal with mean $\theta_1$ and variance $(1-\theta_1^2)/n$.

2. MA(2): It follows from Equation 7.56 that

$$\sigma_a^2\mathbf{\Gamma}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1-\theta_2^2 & -\theta_1(1+\theta_2)\\ -\theta_1(1+\theta_2) & 1-\theta_2^2\end{bmatrix}.\tag{7.65}$$

As in the AR(2) case, it can be seen from the diagonal elements in Equation 7.65 that, asymptotically, $\hat{\theta}_1$ is distributed as a normal random variable with mean $\theta_1$ and variance $(1-\theta_2^2)/n$ while $\hat{\theta}_2$ has mean $\theta_2$ and the same asymptotic variance as $\hat{\theta}_1$.

### EXAMPLE 7.7: ASYMPTOTIC DISTRIBUTIONS FOR AN ARMA(1,1) MODEL

Consider the ARMA(1,1) model

$$(1-\phi_1 B)X_t=(1-\theta_1 B)a_t.\tag{7.66}$$

In this case, each of the submatrices in Equation 7.64 is $1\times1$, and $\mathbf{\Gamma}$ is given by

$$\mathbf{\Gamma}=\begin{bmatrix}\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}(0) & \gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0)\\ \gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0) & \gamma_{V}(0)\end{bmatrix}$$

where $\gamma_{\mathcal{U}}(0)=\sigma_a^2/(1-\phi_1^2)$ and $\gamma_V(0)=\sigma_a^2/(1-\theta_1^2)$. Now,

$$\gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0)=E[\mathcal{U}_t V_t]=E[(\phi_1\mathcal{U}_{t-1}+a_t)(\theta_1 V_{t-1}+a_t)]=\phi_1\theta_1E[\mathcal{U}_{t-1}V_{t-1}]+\theta_1E[V_{t-1}a_t]+\phi_1E[\mathcal{U}_{t-1}a_t]+\sigma_a^2,$$

so that $$\gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0)=\phi_1\theta_1\gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0)+\sigma_a^2,$$ and $\gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0)$ is given by $$\gamma_{\mathcal{U}V}(0)=\frac{\sigma_a^2}{1-\phi_1\theta_1}.$$

Thus, we have

$$\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=\sigma_a^2\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{1-\phi_1^2} & \frac{1}{1-\phi_1\theta_1}\\\frac{1}{1-\phi_1\theta_1} & \frac{1}{1-\theta_1^2}\end{bmatrix},$$

so that

$$\sigma_a^2\Gamma^{-1}=\frac{1-\phi_1\theta_1}{(\phi_1-\theta_1)^2}\begin{pmatrix}(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\phi_1\theta_1) & -(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\theta_1^2)\\-(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\theta_1^2) & (1-\theta_1^2)(1-\phi_1\theta_1)\end{pmatrix}.\tag{7.67}$$

Asymptotically, $\hat{\phi}_1$ has a normal distribution with mean $\phi_1$ and variance

$$\operatorname{var}(\hat{\phi}_1)=\frac{(1-\phi_1^2)(1-\phi_1\theta_1)^2}{n(\phi_1-\theta_1)^2},$$

while $\hat{\theta}_1$ is normal with mean $\theta_1$ and variance

$$\operatorname{var}(\hat{\theta}_1)=\frac{(1-\theta_1^2)(1-\phi_1\theta_1)^2}{n(\phi_1-\theta_1)^2}.$$

### 7.5.2.1 Confidence Intervals for ARMA(p,q) Parameters

The result in Equation 7.61 provides large sample confidence intervals for the model parameters of an ARMA(p,q) model. A confidence ellipsoid corresponding to Equation 7.57 and individual confidence intervals corresponding to Equation 7.58 are given by

$$\left(\hat{\beta}-\beta\right)'\hat{\Gamma}\left(\hat{\beta}-\beta\right)\le \frac{\hat{\sigma}_a^2}{n}\chi_{1-\alpha}^2(p+q),\tag{7.68}$$

and

$$\hat{\beta}_j\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\hat{\sigma}_a\hat{\gamma}_{jj}^{1/2}}{\sqrt{n}}.\tag{7.69}$$

be estimated using the estimated AR models in Equations 7.70 and 7.71, finding the appropriate weights for each model, and approximating the corresponding infinite series in Equation 7.72.

## 7.5.3 Asymptotic Comparisons of Estimators for an MA(1)

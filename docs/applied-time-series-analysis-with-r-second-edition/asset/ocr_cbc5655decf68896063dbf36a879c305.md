The MRA in the right panel of Figure 12.9 provides a visualization of the fact that the wavelet approximations, S_j, can be viewed as output of low-pass filters applied to the original data. The quality of the associated filtering can depend on the wavelet selected. The MRD and MRA in Figure 12.9 are based on the relatively smooth Symlet(8) wavelet. In Figure 12.10 (left panel) we show the MRA of the bumps signal using the Haar wavelet, and Figure 12.10 (right panel) shows the MRA based on the Daubelet(4) wavelet. While each of these wavelets eventually reproduces the data (in S_0) the preceding “filtered versions” show the behavior of the underlying wavelets. For example, the Haar version is “blocky” and the Daubelet(4) version shows vestiges of the shark fin. Consequently, for the purpose of approximation, a relatively smooth wavelet (such as the Symlet(8)) is best applied to a signal that is relatively smooth (as is the bumps signal). However, Donoho and Johnstone (1994) show a “blocks” signal for which the Haar wavelet is preferable. See also Bruce and Gao (1996).

The actual plots were created using `tswge` functions `plotts.dwt.wge` and `plotts.mra.wge`. See Appendix 12B for more details.

$$\Gamma_{\epsilon}(\ell)=E\left[e_{t_0}(\ell)e_{t_0}(\ell)'\right]$$ $$=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}\Psi_j\Gamma_a\Psi_j'.$$

In the case of normal errors, forecast limits analogous to Equation 6.33 can be obtained using the diagonal elements of Equation 10.31.

# 10.3.2 Spectrum of a VAR(p) Model

In the VAR(p) case, the multivariate spectrum, P_X(f), defined in Equation 10.7, is the m × m matrix given, for each −0.5 ≤ f ≤ 0.5, by

$$\mathbf{P_X}(f)=\Phi^{-1}\left(e^{2\pi i f}\right)\Gamma_a\left[\Phi^{-1}\left(e^{-2\pi i f}\right)\right]'\tag{10.32}$$

where for real or complex z, the polynomial $\Phi(z)=I-\Phi_1z-\dots-\Phi_pz^p$ is the algebraic equivalent of the operator $\Phi(B)$ defined in Equation 10.16. Note that Equation 10.32 is the multivariate counterpart to the AR(p) spectrum given in Equation 3.53. We mention again that the use of the term spectrum and spectral density is typically used synonymously in the multivariate case to indicate the Fourier transform of the autocovariance.

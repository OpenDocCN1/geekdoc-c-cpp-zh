$\hat{X}_{t_0}(\ell) \pm 1.96 \hat{\sigma}_a \left\{ \sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k^2 \right\}^{1/2}, $

where $\hat{\sigma}_a^2$ is

$\hat{\sigma}_a^2 = \frac{1}{n-p} \sum_{t=p+1}^{n} \hat{a}_t^2$
$= \frac{1}{23} \sum_{t=3}^{25} \hat{a}_t^2$
$= 0.7497,$

and $n = t_0 = 25$. The $\psi_k$'s are the $\psi$-weights from the estimated model. The half width of the approximate 95% prediction interval for $\ell = 3$ is given by

$1.96 \hat{\sigma}_a \left\{ \sum_{k=0}^{2} \psi_k^2 \right\}^{1/2} = 1.96 \sqrt{.7497} (1 + 0.7^2 + 0.24^2)^{1/2}$
$= 2.11.$

We will present approximate prediction intervals obtained by replacing $\sigma_a$ in Equation 6.33 by $\hat{\sigma}_a$ (and $\psi$-weights based on the estimated model), and without further comment will refer to these as prediction intervals. In Figures 6.3 and 6.4, we redisplay the realizations and forecasts shown in Figures 6.1 and 6.2 along with 95% prediction limits. It can be seen that the half widths of the prediction intervals increase as $\ell$ increases, but tend to level off as $\ell$ becomes large, converging to $1.96 \sigma_X$, if in fact the true $\sigma_a$ and model parameters were used. These half widths level off for stationary ARMA models because in this case, as mentioned in the discussion following Equation 6.34, $\sigma_a \left( \sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1} \psi_k^2 \right)^{1/2} \to \sigma_X (< \infty)$ and $\psi_k \to 0$ as $k \to \infty$. In our case, using $\hat{\sigma}_a$, the half widths converge to 3.38 and 2.27 for the AR(1) and ARMA(2,1) models, respectively.

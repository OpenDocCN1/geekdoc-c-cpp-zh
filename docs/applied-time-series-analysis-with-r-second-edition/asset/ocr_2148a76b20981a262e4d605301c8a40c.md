One technique for calculating the autocovariances is to truncate the series in Equation 11.24 after a sufficient number of terms. Table 11.1 (given by Ford, 1991) illustrates that, depending on the parameters, the terms in this series may not damp quickly enough to make truncation feasible.

The table considers the problem of using Equation 11.24 to calculate γ₀ for the case u = 1. In this case using Theorem 11.1(4) and the fact that d = 2λ, the sum of the infinite series in Equation 11.24 is known in closed form to be γ₀ = Γ(−4λ + 1)/[Γ(−2λ + 1)]². Table 11.1 compares values of the infinite sum in Equation 11.24 to the results of truncating the sum after 200,000 terms and after 10 million terms. In the table it is seen that for λ = 0.05 and λ = 0.125 the two truncated sums approximate the infinite sum quite well. However, in the near-nonstationary case λ = 0.24, the convergence is extremely slow and a sum to 10 million terms is a very poor approximation!

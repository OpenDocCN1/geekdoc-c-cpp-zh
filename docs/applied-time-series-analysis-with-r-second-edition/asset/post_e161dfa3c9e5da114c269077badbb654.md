## 3.2.8 Summary of AR(1) and AR(2) Behavior

Before proceeding to further examination of general AR(p) models, we summarize the information about AR(1) and AR(2) models

- 1. *Facts about the stationary AR(1) model* $X_t - \phi_1 X_{t-1} = a_t$: In this case, the characteristic equation is given by $1 - \phi_1 z = 0$, so that the root, $r_1$, of the characteristic equation is given by $r_1 = \phi_1^{-1}$. As a consequence, an AR(1) process is stationary if and only if $|\phi_1| < 1$. It is instructive to note that since $| r_1^{-1} | = | \phi_1 |$ the proximity of the root to the unit circle can be determined by simple examination of $\phi_1$.
- a. The autocorrelation function of a stationary AR(1) process is given by $\rho_k = \phi_1^k$ for $k \ge 0$. That is, in Equation 3.49, $C_1 = 1$ and $r_1 = \phi_1^{-1}$. The
autocorrelation function is a damped exponential if φ₁ > 0 and an oscillating damped exponential if φ₁ < 0.
- b. As a result of the autocorrelations in (a), realizations from an AR(1) model with φ₁ > 0 tend to be aperiodic with a general “wandering” behavior. When φ₁ < 0 the realizations will tend to oscillate back and forth across the mean.
- c. The spectral density S_X(f) has a peak at f = 0 if φ₁ > 0 and at f = 0.5 if φ₁ < 0.

## 2. Facts about a stationary AR(2) model X_t − φ₁ X_{t−1} − φ₂ X_{t−2} = a_t: In this case, the characteristic equation is 1 − φ₁ z − φ₂ z² = 0, and the features of the AR(2) process depend on the nature of the roots of this quadratic equation.

Case 1—The roots of the characteristic equation are complex: From Equation 3.61, it follows that |r_k⁻¹| = √−φ₂ where r_k, k = 1, 2 are complex conjugate roots of the characteristic equation. This provides a useful measure of how close complex roots are to the unit circle.

- a. In the complex roots case, the general solution in Equation 3.49 satisfied by ρ_k has two terms, C₂ = C₁*, and r₂ = r₁*, where z* denotes the complex conjugate of z. The autocorrelation function is a damped sinusoidal of the form

```
ρ_k = d(√−φ₂)^k sin(2π f₀ k + ψ)   (3.72)
```

where d and ψ are real constants and system frequency f₀ = 1/(2π) cos⁻¹( φ₁ / (2√−φ₂) ).

Case 2—The roots of the characteristic equation are real: In this case the
roots, $r_1$ and $r_2$, of the characteristic equation are real, and the characteristic equation factors as $(1-r_1^{-1}z)(1-r_2^{-1}z)=0$. Plots of $\rho_k$ will have the appearance of a damped exponential, damped oscillating exponentials, or a mixture of damped exponentials and oscillating exponentials. Realizations from these models will have a mixture of the behaviors induced by their autocorrelations. Depending on the signs of $r_1$ and $r_2$, the spectral density will have peaks at $f=0$, at $f=0.5$, or at both $f=0$ and $f=0.5$.

### 3.2.9 AR(p) Model

The general behavior of the autocorrelation function (and to a large degree the time series itself) of an AR($p$) model can be understood by examining the roots of the characteristic equation. The information summarized earlier for the AR(1) and AR(2) models provides valuable information concerning the characteristics of higher-order AR($p$) models. We begin our discussion of AR($p$) models by considering two examples.

## **EXAMPLE 3.8: AN AR(3) MODEL WITH A POSITIVE REAL ROOT AND COMPLEX CONJUGATE ROOTS**

Consider the AR(3) model

$X_t - 2.55X_{t-1} + 2.42X_{t-2} - 0.855X_{t-3} = a_t.$  (3.73)

For this model, the characteristic polynomial $1 - 2.55z + 2.42z^2 - 0.855z^3$ can be factored as $(1 - 0.95z)(1 - 1.6z + 0.9z^2)$, that is, as the product of a linear factor and an irreducible quadratic factor. From this factorization, it follows that the roots of the characteristic equation are $r_1 = 1/0.95 = 1.05$ along with the pair of complex conjugate roots $0.89 \pm 0.57i$. For each of the three roots of the characteristics equation, we have $|r^{-1}| = \sqrt{0.9}$. Based on the factorization of the characteristic equation, it is often useful to write the model in [Equation 3.73](#) in the factored form

(3.74)

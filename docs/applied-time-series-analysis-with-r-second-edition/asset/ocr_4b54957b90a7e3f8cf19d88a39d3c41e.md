# 3.2.10 AR(1) and AR(2) Building Blocks of an AR(p) Model

In Examples 3.8 and 3.9, we were able to determine the characteristics of the AR(3) models by examining the first- and second-order factors of the model. These examples suggest that the key to understanding AR(p) models is to recognize that the AR(1) and AR(2) models serve as “building blocks.” The pth-order characteristic equation \(\phi(z) = 1 - \phi_1z - \cdots - \phi_pz^p = 0\) will have real roots, complex roots (which occur as complex conjugates), or a combination of real and complex roots. As a consequence, \(\phi(z)\) can be factored as

$$\phi(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-\alpha_{1j}z-\alpha_{2j}z^2)$$  
(3.76)

where there are two types of factors.

+   1. *Factors associated with real roots (linear factors):* Factors of **Equation 3.76** associated with real roots are linear factors, that is, \(\alpha_{2j}=0\). The absolute value of the reciprocal of the associated root is \(|r_j^{-1}|=|\alpha_{1j}|\), and, thus, it is necessary that \(|\alpha_{1j}|<1\) in order for the associated root to be outside the unit circle. “System frequencies,” \(f_0\), for these real roots are assigned as
+   
+   $$
+   f_0=0 \quad \text{if } \alpha_{1j}>0
+   $$
+   $$
+   \phantom{f_0}=0.5 \quad \text{if } \alpha_{1j}<0.
+   $$

+   2. *Factors associated with complex roots (quadratic factors):* In this case the factor \(1-\alpha_{1j}z-\alpha_{2j}z^2\) is an irreducible quadratic factor, that is, \(\alpha_{1j}^2+4\alpha_{2j}<0\), and the two roots associated with this factor are complex conjugate pairs. For measuring the proximity of a root to the unit circle, it is useful to note that \(|r_j^{-1}|=\sqrt{-\alpha_{2j}}\).

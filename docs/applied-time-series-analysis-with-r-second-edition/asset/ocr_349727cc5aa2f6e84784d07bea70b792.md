$$\sum_{m=0}^{\infty} \pi_m B^m = \theta^{-1}(B)\phi(B) \prod_{j=1}^{k} (1 - 2u_j B + B^2)^{\lambda_j}$$ $$= \theta^{-1}(B)\phi(B) \prod_{j=1}^{k} \left\{\sum_{h=0}^{\infty} C_h^{(-\lambda_j)} (u_j) B^h \right\}.$$

The truncated form (assuming only the data X₁, …, Xₜ₀ are available) is given by

$$\hat{X}_{t_0}^{(T)}(\ell) = \bar{X} - \sum_{j=1}^{t_0-1+\ell} \pi_j \left(\hat{X}_{t_0}^{(T)}(\ell - j) - \bar{X}\right).$$ (11.32)

In Section 6.4 we noted that forecasts based on Equation 11.32 are based on the assumption that the πⱼ are sufficiently close to zero for j > t₀ − 1 + ℓ. This will be the case for most ARMA models and for t₀ of moderate size. However, for long-memory models, the π-weights damp very slowly which can cause the truncation in Equation 11.32 to have a substantial effect. For information concerning the relationship between the best linear prediction (based on the available data) and forecasts using the truncated π-weights in the context of the fractional and FARMA models and GARMA models, see Miller (1994) and Fu (1995). (See also Ferrara and Guégan, 2001.)

Using R: The tswge package contains two functions for producing forecasts from long-memory models: fore.farma.wge and fore.garma.wge. For more details see Appendix 11A.

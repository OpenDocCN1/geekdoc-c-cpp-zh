As expected, the realizations in Figure 3.5b and d associated with negative φ₁ have an oscillatory nature. Also note that when |φ₁| = 0.95, the variance of the realization appears to be larger. As φ₁ → 0, the AR(1) approaches white noise, so that realizations with φ₁ ≈ 0 can be expected to look much like white noise. In Example 1.9, we examined AR(1) processes with φ₁ = ±0.9, and the observations made in that example are consistent with those discussed here. In Figure 3.6, we show spectral densities for the four AR(1) models considered in Figures 3.4 and 3.5. From the figure, it can be seen that when φ₁ > 0, the spectral densities have a peak at f = 0 (i.e., the denominator in the spectral density formula is a minimum at f = 0) indicating, as mentioned in Example 1.9, that the associated realizations will tend to wander about the mean with no distinct periodic behavior. That is, the data are primarily low frequency with no real periodic tendency. The spectral densities for φ₁ < 0 have a maximum at f = 0.5, indicating that the data will tend to have a highly oscillatory nature with period of about 2. These behaviors are seen in the realizations in Figure 3.5. It should also be noticed that the spectral densities associated with |φ₁| = 0.95 have a higher peak and less power away from the peak than those associated with |φ₁| = 0.7, indicating that the associated behaviors in the realizations will be more pronounced for |φ₁| = 0.95.

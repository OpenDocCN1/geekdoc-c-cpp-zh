# EXAMPLE 11.5: GARMA PROCESSES

In this example we show true autocorrelations for Gegenbauer and GARMA models. The procedure for calculating these autocorrelations will be discussed in Section 11.4.1.

+   1. Figure 11.8 shows true autocorrelations for GARMA processes with u = 0.9 and λ = 0.45. Figure 11.8a and b are for GARMA (1,0.45,0,9,0) processes with φ₁ = 0.9 and φ₁ = 0.95, respectively. These autocorrelations have the general appearance of the autocorrelations in Figure 3.16b for the AR(3) process (1 − 0.95B) (1 − 1.6B + 0.9B²)Xₜ = aₜ. That is, the AR(1) factor with positive φ₁ induces a damped exponential behavior, and the appearance in both settings is that of a damped sinusoidal behavior along a damped exponential path. It is instructive to compare Figures 3.16b and 11.8b. In Figure 3.16b, the damped exponential and damped sinusoidal behaviors have almost disappeared by lag 40. However, in Figure 11.8b, while the damped exponential behavior is almost nonexistent by lag 40, the sinusoidal behavior associated with the G-frequency, f_G = 0.07, is still visible (and “going strong”) at lag 70. Figure 11.8c and d show very unusual autocorrelation behavior. The GARMA(1,0.45,0,9,0) model associated with the autocorrelations in Figure 11.8c has an AR(1) factor with φ₁ = −0.85 which introduces oscillating damped exponential behavior. In Figure 11.8c it is seen that this high-frequency/oscillating behavior is noticeable for low lags, but after k = 20 the only behavior visible is that due to the long-memory damped sinusoidal. Figure 11.8d shows autocorrelations for a GARMA(2,0.45,0,9,0) with AR(2) factor 1 − B + 0.9B² which, from Equation 3.63 is associated with a system frequency of f₀ = 0.16 (or period of 6.25). The shorter period of about 6 can be seen within the first 15 lags, but for larger lags, the only observable behavior is due to the G-frequency, f_G = 0.07 (i.e., periods of about 14).

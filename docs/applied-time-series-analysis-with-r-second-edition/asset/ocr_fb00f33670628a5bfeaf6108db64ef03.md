where χ²(p) denotes a chi-square distribution with p degrees of freedom, and where σ̂ₐ² and Γ̂ denote data-based estimates of σₐ² and Γ (see Brockwell and Davis, 1991). From Equation 7.57, an approximate (1 − α) × 100% confidence ellipsoid for φ is given by

(\hat{φ} - φ)' \hat{Γ} (\hat{φ} - φ) ≤ \frac{\hat{σ}_a^2}{n} χ²_{1-α}(p),  (7.58)

where χ²_β denotes the β × 100th percentile of the chi-square distribution with p degrees of freedom. If confidence intervals are desired for an individual parameter, φ_j, then Equation 7.58 can be modified to yield

\hat{φ}_j ± z_{(1-α)/2} \frac{\hat{σ}_a^2 \hat{Γ}_{jj}^{1/2}}{√n},  (7.59)

where z_β denotes the β × 100th percentile of the standard normal distribution, and \hat{Γ}_{jj} denotes the jth diagonal element in \hat{Γ}^{-1}.

Before continuing to the ARMA(p,q) case, a few comments can be made concerning the estimation of σₐ² and Γ. First, for the AR(1) and AR(2) cases, σₐ² \hat{Γ}^{-1} can be estimated directly by inserting parameter estimates into Equations 7.53 and 7.56, respectively. For higher order models, σₐ² can be estimated by \hat{σ}_a², the estimated white noise variance associated with the particular estimation procedure employed using backcasting as discussed in Section 7.4. Then, the matrix \hat{Γ} can be obtained by estimating the elements γ_k using \hat{γ}_k in Equation 7.5.

One final comment about AR(2) models associated with complex conjugate roots of the characteristic equation is that if the system frequency is sufficiently removed from 0 or 0.5, the spectral density will have a peak at \(f_s = \frac{1}{2\pi}\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{\phi_1(\phi_2^{-1})}{4\phi_2}\right)\) (see Problem 3.20). It is interesting to note that \(f_s \ne f_0\) but as \(\phi_2 \to -1\), then \(f_s \to f_0\). That is, the peak in the spectral density occurs at a frequency close to but not equal to the system frequency \(f_0\).

# EXAMPLE 3.7

Figure 3.15a (data set lynx in tswge) shows the annual number of the Canadian lynx trapped in the Mackenzie River district of North-West Canada for the period 1821–1934. Figure 3.15b shows the log (base 10) of the annual numbers trapped, and this log data is a classical data set that has been analyzed by many authors. These data have interested researchers because of the pseudo-periodic behavior with cycle lengths about 10 years. See for example Tong (1977), Bhansali (1979), and Woodward and Gray (1983). Also shown in Figure 3.15b and c is the sample autocorrelations and the Parzen spectral density estimate of the log lynx data. This shows the 10-year cyclic nature of the data. The log of the lynx data is usually used for analysis because it makes the peaks and troughs of the cycles behave in similar manners, and is more AR-like. If an AR(2) model is fit to the data (we will discuss alternative models in later chapters) then the fitted model is \(X_t - 1.38X_{t-1} + 0.75X_{t-2} = a_t\) which has system frequency \(f_0 = 0.10\). Also, the sample autocorrelations have the appearance of a damped sinusoid with period of about 10, and the spectral density estimate in Figure 3.15d has a peak at about \(f = 0.10\).

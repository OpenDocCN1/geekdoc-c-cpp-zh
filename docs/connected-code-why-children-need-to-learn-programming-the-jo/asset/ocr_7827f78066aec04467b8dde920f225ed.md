# Geeking Out: Remix as a Creative Challenge

With Katie and Nadia's remixes, the exchange is between multiple individuals. This element of exchange that is characteristic of remix can also be an individual endeavor, however. Karen Brennan notes how such individuality plays out in terms of remix in her interview with eleven-year-old Jackson, who remixes at Scratch less for socialization purposes and more for personal edification. Jackson remixes games that he finds compelling to reduce the overall number of programming bricks without altering gameplay in any manner.15 Identifying the crucial code that cannot be replaced as "peaks," Jackson explains that the goal is to sort out the "noise" or extraneous blocks and thereby create a more economical and efficient coding schema. Remix in this instance is a pathway for learning and a challenge, touching on what Papert refers to as the "hard fun" component characteristic of coding.16

This third category of remix is unique in that its level of socialization is characteristic of the "adding" remixes and the intent to spur increased productivity is characteristic of the "how-to" remixes. The first of these two goals is epitomized by the popularity of remixable "coloring contests" at the Scratch site. In these contests, an individual user posts a colorless self-drawn or imported image, a set of rules in the Project Notes section (such as what colors can be used and whether music and backgrounds can be added), and a deadline. The creator of the contest typically selects the winning colored remix, although winners occasionally are determined based on the number of project views or "Love Its" that a remix receives. Winners thus gain status at the Scratch site, and the contest's creator has a chance to be featured on the front page if the contest is especially popular with the community.17

Like "adding" remixes, these coloring contests generate hundreds of remixes from a single entry, but as is typical with remixes that are developed largely out of socializing, what they gain in breadth, they lose in depth. The majority of coloring contest remixes entail little coding complexity, if any code at all, and entries typically are judged solely on the static image. Other contests at the Scratch site are more sophisticated.

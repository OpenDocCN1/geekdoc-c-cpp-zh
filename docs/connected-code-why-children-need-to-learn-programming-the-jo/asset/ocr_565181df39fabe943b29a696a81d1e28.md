Seymour Papert envisioned the computer as a protean machine, a uni-
versal device to construct objects-to-think-with in intellectually rich, per-
sonally meaningful, culturally diverse and socially connected ways. In our
vision, it is this fourth component—connectivity—that emerges as cru-
cial if children are to learn computing as a fundamental skill. Objects-to-
share-with represent the new imperative and new standard when it comes
to learning and making with digital media. It is here, in the moment of
exchange, that the computer may deliver on its protean promise for con-
necting in a networked world.

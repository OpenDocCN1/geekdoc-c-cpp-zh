In an extension to their earlier examples of β-hydroxyketone dehydration (Scheme 7), Tanaka et al. (2007) evaluated the continuous flow synthesis of an immunoactivating natural product, pristane (2,6,10,14-tetramethylpentadecane) (249). Due to limited commercial availability of pristane (249), the authors investigated the compound's preparation in a microreactor as a means of obtaining a method suitable for production of pristane (249) to meet demand, which is currently ∼5 kg week⁻¹. The first step of the reaction involved treating farnesol (250) with MnO2 to afford the respective aldehyde, which subsequently underwent a reaction with isobutyl magnesium chloride to afford an allylic alcohol 251.

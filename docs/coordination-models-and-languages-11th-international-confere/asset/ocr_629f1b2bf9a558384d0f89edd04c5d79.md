In order to obtain the stress for phases I and II, integral calculus of Equation (4) is expressed as

$$
\tau_{zx}^I = \frac{\Delta P}{L} x + C_1^I \\
\tau_{zx}^{II} = \frac{\Delta P}{L} x + C_1^{II}
\tag{6}
$$

where \(C_1\) is constant and superscripts I and II mean phases I and II. Here, continuity of shear stress is assumed as a boundary condition. Namely,

$$
\tau_{zx}^I = \tau_{zx}^{II} \text{ at } x = 0
\tag{7}
$$

By substituting Equation (6) with Equation (7), the following relationship is obtained.

$$
C_1^I = C_1^{II} = C_1
\tag{8}
$$

Here, Newton's law of viscosity is used,

$$
\tau_{zx} = \mu \frac{dv_z^{II}}{dx}
\tag{9}
$$

where \(v_z\) is flow velocity in the z-direction. By using Equations (6)–(9), the velocities of phases I and II, \(v^I\) and \(v^{II}\), are expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_z^I &= \frac{\Delta P}{2\mu^I}x^2 + \frac{C_1}{\mu^I}x + C_2^I \\
v_z^{II} &= \frac{\Delta P}{2\mu^{II}}x^2 + \frac{C_1}{\mu^{II}}x + C_2^{II}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

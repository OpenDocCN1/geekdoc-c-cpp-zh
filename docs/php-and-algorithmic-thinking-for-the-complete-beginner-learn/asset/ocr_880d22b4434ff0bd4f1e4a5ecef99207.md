# ## Solution

The insertion sort algorithm is inefficient for large scale data, as are the selection and the bubble sort algorithms, but it generally performs better than either of them. Moreover, the insertion sort algorithm can prove very fast when sorting very small arrays—even faster than the quicksort algorithm.

The insertion sort algorithm resembles the way you might sort playing cards. You start with all the cards face down on the table. The cards represent the unsorted “array.” In the beginning your left hand is empty, but in the end this hand will hold the sorted cards. The process goes as follows: you remove from the table one card at a time and insert it into the correct position in your left hand. To find the correct position for a card, you compare it with each of the cards already in your hand, from right to left. At the end, there should be no cards on the table and your left hand will hold all the cards, sorted.

Because the system is linear, using the superposition of the forward and backward signals along the path, we have the incident and reflected voltage and current waves given in Eqs. (21.26) and (21.27).

\( V = V_0^+ - V_0^- \) (21.26)

V is a phasor voltage at any point along the line: |V|·\(\Phi\)

\( I = I_0^+ - I_0^- \) (21.27)

Observe that the current has changed direction and that the voltage has not. The voltage remains the potential between the two conductors. From Thevenin and Ohm, we can express the characteristic impedance at any point along the line as Eq. (21.28):

\( Z_0 = \frac{V_0^+ + V_0^-}{I_0^+ - I_0^-} \) (21.28)

Remember the voltages and currents are phasors of the form given in Eq. (21.29),

\( v(x) = v_i e^{-\gamma x} + v_r e^{\gamma x} \) (21.29)
\( i(x) = i_i e^{-\gamma x} + i_r e^{\gamma x} \)

If we let \( V_s \) be a sinusoid with frequency \( \omega \), then we have the forward and reverse forms of \( V_0 \) given in Eq. (21.30):

\( V_0^+ \sin(\omega t - \phi) \)
\( V_0^- \sin(\omega t + \phi) \)
(21.30)

From Ohm, the ratio of \( \frac{V}{I} \) at the load end must equal \( Z_L \). Using the superposition of the incident and reflected waves, V at any point is given in Eq. (21.31) and I at any point is given in Eq. (21.32).

\( V = V_0^+ + V_0^- \) (21.31)
\( I = I_0^+ - I_0^- \) (21.32)

Thus, we get Eq. (21.33):

\( \frac{V}{I} = \frac{V_0^+ + V_0^-}{I_0^+ - I_0^-} = Z_L \) (21.33)

Rearranging,

\( Z_L = \frac{V_0^+ + V_0^-}{V_0^+ - V_0^-} \frac{V_0}{Z_0} \)

With a little bit of math we get Eq. (21.34):

\( \Gamma = \frac{V_0^-}{V_0^+} = \left( \frac{Z_L - Z_0}{Z_L + Z_0} \right) \) (21.34)
\(\Gamma\) Gamma = reflection coefficient
\( V_0^- \) reflected wave
\( V_0^+ \) incident wave

increases, fewer and fewer component frequencies contribute much. Bear in mind, however, that the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) and the power spectral density are not the same thing.

```
f(t) = \frac{2}{\pi} \left( \sum_{n=1,3,5...} \left( \frac{1}{n} \sin(2\pi n F t) \right) \right)
```

F is the signal frequency and t is time

We can heuristically define the knee frequency using Eq. (21.2):

```
F_{knee} = \frac{k}{\tau_{rise}}
```

$F_{knee}$ – Frequency below which most of the energy in a digital pulse is concentrated.
\(\tau_{rise}\) – Pulse rise time measured at the 10–90% points of the rise time.
k – Various given as 0.35 or 0.5. The latter is more common.

Important time domain characteristics of any digital signal are determined primarily by the signal's spectral power density below $F_{knee}$.

$$L_M = L \left( \frac{1}{1 + \left( \frac{s}{h} \right)^2} \right)$$ (21.14)

$L_M$ – Mutual inductance
$L$ – Inductance of a single wire – From above 84 nH
$s$ – Separation of two wires – assume 0.1 in
h – Height above board (ground plane) – assume 0.2 in.
$L_M$ – Mutual inductance 67 nH

The voltage induced in second loop given by Eq. (21.15):

$$V = L_M \frac{di}{dt}$$ (21.15)

$\frac{di}{dt}$ – the maximum rate of change in the current in the driving loop

Using Eq. (21.16), for a step change in voltage in loop A we get:

$$i = C \frac{dv}{dt}$$ (21.16)

If we differentiate Eq. (21.16) then solve for the maximum rate of change in the current, we can show that the maximum rate of change in the current in the above load capacitor is given by Eq. (21.17):

$$\frac{di}{dt} = \frac{1.52 \Delta V}{(\tau_{rise})^2} C$$ (21.17)

$\Delta V$ – Voltage swing – assume 3.7 V
\(\tau_{rise}\) – Rise time – assume 4 ns
C – Load capacitance assume 15 pf

$$\frac{di}{dt} = 5.3 \times 10^6 A/s$$

We can now substitute into Eq. (21.15) to get:

$$V = 355 \text{ mV}$$

which, in context, is a significant amount of voltage since, for a TTL circuit, we have \(V_{OH} \approx 3.7 \text{ V}\) and \(V_{OL} \approx 0.4 \text{ V}\).

We can effectively reduce the EMI and the resulting crosstalk by constraining the return current to small loops.

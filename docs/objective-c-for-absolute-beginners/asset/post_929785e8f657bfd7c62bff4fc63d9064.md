# Introduction

Over the last three years, we've heard the following countless times:

- “I’ve never programmed before, but I have a great idea for an iPhone/iPad app.
- “Can I really learn to program the iPhone or iPad?”

Many answer, "Yes, but you have to believe you can." Only you are going to tell yourself you can't do it.

# For the Newbie

This book assumes you may have never programmed before using object-oriented programming (OOP) languages. There are many Objective-C books out there, but all of these books assume you have programmed in Objective-C and know OOP and logic. We wanted to write a book that takes readers from knowing little or nothing about computer programming and logic to being able to program in Objective-C. Objective-C is the native language for the iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Over the last three years, we have taught well over a thousand students at xcelMe.com to be iPhone/iPad (iOS) developers. Many of our students have developed some of the most successful iOS apps in their category in the iTunes App Store. We have incorporated what we have learned in our first two courses, Introduction to Object-oriented Programming and Logic and Objective-C for iPhone/iPad Developers, in this book.

# For the More Experienced

Many developers who programmed years ago or programmed in a non-OOP language need a background in OOP and Logic before they dive into Objective-C. This book is for you. We gently walk you through OOP and how it is used in iOS development to help you make a successful iOS developer.

# INTRODUCTION

Longer than average time to graduation
One of the biggest challenges to learning OOP languages like Java, C++, or Objective-C is the steep learning curve from the very beginning. In the past, students had to learn the following topics all at once:

- Object-oriented principles
- A complex Integrated Development Environment (IDE), i.e., Xcode, Eclipse, Visual Studio
- The syntax of the programming language and principles
- Programming logic and principles

As a result, Carnegie Mellon University received a grant from the US government and developed Alice. Alice, an innovative 3D programming environment, makes it easy for developers to create rich applications. Alice is a teaching tool for students to learn in an OOP environment. The software uses 3D graphics and a drag-and-drop interface to facilitate a more engaging first programming experience.
Alice helps students focus on learning the principles of OOP without having to focus on learning a complex IDE and Objective-C principles all at once. You get to focus on each topic individually. Drag-and-drop programming removes all the complexity of learning an IDE and programming language syntax. You'll see learning is actually fun, and you can develop your own sophisticated applications.
After we introduce Alice and readers feel comfortable with the material, we will move into Xcode, where you can use your new OOP knowledge in writing Objective-C applications. Thus, you can focus on the Objective-C syntax and language without having to learn OOP at the same time.

# Learning Objective-C Without Alice

More than a thousand students have used this book to become iOS developers. At the end of each course, we ask our students if the Alice sections in the first four chapters were useful. More than half of the students using Alice at the beginning of the first chapters found the chapter was critical to their success. However, some of the students didn't feel they needed the Alice examples at the beginning of the first chapters.
We have laid out the first four chapters of this book with the first part of each chapter introducing OOP with Alice; the remaining part of chapter introduces the topic using Objective-C. Thus, you can skip the Alice if you feel comfortable with the topic.

# How This Book Is Organized

You'll notice that we are all about successes in this book. We introduce the OOP and Logic concepts in Alice and then move those concepts to Xcode and Objective-C. Many students are visual or learn by doing. We use both techniques. We walk you through topics and concepts with visual examples and then take you through step-by-step examples reinforcing the concepts.
We repeat topics in different chapters to reinforce what you have learned and applied these skills in new ways. This enables new programmers to replay development skills and feel a sense of accomplishment as they progress. Don't worry if you haven't mastered a topic keep moving forward!

# The Formula for Success

Learning to program is an interactive process between your program and you. Just like learning to play an instrument, you have to practice. You must work through the examples in this book. Understanding the concept doesn't mean you know how to apply it.

You will learn a lot from this book. You will learn a lot working through the exercises in this book. However, you will really learn when you debug your programs. an unparalleled learning process. The downside of debugging is a new developer can find it especially frustrating. If you have never thrown your code out the window, you will question why you are failing to debug and whether you are smart enough to solve the problem. Programming is very humbling, even for the most experienced developer.

Like a musician, the more you practice the better you get. By practicing, you can do some amazing things as a programmer. The world is your oyster. Seeing your app in the iTunes App Store is one of the most satisfying accomplishments. However, there is a price, and that price is time spent coding and learning.

Having taught more than a thousand students iOS development, we have put together a formula for what makes students successful. Here is our formula for success:

- Believe you can do it. You will be the only one who says you can't do this. So don't tell yourself.
- Work through all the examples and exercises in this book.
- Code, and keeping code. The more you code, the better you'll get.
- Be patient with yourself. If you're fortunate enough to have been a 4.0 student who can memorize material just by reading it, this will not happen with Objective-C coding.
- You learn by reading this book. You really learn by debugging your code.
- Use the free xcelMe com webinars and YouTube videos explained at the end of this chapter.
- Don't give up!

The design is more cohesive because aCommandButton should be responsible for displaying a clickable widget and notifying the system when the button is clicked. Requiring CommandButtonto understand calculator states encroaches on the independence of their abstraction. Instead of just being generic clickable buttons with two commands, the buttons become integrally tied to the concept of the calculator’s global state. Additionally, by forcingCommandButtonto understand the calculator’s state, the coupling in the system is increased by forcing CommandButton to be unnecessarily interconnected to either the shift button or to the class they must poll. The only advantage gained by notifying every CommandButton when the shift button is pressed is the ability to swap the labels for the primary and secondary commands. Of course, label swapping could be implemented independently of the CommandButton’s signal arguments.

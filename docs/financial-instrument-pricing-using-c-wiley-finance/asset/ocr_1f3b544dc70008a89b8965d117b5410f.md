# Lemma 20.1 Let the discrete function $w_j^n$ satisfy $L_k^h w_j^n \le 0$ in the interior of the discrete mesh domain with $w_j^n \ge 0$ on the discrete boundary. Then:

$w_j^n \ge 0, \ \forall j = 0, \dots, J, n = 0, \dots, N.$

*Proof:* We transform the inequality $L_k^h w_j^n \le 0$ into an equivalent vector inequality. To this end, define the vector $W^n = (w_1^n, \dots, w_{J-1}^n)$. Then the inequality $L_k^h w_j^n \le 0$ is equivalent to the vector inequality:

$A^n W^{n+1} \ge W^n$ (20.58)

where:

$A^n = \begin{pmatrix} \ddots & \ddots & & 0 \\ & \ddots & t_j^n \\ \ddots & & s_j^n & \ddots \\ & r_j^n & & \ddots \\ 0 & & \ddots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}$

$r_j^n = \left(-\frac{\rho_j^n}{h^2} + \frac{\mu_j^n}{2h}\right) k, \quad s_j^n = \left(-\frac{2\rho_j^n}{h^2} - b_j^n + k^{-1}\right) k, \quad t_j^n = \left(-\left(\frac{\rho_j^n}{h^2} + \frac{\mu_j^n}{2h}\right)\right) k.$

It is easy to show that the matrix $A^n$ has non-positive off-diagonal elements, has strictly positive diagonal elements and is irreducibly diagonally dominant. Hence (see Varga, 1962, pp. 84–85) $A^n$ is non-singular and its inverse is non-negative; that is, all its elements are non-negative, $(A^n)^{-1} \ge 0$. This matrix is an $M$-matrix.

Using this result in (20.58) gives the desired result.

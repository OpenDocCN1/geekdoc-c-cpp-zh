The coefficients $\alpha_j$, $\beta_j$ and $\gamma_j$ are given by:

$$\alpha_j=\frac{\Delta t\sigma^2S_j^2}{2h^2}-\frac{r\Delta tS_j}{2h}=\frac{\Delta t\sigma^2(jh)^2}{2h^2}-\frac{r\Delta t(jh)}{2h}=\Delta t\sigma^2j^2/2-\frac{r\Delta tj}{2}\quad(S_j=jh)$$

$$\beta_j=1-\frac{2\Delta t\sigma^2(jh)^2}{2h^2}-r\Delta t=1-\sigma^2j^2\Delta t-r\Delta t$$

$$\gamma_j=\Delta t\sigma^2j^2/2+\frac{r\Delta tj}{2}.$$

It is possible to get incorrect answers (negative values, for example) if the time step $\Delta t$ is inappropriately chosen. To motivate what we mean, we note that the scheme (12.33) is in fact equivalent to the trinomial method. Furthermore, the coefficients in (12.34) correspond to *positive* probabilities in the trinomial method:

+   - $\alpha_j$: probability that the stock price decreases.
+   - $\beta_j$: probability that the stock price remains the same.
+   - $\gamma_j$: probability that the stock price increases for all $j=0,\ldots,NT$.

These values should be positive if we are to retain the physical or financial meanings in the numerical results. We say that the explicit scheme is *conditionally stable*. This means that there are restrictions on the mesh sizes $\Delta t$ and $h$ and there is a constraint that must be satisfied at all times if we wish to have a stable approximation.

We interpreted jumps in the trinomial method as probabilities. In general, the sum of the probabilities is equal to one and each probability should be non-negative. A probability can become negative depending on the relative sizes of the drift and volatility terms. We can analyse the stability by more methods such as *von Neumann stability analysis* and the *maximum principle* (see Duffy, 2006).

We now examine the stability of the *explicit finite difference method* (also known as *Forward in Time Centred in Space* (FTCS)) applied to the model convection–diffusion equation in *non-conservative form*:

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-\mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\sigma\frac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}$$

where:

$u$ = vorticity (or other advected (convected) quantity)

$\sigma$ = diffusion $=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}}$ where Re is the Reynolds number

$\mu$ = linearised advection speed.

The *FTCS* (explicit Euler) scheme now becomes:

$$\frac{u_j^{n+1}-u_j^n}{\Delta t}=-\mu\left(\frac{u_{j+1}^n-u_{j-1}^n}{2h}\right)+\sigma\left(\frac{u_{j+1}^n-2u_j^n+u_{j-1}^n}{h^2}\right)$$

or:

$$u_j^{n+1}=(\beta+\alpha)u_{j-1}^n+(1-2\beta)u_j^n+(\beta-\alpha)u_{j+1}^n$$

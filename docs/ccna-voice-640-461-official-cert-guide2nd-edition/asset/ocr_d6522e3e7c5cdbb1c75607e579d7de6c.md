# Understanding RTP and RTCP

When you walk into the VoIP world, you encounter a whole nest of protocol standards. Think of the Real-time Transport Protocol (RTP) and Real-time Transport Control Protocol (RTCP) as the protocols of video. RTP operates at the transport layer of the OSI model on top of UDP. Having two transport layer protocols is odd, but that's exactly what is happening here. UDP provides the services it always does: port numbers (that is, session multiplexing) and header checksums (which ensure that the header information does not become corrupted). RTP adds time stamps and sequence numbers to the header information. This allows the device to put the packets back in order when it receives them from the remote end (function of the sequence number) and use a buffer to remove jitter (slight delays) from the packets to get a smooth audio playback (function of the time stamp).

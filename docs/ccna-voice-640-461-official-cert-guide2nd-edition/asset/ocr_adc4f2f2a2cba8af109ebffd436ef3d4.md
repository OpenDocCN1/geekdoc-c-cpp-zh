However, in light of Rule 1, we could have not assign an outgoing COR list to dial-peer 10 (which was used for emergency calls). To combat these issues, you to ensure not only that there is bandwidth available for VoIP traffic, this means if a bottleneck is in the network where a router queues traffic before it is sent, the router must be waiting the voice traffic ahead of the data traffic to be sent in the first interval. Accomplishing this is the job of QoS. QoS is not a tool in itself, but rather category of many tools aimed at getting complete control over the traffic crossing your network. There might be times when you use a single QoS tool at the delay of traffic. Other times, you may employ multiple QoS tools to control delay, reserve bandwidth, and compress data that is heading over the WAN. How and when you use each of the QoS tools depends on the network requirements of traffic and the characteristics (such as bandwidth, delay, and so on) of the network supporting the traffic.

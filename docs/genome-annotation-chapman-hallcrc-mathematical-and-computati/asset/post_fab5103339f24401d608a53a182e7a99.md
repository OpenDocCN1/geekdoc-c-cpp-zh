The HGVS syntax allows for the description of the variant at the protein level as well. A change of glutamine to arginine at residue position 488 would be “p.Gln488Arg”. Single amino acid IUPAC codes (LiBecq, 1992) can also be used. SNPs can affect the protein translation in other ways as well. A SNP introducing a premature stop codon would be noted as “p.Gln488Ter”. Read through has the syntax “p.Ter797Cys*25” if the change creates a cysteine and the next in-frame stop is 25 residues (i.e., 75 bases) downstream.

Some variants located in exons may create new (“cryptic”) acceptor or donor slice sites (e.g., Dunn et al., 2002). SNP variants in introns may also disrupt the donor or acceptor site. They may also, in rare instances, create a small novel exon inside of what would normally be an intron (also known as a cryptic exon). In the 5’ UTR, a SNP may disrupt the donor of a transcription factor or create a new preferred codon. In the 3’ UTR a SNP may disrupt the polyadenylation signal for a gene, also disrupt a microRNA suppression mechanism, and these are starting to be catalogued (Bruno et al., 2012). All of these possibilities point to the importance of correct annotation of genes and associated control elements.

VCF files from the 1000 Genomes Project SNP variants and small insertions or deletions (indels) reported in the literature are available from the NCBI’s dbSNP database (Day, 2010), with links to other resources such as information about related diseases.

## 4.4.3 Insertions and Deletions

### 4.4.3.1 Nomenclature

Insertions are extra bases (as opposed to base substitutions) that exist in the individual but not in the reference genome. These are also reportable in VCF files. The HGVS syntax is “c.22_23insTTG” for the insertion of TTG between bases 22 and 23 of the coding sequence. Deletions are the removal of bases in the individual, relative to the reference genome. The HGVS syntax is “c.22_26del” for the deletion of five (end–start+1) bases, or in the case of a single-base deletion the syntax “c.22del” is allowed. The sequence, which is deleted in comparison to the reference sequence, can optionally be appended to this syntax (e.g., c.11delT).

Complex variants composed of simultaneous insertion deletion events are often referred to as indels. The HGVS syntax is “c.345_347delinsTTTG” for the replacement of three bases in the reference sequence by the four bases TTTG. A special case of an indel is an inversion, where the insertion sequence is of the same length as the deletion, and the two sequences are reverse complement. The HGVS syntax for this case is “c.665_670inv” for a six-base inversion.

Because they span multiple base positions, insertions and deletions can be quite complex in their nomenclature. For example, “c.344_347+2del” is a six-base deletion, removing four coding bases and the (presumable) intron donor GT. The syntax “c.89-7*_89delinsGAA” is a five-base indel, just before the acceptor site of a 3’ UTR exon. A special case of insertions is duplication of short sequences from the reference genome, with the syntax “c.345_348dup.

### 4.4.3.2 Effects

In addition to the case effects as SNPs, insertions and deletions may cause the insertion or deletion of amino acids in the protein region. If the variant lengths are not multiples of three, translation frameshifts will also occur. ## 4.4.4 Copy Number Variation

In polyploid genomes, variants in the number of copies of a chromosomal segment occur in individuals (Freeman et al., 2006). These copy number variants (CNVs) may affect the genome via gene dosage effects and can lead to disease (Alman and Path, 2012). Methods for high-throughput sequencing of CNV calls are nascent, with most CNV data generated using comparative genomic hybridization (CGH) microglia (Alkan et al., 2011). These methods can be used to generate a relative copy number estimate (e.g., -1 or 1) over a region defined by the density of probes on the microarray.

Where high-resolution sequence data is available, the Wiggle file format (Kent et al., 2010) is suitable for storing and subsequently plotting the data in the UCSC genome browser and others (see Chapter 9). Where CNV data is coarse-grained, the BedGraph file format (Kent et al., 2010) can succinctly represent the information and is accepted by many of the same browsers.

Public repositories for CNVs, large insertions and deletions, and trans-locations (i.e., structural variants), called dVAR and DGva, now being coordinated by an international consortium (Church et al., 2010). Cancer is generally the product of de novo mutations in particular genes, or the gain or loss of larger chromosomal segments. An excellent public resource for CGH information focused on cancer-related structural changes is Progenetix (Baudis, 2006).

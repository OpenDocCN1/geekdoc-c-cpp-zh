In addition to the GBrowse family, there are a number of comparative genome browsers that have a built-in capability for the linear representation of genomes. These include Bluejay (Soh et al., 2012; Soh et al., 2008), Centeny (Sinha and Muller, 2007), Apollo, MEDEA (www.broadinstitute.org/annotation/medea), Sybil (http://sybil.sourceforge.org/), PHIGs (Dehal and Boore, 2006), VISTA (Mayor et al., 2000), VISTA Syntview (http://genome.jgi-psf.org/synteny), UCSC Genome Browser, CMap (Yeous-Clark et al., 2009), ACT (Carver et al., 2008), Comp (Engels et al., 2006), and MultiPipMaker (Elnitz et al., 2005). Figure 9.6 shows the comparison of two archaeal genomes in Bluejay, where the two genomes are represented as two parallel linear stretches and two genes are linked if they share the same Genome Ontology (GO) classification. The comparative genomics functionality of Bluejay can be repurposed for comparing multiple chromosomes in a single genome. An example is shown in Figure 9.7, where the ability of Bluejay to compare more than two genomes is applied to the comparison of four human chromosomes or chromosomal arms, by treating the chromosomes as if they were genomes for visualization purposes.

A multiple sequence alignment (MSA) is an alignment of more than two biological sequences, where the sequences are in most cases presumed to have an evolutionary relationship. MSAs are computationally more complex than pairwise alignments, since finding the optimal alignment of a relatively small number of sequences of even moderate length quickly becomes computationally hard. An MSA is most often represented as a matrix of rows of sequences, in which each row is a sequence, and columns indicate positions that are equivalent across all of the compared sequences.

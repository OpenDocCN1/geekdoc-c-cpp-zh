The viral 3' flank includes a polyuridine tract (PPT; i.e., polyA/G), which primes DNA synthesis during reverse transcription. This is followed by the proviral transcriptional primer U3 and finally R.

The genes of any retrovirus are referred to as gag, pol, and env (Jurka et al., 2007), and are always found in this order. Gag proteins are cleaved, typically by a viral protease, into “mature” peptides forming essential virus particle structures: the matrix, capsid, and nucleocapsid. Pol proteins nucleic acid processing enzymes covering important roles in the life cycle of the virus, such as reverse transcription (polymerase), genome integration (integrase), and RNase H activity. The Env protein encodes the viral envelope protein.

Integrated retroviruses that can be referred to as endogenous retroviruses (ERVs), with the integrated DNA referred to as a provirus (Gifford and Tristem, 2003). Retransposons near genes can affect their transcription. ERVs expression has also been linked to chronic diseases, their annotation is of more than academic interest. Depending on how long the retrovirus has been integrated into the genome, many parts of the original retroviral structure may be missing or mutated too to be easily detectable using a BLAST-based method. LTR_STRUC (McCarthy and McDonald, 2003) can be used to detect retroviral-like elements, which are flanked by long terminal repeats (LTRs). Retrosceptor (Sperber et al., 2007) uses less stringent criteria, therefore false positives must be taken into account. A combination of all three methods may be the best choice to find a balance between sensitivity and specificity. The nonviral Penelope of retrotransposons also contains a distinct LTR that likely reflects its lack of integration and dependence on hostomerase.

Retrotransposons with LTRs are either LINEs or SINEs (Jurka et al., 2007). Long-spersed nuclear elements (LINEs) in eukaryotes over 5 kb or greater genomic regions (Gogvaze and Buzdin, 2009), with a RNA polymerase 2 binding site 5' of a high-specificity reverse transcription gene. Inactivated LINEs are often truncated at the 5' end. The LINE may also contain an endonuclease with site-specific integration activity. The 3' end contains a polyadenylation signal (AATAAA), usually followed by a G/T-rich region. There are three families of LINEs, based on their origin: in humans, only LINE1 elements are actively transposable, with LINE2 and CR1/LINE3 elements mostly degraded (Gogvaze and Buzdin, 2009).

# 2.6 SPLICE VARIANTS

For many eukaryotic genes, there is in fact not just one correct gene structure, but multiple versions are true, at least due to splice variants. The general categories of splicing events include (in descending order of frequency) skipped exons, alternative donors and acceptors, retained introns, and novel introns. Many gene prediction programs can be utilized to generate models of alternate transcripts, at least some of which will respond to actual splice variants. The validation of alternate models, like that of the primary models, involves the mapping of mRNA data to the genome. Traditionally this was done using EST sequences elucidated by Sanger sequencing. To improve the depth of transcript coverage to find rare splice events, ESTs from related species can be mapped to the target genome, when genes were highly conserved. This resolution of variants can now be greatly enhanced by the mapping of short next-generation sequencing mRNA data to the genome.

Skipped exons fall into two general categories: inner and outer. Skipped exons lead to proteins that are truncated at the N- or C-terminus, or sometimes the protein translation is unchanged and only the UTRs are affected. Figure 2.2 illustrates how two programs disagree on the gene structure, but both are likely correct. GeneMark captures the form of the gene that skips the exon (the stop codon is just what is normally the intron). AUGUSTUS identifies the more common form that donors include the exon.

Alternative splice donors and acceptors yield shorter or longer versions of a protein, and sometimes dramatically so, because of changes in the reading frame caused by different splice sites. The same holds for skipped inner exons, if their length is not a multiple of three. Alternate acceptors and donors are caused by the presence of valid intron structures, which the spliceosome can excise. While splice repressor proteins are known, the complete mechanisms behind the preferential splicing of one form or another under various environmental conditions are not completely understood.

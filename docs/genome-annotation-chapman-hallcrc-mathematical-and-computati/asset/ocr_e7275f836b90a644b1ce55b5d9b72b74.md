Although mapping of short reads or assembled mRNAs back to the genome can be very helpful in determining correct intron and exon boundaries, it is only one component of the overall gene prediction process. This is because (1) it is unlikely that full-length mRNA sequences can be obtained for all genes; (2) in some species transcripts may encode operons of more than one gene; and (3) start stop codons cannot be determined from the mRNA alone due to multiple translation frames and factors affecting translation. As such, transcript information is therefore supplemented in most gene prediction software by statistical models of gene feature sequences.

# 2.3 STATISTICAL MODELS

Most initio gene predict models are based on a data structure known as a generalized hidden Markov model (GHMM). Conceptually, it can be thought of as a graph where the nodes represent events in the gene's expression, and arrows connecting the nodes represent the scanning of the sequence, as shown in Figure 2.1. It should be noted that the actual GHMMs used in prediction programs have many more states (nodes) and transitions (arrows) than in this example.

Each gene model is calculated by progressively scanning each base of the genomic sequence, looking for signals to transition from one state to the next. In the case of a transcription start, the signal being searched for is not necessarily the exact string GCCCTA but perhaps a pattern such as (G/C) CCXTA.

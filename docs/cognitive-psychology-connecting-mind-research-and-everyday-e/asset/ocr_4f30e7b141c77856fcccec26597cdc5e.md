Think back to your childhood. Which events stand out? A family trip to the beach? Birthday parties? Playing with special friends? Events such as these, as well as routine things that have happened more recently, are part of your long-term memory. After comparing short- and long-term memory, this chapter describes a number of types of long-term memory, including episodic memory—memory for specific experiences; semantic memory—memory for facts; and procedural memory—memory for how to do things. We consider how these different types of memory are represented in the mind and brain, how they interact with each other, and what happens to these memories as time passes.

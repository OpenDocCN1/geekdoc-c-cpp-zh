We are interested in what happens to $a(k)$ as $k$ gets larger and larger without bound. (In other words, when we iterate many times.) It may happen that for large values of $k$, $a(k)$ is close to or even equal to some number $L$. If increasing $k$ (doing more iterations) causes $a(k)$ to get even closer to $L$ until for very large values of $k$, $a(k)$ is essentially equal to $L$, then $L$ is the limit of $a(k)$. If $a(k)$ has a limit, then we also say $a(k)$ converges to $L$ or simply $a(k)$ converges. It is important to understand that if $a(k)$ converges to $L$, then further increases in $k$ will never cause $a(k)$ to move “too far” away from $L$. Also remember that we are looking at large values of $k$. How large is large depends upon the DDS. Compare how large $k$ must be for the two sequences DDS$_1$: $a(k)=e^{-k}$ and DDS$_2$: $b(k)=1/(\ln(k)+1)$.

If increasing $k$ causes $a(k)$ to continue to increase or to oscillate, and $a(k)$ does not begin to converge to a number $L$, we say $a(k)$ is diverging and the limit does not exist.

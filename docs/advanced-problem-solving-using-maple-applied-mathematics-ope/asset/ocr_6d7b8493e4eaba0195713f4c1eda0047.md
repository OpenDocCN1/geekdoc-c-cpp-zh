Notice that the leading principal minors of a matrix are just the determinants of all the submatrices formed along the main diagonal.

How do we use all these determinants, the leading principal minors, of the Hessian matrix to determine the convexity of the multivariate function? The following theorem answers our question.

# Theorem. Convexity and the Hessian Matrix.

Suppose $f(x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$ has continuous second partial derivatives at all points of its domain $D$. Let $H$ be $f$’s Hessian matrix. Then

+   a. if all the leading principal minors of $H$ are nonnegative, then $f$ is convex;
+   
+   b. if all the leading principal minors of $H$ match the sign of $(-1)^k$ where $k$ is the minor’s order (positive for even order, negative for odd), then $f$ is concave;
+   
+   c. otherwise, $f$ is neither convex, nor concave.

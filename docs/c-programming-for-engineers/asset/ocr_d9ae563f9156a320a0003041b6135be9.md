# Microcontrollers

+   - A microcontroller (also known as a microcontroller unit or MCU) is a small self-contained computer system that includes a microprocessor, memory, and input/output (I/O) peripherals on a single chip.
- They are also called bare-metal systems because they operate with very minimal resources and without an operating system.
- Bare-metal systems can be extremely compact, consume very little power and cost very little, and buried deep inside devices such as computer mice, USB drives and even certain USB cables.

The choice between SoC and an MCU depends on the specific needs of the application. SoC-based computers offer more flexibility and general-purpose capabilities, while bare-metal systems prioritize efficiency and cost-effectiveness for specific tasks.

# 1.2 Computer Architecture

Computer architecture refers to the design and organization of computer systems, including hardware, firmware, and software components. It organizes computer systems using a layered structure, making it easier to design, develop, and maintain complex systems. Each layer builds upon the one below it, simplifying the design process.

A computer system typically consists of four layers: hardware, firmware, operating system, and application.

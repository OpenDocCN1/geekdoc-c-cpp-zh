### Jump Statements

As the name suggests, jump statements allow you to jump from the current statement to another statement in your program. Jump statements are mainly used with loops and switch statements. In loops, you can use jump statements to break out of a loop or to skip the statements after a particular statement in the loop. C# provides the following two jump statements:

- The break statement
- The continue statement

Let’s discuss the use of each of these statements, one by one.

### Using the break Statement

We have already used the **break** statement in switch statements, where it helps in avoiding fall through. However, it can also be used in loops to terminate or stop the execution of the loop. This implies that the execution of the loop stops immediately and the control passes to the statement that follows the loop. If the loop has statements after the **break** statement, then all those statements are skipped. In nested loops, the break statement terminates only the current loop, that is, the loop in which it is used. For example, if there are two for loops nested within each other and you use the **break** statement in the inner for loop, then only the inner for loop is terminated without affecting the outer for loop.

The syntax of the break statement is as follows:

```csharp
break;
```

In the preceding syntax, the **break** keyword is used to indicate a break statement.

### Using the continue Statement

The continue statement causes immediate termination of the current iteration of the loop in which it is used. It does not cause termination of the entire loop. The continue statement passes the control to the next iteration of the loop. If there are any statements that are written after the continue statement, then those statements are skipped.

The syntax of the continue statement is as follows:

```csharp
continue;
```

In the preceding syntax, the continue keyword indicates a continue statement.

The concept of OOP has been introduced to overcome the difficulty of limited or no reusability of code. OOP uses the concept of object to reuse the existing code. An object in a programming language does not mean a tangible or visible entity; rather it is an organization of code. It may contain certain behaviors and properties. In programming languages, behaviors of an object are represented as methods and the properties of the object are represented as attributes. Examples of languages that follow OOP concepts are C++, JAVA, C#, and Visual Basic.

### Encapsulation

Encapsulation is the process of showing only the relevant information of a specific object and hiding the irrelevant information from a user. It binds the variables and the functions together into a single unit, called class. To understand the concept of encapsulation, let's take an example. When a customer goes to purchase a refrigerator, the customer generally looks for its color, functionalities, and the storage capacity; whereas, its internal working and wiring system are hidden from the customer. The company does not want the customer to tamper with the wiring system; and therefore, keeps the details of internal wiring system hidden from the customer. This process of hiding internal facts is known as encapsulation.

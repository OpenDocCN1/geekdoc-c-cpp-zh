WPF 4.0 Designer provides various tools, windows, and features to help you develop visually appealing WPF applications. Fig.VB-5.10 shows some important components of WPF 4.0 Designer:

### Design View

As the name suggests, the Design view is the area where you design the visual aspect or UI of a WPF application. It allows you to build the UI of the WPF application easily and quickly in a What You See Is What You Get (WYSIWYG) manner. A rectangular area, called window (in standalone applications), is displayed at the center of the Design view, which is the main design area where you can add controls and manipulate their appearance, as shown in Fig.VB-5.11:

![](img/9862a98a1a2d3bb14334137965a1ec60_190_0.png)

Fig.VB-5.11

As you can see in Fig.VB-5.11, the Design view has several design-related elements, such as the Zoom control to zoom in or out of the Design view and margin lines to set the margins of a control. Let’s briefly discuss the various elements of the Design view.

### Grid Rails, Gridlines, and Gridline Indicators

The grid rails, gridlines, and gridline indicators appear only for the Grid control, which is a WPF control that is automatically added to a WPF application at the time of its creation. The Grid control allows you to represent the WPF application as a grid, which is enclosed by grid rails that span horizontally and vertically on the top and left of the WPF window (Fig.VB-5.11).

By default, the grid has one cell, that is, one row and one column. You can create additional rows and columns in the grid by clicking the desired positions on the grid rails. When you click the grid rails, gridlines and gridline indicators appear at those positions (Fig.VB-5.11). If you click the horizontal grid rail, then a vertical gridline appears dividing the grid into two columns. Similarly, if you click the vertical grid rail, then a horizontal gridline appears dividing the grid into two rows. You can control the height and width of the rows and columns by moving the gridline indicators, which appear as triangles on the grid rails.

### The Zoom Control and the Zoom to Fit Button

You can find the Zoom control and the Zoom to Fit button at the upper-left corner of the Design view (Fig.VB-5.11). The Zoom control allows you to zoom in and zoom out of the Design view. You can drag the Zoom control slider up or down to zoom in or out. The current zoom level displays at the top of the Zoom control, as shown in Fig.VB-5.12.

![](img/9862a98a1a2d3bb14334137965a1ec60_190_1.png)

Fig.VB-5.12

In case you want to zoom the window according to the available space in the Design view, then you can quickly do so by clicking the Zoom to Fit button, which appears just below the Zoom control (Fig.VB-5.12).

### Move and Resize Handles

You can move and resize the controls in the Design view by using the move and resize handles, respectively. The move and resize handles appear only when you select the control. The move handle appears at the upper-left corner of the control and the resize handle appears at all the four corners of the control. Fig.VB-5.13 shows the move and resize handles for the Grid control:

![](img/9862a98a1a2d3bb14334137965a1ec60_191_0.png)

Fig.VB-5.13

Fig.VB-5.14 shows the move and resize handles for the Button control:

![](img/9862a98a1a2d3bb14334137965a1ec60_191_1.png)

Fig.VB-5.14

You need to click and drag the move handle if you want to reposition a control in the Design view. The move handle is disabled for the Grid control that is automatically added to a WPF application. If you want to resize a control, you can click any of the resize handle and drag it outwards or inwards.

### Margin Lines and Margin Stubs

WPF controls exist within the container controls, one of which is the Grid control that is automatically added to a WPF application. A container control is a control that can contain one or more controls, called child controls. When you select a control on the grid, its vertical and horizontal lines are called *margin lines*, which appear from the edges of the control to the grid, as shown in Fig.VB-5.15:

![](img/9862a98a1a2d3bb14334137965a1ec60_191_2.png)

Fig.VB-5.15

The margin lines represent the distance or the *margin* between the edges of the control and the grid. In case the grid has multiple rows and columns, then the margin lines appear from the edge of the control to the nearest gridline. There can be a maximum of four margin lines—left, top, right, and bottom for each control. You can notice in Fig.VB-5.15 that only the left and top margin lines of the button appear; right and bottom margin lines do not appear. Instead, there is a small circle, called the *margin stub*, on the right and bottom edges of the button. The margin stub indicates that the respective margin is set to zero.

The margins for a control are collectively accessed through the **Margin** property of the control. You can set the **Margin** property of a control in the Design view, XAML view, or code-behind file. You can set the **Margin** property of a control in the Design view by selecting the control and dragging it to the desired location. You can also use the **Properties** window to set the **Margin** property by entering the values for the margins in a clockwise manner starting from the left margin, that is, left, top, right, and bottom.

### Snaplines

Snaplines are a feature in the Design view to assist you to align one control with respect to another that was earlier added to the WPF window (or page for XBAPs). A snapline appears only when you move or resize a control on the window. It appears as a thin light brown line along the edges of the controls, as shown in Fig.VB-5.16:

![](img/9862a98a1a2d3bb14334137965a1ec60_192_0.png)

You can notice in Fig.VB-5.16 that at the ends of the snaplines, the number 95 is displayed, which represents the distance between the edges of the two buttons. In case you do not want to see the snaplines, you need to press the ALT key on the keyboard while moving or resizing the control.

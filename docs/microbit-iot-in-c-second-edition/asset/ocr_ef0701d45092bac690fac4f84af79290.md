We already know about DIR and PULL from the previous chapter and to understand DRIVE all you really need to know is that there are two possibilities for the push-pull transistor arrangement.
The two transistors can either be Standard Drive, i.e. 0.5mA, or High Drive,

![](bbox=[0.303, 0.187, 0.696, 0.335])

i.e. 5mA. The bottom transistor pulls the output low to give a 0 and the top transistor pulls the output high to give a 1. Hence a DRIVE, or S0H1, has a high drive for the top transistor and a standard drive for the bottom transistor. That is, the GPIO line can sink 0.5mA and supply 5mA to a load.

The D in the specification means Disconnected, i.e. there is no transistor in that position. So H0D1 has the pull-up transistor missing, giving an open collector driver.

In most cases you want S0S1 or H0H1 - the others are for situations.

The INPUT bits control when the pin will contribute to generating a DETECT signal. This is used to bring the processor out of power-saving mode and in more complex ways to automatically trigger other actions. You can ignore this for the moment.

The only question we have to answer now is, where are the registers?
P0_ GPIO registers occupy a block of memory starting at 0x50000000, microbit V2 has a second bank of registers, P1_, starting at 0x50000300. The register addresses are specified as offsets from these starting point in two blocks:

```
OUT 0x504
OUTSET 0x508
OUTCLR 0x50C
IN 0x510
DIR 0x514
DIRSET 0x518
DIRCLR 0x51C
PIN_CNF[n] 0x700 +4*n
```

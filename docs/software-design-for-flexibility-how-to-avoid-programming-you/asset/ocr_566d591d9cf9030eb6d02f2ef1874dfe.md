# Dependencies mitigate inconsistency

Dependency annotations on data give us a powerful tool for organizing human-like computations: For example, all humans harbor mutually inconsistent beliefs: an intelligent person may be committed to the scientific method yet have a strong attachment to superstitious or ritual practices; a person may have a strong belief in the sanctity of all human life, yet also believe that capital punishment is sometimes justified. If we were really logicians this kind of inconsistency would be fatal: if we were really to simultaneously believe both propositions P and NOT P, then we would have to believe all propositions! But somehow we manage to keep inconsistent beliefs by inhibiting all useful thought. Our personal belief systems appear to be locally consistent, in there are no contradictions apparent. If we observe inconsistencies we do not crash; we may feel conflicted or we may chuckle.

We can attach to each proposition a set of supporting assumptions, allowing deductions to be conditional on the assumption set. Then, if a contradiction occurs, a process can determine the particular “nogood set” of inconsistent assumptions. The system can then “uckle,” realizing that no deductions based on any superset of those assumptions can be believed. This chuckling process, dependency-directed backtracking, can be used to optimize a complex search process, allowing a search to make the best use of its mistakes. By enabling a process to simultaneously hold beliefs based on mutually inconsistent sets of assumptions without logical disaster is revolutionary.

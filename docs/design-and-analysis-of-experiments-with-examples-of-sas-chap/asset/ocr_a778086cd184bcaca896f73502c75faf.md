\(\beta\) represents the vector of fixed effects, while \(\gamma\) and \(\epsilon\) represent the vectors of random effects. The assumption of independent and normally distributed random effects can be expressed by \(\gamma \sim MVN(0, \sigma_\gamma^2 I_{abr})\), and \(\epsilon \sim MVN(0, \sigma^2 I_{abrs})\); therefore, \(y \sim MVN(X\beta, V)\), where \(V = Z(\sigma_\gamma^2 I_{abr})Z' + \sigma^2 I_{abrs}\). The least squares estimator of \(\beta\) would be \(\hat{\beta} = (X'V^{-1}X)^{-1}X'V^{-1}y\); however, \(\sigma_\gamma^2, \sigma^2\) are unknown, and therefore \(V\) is unknown.

SAS proc glm solves the problem by treating both \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) as fixed. Then the least squares estimates are given by

\(\left( \begin{array}{ c }
\hat{\beta} \\
\hat{\gamma} \\
\end{array} \right) = \left( \begin{array}{ c c }
X'X & X'Z \\
Z'X & Z'Z \\
\end{array} \right)^{-1} \left( \begin{array}{ c }
X'y \\
Z'y \\
\end{array} \right).\) (5.15)

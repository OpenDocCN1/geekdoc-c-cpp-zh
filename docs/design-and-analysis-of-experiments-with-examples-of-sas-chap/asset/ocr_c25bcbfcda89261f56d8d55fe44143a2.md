# 5.4.3 Maximum Likelihood and REML Estimators

Although the method of moments estimators are uniformly best unbiased estimators, they have one unfortunate property. When \(msT\) is less than \(msE\) in the analysis of variance, the estimator of \(\sigma_t^2\) will be negative. This can happen quite frequently if \(\sigma_t^2/\sigma^2 \le 0.10\), and there are less than \(T = 10\) levels of the random factor \(t_i\). Maximum likelihood (ML) and (reduced or restricted) maximum likelihood (REML) are preferred methods of estimation that avoid this problem. REML is an adaptation of the maximum likelihood technique that maximizes part of the likelihood. The fact that maximum likelihood estimators cannot lie outside their parameter space prevents both the ML and REML methods from obtaining negative estimates of \(\sigma_t^2\).

The first design has a smaller chance for confusion since the hierarchical ordering principle tells us that three-factor interactions are less likely to be important than two-factor interactions.

Three general criteria have been proposed for guiding the choice among the various possible sets of generators for any possible 2^{-k-p} design. These criteria are the resolution criteria, the aberration criteria and the clear effects criteria.

Box and Hunter (1961) first proposed the resolution criteria. The resolution of the design is defined to be the length of the shortest word in the defining relation. For example, in the first defining relation shown above, the shortest word has length 4. Thus it is a resolution IV design. The shortest word for the second defining relation for a 2^{-2} design shown above has length 3, and it is therefore a resolution III design. In general, if the number of runs in two designs is the same, the design with the higher resolution is preferred.

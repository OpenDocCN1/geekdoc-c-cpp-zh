Near the upper-left corner is a zoom bar. You can click at the bottom of it
and Zoom To Fit, or move the slider. This is invaluable for working on an
application that is bigger than your screen, or lightening things just so.

At the left side of the dividing line between the Design and XAML frames is
a little-double-arrow icon. Clicking this icon changes whatever is in the bottom
frame to the top frame, and vice versa.

On the right side of the same dividing line are three buttons that determine
t kind of split you use — a vertical split, a horizontal split (which is
default), or no split.

A few point-and-click adjustment tools are in the designer itself. Here's
just one: Click in the red area of the window in the designer. Put
your mouse above where the "146" size designation appears.

See the little box that appears, with an asterisk, a hash, and the
word Auto? That’s just one of the many helpers that you find in the
WPF designer. The hash h sets the column width to a set number (as it is
default). The asterisk sets it to a percentage of the table — useful for resizing-
ing. The word Auto sizes the column based on the contents.

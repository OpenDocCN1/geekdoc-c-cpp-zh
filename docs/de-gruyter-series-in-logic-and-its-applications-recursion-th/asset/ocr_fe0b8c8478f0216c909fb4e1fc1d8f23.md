Let \( l_0 = 0 \) and let \( l_n = 2^n \) for \( n > 0 \). To compute \( z(n) \) from \( y \), \( y \)-recursively find the \( n \)-th \( l_m \) such that for all \( i \), \( j \) with \( l_m \le i < j < l_{m+1} \), \( y(i) = y(j) \). Then \( z(n) = y(l_m) \).

We call \( A \subseteq \omega \times 2^\omega \) a universal \( \Pi_1^1 \)-closed set if (i) it is \( \Pi_1^1 \), (ii) for every \( n \), \( A_n = \{x \mid (n, x) \in A\} \) is a \( \Pi_1^1 \)-closed set, and (iii) every \( \Pi_1^1 \)-closed set is \( A_n \) for some \( n \). By Theorems 13.1.3 and 13.1.14, the real \( x_0 = \{n \mid \mu(A_n) = 0\} \) is \( \Sigma_1^1 \). Let

\( c = \{(n, \sigma) \mid n \in x_0 \wedge (\exists k)(n, x) \in A \wedge \sigma \prec x\} \subseteq \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \).

Then \( c \) may be viewed as a \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-real. Since every \( \Pi_1^1 \)-null closed set is \( \Pi_1^1(c) \), every \( c \)-Kurtz-random real is \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz random.

# Theorem 14.1.6. The hyperdegree of \( c \) is the base of a cone for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

Proof. Lemma 14.1.5 implies that the Turing degree of any \( y \ge_T c' \) contains a member which is \( c \)-Kurtz random and therefore \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz random. Thus \( c \) is a base for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

We analyze the complexity of \( c \). Since every \( \Pi_1^1 \)-singleton is recursive in \( c \), \( c \notin L_{\delta_1^1} \). Moreover, the set \( |x| \in x \) is a \( \Pi_1^1 \)-singleton] is \( \Delta_1^1(c) \). In other words, \( |x \in x \) is a \( \Pi_1^1 \)-singleton] \( \in L_{\omega_1^{ck}}[c] \). Hence one has \( \omega_1^c > \delta_1^1 \). Applying the same argument as that in Proposition 4.4.4, the reals in \( L_{\delta_1^1} \) are precisely those that are \( \Delta_1^1 \) and so \( c \) is not \( \Delta_1^1 \). Moreover, since \( c \) is \( \Sigma_2^1 \), it is \( \Sigma_1^1 \) definable over \( L_{\delta_1^1} \) and hence \( c \in L_{\delta_1^1+1} \). Indeed, all \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-reals belong to \( L_{\delta_1^1+1} \). Thus,

\( c \) has the largest hyperdegree among all \( \Sigma_2^1 \) reals.

$\mathbb{P}_{\ge \aleph_0}$ preserve cardinals. Moreover, $\mathcal{M}[G_{\ge \aleph_0}] \vDash 	ext{GCH}$. Thus without loss of generality, we may assume that $\mathcal{M}[G_{\ge \aleph_0}] = \mathcal{M}$. In other words, for any $\alpha \le \omega_1$, there is a real $x_\alpha \in M$ such that $p_0 \Vdash \dot{f}(\alpha) = x_\alpha$.

Now for $a < \omega_1 < b < \omega_2$, we have
$p_0 \Vdash 	ext{``}\dot{x}_{\omega_1} \le_T \dot{f}(eta) \oplus x_\alpha	ext{''} \Leftrightarrow \alpha \in X_eta$.

By Proposition 6.11.7 and a rearrangement of $p_0$ (if necessary), we may assume that $p_0 \in \mathbb{P}_{<\omega_1}$. Let $a_{p_0} = \min\{n \mid (\forall eta < a)(p_0(\alpha) 	ext{ is undefined})\}$. Then $a_{p_0}$ is a countable ordinal. Since only at most countably many ordinals occur in $\dot{f}(eta)$, for $eta > \omega_1$, if $\dot{f}(eta)$ is not a $\mathbb{P}_{<\omega_0}$-name then there is a permutation $\iota_g$ switching ordinals in $\dot{f}(eta)$ to countable ordinals greater than $a_{p_0}$. In other words, $\iota_g$ induces an automorphism of $\mathbb{P}$ so that $\iota_g(p_0) = p_0$ but $\iota_g(\dot{f}(eta)) \in \mathbb{P}_{<\omega_0}$. By Proposition 6.11.7 again,
$p_0 \Vdash 	ext{``}\dot{x}_{\omega_1} \le_T \iota_g(\dot{f}(eta)) \oplus x_\alpha	ext{''} \Leftrightarrow \alpha \in X_eta$.

Now in $\mathcal{M}$, there are at most $2^{\aleph_0} = \aleph_1$ many $\mathbb{P}_{<\omega_0}$-names for reals. Thus there exist $eta_0 
eq eta_1$ such that $\iota_g(\dot{f}(eta_0)) = \iota_g(\dot{f}(eta_1))$. Hence $X_{eta_0} = X_{eta_1}$, contradicting the assumption.
Let $N = M[G]$. Then $N \models$ "U is not embeddable into $(\mathscr{D}, \le, U)$". This completes the proof of Theorem 9.2.3.

**Remark 9.2.6.** Both the conclusions of Theorem 9.1.4 (see [4]) and Theorem 9.2.2 (see [167]) are provable under ZFC + MA + $
eg$CH.

# ## 9.3 Cofinal chains in $\mathscr{D}$

# ### 9.3.1 Existence of a cofinal maximal chain of order type $\omega_1$

**Definition 9.3.1.** Given a partial ordering $(P, <_P)$, a set $A \subseteq P$ is a _cofinal chain_ if the members of $A$ are linearly ordered under $P$ and for any $z \in P$, there is an $x \in A$ such that $z \le_P x$.

It is not difficult to see that under ZFC, there is a cofinal chain of Turing degrees if and only if CH holds. A natural question is whether there exists a cofinal maximal chain of order type $\omega_1$ in $(\mathscr{D}, \le)$ assuming ZFC + CH. The question is considered in this section.

Note that g is total since both p and q are total. Then

$$ W_{g(n)} = \begin{cases} \emptyset & n=1, \\ \{\langle i, k \rangle \mid i <_O k\} \cup W_{g(k)} & n=2^k, \\ \bigcup_i W_{g(\Phi_0(m))} \mid m \in \omega\} & n=3 \cdot 5^k, \\ W_0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases} $$

An easy induction shows that $W_{g(n)} = \{\langle i, j \rangle \mid i <_O j <_O n\}$ for $n \in O$.

Theorem 1.3.4 allows one to carry out transfinite induction uniformly for $e \in O$. Here we give two applications of Proposition 1.3.3.

# Lemma 1.3.5. There is a recursive function $g$ such that for all $e$:

+   (i) $g(e) \in O \Leftrightarrow W_e \subseteq \emptyset$.
+   (ii) $g(e) \in O \leftrightarrow |n|_O < |g(e)|$ for every $n \in W_e$.

Proof. By the s-m-n Theorem, there is a recursive function $r$ such that $\Phi_{r(e)}(0) = 1$ and the range of $\Phi_{r(e)}$ is $(W_e \setminus \{0\}) \cup \{1\}$. By the s-m-n Theorem again, there is a recursive function $s$ such that $\Phi_{s(e)}(0) = 1$ and $(\forall n)(\Phi_{s(e)}(n+1) = \Phi_{s(e)}(n) \oplus 2^{\Phi_{r(e)}(n+1)})$. Define

$g(e) = 3 \cdot 5^{s(e)}$.

Note that if $W_e \subseteq \emptyset$, then $g(e) \in O$ by Proposition 1.3.3. If $g(e) \in O$, then $\Phi_{s(e)}(n) \in O$ for each $n$. Hence $W_e \subseteq O$ by Proposition 1.3.3.

Assume $g(e) \in O$ and $n \in W_e$. Then $n <_O \Phi_{s(e)}(m) + 2^n = \Phi_{s(e)}(m+1) <_O 3 \cdot 5^{s(e)} = g(e)$ where $m$ is the least number such that $\Phi_{r(e)}(m+1) = n$.

As a consequence of the proof of Lemma 1.3.5, we have

# Corollary 1.3.6. There is a recursive function $g$ such that for all $e$, if $W_e \subseteq O$ and $<_O$ on $W_e$ is linear, then $g(e) \in O$ and $(\forall n)(n \in W_e \rightarrow n <_O g(e))$.

Corollary 1.3.6 may be viewed as a $\Sigma^0_1$-boundedness principle, i.e. there is an effective way to find an upper bound for any r.e. subset of $O$. Lemma 1.3.5 will be significantly strengthened in Theorem 1.5.5.

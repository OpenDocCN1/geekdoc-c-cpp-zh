
- (1) \( T_1^i = \{\sigma \mid (\sigma, i) \in T^0\} \);
- (2) \( (\sigma, \tau) \in T_i \Rightarrow (\exists \tau' \supseteq \tau)(\tau' \in T_1^i \land \Delta_{c_0}^{T^0}(\sigma)[\tau'] = 1) \);
- (3) \( T_0^i = \sigma \mid (\exists \tau > \emptyset)(\langle \sigma, \tau \rangle \in T_i) \);
- (4) \( \psi_i : T_{i+1}^i \to T^0 \) is \( \emptyset^{(i+1)} \);
- (5) \( \psi_i \) is a homeomorphism from \( [T_{i+1}^i] \) to \( [T_0^i] \) such that \( (\forall x \in [T_i])(\psi_i(x^{-1}) \ominus \emptyset^{(i+1)} \equiv_T (x \ominus \emptyset^{(i)})') \) via (c2, c3) and
- (5a) \( \sigma < \tau \Rightarrow \psi_i(\sigma) \le \psi_i(\tau) \);
- (5b) If \( \sigma \nsubseteq T_0^i \), then \( |\psi_i(\sigma)| \ge |\sigma| \);
- (6) \( T_0^i \cap \emptyset^{<i} = 2^{<i} \), and
- (7) \( (\forall \sigma \in T_{i+1}^i \cap \emptyset^i)(\sigma \le \psi_i(\sigma)) \).

Assume that these sequences have been defined.

### Lemma 11.2.3.
For each \( x \in [T_0^0] \) and \( i < \omega \),
(i) \( x^0 \equiv x \ominus \emptyset^0 \) and
(ii) \( x^{(i)} \not\le_T \emptyset^{(i+1)} \).

**Proof.** By (4), (5) and an induction on \( i \), (i) is immediate. If \( x^{(i)} \ge_T \emptyset^{(i+1)} \), then \( x^{(i+1)} \equiv_T x \ominus \emptyset^{(i+1)} \le_T x^0 \), a contradiction. Hence (ii) holds.

### Lemma 11.2.4.
\( [T_0^0] \) is homeomorphic to \( 2^\omega \).

**Proof.** We define a bijection \( f \) between \( [T_0^0] \) and \( 2^\omega \) as follows: For each \( x \in [T_0^0] \), let
\( f(x) = \bigcup_{i < \omega} \psi_1^{i-1} \circ \psi_{i-1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid i \).

Let \( x \in [T_0^0] \). For \( i < \omega \), let \( x_i = \psi_1^{i-1} \circ \psi_{i-1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid i \). Then \( \psi_1^{i-1} \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \in [T_{i+1}^i] \cap [\sigma_i] \). Then \( \psi_{i+1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \in [T_{i+2}^i] \). Since \( \psi_{i+1}^1 \) is one-one, \( \psi_{i+1} \circ \psi_i^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \in [T_{i+1}^i] \cap [\sigma_i] \). By (7), \( \psi_{i+1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid (i+1) \ge \psi_{i+1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid i+1 = \sigma_{i+1} \). Hence \( \sigma_{i+1} \ge \sigma_i \). By (5b), \( f(x) \in 2^\omega \).

Let \( y \in 2^\omega \) and \( \tau_{n,i+1} = \psi_n \circ \cdots \circ \psi_{i+1}(y \mid n+t) \) for \( n, i < \omega \). By (5a), (6) and (7),
\( y \upharpoonright n \le \tau_{n,n} \le \tau_{n,n+1} \le \tau_{n,n+2} \cdots \).

Moreover, by (5b), for each \( i \), \( |\tau_{n,n+i}| \ge n+i \). Let \( X_n = \bigcup_{i < \omega} \tau_{n,n+i} \). Then \( X_n \in [T_0^0] \cap [y \mid n] \). Since \( \psi_n^{-1} \) is one-one, by (7) \( \psi_n^{-1} \circ \psi_{n+1}^{-1} \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^{-1}(x_0) \mid n = X_n \mid n = y \mid n \). By the definition of \( f \), we have \( f(x_0) = y \). So \( f \) is onto.

Now assume \( x_0, x_1 \in [T_0^0] \) such that \( f(x_0) = f(x_1) \) and for some \( n_0 \), and \( i \neq j \), \( x_0 \mid n_0 = x_1 \mid n_0 \), and \( x_0 \supset x_0 \mid n_0, x_1 \supset x_1 \mid n_0 \). By (5b) and (7), \( \psi_{n_0+1}^1 \circ \psi_{n_0}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x_0) \mid n_0 + 1 = \psi_{n_0+1}^1 \circ \psi_{n_0}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x_1) \mid n_0 + 1 \). Then by (5a) and (5b), \( x_0 \mid n_0 + 1 = x_1 \mid n_0 + 1 \), a contradiction.
Clearly both \( f \) and \( f^{-1} \) are continuous. Thus \( [T_0^0] \) is homeomorphic to \( 2^\omega \).

Note that \( T_0^0 = \{\sigma \mid \Phi_{e_0}^0(\sigma) = 1\} \) is a recursive tree. Hence the set \( A = [T_0^0] \) is an uncountable \( \Pi_1^0 \)-set with the desired property.

We turn now to defining the sequence of trees and functions as well as constants that satisfy (1)-(7). Given a tree \( T \), write \( T = \Phi_i^t \) if for any \( \sigma, \tau \in T \) if and only if \( \Phi_i^t(\sigma) = 1 \).

### **Lemma 11.2.5.** There is a recursive function \( t : \omega \to \omega \) such that for any \( x \in 2^\omega \) and tree \( T = \Phi_{e_i}^x \) in \( \omega^\omega \), \( \Phi_{i(x)}^0 \) is a tree \( \hat{T} \) in \( \omega^\omega \) and \( [\hat{T}] = \{z \oplus h_x \mid z \in [T]\} \), where \( h_x \) is an \( x \)-recursive function so that \( h_x(e, n) \) is either 0 or \( \min\{s + 1 \mid \Phi_{e(x)}^x(n)[s] \downarrow\} \).

**Proof.** Let

\(\hat{T} = \{\sigma \oplus \tau \mid (\forall e, n)((\langle e, n \rangle < |\tau| \land \tau(\langle e, n \rangle) > 0 \to \tau(\langle e, n \rangle) = \min\{s + 1 \le |\tau| \mid \Phi_{e(x)}^{t_k}(n)[s] \downarrow\}) \land (\exists v < 2^{|\tau|}(\forall e \le |v|)(v(e) = 1 \to \Phi_{e(\tau(\langle e, e \rangle))}^{t_k(\langle e, e \rangle)}(e)[\tau(\langle e, e \rangle)] \downarrow) \land \Phi_i^0(\sigma) = 1)\}.\)

Then \( \hat{T} \) is as required. Since \( \hat{T} \) is obtained uniformly, we have a recursive function \( t : \omega \to \omega \) such that \( \hat{T} = \Phi_{i(0)}^t \).

Now for \( i, j < \omega \) such that \( T_{i+1} = \Phi_{i+1}^{\psi(i+1)} \), we define a tree \( T_i \) such that \( T_i \) satisfies (1)-(7) upon substituting \( \{c_i\}_{i \le j} \) with parameters depending on \( T_{i+1} \). The construction will be uniform in \( i \) and \( j \). This means that there is a recursive function \( t \) such that \( T_j^0 = \Phi_{i(j)}^{\psi(j)} \). By the Recursion Theorem, there is a \( c_0 \) such that \( T_0^0 = \Phi_{c_0}^0 = \Phi_{i(0)}^{\psi(0)} \). Let \( T_i = \{(\sigma, \tau) \mid (\exists t' \ge t)(\langle i, t' \rangle \in T^{\omega \omega} \Phi_{c_i}^{t'}(\sigma)[t'] = 1)\} \).

The other parameters \( c_i \) for \( 1 \le i \le 3 \) are obtained in the same way. Thus we may assume that \( T_{i+1}^0 \) has properties (1)-(7). \( T_i^0 \) is defined from \( T_{i+1}^0 \).

### **Lemma 11.2.6.** Given a recursive tree \( T_0 \le \omega^\omega \), there is a recursive tree \( T_1 \) and an \( A_2^0 \)-homomorphism \( \psi : T_0 \to T_1 \) such that for any \( x \in [T_1] \), \( x' \equiv_T x \oplus 0' \).

**Proof.** The proof is a finite injury argument. Given a recursive tree \( T_0 \le \omega^\omega \), we define \( \psi \) and \( T_1 \) by a full approximation construction.

### Stage 0.
Let \( \psi_0(\emptyset) = \emptyset \) and \( T_{0,0} = \emptyset \). We declare that all nodes \( \sigma \in T_0 \) are fresh.

### Stage \( s + 1 \).
Suppose \( \psi_s : T_0 \to T_{1,s} \le 2^{<\omega} \) is partially defined. Search for the least \( t \le s \) with a fresh \( \sigma \in T_0 \) for which there is a \( \tau \in T_{1,s} \upharpoonright (\psi_s(\sigma)) \cap 2^{>s} \) so that \( \Phi_i^t(t)[\tau] \downarrow \). For each such \( \sigma \), define \( \psi_{s+1}(\sigma) = \tau' > \tau \) where \( |\tau'| = \tau' \cap 0^{t+1} \). For all \( v \)'s in \( T_0 \cap \text{Dom}(\psi_s) \) which are incompatible with such \( \sigma \)'s, set \( \psi_{s+1}(v) = \psi_s(v) \). Define \( \psi_{s+1}(\nu') = \psi_s(\nu') \) if \( v \in \text{Dom}(\psi_s) \) is incompatible with the \( \sigma \)'s and \( \forall \tau' \in T_0 \upharpoonright \text{Dom}(\psi_s) \). Declare that \( \sigma \) is not fresh for each such \( \sigma \) and \( \tau \) is fresh for any \( \tau \) extending such a \( \sigma \). Let \( T_{1,s+1} = \text{Range}(\psi_{s+1}) \).

This completes the construction at stage \( s + 1 \). Note that for each \( \sigma \), \( |\{s \mid \psi_s(\sigma) \neq \psi_{s+1}(\sigma)\}| \le |\sigma| \). Hence \( \psi(\sigma) = \lim_{s \to \infty} \psi_s(\sigma) \) exists for every \( \sigma \). Then \( \psi \) is \( A_2^0 \).

Let \( T_1 = \bigcup_{s < \omega} T_{1,s} \). By the construction \( \psi : [T_0^0] \to [T_1] \) is a homomorphism. Suppose \( x \in [T_1] \). Given \( t \), to decide if \( \Phi_i^t(t) \) converges, we \( \emptyset' \)-recursively search for a

\(\sigma \in T_1 \cap 2^{<\omega}\) and a stage \( s \ge t \) such that \( \psi_s(\sigma) = \psi(\sigma) < x \). Then \( \Phi_i^t(t) \) is convergent if and only if \( \Phi_i^{\psi(\sigma)}(t)[s] \). Thus \( x' \le_T x \oplus \emptyset' \).

Now if \( i < \omega \) and \( \sigma \in 2^{<\omega} \), let \( \hat{T}_{i+1,l,\alpha} \) be a \( \emptyset^{(i)} \)-recursive tree as in Lemma 11.2.5 by setting \( T \) as \( \{\tau \mid \sigma^\frown \tau \in T_{i+1}^0\} \) and \( x \) as \( \emptyset^{(i)} \). Realizing Lemma 11.2.6 to \( \emptyset^{(i)} \) by replacing \( T_0 \) with \( \hat{T}_{i+1,l,\alpha} \), we have a \( \emptyset^{(i)} \)-recursive homeomorphism \( \psi_i : \hat{T}_{i+1,l,\alpha} \to T_{l,\alpha} \) where \( T_{l,\alpha} \) is \( T_1 \) in Lemma 11.2.6. Define \( T_i^0 \) to be a \( \emptyset^{(i)} \)-recursive tree so that \( \tau \in T_i^0 \) if and only if either

- \( |\tau| \le 1 \), or

- \( \tau = (\tau \upharpoonright \gamma)^\frown \tau' \) for some \( \tau' \in T_{1, \upharpoonright \gamma} \).

Define \( \psi_i \le_T \theta^{(i+1)} : T_{i+1}^0 \to T_i^0 \) such that

- if \( \tau \in 2^{<\omega} \), then \( \psi_i(\tau) = \tau \);

- otherwise, let \( \tau = (\tau \upharpoonright \gamma)^\frown \tau' \). Define \( \psi_i(\tau) = \tau \upharpoonright \gamma \psi_i(\tau' \oplus (h_{00} \upharpoonright |\tau'|)) \).

Hence for any path \( x \in [T_i^0] \),

\( (x \oplus \theta^{(i)})' \equiv_T x \oplus \theta^{(i+1)} \equiv_T \psi_i^{-1}(x) \oplus \theta^{(i+1)} \).

Since the construction is uniform, it is straightforward to verify that (1)-(7) are satisfied. This completes the proof of Theorem 11.2.2.

## 11.2.2 The proof of Harrington's Theorem

We follow the presentation of Hjorth [50]. By Theorem 2.6.5, for any recursive ordinal \( \beta \ge \omega \), there is an \( n \in \omega \) such that \( [n] = \beta \) and the set \( \{m \mid m <_n n\} \) is recursive. We choose such an ordinal \( \beta \) and the corresponding \( n \).

For each \( m = 3 \cdot 5^k < \alpha_0 \), let \( \{i_{j,m}\}_{j<\omega} \) be a sequence of numbers such that

\( i_{0,m} = \Phi_k(m) \)

and

\( i_{j+1,m} = \Phi_k(m+j+1) \).

By the proof of Theorem 2.6.5, we may assume that \( m \le i_{j,m} < i_{j+1,m} \) for all \( j \). Clearly the set \( \{(i_{j,m}, m) \mid (\exists k)(m = 3 \cdot 5^k \land \alpha_0 < n \land j \in \omega)\} \) is recursive.

### Lemma 11.2.7.

- (i) For any \( m = 3 \cdot 5^k < \alpha_0 \) and \( j < \omega \), \( i_{j,m} < i_{j+1,m} \).

- (ii) For any \( l < \alpha_0 \), the set \( \{m \mid (\exists k)(i_{0,m} < l < m = 3 \cdot 5^k < \alpha_0)\} \) is finite;

- (iii) the set \( \{(m,l) \mid (\exists k)(i_{0,m} < l < m = 3 \cdot 5^k < \alpha_0)\} \) is recursive;

- (iv) if \( m = 3 \cdot 5^k < \alpha_0 \) and \( |m| = \lim_{n \to \infty} \alpha_n \) where \( \{\beta_i\} \) is an increasing sequence of limit ordinals, then for any \( i \) there is an \( m' = 3 \cdot 5^{k'} < \alpha_0 \) with \( |m'| > \beta_i \), such that for any \( m'' = 3 \cdot 5^{k''} < \alpha_0 \) with \( |m'| > |m''| \), we have \( |i_{0,m'}| > |m''| \).

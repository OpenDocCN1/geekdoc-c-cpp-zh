# Claim 1. If \(x \le_T y\) are such that \(x \in F^*\) and \(y \in \tilde{F}^*\), then \(O^x \le_h y\).

Proof. As \(y \notin F^*\), there are \(a < \omega_1^x\) and \(a \le \omega\) with \(a \in L_{a_1^x}[L_{a+1}(y)]\). Then \(a \notin L_{a+1}[x]\) and \(\omega_1^x \le a < \omega_1^y\). As \(x \le_T y\), \(O^x <_h y\).

By Claim 1, if \(x \le_T y\) are such that \(x \in F^*\) and \(y \in \tilde{F}^*\), then \(O^{O^x} \le_T O^y\).

+   (1) \((y)_0 = 0^{O^x} \land x \in F^* \text{ or } (y)_0 = 1^{O^{O^x}} \land (\exists v \le_T y)(y)_0 \neg P(x, v)\),
+   (2) \((y)_1 = O^x\),
+   (3) \(\Phi_e^x\) is partial \(\Rightarrow (y)_{e+2} = 0\),
+   (4) \(u = \Phi_e^x\) is total \(\Rightarrow\)
+   (i) \((y)_0(0) = 0 \land (\exists v \le_T (y)_1)\neg P(u, v) \land (y)_{e+2} = 1^{O^{0^{y(e_i)}}}\) where \(i\) is the least index so that \(O^{y(e_i)} = O^{0^{y(e_i)}}\), or
+   (ii) \((y)_0(0) = 1 \land (\exists v \le_T (y)_1)\neg P(u, v) \land (y)_{e+2} = 0^{O^{O^x}}\), or
+   (iii) \((\forall v \le_T (y)_1)P(u, v) \land (y)_{e+2} = 0^{O^{O^x}}\).

It is straightforward to verify that \(G\) is \(\Pi^1_1\).

# Claim 2. If \(x \in F^*\) then \(G(x) \equiv_T O^{O^{O^x}}\).

Proof. Clearly \(x \in F^* \Rightarrow O^{O^{O^x}} \le_T G(x)\) and \((G(x))_0 \oplus (G(x))_1 \le_T O^{O^{O^x}}\). Given \(e < \omega\), \(O^{O^{O^x}}\) can uniformly decide whether \(\Phi_e^x\) is total. Suppose that \(\Phi_e^x\) is total. To calculate \((G(x))_{e+2}(n)\) we need to check clauses (4 ii, 4 iii) above. Now the predicate \((\forall v \le_T (y)_1)P(u, v)\) is \(\Delta_1^1(O^x)\), and hence recursive in \(O^{O^x}\). Once this predicate is decided, \(O^{O^{O^x}}\) will be able to complete the calculation with the use of two recursive functions \(f, g\) such that \(u = \Phi_e^x \Rightarrow O^{O^x} = \Phi_{f(e)}^{O^{O^{O^x}}}\) and \(u = \Phi_e^w \Rightarrow O^{O^{O^w}} = \Phi_{g(e)}^{O^{O^{O^x}}}\).

# Claim 3. If \(x \in \tilde{F}^*\) then \(G(x) \equiv_T O^{O^x}\).

Proof. This is similar to the proof of Claim 2, except for the final step in the calculation of \(((G(x))_{e+2}(n))\). Now \(O^{O^x}\) can decide whether (4 i) or (4 iii) holds, as in the above claim. If (4 iii) holds, then the calculation is again similar to the above. Suppose that (4 i) holds. Then \(u \in F^*\). By Claim 1, \(O^x \le_h x\) and thus \(O^{O^x} \le_T O^x = (G(x))_1\). Now the index \(i\) prescribed in (4) may be found uniformly via a \(\Pi_1^1(O^x)\) procedure. Hence \(O^{O^x}\) computes \((G(x))_{e+2}(n)\) uniformly.

For \( i \le 1 \), let \( \sigma_{0,k+1}^{n_0[\alpha]+i+1}[a] \) be the next local splitting node in \( T_0[\alpha] \) over \( (\sigma_{0,k}[a], \tau_{0,k}[a]) \) extending \( (\sigma_{0,k+1}^{n_0[\alpha]+i+1}[a])^\frown 1 \).

Suppose that \( t_{-1} \) is defined where \( t \ge 1 \). Search for the least stage \( \alpha > \alpha_{t-1} \) so that \( x_0 \oplus x_1 \) consistently computes \( f_\emptyset \) up to \( t \) at \( \alpha \). Let \( \alpha_t = \alpha \) and \( \hat{g}_{\emptyset,t}(j) = f_\emptyset^\alpha(j)[a_t] \) for each \( j \le t \).

This completes the construction at step \( t \).

We verify that the decoding construction yields an algorithm to hyperarithmetically compute \( z \) from \( x_0 \oplus x_1 \).

Let \( \gamma = \bigcup_{t \in \omega} \alpha_t \).

Clearly \( \gamma \le \omega_1^{CK} \) and is a limit ordinal. Furthermore \( \gamma < \omega_1^{x_0 \oplus x_1} \). We prove that \( \gamma = \omega_1^{CK} \).

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that \( \gamma < \omega_1^{CK} \). Using the fact that neither \( x_0 \) nor \( x_1 \) is hyperarithmetic, we will show that for \( i \le 1 \), each sequence of parameters \( n_i^1[a_t] \), \( \sigma_{i,t}[a_t] \) and \( \tau_{i,t}[a_t] \) introduced in the decoding procedure above eventually stabilizes as \( t \to \omega \). Suppose this is false. Then there is a least \( j_0 \) and a corresponding least \( t_0 \) such that \( f_\emptyset \) is consistently computed up to \( j_0 - 1 \), but not \( j_0 \), at \( a_t \) for all \( t \ge t_0 \). We argue that such a situation does not occur.

The proof proceeds by induction in the order of the introduction of the parameters at stage \( a_{t_0} \). For convenience, we only show that \( \sigma_{0,j_0}^{n_0[\alpha]+1}[a_1] \) reaches a limit after some \( t \) (essentially the same argument applies to show that the other sequences of parameters also stabilise). This means that \( \hat{g}_{\emptyset,t}(j_0) \neq \hat{g}_{\emptyset,t+1}(j_0) \) for at most finitely many \( t \)’s.

Assume that \( \sigma_{0,j_0}^{n_0[\alpha]}[a_1] \) does not change from stage \( a_{t_0} \) onwards (\( \sigma_{0,j_0}^{n_0[\alpha]}[a_1] \) is as defined above before the decoding of \( f_\emptyset \upharpoonright k \) in (2)). If \( \sigma_{0,j_0}^{n_0[\alpha]+1}[a_{t+1}] \) changes infinitely often, then \( \hat{g}_{\emptyset,t}(j_0) \neq \hat{g}_{\emptyset,t+1}(j_0) \) for infinitely many \( t \)’s. Then by the decoding construction, we have the following claim.

### Claim 1. \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t+1}(j_0) \ge \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0)\) for any \(t > t_0 + 1\) and hence \(\lim_{t \to \omega} \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0) = +\infty\).

Proof. If we assign a new value \(k\) to \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0)\) at some stage \(\alpha_{t+1} > \alpha_t\), it must be the case that \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(0)^\frown \cdots \frown \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0 - 1)^\frown k \in T_2[\alpha_t]\). However, \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(0)^\frown \cdots \frown \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0 - 1) \in T_2[\alpha_t]\) by the assumption on \(j_0\), so that \(k > \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t+1}(j_0)\).

Claim 1 implies that at infinitely many \(t\)'s, left turns were selected at locally splitting nodes in \(T_0[\alpha_t]\) during the decoding phase for the purpose of approximating \(f_\emptyset(j_0)\).

Let \(\sigma = \sigma_{0, j_0}^{n_0[\alpha_t]}[\alpha_t]\) and \(\tau = \tau_{0, j_0}[\alpha_t]\). Note that \(|\sigma| > |\tau|\). Define

\(X = \{x \succ \bar{\sigma} \mid \forall n \exists \beta < \gamma \exists \beta' \exists \tau' (\beta \le \beta' < \gamma \land |\tau'| = n \land (x \upharpoonright n, \tau') \in T_0[\beta'])\}\).

If we let \(p(\tau) = \{ \sigma \mid \exists \tau' (\sigma, \tau') \in T \}\) is a "local projection" of a tree \(T \subseteq \tau^{<\omega} \times \omega^{<\omega}\), then it is not difficult to see that \(\cap_{\beta < \gamma} p(T_0[\sigma, \tau][\beta])\) is a tree (Note: \(T_0[\sigma, \tau][\beta]\) denotes the analog of \(T_0[\sigma, \tau]\) for \(T_0[\beta]\)). Moreover,

\(X = \bigcap_{\beta < \gamma} p(T_0[\sigma, \tau][\beta])\).

In other words, \(X\) consists of all the reals \(x\) "potentially" with an accompanying witness \(f\) so that \((x, f)\) is an infinite path in \(T_0[\sigma, \tau][\gamma]\). Note that since \(\gamma < \omega_1^{CK}\), \(X\) is a \(\Delta_1^1\) closed subset of \(2^\omega\).

### Claim 2. \(x_0\) is the leftmost path in \(X\).

Proof. Suppose not. Then there exist \(y_0\) and \(\sigma\) such that \(\tilde{\sigma} < \sigma\), \(y_0 \succ \sigma^\frown \langle 0 \rangle\), \(x_0 \succ \sigma^\frown \langle 1 \rangle\), and \(\forall \beta < \gamma \exists \beta' \exists \tau' (\beta \le \beta' < \gamma \land |\tau'| = |\sigma| + 1 \land (y_0 \upharpoonright |\sigma| + 1, \tau') \in T_0[\beta'])\).

Now by Claim 1 let \(t_1 > t_0\) be such that \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0) > |\sigma| + 1\) for all \(t \ge t_1\). Then there are \(\tau, \tau'\) of length \(|\sigma|\) so that \((((x_0 \upharpoonright |\sigma| + 1, \tau), (y_0 \upharpoonright |\sigma| + 1, \tau')) \in T_0[\alpha_{t+1}]\). However, since \(y_0\) is to the left of \(x_0\), the value of \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t+1}(j_0)\) coded by \(x_0\) is at most \(|\sigma| + 1\). Thus \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t+1}(j_0) \le |\sigma| + 1\), a contradiction.

Claim 2 implies that \(x_0\) is hyperarithmetic which is a contradiction. Hence there is a \(t^0\) such that for any \(t \ge t^0\), \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j_0) = \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t+1}(j_0)\) and so \(\sigma_{0, j_0}^{n_0[\alpha_{t+1}]}[\alpha_t]\) is a constant for all \(t > t^0\).

We leave it to the reader to verify the stabilization of the other sequences of parameters.

It follows that for each \(j\), there is a \(t_j\) such that \(\tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j) = f_\emptyset^j(\alpha_t)\) for \(t \ge t_j\). Define \(\tilde{g}(j) = \lim_{t \to \omega} \tilde{g}_{\emptyset, t}(j)\) for \(j \in \omega\). Then \(\tilde{g}(j) = \lim_{t \to \omega} f_\emptyset^j(\alpha_t)[t]\) and \(\tilde{g} \in L_{\gamma+1}\). Now \(\tilde{g}\) is the leftmost path in \(T_2[\gamma]\) and so in \(T_2\) as well. Thus \(\tilde{g} = f_\emptyset \in L_{\gamma+1}\). This contradicts the assumption that \(\gamma < \omega_1^{CK}\). Hence \(\gamma = \omega_1^{CK}\) and so \(f_\emptyset = x_0 \le_h x_1\).

Using this, one may decode by the entire construction in \(T_1[\omega_1^{CK}]\) and conclude that \(z \le_h x_0 \oplus x_1\), completing the proof of the Theorem.

## 13.3 Hyperarithmetic computation

In this section, we discuss some notions concerning effective bounding in the context of hyperarithmetic theory. The results will be applied to study higher randomness.

### 13.3.1 Hyperarithmetic domination

Definition 13.3.1. A real \(x\) is \(\Delta^1_1\)-dominated if for any function \(f : \omega \to \omega\) with \(f \le_T x\), there is a hyperarithmetic \(g\) such that \((\forall n)(g(n) > f(n))\) (written as \(g > f\)).

The first item to note is that there is an abundance of \(\Delta^1_1\)-dominated reals.

Theorem 13.3.2 (Chong, Nies and Yu [12]). \(\mu(\{x \mid x \text{ is } \Delta^1_1\text{-dominated}\}) = 1\).

Proof. We prove that for any random number \(p\), \(\mu(\{x \mid x \text{ is } \Delta^1_1\text{-dominated}\}) \ge p\).

This is achieved with the use of a fusion argument.

First we show that for any \(e\), rational \(r\), notation \(n \in \mathcal{O}\) and \(\Delta^1_1\)-set \(A\) where \(p + r < \mu(A)\), there is a hyperarithmetic function \(f\) such that \(\mu(\{x \mid x \in A \wedge \Phi_e^{B_k} \text{ is total} \to \Phi_e^{B_k} < f\}) > p + \frac{r}{2}\).

Note that the set \(\{(x, i, m) \mid \Phi_e^{B_k}(i) \downarrow \to \Phi_e^{B_k}(i) < m\}\) is \(\Delta^1_1\). Since \(A\) is \(\Delta^1_1\), Proposition 13.1.6 implies that the set \(C = \{(i, m, k) \in \omega^3 \mid \mu(\{x \mid x \in A \wedge (\Phi_e^{B_k}(i) \downarrow \to \Phi_e^{B_k}(i) < m)\}) > \mu(A) - \frac{r}{2^{k+2}}\}\) is \(\Delta^1_1\). Note that for each \(k\), there is an \(m\) such that \((k, m, k) \in C\). Hence there is a \(\Delta^1_1\) total function \(f\) such that for all \(k\), \((k, f(k), k) \in C\). Define \(B_k = \{x \mid x \in A \wedge (\Phi_e^{B_k}(k) \downarrow \to \Phi_e^{B_k}(k) < f(k))\}\).

Then the set \(\{(k, x) \mid x \in B_k\}\) is \(\Delta^1_1\). Moreover, for every \(k\), \(B_k \subseteq A\) and \(\mu(B_k) > \mu(A) - \frac{r}{2^{k+2}}\). Hence \(B = \cap_k B_k\) is \(\Delta^1_1\) and \(\mu(B) \ge \mu(A) - \sum_{k \ge 0} \mu(A - B_k) \ge p + r - \sum_{k \ge 0} \frac{r}{2^{k+2}} = p + \frac{r}{2}\).

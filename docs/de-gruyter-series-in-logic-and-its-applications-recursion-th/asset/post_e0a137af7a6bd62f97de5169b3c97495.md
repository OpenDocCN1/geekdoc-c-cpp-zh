Hence every real \(\Sigma_2\)-definable in (HF, \(\epsilon\)) is \(\Sigma_1\)-definable in (HF, \(\epsilon\), A) and so r.e. in x. Thus the \(\Sigma_1\)-master codes are precisely the reals that are r.e. and complete (hence Turing equivalent to \(\emptyset'\)). In general, a real which is a \(\Sigma_n\)-master code in \(\langle J_1, \epsilon \rangle\) is \(\Sigma_n\)-complete and hence Turing equivalent to \(\emptyset^{(n)}\). We may therefore consider master codes to be Turing jumps in the set-theoretic sense. Second, suppose that \(\rho_2^\alpha = 1\). A \(\Sigma_1\)-master code for \(\langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle\) is a set \(A \in HF\) such that every \(\Sigma_2(J_\alpha)\)-definable real is \(\Sigma_1\) in (HF, \(\epsilon\), A). Again by Proposition 3.2.6 and Theorem 3.2.5, every \(\Sigma_2(J_\alpha)\)-definable real is r.e. in some real x which is \(\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)\)-definable. In particular, every real \(x \in J_\alpha\) is recursive in x. Thus if \(\alpha\) is much larger than \(\rho_2^\alpha\), the real x must be remarkably strong in computational power. Historically, the notion of a master code for \(\rho = 1\) was first introduced by Boolos and Putnam [8] when they studied the Turing degrees of reals in the constructible universe. We now show that \(\Sigma_1\)-master codes exist for any admissible ordinal \(\alpha\).

### Theorem 3.4.10. Let \(\alpha > 0\). There is a \(\Sigma_1\)-master code for \(\langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle\), i.e. a \(\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)\)-definable \(A \subseteq J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}\) such that for any \(B \in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}\), if \(B\) is \(\Sigma_{m+1}(J_\alpha)\)-definable, then it is \(\Sigma_m\)-definable in \(\langle J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}, \epsilon, A \rangle\).

Proof. We only prove the case m = 1 for simplicity. Suppose that \(f\) is \(\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)\)-definable with parameter p mapping a subset of \(\rho_2^\alpha\) onto \(\alpha\). Fix a \(\Delta_1\)-enumeration \(\langle \varphi_i(x, p) \rangle_{i < \omega}\) of \(\Sigma_1\)-formulas with parameter p. Let
\[
A = \{\langle i, x \rangle \mid i < \omega \land x \in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha} \land \langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle \models \varphi_i(x, p)\}.
\]

It is obvious that A is \(\Sigma_1\)-definable over \(\langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle\). Now suppose that \(B \in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}\) is \(\Sigma_2\)-definable over \(\langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle\). Then there is a \(\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)\)-formula \(\varphi(u, v, w)\) such that
\[
x \in B \Leftrightarrow \langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle \models (\exists y) \neg \varphi(x, u, v, s),
\]

where s is a parameter in \(J_\alpha\). Since f is onto, there is an \(s_0 \in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}\) such that \(f(s_0) = s\). Then
\[
x \in B \Leftrightarrow (\exists y \in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha})(\langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle \models \neg \varphi(x, f(y), f(s_0))).
\]

Since f is \(\Sigma_1(J_\alpha)\)-definable with parameter p, there is an index i such that for x, y \(\in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}\),
\[
\langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle \models \neg \varphi(x, y, s_0, p) \Leftrightarrow \langle J_\alpha, \epsilon \rangle \models \neg \varphi(x, f(y), f(s_0)).
\]

Thus
\[
x \in B \Leftrightarrow (\exists y \in J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha})((i, x, y, s_0) \notin A \Leftrightarrow (\exists y)((i, x, y, s_0)) \notin A).
\]
Hence B is \(\Sigma_1\)-definable over \(\langle J_{\rho_\alpha^\alpha}, \epsilon, A \rangle\). \(\square\)

As an example, \(0'\) is a \(\Sigma_1(J_1)\)-master code since it satisfies the property described in Theorem 3.4.10. Jensen [55] proved the following general result:

### Theorem 3.4.11. For any ordinal \(\alpha > 0\) and \(n > 0\), there is a \(\Sigma_n(J_\alpha)\)-master code \(A \subset J_{\rho^p_\alpha}\). Moreover, \(\rho^p_\alpha\) is the least ordinal \(\gamma\) for which there is a \(\Sigma_n(J_\alpha) A \subseteq \omega \gamma\) not in \(J_\alpha\).

A \(\Sigma_n\)-master code corresponds to \(0^{(\alpha n)}\) in recursion theory. The equality \(0^{(\alpha n+m)} = (0^{(\alpha n)})^{(m)}\) has a natural analog in fine structure theory. See [55] for details.

## 3.5 \(\omega\)-models

### Definition 3.5.1. A structure \(\mathfrak{M}\) is an \(\omega\)-model if \(\omega^\mathfrak{M} = \omega\).

\(\omega\)-models are widely used in recursion theory and descriptive set theory. A major point of interest in an \(\omega\)-model is its standard part.

### Definition 3.5.2. Let \(\mathfrak{M} = \langle M, E \rangle\) be a structure and \(M_1 \subseteq M\). Then \(M_1\) is \(E\)-closed if for every \(x \in M_1\), \(yEx\) and \(y \in M\) implies \(y \in M_1\). The standard part of \(\mathfrak{M}\) is the maximal \(E\)-closed wellfounded subset of \(M\).

Obviously if \(M_1 \subseteq M\) is \(E\)-closed, then for any \(\Sigma_0\)-formula \(\varphi(v)\) and \(x \in M_1\), \(\langle M, E \rangle \models \varphi(x)\) if and only if \(\langle M_1, E \rangle \models \varphi(x)\).

### Lemma 3.5.3. Suppose that \(\mathfrak{M} \models \text{KP}\). Then \(x\) belongs to the standard part of \(M\) if and only if \(\text{rk}(x)\), the rank of \(x\), belongs to the standard part of \(\mathfrak{M}\).

**Proof.** Since \(\mathfrak{M} \models \text{KP}\), by the \(\Sigma\)-induction Theorem (3.1.8) the rank function rk is \(\Delta_1\)-definable.

If \(x\) belongs to the standard part of \(M\), then by induction on the rank of \(x\), it is straightforward to verify that rk\((x)\) also belongs to the standard part.

If \(x\) does not belong to the standard part of \(M\), then there is a descending sequence \(\langle x_n \rangle_{n < \omega}\) in \(M\) so that \(x_{n+1} E x_n\) for every \(n\) and \(x_0 \text{rk}(x_n) \text{Erk}(x_n)\) for every \(n\) and \(\text{rk}(x_0) \text{Erk}(x)\) does not belong to the standard part of \(M\) either.

### Lemma 3.5.4. Suppose that \(\mathfrak{M} \models \text{KP}\) and \(M_1 \subseteq M\) is the standard part of \(M\). Then \(\langle M_1, E \rangle \models \text{KP}\).

**Proof.** Since \(M_1 \subseteq M\) is \(E\)-closed, the extensionality axiom holds in \(\mathfrak{M}_1 = \langle M_1, E \rangle\). \(\mathfrak{M}_1\) satisfies the foundation axiom since \(M_1\) is the standard part of \(M\). Similarly it is straightforward to verify that pairing, union and the \(\Delta_0\)-comprehension axioms hold in \(\mathcal{M}_1\). We prove \(\mathcal{M}_1 \models \Delta_0\)-collection.

Suppose that \(\varphi(u, v)\) is a \(\Delta_0\)-formula and \(a \in M_1\) such that

\(\langle M_1, E \rangle \models (\forall x Ea)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y).\)

Since \(\varphi(u, v)\) is \(\Delta_0\) and \(M_1\) is an E-closed subset of M, we have

\(\langle M, E \rangle \models (\forall x Ea)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y).\)

Now \(\langle M, E \rangle \models \text{KP}\) implies that there is a \(b \in M\) such that

\(\langle M, E \rangle \models (\forall x Ea)(\exists y E b)\varphi(x, y).\)

By Lemma 3.5.3, there is a \(b_0\) such that \(\text{rk}(b_0) = a_0\) is the least ordinal in \(\mathcal{M}\) to satisfy the collection axiom for \(\varphi\). If \(a_0 \in M_1\), then by Lemma 3.5.3, \(b_0 \in M_1\) and we are done. Otherwise, \(a_0 \in M \setminus M_1\). But then there is a nonstandard \(a_1 E a_0\) such that for every \(x E a\), there is a \(y E b_0\) in \(M_1\) with \(\text{rk}(y) E a_1\) and \(\langle M, E \rangle \models \varphi(x, y)\). Note that \(\varphi(x, y) \land \text{rk}(y) E a_1\) is a \(\Sigma\)-formula and

\(\langle M, E \rangle \models (\forall x Ea)(\exists y)(\varphi(x, y) \land \text{rk}(y) E a_1).\)

By the \(\Sigma\)-collection Theorem (3.1.6), there is a \(b_1 \in M\) such that

\(\langle M, E \rangle \models (\forall x Ea)(\exists y E b_1)\varphi(x, y) \land \text{rk}(y) E a_1).\)

Define

\(c = [y \mid y E b_1 \land \langle M, E \rangle \models \text{rk}(y) E a_1 \land \exists x E a (\varphi(x, y))].\)

By Theorem 3.1.6, \(c \in M\).

Then \(\text{rk}(c) E a_0\) and \(a_0 = \text{rk}(b_0)\). Since \(a_0\) is the least ordinal in \(\mathcal{M}\) to satisfy the collection axiom for \(\varphi\), we have a contradiction. \(\square\)

### Proposition 3.5.5. Let \(\mathcal{M} \models \text{KP}\) be an \(\omega\)-model. Then \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}} \in M\).

Proof. Let \(M_1 \le M\) be the standard part of M. By Lemma 3.5.4, \(\langle M_1, E \rangle \models \text{KP}\). Hence \(\langle M_1, E \rangle\) is also an \(\omega\)-model of KP. Since \(\omega_1^u(u) \in M_1\), by \(\Delta_0\)-separation every recursive set belongs to \(\langle M_1, E \rangle\). By \(\Sigma\)-induction, \(\omega_1^{CK} \in M_1\). Let \(\pi : \langle M_1, E \rangle \to \langle N, \in \rangle\) be the collapsing map. Then \(\langle N, \in \rangle\) is admissible so that \(\omega_1^{CK} \le N\). By Theorem 3.3.4, \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}} \in N\). We prove by induction on \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\) that \(\pi^{-1} L_{\omega_1^{CK}}\) is the identity map.

Note that by Proposition 3.3.3 there exists a \(\Delta_1\)-formula \(\varphi(u, v)\) such that for any ordinal \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\), \(\langle N, \in \rangle \models \varphi(\beta, x)\) if and only if \(x = J_\beta\). Moreover,

\(\langle N, \in \rangle \models \varphi(\beta, J_\beta) \land (\forall x E J_\beta)(\exists y \in \beta)(\forall y)(x \in y \leftrightarrow \varphi(y, y)).\)

We prove by induction that \(\pi^{-1}(J_\gamma) = J_\gamma\) for every \(\gamma < \omega_1^{CK}\).

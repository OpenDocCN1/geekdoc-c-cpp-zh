+   (1) \( T_1^i = \{\sigma \mid (\sigma, i) \in T^0\} \);
+   (2) \( (\sigma, \tau) \in T_i \Rightarrow (\exists \tau' \supseteq \tau)(\tau' \in T_1^i \land \Delta_{c_0}^{T^0}(\sigma)[\tau'] = 1) \);
+   (3) \( T_0^i = \sigma \mid (\exists \tau > \emptyset)(\langle \sigma, \tau \rangle \in T_i) \);
+   (4) \( \psi_i : T_{i+1}^i \to T^0 \) is \( \emptyset^{(i+1)} \);
+   (5) \( \psi_i \) is a homeomorphism from \( [T_{i+1}^i] \) to \( [T_0^i] \) such that
+   \( (\forall x \in [T_i])(\psi_i(x^{-1}) \ominus \emptyset^{(i+1)} \equiv_T (x \ominus \emptyset^{(i)})') \)
+   via (c2, c3) and
+   (5a) \( \sigma < \tau \Rightarrow \psi_i(\sigma) \le \psi_i(\tau) \);
+   (5b) If \( \sigma \nsubseteq T_0^i \), then \( |\psi_i(\sigma)| \ge |\sigma| \);
+   (6) \( T_0^i \cap \emptyset^{<i} = 2^{<i} \), and
+   (7) \( (\forall \sigma \in T_{i+1}^i \cap \emptyset^i)(\sigma \le \psi_i(\sigma)) \).

Assume that these sequences have been defined.

**Lemma 11.2.3.** For each \( x \in [T_0^0] \) and \( i < \omega \),
(i) \( x^0 \equiv x \ominus \emptyset^0 \) and
(ii) \( x^{(i)} \not\le_T \emptyset^{(i+1)} \).

**Proof.** By (4), (5) and an induction on \( i \), (i) is immediate. If \( x^{(i)} \ge_T \emptyset^{(i+1)} \), then \( x^{(i+1)} \equiv_T x \ominus \emptyset^{(i+1)} \le_T x^0 \), a contradiction. Hence (ii) holds.

**Lemma 11.2.4.** \( [T_0^0] \) is homeomorphic to \( 2^\omega \).

**Proof.** We define a bijection \( f \) between \( [T_0^0] \) and \( 2^\omega \) as follows: For each \( x \in [T_0^0] \), let
\( f(x) = \bigcup_{i < \omega} \psi_1^{i-1} \circ \psi_{i-1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid i \).

Let \( x \in [T_0^0] \). For \( i < \omega \), let \( x_i = \psi_1^{i-1} \circ \psi_{i-1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid i \). Then \( \psi_1^{i-1} \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \in [T_{i+1}^i] \cap [\sigma_i] \). Then \( \psi_{i+1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \in [T_{i+2}^i] \). Since \( \psi_{i+1}^1 \) is one-one, \( \psi_{i+1} \circ \psi_i^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \in [T_{i+1}^i] \cap [\sigma_i] \). By (7), \( \psi_{i+1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid (i+1) \ge \psi_{i+1}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x) \mid i+1 = \sigma_{i+1} \). Hence \( \sigma_{i+1} \ge \sigma_i \). By (5b), \( f(x) \in 2^\omega \).

Let \( y \in 2^\omega \) and \( \tau_{n,i+1} = \psi_n \circ \cdots \circ \psi_{i+1}(y \mid n+t) \) for \( n, i < \omega \). By (5a), (6) and (7),
\( y \nmid n \le \tau_{n,n} \le \tau_{n,n+1} \le \tau_{n,n+2} \cdots \).

Moreover, by (5b), for each \( i \), \( |\tau_{n,n+i}| \ge n+i \). Let \( X_n = \bigcup_{i < \omega} \tau_{n,n+i} \). Then \( X_n \in [T_0^0] \cap [y \mid n] \). Since \( \psi_n^{-1} \) is one-one, by (7) \( \psi_n^{-1} \circ \psi_{n+1}^{-1} \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^{-1}(x_0) \mid n = X_n \mid n = y \mid n \). By the definition of \( f \), we have \( f(x_0) = y \). So \( f \) is onto.

Now assume \( x_0, x_1 \in [T_0^0] \) such that \( f(x_0) = f(x_1) \) and some \( n_0 \), and \( i \neq j \), \( x_0 \mid n_0 = x_1 \mid n_0 \), and \( x_0 > x_0 \mid n_0, x_1 > x_1 \mid n_0 \). By (5b) and (7), \( \psi_{n_0+1}^1 \circ \psi_{n_0}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x_0) \mid n_0 + 1 = \psi_{n_0+1}^1 \circ \psi_{n_0}^1 \circ \cdots \circ \psi_0^1(x_1) \mid n_0 + 1 \). Then by (5a) and (5b), \( x_0 \mid n_0 + 1 = x_1 \mid n_0 + 1 \), a contradiction.
Clearly both \( f \) and \( f^{-1} \) are continuous. Thus \( [T_0] \) is homeomorphic to \( 2^\omega \).

Proof. (i) Let $A = \{r \mid r \text{ codes a well-ordering of } \omega \land x \in L_{|r|}\}$, where $|r|$ denotes the order type of $r$ as a well-ordering. Then $A$ is $\Delta^1_2(x)$. Let $r \in A$. Since $x$ is not $\Delta^1_2$ we must have $|r| > \delta^1_2$. Hence $\delta^1_2 > \delta^1_2$.

(ii) If $V = L$, then by Proposition 4.4.2, $\le_L$ is a $\Delta^1_2$ well-ordering of the reals. Suppose that $x \le_L y$. Then by the proof of (i), there is a $\Delta^1_2(y)$-real $r$ coding a well-ordering of $\omega$ such that $y \in L_{|r|}$ and so $x \in L_{|r|}$. Then $x \in L_{\omega_1^x}$ and hence $x \le_H y \le_A y$. Thus $x \le_A y$. So $\le_A$ is a well-ordering of $\Delta^1_2$-degrees and its order type is $\omega_1$. If $x <_A y$, then by the same argument as that in (i) but relativized to $y$, we have $\delta^{1,x}_2 < \delta^{1,y}_2$.

Proposition 4.4.6 indicates that while $L$ is an appropriate ground model for hyperarithmetic theory, it is less so for a $\Delta^1_2$-theory as the well-ordering of the $\le_A$ relation makes the structure of the corresponding degrees relatively simple and hence less interesting.

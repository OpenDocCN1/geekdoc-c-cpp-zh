Proof. It is immediate that \( R_{\beta_1} \le_T R_{\alpha_1} \). Let \( f \) be an \( R_{\alpha_1} \)-recursive function which is an isomorphism between \( R_{\beta_1} \) and an initial segment of \( R_{\alpha_1} \). Then for any \( n, j \), \( 2^n \cdot 3^j \in \bigoplus_{R_{\beta_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1} \) if and only if \( 2^{f(n)} \cdot 3^j \in \bigoplus_{R_{\alpha_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \alpha_1} \). Hence \(\bigoplus_{R_{\beta_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1} \le_T \bigoplus_{R_{\alpha_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \alpha_1} \).

The following lemma is used in the construction of a cofinal chain.

Lemma 9.3.12. For any real x and \(\alpha \in E_1\), if \( x \in L[G_\alpha] \) then there is a \(\beta \ge \alpha\) in \( E_1 \) such that \( x \le_T (\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta})^\beta \).

Proof. Note that for any \(\alpha < \omega_1\), \( L[G_{\text{c}\alpha}] = L[(\bigoplus_{R_\alpha} (G_y)_{\gamma < \alpha})] \) since \( R_\alpha \in L \). Suppose that \( x \in L[G_\alpha] \). There is a \(\beta_1 \ge \alpha\) in \( E \) such that \( x \in L_{\beta_1} [(\bigoplus_{R_\alpha} (G_y)_{\gamma < \alpha})] \). By Lemma 9.3.11, \(\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \alpha} \le_T \bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1} \). Thus \( x \in L_{\beta_1} [(\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1})] \). Since \(\beta_1 < \omega_1 \iff \bigoplus_{R_{\beta_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1} \), by relating Theorem 3.7.1 to \(\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1} \) we have \( x \) to be \( A^1 \) in \(\bigoplus_{R_{\beta_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1} \). Hence there is a \(\beta_2 \ge \beta_1\) below \(\omega_1^{\bigoplus_{R_{\beta_1}} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1}}\) such that \( x \le_T (\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1})^{(\beta_2)} \). By Lemma 9.3.11 again, \( x \le_T (\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta_1})^{(\beta_1)} \). Then \(\beta_2\) is the required \(\beta\).

In \( L[G] \), if \(\alpha \in E_1\) let \( x_\alpha^G = (\bigoplus_{R_\alpha} (G_y)_{\gamma < \alpha})^{(\alpha)} \), and let \( r_\alpha \) be the canonical \(\text{P}_{\aleph_1}\)-name of \( x_\alpha^G \). Let

\( A = \{z \mid (\exists \alpha \in E_1) (z \le_T r_\alpha^G) \} \).

Obviously \( A \) is a chain of Turing degrees in \( L[G] \). Set

\( N = (\text{HOD}(\text{TC}([A])))^{L[G]} \).

where \(\text{TC}([A]) \in L[G] \) is the transitive closure of \( [A] \).

Lemma 9.3.13. For any \(\alpha < \omega_1\), (i) \(\text{P}_{\text{c}\alpha}\) preserves cardinals; (ii) \( G_{\text{c}\alpha} \) is \(\text{P}_{\text{c}\alpha}\)-generic over \( L \); (iii) \( A \in \mathbb{N} \) is a cofinal chain of Turing degrees of order type \(\omega_1 \); (iv) \( (2^\omega)^{L[G]} = (2^\omega)^{\mathbb{N}} \).

Proof. (i) follows from Theorem 6.11.1 and (ii) from Theorem 6.1.9, while (iv) is an immediate consequence of (iii). We prove (iii). Given \( x \in L[G] \), there is an ordinal \(\alpha < \omega_1 \) such that \( x \in L[G_{\text{c}\alpha}] \). Then there is a countable ordinal \(\alpha_1 \ge \alpha \) such that \( x \in L_{\alpha_1} [G_{\text{c}\alpha}] \). By Lemma 9.3.12, for any \(\beta \ge \alpha_1\) in \( E_1 \), \( x \le_T (\bigoplus_{R_\beta} (G_y)_{\gamma < \beta})^{(\beta)} \). Thus \( x \le_T x_\beta^G \).

Hence \(\mathbb{N}\) is a cardinal preserving extension of \( L \) satisfying \(\text{ZF}^+\)-"There exists a cofinal chain of Turing degrees". If we take \(\mathbb{N}' = \text{HOD}((G_\alpha \mid \alpha < \omega_1))\), then in \(\mathbb{N}'\) there is no well-ordering of \( 2^\omega \) by the proof of Theorem 6.2.15. However, to apply the argument for \(\mathbb{N}\), additional precaution is required to preserve the name of \( A \). For this purpose, we work with a subset of permutations of \(\omega_1\).

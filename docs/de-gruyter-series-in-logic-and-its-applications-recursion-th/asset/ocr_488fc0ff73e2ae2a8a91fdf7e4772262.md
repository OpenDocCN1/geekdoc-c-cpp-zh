Using \( q^{-1} \circ g_1 \), starting with \( \sigma_0 = \emptyset \), hyperarithmetically in x construct a sequence \( \sigma_0 < \sigma_1 < \cdots \) of strings such that for each n,
- \( P_{g(n)} \neq \emptyset \) and
- \( \sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n \cup \{q^{-1}(g_1(\sigma_n))\} \).

Hence \( \cap_{n<\omega} P_{g(n)} \neq \emptyset \) and for any \( z \in \cap_{n<\omega} P_{g(n)} \), the real \( y = \bigcup_{n<\omega} \sigma_n \) is a subset of z.

# **Corollary 14.6.5.** If x is not \( \Delta^1_1 \)-semiretraceable, then x is not low for \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

Proof. Suppose that x is not \( \Delta^1_1 \)-semitraceable. Let T be a \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-tree so that \( \mu([T]) > 0 \) and [T] contains only \( \Pi^1_1 \)-ML random reals. Then by Lemma 14.6.4, there is a \( z \in [T] \) and a \( y \le_T x \) such that z is a member of the \( \Delta^1_1(x) \)-closed nullset \( \{r \mid y \le_T r\} \). Hence x is not low for \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

This leads us to the following theorem.

# **Theorem 14.6.6** (Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan and Yu [68]). For any \( x \in 2^\omega \), the following are equivalent:

+   (i) x is low for \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz tests;
+   (ii) x is low for \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness;
+   (iii) x is \( \Delta^1_1 \)-dominated and \( \Delta^1_1 \)-semiretraceable.

It is not known if there exists a nonhyperarithmetical real which is low for \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness. However, we can prove the following set inclusion.

# **Proposition 14.6.7** (Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan and Yu [68]). If x is low for \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness, then x is low for \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

Proof. Assume that x is low for \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness, y is \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz random and there is a \( \Delta^1_1(x) \)-closed nullset A with \( y \in A \). By Proposition 14.1.2, the set

\[
B = \bigcup \{[C] \mid C \text{ is a } \Delta^1_1\text{-closed nullset}\}
\]

is a \( \Pi^1_1 \)-nullset. Then A \\ B is \( \Sigma^1_1(x) \). Since y is \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz random, \( y \notin B \). Hence \( y \in A \\ B \) and so \( A \\ B \) is \( \Sigma^1_1(x) \) and nonempty. Thus there is a \( z \in A \\ B \) with \( \omega_1^x = \omega_1^z = \omega_1^y \). Since \( z \notin B \), z is \( \Delta^1_1 \)-Kurtz random. By Proposition 14.1.4, z is \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Kurtz random. This contradicts the fact that x is low for \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

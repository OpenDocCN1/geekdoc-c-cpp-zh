For (iii), suppose \( f : \omega \to \omega \) is a \(\Delta^1_1\)-function such that for every \( n \), there is an \( m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1}) \) with \( f(m) = x(m) \). Then there is a ranked formula \( \varphi \) such that \( f(n) = m \Leftrightarrow \mathcal{A}(\omega^1, x) \models \varphi(\bar{n}, \bar{m}) \). Moreover, \(\mathcal{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \models (\forall n)(\exists m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1}))(\varphi(m, x(m)))\). Hence there is a condition \( T \) which forces \( (\forall n)(\exists m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1}))(\varphi(m, x(m))) \) and \( x \in T \). Let \( n \) be such that \( T \) is fully splitting at level \( n \) and let \( \sigma \in 2^{n-1} \) be a string in \( T \). Let \( v \) be a string such that \( v(m) = 1 - f(m + 2^{n-1}) \). Define \( S = \{\sigma^\frown \tau \mid \tau \in T\} \subseteq T \). Then \( S \Vdash (\forall m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1}))(\neg \varphi(m, x(m)))\). But \( S \) is stronger than \( T \), which is a contradiction. By Corollary 13.3.12, \( x \) is not \(\Delta^1_1\)-traceable.

We may now separate \(\Delta^1_1\)-traceability from the conjunction of \(\Delta^1_1\)-semirecacability and \(\Delta^1_1\)-dominability.

# **Theorem 13.3.18** (Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan and Yu [68]). There are \( 2^{\aleph_0} \) many \(\Delta^1_1\)-dominated and \(\Delta^1_1\)-semirecacable reals which are not \(\Delta^1_1\)-traceable.

**Proof.** This is immediate from Lemma 13.3.17. Note that there are \( 2^{\aleph_0} \) many generic reals.

By exactly the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 5.3.12, we have the following.

# **Proposition 13.3.19.** There exists an uncountable \(\Sigma^1_1\)-set which contains only \(\Delta^1_1\)-dominated and \(\Delta^1_1\)-semirecacable reals.

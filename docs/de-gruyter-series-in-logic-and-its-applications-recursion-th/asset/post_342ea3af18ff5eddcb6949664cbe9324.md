
## 1.1.5 Relativization

As in classical recursion theory, all the results above may be relativized to a real \( x \). We use boldface symbols with tilde \( \Sigma_n^0, \Pi_n^0, \Delta_n^0, \Sigma_n^1, \Pi_n^1 \) and \( \Delta_n^1 \) to denote the corresponding sets (of numbers or reals) relativized to a real. A set is *projective* if it belongs to \( \Pi_n^1 \) for some \( n \).

## 1.2 Ordinal notations

A key goal in descriptive set theory is to investigate properties of real numbers and sets of real numbers within the system ZFC. However, many statements about complexity classes above the \( \Pi_1^1 \)-level are independent of ZFC. Hence it is natural to study in greater depth properties of \( \Pi_1^1 \)-sets. Higher recursion theory provides an ideal platform as well as powerful tools for this purpose. In this section, we introduce the notion of a recursive ordinal and an ordinal notation which is at the heart of analysis of \( \Pi_1^1 \)-sets. The recursive ordinals allow one to carry out effective transfinite induction over the ordinal \( \omega_1^{CK} \) similar to what is done over \( \omega_1 \) for transfinite induction.

## 1.2.1 Kleene's O

Let \( \langle . \rangle : \omega \times \omega \to \omega \) be a recursive bijection. Define an arithmetical set \( P \subseteq 2^\omega \) such that \( x \in P \) if and only if \( x \) satisfies the following conditions:

- (i) \( (1, 2) \in x \);
- (ii) \( (\forall m)(\forall n)((m, n) \in x \to \langle n, 2^n \rangle \in x) \);
- (iii) \( (\forall e)((\forall n)(\Phi_e(n) \downarrow \land (\forall n)(\Phi_e(n), \Phi_e(n+1)) \in x) \to (\forall n)((\Phi_e(n), 3 \cdot 5^e \in x)) \);
- (iv) \( (\forall n)(\forall m)(\forall k)((n, m) \in x \land (m, k) \in x \to \langle n, k \rangle \in x) \).

Thus (i) implies that \(x\) is nonempty; (ii) says that \(x\) is “closed under successor”, and (iii) that it is “closed under limit”; (iv) asserts that \(x\) defines a transitive relation. We introduce the following notations.

### Definition 1.2.1.

\(<_\mathcal{O} = \bigcup_{\beta < \alpha} x\). \(\mathcal{O} = \{n \mid (\exists m)(n <_\mathcal{O} m)\}\).

By Proposition 1.1.5, \(<_\mathcal{O}\) is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-relation and \(\mathcal{O}\) is \(\Pi^1_1\). We define a set \(a\) by induction:

- Stage 0. Put (1, 2) into \(a_0\).
- Stage \(\beta + 1\). Put \((n, 2^n)\) and \((m, 2^n)\) into \(a_{\beta+1}\) if \((m, n) \in a_\beta\) at stage \(\beta\).
- Stage \(\lambda\). For \(\lambda\) a limit ordinal. For each \(e\) such that \(\Phi_e\) is total and \((\forall n)(\exists \beta < \lambda)((\Phi_e(n), \Phi_e(n+1)) \in a_\beta)\), put \((\Phi_e(n), 3 \cdot 5^e)\) into \(a_\lambda\) for each \(n\). Furthermore, if \((m, \Phi_e(n)) \in a_\beta\) for some \(\beta < \lambda\), then \((m, 3 \cdot 5^e) \in a_\lambda\).

Let \(a = \bigcup_\beta a_\beta\).

It is straightforward to verify that \(a \in P\) and for each \(x \in P\), \(a \subseteq x\). Hence \(a = <_\mathcal{O}\).

Define a function \(|\cdot| : \mathcal{O} \mapsto \omega_1\) such that \(|1|=0\) and \(|n|\) is the least ordinal \(\beta\) for which there is a number \(m\) with \((m, n) \in a_\beta\). If \(n \in \mathcal{O}\), then \(n\) is said to be a notation for the ordinal \(|n|\). An ordinal \(\beta\) is constructive if \(\beta = |n|\) for some \(n \in \mathcal{O}\). Obviously \(m <_\mathcal{O} n\) implies \(|m| < |n|\). Hence \(<_\mathcal{O}\) is a wellfounded partial ordering.

### Definition 1.2.2.

\(\omega_1^{CK} =\) the least ordinal \(\alpha\) greater than \(|n|\) for every \(n \in \mathcal{O}\).

By induction on ordinals \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\), it is easy to see that for each \(n \in \mathcal{O}\), \(\{m \mid m <_\mathcal{O} n\}\) is a linear ordering under \(<_\mathcal{O}\).

### Remark 1.2.3.

- (i) A path \(x \subseteq \mathcal{O}\) is a set such that

\((\forall m, n \in x)(n <_\mathcal{O} m \lor m <_\mathcal{O} n \lor m = n) \land\)
\((\forall m)(\forall n \in x)(m <_\mathcal{O} n \to m \in x)\).

An interesting question is whether every path can be extended to one whose ordinal height is \(\omega_1^{CK}\). Such a path is necessary for carrying out effective transfinite induction. However, this is in general not true. Note that there are many possible choices of notations at a limit stage \(\beta\) below \(\omega_1^{CK}\). If every path up to \(\beta\) were extendible to one of ordinal height \(\omega_1^{CK}\), then there would be \(2^{\aleph_0}\) many sets of notations. Hence to obtain a path through \(<_\mathcal{O}\), one must choose the notations judiciously. We will show that there exists a “well-behaved” \(\Pi^1_1\)-path through \(<_\mathcal{O}\).

- (ii) It is not difficult to see that the set of constructive ordinals forms an initial segment, and hence is equal to \(\omega_1^{CK}\).

## 1.2.2 Relativized O

As in § 1.2.1, one may relativize the notion of ordinal notations to any real \(x \in \omega^\omega\). Let \(\{\Phi_e(x, n)\}_{e<\omega}\) be an effective enumeration of partial recursive functions with oracle \(x\) (cf. § 1.1.1). Define an arithmetical set \(P \subseteq \omega^\omega \times 2^\omega\) such that \((x, y) \in P\) if and only if \(y\) satisfies the following conditions:

- (i) \((1, 2) \in y\);
- (ii) \((\forall m)(\forall n)((m, n) \in y \to (n, 2^n) \in y)\);
- (iii) \((\forall e)(\langle \Phi_e(x, \cdot) \rangle \text{ is total} \land (\forall n)(\Phi_e(x, n), \Phi_e(x, n + 1)) \in y) \to (\forall n)(\langle \Phi_e(x, n), 3 \cdot 5^e \rangle \in y)\), and
- (iv) \((\forall n)(\forall m)(\forall k)((n, m) \in y \land (m, k) \in y \to (n, k) \in y)\).

### Definition 1.2.4.

\(<_{\mathcal{O}}^P = \bigcap_{(x,y)\in P} y\). \(\mathcal{O}^P = \{n \mid (\exists m)(n <_{\mathcal{O}}^P m)\}\).

One may also let relativized \(a^x = \bigcup_\beta a_\beta^x\). Then \(a^x <_{\mathcal{O}}^x\) and the function \(|\cdot|_x : \mathcal{O}^x \mapsto \omega_1\) is a well-defined rank function so that \(|1|_x = 0\) and \(|n|_x\) is the least ordinal \(\beta\) for which there is a number \(m\) with \((m, n) \in a_\beta^x\).

Note that there is a subtle difference between \(\mathcal{O}\) and \(\mathcal{O}^P\): An enumeration of oracle functionals is used to define \(\mathcal{O}^P\). Hence the set of indices used in the definition of \(\mathcal{O}^P\) may be different from that of \(\mathcal{O}\), resulting in possibly different notations at limit stage. However, such a difference does not affect the overall properties of \(\mathcal{O}\). Indeed, one may redefine \(\mathcal{O}\) as \(\mathcal{O}^P\) if desired.

By the relativized version of Proposition 1.1.15, both \(<_{\mathcal{O}}^P\) and \(\mathcal{O}^P\) are \(\Pi_1^1(x)\). The following proposition is immediate.

### Proposition 1.2.5.

- (i) The sets \(\{(n, x) \mid n \in \mathcal{O}^x\}\) and \(\{((m, n), x) \mid m <_{\mathcal{O}}^x n\}\) are \(\Pi_1^1\).
- (ii) The sets \(\{(x, y) \mid y = <_{\mathcal{O}}^x\}\) and \(\{(x, y) \mid y = \mathcal{O}^x\}\) are \(\Pi_1^1\).

Proof. Let \(P\) be the set of reals defined before Definition 1.2.4. For (i), \(n \in \mathcal{O}^x\) if and only if \((\exists m)(m, n) \in <_{\mathcal{O}}^x\) if and only if \((\exists m)(\forall y)((x, y) \in P \to (n, m) \in y)\).

For (ii), note that \(y = <_{\mathcal{O}}^x\) if and only if \((x, y) \in P\) and \((\forall z)((x, z) \in P \to (\forall n)(n \in y \to n \in z))\) which is \(\Pi_1^1\). We leave the other part of (ii) as an exercise.

Proposition 1.2.5 has some interesting consequences. Consider for example the set \(\mathcal{O} = \{0\}\). Then \(x \in \mathcal{O}\) if and only if \((0, x) \in \{(x, y) \mid y = \mathcal{O}^x\}\). Hence \(\mathcal{O}\) is a \(\Pi_1^1\)-singleton whose unique member is only \(\Pi_1^1\).

As in § 1.2.1, one may define the notion of an \(x\)-constructive ordinal and denote the least non \(x\)-constructive ordinal as \(\alpha_1^x\). Note that at limit stage, different oracle sets \(x\) may use different indices \(e\) for the notation. Hence \(\mathcal{O}^x\) may be different for different \(x\)'s. Moreover, for \(x\)'s with "sufficient computational power", one may have \(\alpha_1^x > \omega_1^{CK}\). We will present a characterization of this property in due course.

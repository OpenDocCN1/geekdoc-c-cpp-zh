
The proof of the following lemma is left to the reader.

### Lemma 14.6.3 (Kučera [76]). Suppose $T \subseteq 2^{<\omega}$ and $P \subseteq \omega \times 2^{<\omega}$ are $\Sigma^1_1$-trees with $\mu([T]) > 0$. Then there is a $\Sigma^1_1$-tree $S \subseteq T$ and a constant $c$ such that for any $e$, if $[P_e] \subseteq S$ is not empty, then $\mu([P_e]) > 2^{-c}$, where $P_e = \{ \sigma \mid (\sigma, e) \in P \}$.

The following lemma is essentially due to Joseph Miller.

### Lemma 14.6.4. For any non-$\Delta^1_1$-semiretraceable real $x$ and $\Sigma^1_1$-tree $T \subseteq 2^{<\omega}$ with $\mu([T]) > 0$, there exist $z \in [T]$ and $y \le_h x$ such that $y \notin z$ and $|\{n \mid y(n) = 1\}| = \aleph_0$.

Proof. Let $x$ be non-$\Delta^1_1$-semiretraceable and let $T \subseteq 2^{<\omega}$ be a $\Sigma^1_1$-tree with $\mu([T]) > 2^{-k}$ for some $k > 0$. By Lemma 13.3.10, there is a function $f \le_h x$ such that for any partial $\Pi^1_1$-function $p : \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \to \omega$, there are at most finitely many $(n, \sigma)$'s satisfying $f(n, \sigma) = p(n, \sigma)$.

Let $P \subseteq \omega \times 2^{<\omega}$ be a universal $\Sigma^1_1$-tree, i.e. $P$ is a $\Sigma^1_1$-tree and for any $\Sigma^1_1$-tree $Q \subseteq 2^{<\omega}$, there is an $e$ such that $P_e = Q$. We may assume that there is a recursive function $h$ such that for any $(e_0, e_1)$, $P_{h(e_0, e_1)} = P_{e_0} \cap P_{e_1}$.

Let $S \subseteq T$ be as in Lemma 14.6.3. Let $S = P_{e_0}$ for some $e_0$. We may also assume that there is a recursive function $g : 2^{<\omega} \to \omega$ such that $P_{g(\sigma)} = \{ \tau \mid \tau \in P_{e_0} \land \forall n (\sigma(n) = 1 \to \tau(n) = 1) \}$. For $\sigma \in 2^{<\omega}$, let

$$
A_\sigma = \{ n \mid [S] \cap \{ y \in 2^\omega \mid y(n) = 1 \land (\forall m)(\sigma(m) = 1 \to y(m) = 1) \} = \emptyset \}.
$$

Then the set $\{ (\sigma, A_\sigma) \mid \sigma \in 2^{<\omega} \}$ is a $\Pi^1_1$-set. Moreover, if $[P_{g(\sigma)}] \neq \emptyset$, then

$$
2^{-h(\sigma, c)} - c \le \mu([P_{g(\sigma)}]) \le \mu(\{ y \mid A_\sigma \cap \gamma \neq \emptyset \}) = 2^{-|A_\sigma|}.
$$

Thus $|A_\sigma| \le h(\sigma, c) + c$. It follows that there is a partial $\Pi^1_1$-function $p : \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \to \omega$ such that for any $n$, if $p(n, \sigma) = k$ then

- $k \in A_\sigma$, and
- $(\forall m < n)(\exists k')(k' \neq k \land p(m, \sigma) = k')$.

Hence $\{ p(n, \sigma) \}_{n \le h(\sigma, c) + c}$ is a one-one enumeration of $A_\sigma$. Note that

$$
|\{ n \mid p(n, \sigma) \text{ is defined} \}| \le h(\sigma, c) + c.
$$

Hyperarithmetically in $x$ compute a $k$ such that $k \notin A_\sigma$ and $k > |\sigma|$ as follows. Fix a recursive bijection $q : \omega \to \omega^{<\omega}$. We may assume that $f(n, \sigma) \neq q(p(n, \sigma))(n)$ for all $n$ and $\sigma$. Define $g_1 : 2^{<\omega} \to \omega^{<\omega}$ hyperarithmetically in $x$ such that $g_1(\sigma)$ is a finite string in $\omega^{h(\sigma, c) + c}$ and

$$
(\forall n \le 2^{h(\sigma, c) + c})(g_1(\sigma)(n) = f(n, \sigma)).
$$

Hence if $[P_{g(\sigma)}] \neq \emptyset$, then $g_1(\sigma) \neq q(p(n, \sigma))$ for all $n \le h(\sigma, c)$. For such a $\sigma$, $q^{-1}(g_1(\sigma)) \neq p(n, \sigma)$ for all $n$. Thus $q^{-1}(g_1(\sigma)) \notin A_\sigma$ and so $P_{g(q^{-1}(g_1(\sigma)))} \neq \emptyset$. Without loss of generality, we may assume that $q^{-1}(g_1(\sigma)) > |\sigma|$.

Using \( q^{-1} \circ g_1 \), starting with \( \sigma_0 = \emptyset \), hyperarithmetically in $x$ construct a sequence \( \sigma_0 < \sigma_1 < \cdots \) of strings such that for each $n$,
- $P_{g(n)} \neq \emptyset$ and
- $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n \cup \{q^{-1}(g_1(\sigma_n))\}$.

Hence \( \cap_{n<\omega} P_{g(n)} \neq \emptyset \) and for any \( z \in \cap_{n<\omega} P_{g(n)} \), the real \( y = \bigcup_{n<\omega} \sigma_n \) is a subset of $z$.

### Corollary 14.6.5. If $x$ is not $\Delta^1_1$-semiretraceable, then $x$ is not low for $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz randomness.

Proof. Suppose that $x$ is not $\Delta^1_1$-semiretraceable. Let $T$ be a $\Sigma^1_1$-tree so that $\mu([T]) > 0$ and $[T]$ contains only $\Pi^1_1$-ML random reals. Then by Lemma 14.6.4, there is a $z \in [T]$ and a $y \le_T x$ such that $z$ is a member of the $\Delta^1_1(x)$-closed nullset $\{r \mid y \le_T r\}$. Hence $x$ is not low for $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz randomness.

This leads us to the following theorem.

### Theorem 14.6.6 (Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan and Yu [68]). For any $x \in 2^\omega$, the following are equivalent:

- (i) $x$ is low for $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz tests;
- (ii) $x$ is low for $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz randomness;
- (iii) $x$ is $\Delta^1_1$-dominated and $\Delta^1_1$-semiretraceable.

It is not known if there exists a nonhyperarithmetical real which is low for $\Pi^1_1$-Kurtz randomness. However, we can prove the following set inclusion.

### Proposition 14.6.7 (Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan and Yu [68]). If $x$ is low for $\Pi^1_1$-Kurtz randomness, then $x$ is low for $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz randomness.

Proof. Assume that $x$ is low for $\Pi^1_1$-Kurtz randomness, $y$ is $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz random and there is a $\Delta^1_1(x)$-closed nullset $A$ with $y \in A$. By Proposition 14.1.2, the set

$$
B = \bigcup \{[C] \mid C \text{ is a } \Delta^1_1\text{-closed nullset}\}
$$

is a $\Pi^1_1$-nullset. Then $A \setminus B$ is $\Sigma^1_1(x)$. Since $y$ is $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz random, $y \notin B$. Hence $y \in A \setminus B$ and so $A \setminus B$ is $\Sigma^1_1(x)$ and nonempty. Thus there is a $z \in A \setminus B$ with $\omega_1^x = \omega_1^z = \omega_1^y$. Since $z \notin B$, $z$ is $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz random. By Proposition 14.1.4, $z$ is $\Pi^1_1$-Kurtz random. This contradicts the fact that $x$ is low for $\Pi^1_1$-Kurtz randomness.

## 14.6.2 Lowness for $\Delta^1_1$-randomness

### Theorem 14.6.8. The following are equivalent for a real $x$.

- (i) $x$ is $\Delta^1_1$-traceable;
- (ii) Every $\Delta^1_1(x)$-nullset is contained in a $\Delta^1_1$-nullset;
- (iii) $x$ is low for $\Delta^1_1$-randomness;
- (iv) Every $\Pi^1_1$-ML random real is $\Delta^1_1(x)$-random.

Proof. (i) \(\Rightarrow\) (ii): Assume that $x$ is $\Delta^1_1$-traceable. Let $S$ be a $\Delta^1_1(x)$-nullset. By Proposition 14.2.2 relativized to $x$, there is a $\Delta^1_1(x)$-ML test $(U_n)_{n \in \omega}$ such that $\mu(U_n) = 2^{-n}$ for each $n$ and $S \subseteq \bigcap U_n$. Let $(S_e)_{e \in \omega}$ be a recursive enumeration of all finite subsets of $2^{<\omega}$. There is a function $f \le_S x$ such that $(S_{f(n,s)})_s = U_{n,s}$ satisfies $U_{n,s} \subseteq U_{n,s+1}$, $U_n = \bigcup_{s < \omega} U_{n,s}$, and $\mu(U_{n,s}) > 2^{-n}(1 - 2^{-s})$. Let $T = (T_e)_{e \in \omega}$ be a $\Delta^1_1$-trace for $f$. We may choose $T$ such that in addition $|T_e| \le e$ for each $e > 0$.

We now define a subtrace $V_n$ via $\hat{T}$ of $T$, i.e., $\hat{T}_{(n,s)} \subseteq T_{(n,s)}$ for each $n, s$. The objective is to define open sets $V_n$, via $\hat{T}$ (in a way to be specified) small enough to give us a $\Delta^1_1$-nullset $\mathcal{V} = \bigcap_{n < \omega} V_n$, yet large enough as to keep all "relevant" reals out of $T_{(n,s)} - \hat{T}_{(n,s)}$, so that $\bigcap_{n < \omega} U_n \subseteq \mathcal{V}$.

Let $\hat{T}_{(n,s)}$ be the set of $e \in T_{(n,s)}$ such that $2^{-n}(1 - 2^{-s}) \le \mu([S_e]) \le 2^{-n}$ and $[S_e] \subseteq [S_d]$ for some $d \in \hat{T}_{(n,s-1)}$ (where $\hat{T}_{(n,-1)} = \omega$). Note that $f((n,s)) \in \hat{T}_{(n,s)}$. Let $V_n = \bigcup \{ [S_e] \mid e \in \hat{T}_{(n,s)}, s \in \omega \}$.

Then $\mu(V_n) \le 2^{-n} \lceil T_{(n,0)} \rceil + \sum_{s < \omega} 2^{-2-n} \lceil T_{(n,s)} \rceil$. Since $\lceil T_{(n,s)} \rceil \le \lceil T_{(n,s)} \rceil \le \langle n,s \rangle$ for $(n,s) \neq 0$, and $(n,s)$ has only polynomial growth in $n$ and $s$, it is clear that $\lim_n \sum_{s < \omega} 2^{-2-n} \lceil T_{(n,s)} \rceil = 0$, and hence $\lim_n \mu(V_n) = 0$. Then $\mathcal{V} = \bigcap_n V_n$ is a $\Delta^1_1$-nullset and $\bigcap U_n \subseteq \mathcal{V}$.

(ii) \(\Rightarrow\) (iii) and (iii) \(\Rightarrow\) (iv) are immediate.

(iv) \(\Rightarrow\) (i). The proof is identical to the proof in [67]. We leave it to the reader.

By Theorem 13.3.18, lowness for $\Delta^1_1$-randomness is different from that for $\Delta^1_1$-Kurtz randomness.

## 14.6.3 Lowness for $\Pi^1_1$-ML randomness

### Theorem 14.6.9 (Hjorth and Nies [51]). Every real low for $\Pi^1_1$-ML randomness is hyperarithmetic.

Proof. We give a sketch. Suppose that $x$ is low for $\Pi^1_1$-ML randomness. Then it is not difficult to verify that $\omega_1^x = \omega_1^{CK}$ and that there is a $\Pi^1_1(x)$-ML random $y \ge_T x$. In fact, one may use a $\Pi^1_1$-version of Turing reduction, termed $\le_{fin-h}$ and introduced in [51], to argue that $x \le_{fin-h} y$. In other words, there is a consistent $\Pi^1_1$-partial map $\Phi : 2^{<\omega} \to 2^{<\omega}$ such that $x = \bigcup_{n < \omega} \Phi(Y \upharpoonright n)$. Applying the method in [48] it can be shown that $x$ is $K_{HJ}$-trivial, where $K_{HJ}$ is an analog of Kolmogorov complexity in the $\Pi^1_1$-setting. It follows as in [51] that every $K_{HJ}$-trivial real is either hyperarithmetic or is hyperarithmetically equivalent to $0^\triangledown$. Since $\omega_1^x = \omega_1^{CK}$, $x$ has to be hyperarithmetic.

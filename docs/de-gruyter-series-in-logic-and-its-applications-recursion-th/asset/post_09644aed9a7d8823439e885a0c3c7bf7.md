
Recursion-theoretic proof. As above, we have a recursive tree \( T \subseteq \omega^{<\omega} \times \omega^{<\omega} \) such that \( x \in P \) if and only if \( (\exists y)(\forall n)(\langle x \mid n, y \mid n \rangle \in T) \). We perform a Cantor–Bendixson style operation on the tree as follows:

At stage 0, let \( T_0 = T \).
At limit stage \( \alpha \), let \( T_\alpha = \bigcap_{\beta < \alpha} T_\beta \).
At successor stage \( \alpha + 1 \), let
\( T_{\alpha+1} = \langle (\sigma, \tau) \in T_\alpha \mid (\exists \sigma_0 > \sigma)(\exists \tau_0 > \tau)(\exists \sigma_1 > \sigma)(\exists \tau_1 > \tau)((\sigma_0, \tau_0) \in T_\alpha \land (\sigma_1, \tau_1) \in T_\alpha \land \sigma_0|\sigma_1) \rangle \).

Observe that \( T_\alpha \subseteq T_\beta \) for all \( \alpha \ge \beta \). Moreover, for any successor ordinal \( \alpha + 1 \), if \( T_{\alpha+1} \neq T_\alpha \), then there is a \( (\sigma_\alpha, \tau_\alpha) \in T_\alpha \setminus T_{\alpha+1} \). Note that \( (\sigma_\alpha, \tau_\alpha) \neq (\sigma_\beta, \tau_\beta) \) for any \( \alpha \neq \beta \) if these are defined. Since the set \( \omega^{<\omega} \times \omega^{<\omega} \) is countable, there is a least countable ordinal \( \gamma \) such that \( (\sigma_\gamma, \tau_\gamma) \) does not exist. Then \( T_\gamma = T_{\gamma+1} \) and so \( T_\gamma = T_\alpha \) for any \( \alpha \ge \gamma \). By replacing \( T' \) in the set-theoretic proof above with \( T_\gamma \), we conclude that \( P \) contains a perfect subset.

### Remark 1.1.11.

- (i) The proofs above raise two interesting questions. First, what is the complexity of the perfect subset obtained? Since the statement, “\( P \) is uncountable” is not second-order definable in arithmetic, the perfect subset defined is generally neither \( \Sigma^1_1 \) nor \( \Pi^1_1 \). However, we will show later by a more powerful recursion-theoretic argument that its complexity can be controlled (see Exercise 1.5.5). Second, what is the least ordinal \( \gamma \) for which \( T_\gamma = T_{\gamma+1} \)? We will give a calculation of this ordinal later (see Proposition 3.6.10).

- (ii) In general, not every uncountable set has the perfect set property. In fact, “\( \Sigma^1_1 \)-ness” is the best possible since it is not provable in ZFC that every \( \Pi^1_1 \)-set has the perfect set property (see Corollary 4.3.6).

### Proposition 1.1.12. Suppose \( \{P_i\}_{i < \omega} \) is a sequence of sets of reals such that for any nonempty \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set \( A \) and \( i < \omega \), \( A \cap P_i \) contains a nonempty \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set. Let \( P \) be \( \Sigma^1_1 \) and nonempty. Then \( P \cap \bigcap_{i < \omega} P_i \neq \emptyset \).

Proof. By hypothesis, there is a nonempty \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set \( F_0 \subseteq P \cap P_0 \). By Proposition 1.1.9, there is a recursive tree \( T \subseteq \omega^{<\omega} \times \omega^{<\omega} \) such that \( x \in F_0 \iff (\exists y)(\forall n)(\langle x \mid n, y \mid n \rangle \in T) \). Fix such a pair \( (x, y) \) so that \( (\forall n)(\langle x \mid n, y \mid n \rangle \in T \). Define \( \sigma_0 = x \mid 1 \) and \( \tau_0 = y \mid 1 \). Note that \( G_0 = \{x > \sigma_0 \mid (\exists y > \tau_0)(\forall n)(\langle x \mid n, y \mid n \rangle \in T) \} \) is a nonempty \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set. Assume by induction that for all \( i' \le i \), a pair \( (\sigma_{i'}, \tau_{i'}) \in T \) and a nonempty \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set \( G_{i'} \subseteq P_{i'} \) are defined such that

- (1) \( (\forall i' < i)(G_{i'} \supseteq G_{i'+1}) \wedge (\tau_{i'} < \tau_{i'+1}) \)
- (2) \( (\forall i' \le i)(G_{i'} \subseteq P_{i'}) \)
- (3) \( (\forall i' < i)(G_{i'} \supseteq G_{i'+1} \wedge (\forall x \in G_{i'})(x > \sigma_{i'} \wedge (\exists y > \tau_{i'})(\forall n)(\langle x \mid n, y \mid n \rangle \in T)) \).

Since \( G_i \) is \( \Sigma^1_1 \), there is a nonempty \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set \( F_{i+1} \subseteq P_{i+1} \cap G_i \). Furthermore, by (3) every \( x \in F_{i+1} \) extends \( \sigma_i \) and there is a \( y > \tau_i \) such that for all \( n, (\langle x \mid n, y \mid n \rangle \in T \). Fix such a pair \((x, y)\). Define \(\sigma_{i+1} = x \uparrow (|\sigma_i| + 1)\) and \(\tau_{i+1} = y \uparrow (|\tau_i| + 1)\). Let

\(G_{i+1} = \{x \in F_{i+1} \mid x > \sigma_{i+1} \land (\exists y > \tau_{i+1})(\forall n)((x \upharpoonright n, y \upharpoonright n) \in T)\}.\)

Then \(G_{i+1}\) is a nonempty \(\Sigma^1_1\)-subset of \(F_{i+1}\) and hence of \(P_{i+1}\). Let \(x(i) = m\) if and only if \(\sigma_{i+1}(i) = m\) and \(y(i) = m\) if and only if \(\tau_{i+1}(i) = m\). Then for all \(i, x \in G_i\) and for all \(n, (x \upharpoonright n, y \upharpoonright n) \in T\). Hence \(x \in P \cap \bigcap_{n < \omega} P_i\).

As a consequence of Proposition 1.1.12, we may define the Gandy topology as follows.

### Definition 1.1.13. The Gandy topology on \(\omega^\omega\) is the topology generated by taking all \(\Sigma^1_1\)-sets as basic open sets.

Proposition 1.1.12 implies that the Gandy topology satisfies the Baire category theorem: The intersection of a countable collection of dense open sets is dense.

## 1.1.4 \(\Pi^1_1\)-sets of reals

By Proposition 1.1.9, we have the following:

### Proposition 1.1.14. A predicate \(P(x, m)\) is \(\Pi^1_1\) if and only if there is a recursive sequence of recursive trees \(T_m \in \omega^{<\omega} \times \omega^{<\omega}\) such that \(P(x, m) \leftrightarrow (\forall y)(\exists n)((x \upharpoonright n, y \upharpoonright n) \notin T_m)\).

For each real \(x\), define a binary relation \(\le_x\) on \(\omega\) such that \(m \le_x n\) if and only if \(x(<m, n>) = 1\). Define

\(WF_1 = \{x \in \omega^\omega \mid \le_x \text{ is a wellfounded relation}\}.\)

Then \(x \in WF_1\) if and only if

\((\forall f \in \omega^\omega)[(\forall n)(\forall m)(n \ge m \rightarrow f(n) \le f(m)) \rightarrow (\exists m)(\forall n)(\forall k)(n > m \land k > m \rightarrow f(n) = f(k))],\)

so that \(WF_1\) is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-set of reals. Fix a standard effective enumeration \(\{\sigma_n\}_{n < \omega}\) of \(\omega^{<\omega}\). We say that \(x\) codes a tree relation if

\(m \le_x n \leftrightarrow \sigma_m \succeq \sigma_n.\)

Now let \(P\) be a \(\Pi^1_1\)-set of reals and let \(T\) be a recursive tree such that \(x \in P \leftrightarrow (\forall y)(\exists n)((x \upharpoonright n, y \upharpoonright n) \notin T)\). Define \(T_x = \{\sigma \mid (|\sigma|, |\sigma|) \in T\}\). Then \(x \in P\) if and only if \(T_x\) is wellfounded. Define the Kleene-Brouwer ordering \(<_{KB}\) on strings so that for any \(\sigma, \tau \in \omega^{<\omega}\), \(\sigma <_{KB} \tau\) if and only if

\(\sigma \succ \tau (\exists i \le \min[|\sigma|, |\tau|])(\sigma \upharpoonright i = \tau \upharpoonright i \land \sigma \upharpoonright i \prec \tau(i)).\)

In other words, \(\sigma \prec_{KB} \tau\) whenever either \(\sigma\) extends \(\tau\) or \(\sigma\) is "to the left" of \(\tau\). Then for each \(x\), \(x\) codes a wellfounded tree if and only if \(x\) codes a well-ordering under \(<_{KB}\).

Let \(T \subseteq \omega^{<\omega} \times \omega^{<\omega}\) be a recursive tree which witnesses \(P\) being \(\Pi^1_1\). Thus \(x \in P\) if and only if \((\forall y)(\exists n)[x \mid n, y \mid n] \notin T\). Define \(f(x) = z\) if and only if \(z\) is the unique real coding the tree \(T_x\). Obviously \(f\) is a well-defined recursive function. Furthermore, \(P(x)\) if and only if \(f(x) \in \text{WF}_1\).

Define \(\text{WO}_1 = \{x \mid x \text{ codes a well-ordering}\}\).

Then \(x \in \text{WO}_1\) if and only if \(x \in \text{WF}_1 \land (\forall n)(\forall m)(n \le_R m \lor m \le_R n)\). Hence \(\text{WO}_1\) is \(\Pi^1_1\). Then for each \(\Pi^1_1\)-set \(P\), there is a recursive function \(f\) such that \(x \in P\) if and only if \(f(x) \in \text{WO}_1\).

Let \(\{R_e\}_{e<\omega}\) be an effective enumeration of recursively enumerable (r.e.) binary relations. Then the set

\(\text{WF}_0 = \{e \mid R_e \text{ is wellfounded}\}\)

is \(\Pi^1_1\). Let \(x \subseteq \omega\) be a \(\Pi^1_1\)-set and \(T \subseteq \omega^{<\omega} \times \omega\) be a recursive tree such that \(n \in x\) if and only if \((\forall y)(\exists m)[y \mid m, n] \notin T\). Clearly there is a recursive function \(f\) such that for any \(n, T_n = \{\sigma \mid (\sigma, n) \in T\} = R_{f(n)}\). Then \(n \in x\) if and only if \(R_{f(n)}\) is a wellfounded binary relation. In other words, \(x\) is many-one reducible to \(\text{WF}_0\). By the same argument above, every \(\Pi^1_1\)-set of numbers is many-one reducible to the \(\Pi^1_1\)-set \(\text{WO}_0 = \{e \mid R_e \text{ is a well-ordering}\}\).

Note that the ordinal height of \(\text{WF}_1\) may be as high as \(\omega_1\). An interesting question is the ordinal height of \(\text{WF}_0\). In other words, what is the least ordinal which is not the ordinal of a wellfounded recursive tree? We shall see that this ordinal, denoted \(\omega_1^{\text{CK}}\), is a countable ordinal.

The following proposition provides an interesting connection between \(\Sigma^1_1\)-sets of reals and \(\Pi^1_1\)-subsets of \(\omega\).

### Proposition 1.1.15 (Specter [153]). Suppose \(P\) is a \(\Sigma^1_n\)-set of reals. Then

- (i) \(\bigcap_{k \in \omega} x\) is a \(\Pi^1_n\)-set of integers.

- (ii) If \(P\) has a unique element \(x\), then \(x\) is \(\Delta^1_n\).

Proof. For (i): \(n \in \bigcap_{k \in \omega} x \iff (\forall x)(P(x) \to n \in x)\).

For (ii): \(n \in x \iff (\forall y)(P(y) \to n \in y) \leftrightarrow (\exists y)(P(y) \land n \in y)\).

Proposition 1.1.15 is very useful. First, (i) says that one obtains a \(\Pi^1_1\)-set by taking the intersection of a \(\Sigma^1_1\) condition. Second, (ii) provides a link between sets of numbers and sets of reals. The unique real in (ii) is called a \(\Sigma^1_n\) singleton. By (ii), each \(\Pi^1_n\) singleton is \(\Delta^1_{n+1}\). As we will see (cf. Proposition 1.2.5), this is the best possible. In general, the link between sets of numbers and sets of reals in terms of singleton sets is weak. For example, it will be shown that a \(\Pi^0_2\)-singleton may itself have a definition much more complex than \(\Pi^0_2\) (see also Exercise 1.1.5).

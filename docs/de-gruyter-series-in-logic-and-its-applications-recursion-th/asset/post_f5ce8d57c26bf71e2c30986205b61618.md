### Proposition 6.4.3 (Solovay [149]). The statement that every uncountable \(\Pi^1_1\)-set has a perfect subset is equiconsistent with the existence of an inaccessible cardinal.

Proof. By Proposition 6.4.2, the statement that every uncountable \(\Pi^1_1\)-set has a perfect subset implies \(\omega_1\) is inaccessible in L. Hence over ZFC, the consistency of the statement implies the consistency of the existence of an inaccessible cardinal.

Now suppose that there exists an inaccessible cardinal \(\kappa\) in a transitive model \(\mathfrak{M}\) of ZFC. Fix an L(\(\kappa\))-generic set \(G\) over \(\mathfrak{M}\). By Proposition 6.2.9, \(\kappa = (\omega_1)^{\mathfrak{M}[G]}\). Let \(x \in \mathfrak{M}[G]\). Then there is a sequence of conditions \(\cdots \le p_n \le \cdots \le p_0\) such that for any \(n\), \(p_n \in G\) forces the value of \(x(n)\). Since \(\kappa = (\omega_1)^{\mathfrak{M}[G]}\), there is a regular cardinal \(\eta < \kappa\) such that \(p_n \in \mathbf{L}(\eta)\) for every \(n\) and hence \(x \in \mathfrak{M}[G \cap \mathbf{L}(\eta)]\). Thus \((\omega_1)^{L[x]} \le (\omega_1)^{\mathfrak{M}[G \cap \mathbf{L}(\eta)]} < \kappa < \omega_1\). In other words,

\(\mathfrak{M}[G] \models \text{"}(\omega_1)^{L[x]} < \omega_1\text{"}.\)

Relativizing the proof of Corollary 4.3.6 to x, we conclude that the largest \(\Pi^1_1(x)\)-thin set \(\{z \mid z \in L_{\omega_1^{x+}}[x]\}\) is countable. Then

\(\mathfrak{M}[G] \models \text{"Every uncountable } \Pi^1_1\text{-set has a perfect subset".}\)

## 6.4.2 Measurable cardinals

For a given infinite cardinal \(\kappa\), consider \(\mathscr{P}(\kappa)\) as a partially ordered set under \(\subseteq\).

### Definition 6.4.4.

- A set \(A \subseteq \mathscr{P}(\kappa)\) is an ultrafilter over \(\kappa\) if it is a filter and for any \(a \subseteq \kappa\), either \(a \in A\) or \(\kappa \setminus a \in A\);
- a filter \(A\) is nonprincipal if for any \(a \in A\), there exists a \(b \in A\) such that \(a \not\subseteq b\);
- let \(\eta \le \kappa\) be a cardinal. A filter \(A\) is \(\eta\)-complete if for any \(\beta < \eta\) and any sequence \(\{a_\alpha\}_{\alpha < \beta}\) in \(A\), \(\cap_{\alpha < \beta} a_\alpha \in A\).

A \(\kappa\)-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter \(A\) over \(\kappa\) induces a \(\kappa\)-additive zero-one measure \(\mu\) on \(\mathscr{P}(\kappa)\) defined so that for any \(a \subseteq \kappa\), \(\mu(a) = 1\) if and only if \(a \in A\). It is not difficult to construct under ZFC an \(\omega\)-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter \(A\) over \(\omega\). For \(\kappa > \omega\), however, no \(\kappa\)-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter \(A\) over \(\kappa\) can be proved to exist within the same axiom system.

### Definition 6.4.5. We say that \(\kappa > \omega\) is measurable if there exists a \(\kappa\)-complete non-principal ultrafilter \(A\) over \(\kappa\).

It can be proved that every measurable cardinal is inaccessible (see [53]). Existence of a measurable cardinal is a very strong large cardinal assumption. Although it is a major notion in set theory, none of the results proved in this book requires this assumption.

## 6.4.3 \(0^\#\)

Compared to a measurable cardinal, \(0^\#\) is a relatively small large cardinal. Nevertheless, it plays a significant role in the study of definability. According to Slaman (Gödel Lecture, 2001), “\(0^\#\) is the recursion theorist’s passport to set theory”. In a rather striking way, the definition of \(0^\#\) is more involved than those of the large cardinals considered thus far.

### Definition 6.4.6. Assume that

- (i) for any pair of uncountable cardinals \(\eta < \kappa\), \((L_\eta, \in) \prec (L_\kappa, \in)\);
- (ii) there is a unique closed and unbounded class \(I\) of ordinals containing all uncountable cardinals such that for any cardinal \(\kappa > \omega\),
  - (a) \(|I \cap \kappa| = \kappa\);
  - (b) for any \(\psi(v_0, \dots, v_k)\), any two increasing sequences \(\gamma_0^0 < \dots < \gamma_k^0\) and \(\gamma_1^1 < \dots < \gamma_k^1\) in \(I \cap \kappa\), we have \((L_\kappa, \in) \vDash \psi(\gamma_0^0, \dots, \gamma_k^0) \Leftrightarrow (L_\kappa, \in) \vDash \psi(\gamma_1^1, \dots, \gamma_k^1)\);
  - (c) every element \(a \in L_\kappa\) is definable in \((L_\kappa, \in)\) with parameters in \(I \cap \kappa\).

Then \(n \in 0^\#\) if and only if \(n\) is the Gödel number of a formula \(\varphi\) such that \((L_{\aleph_\omega}, \in) \vDash \varphi(\aleph_1, \dots, \aleph_{m+1})\).

The term \(0^\#\) was introduced by Solovay. Gaifman [32] proved that the existence of \(0^\#\) follows from the existence of a measurable cardinal. Silver [137] derived the same conclusion assuming the existence of a Ramsey cardinal, known to be weaker than that of a measurable cardinal. Solovay [148] proved that \(0^\#\) is a nonconstructible \(\Delta_2^1\)-set of integers. Since every \(\Delta_2^1\)-real belongs to \(L\) (Corollary 4.2.9 (iii)), this gives \(\Delta_2^1\) as the optimal bound for constructibility of reals under a large cardinal assumption.

Though the definition of \(0^\#\) appears to be undefinable in the language of set theory, it can be reformulated model-theoretically. This enabled Silver to prove that \(0^\#\) is a \(\Pi_2^1\)-singleton and hence \(\Delta_2^1\), \(0^\#\) may well be the weakest large cardinal axiom that is incompatible with \(V = L\).

### Proposition 6.4.7. If \(0^\#\) exists, then every constructible real is recursive in \(0^\#\). Hence there are only countably many constructible reals.

Proof. Suppose that \(0^\#\) exists and \(x\) is constructible. Since \(x \in L_{\omega_1}\), by Definition 6.4.6 (ii) there is a formula \(\varphi_v(v, u_1, \dots, u_m)\) and an increasing sequence \(i_1, \dots, i_m\) in \(I \cap L_{\omega_1}\) such that for any \(n < \omega\), \(n \in x\) if and only if \((L_{\omega_1}, \in) \vDash \varphi_x(n, i_1, \dots, i_m)\).

For each \(n\), let \(\varphi_n(v)\) be a formula defining \(n\) so that

$$(\exists v_0) \cdots (\exists v_n)(\forall v)(\varphi_n(v_0) \land v_1 = v_0 \cup \{v_0\} \land v_2 = v_1 \cup \{v_1\} \land \dots \land v_n = v_{n-1} \cup \{v_{n-1}\} \land v = v_n).$$

Then

$$n \in x \Leftrightarrow (L_{\omega_1}, \in) \models (\exists v)(\varphi_n(v) \land \varphi_x(v, i_1, \dots, i_m)).$$

By Definition 6.4.6 (i) and (ii),

$$n \in x \Leftrightarrow (L_{\aleph_\omega}, \in) \models (\exists v)(\varphi_n(v) \land \varphi_x(v, \aleph_1, \dots, \aleph_m)).$$

Clearly the set \(\{(\exists v)(\varphi_n(v) \land \varphi_x(v, u_1, \dots, u_m)) \mid n < \omega\}\) is recursive and

$$n \in x \Leftrightarrow \ulcorner (\exists v)(\varphi_n(v) \land \varphi_x(v, u_1, \dots, u_m)) \urcorner \in 0^\sharp.$$

Thus \(x \le_T 0^\sharp\).

Proposition 6.4.7 implies that \(0^\sharp\) has extremely high computational power. Recursion-theoretically, one may view \(0^\sharp\) as the "jump" of \(\emptyset\) under the #-operator.

While \(0^\sharp\) collapses cardinals in \(L\), its absence presents quite a different picture provided by Jensen's remarkable Covering Lemma.

### Theorem 6.4.8 (Jensen [21]). If \(0^\sharp\) does not exist then every uncountable set of ordinals is contained in a constructible set of the same cardinality.

## 6.5 Axiom of determinacy

In this book our focus is mostly on low level definable sets of reals such as those that are \(\Pi^1_1\) or \(\Sigma^1_2\). To go "higher up", one resorts to the use of games and strategies which lie at the heart of the Axiom of Determinacy. It turns out that this axiom is at its core a large cardinal axiom.

### 6.5.1 Games and strategies

Let \(A \subseteq \omega^\omega\). An infinite game \(G_A\) with perfect information has two players labeled I and II. The game is played by choosing a natural number alternately between the two players and ends in \(\omega\)-many steps. Each game generates a real \(x = (n_0, m_0, \dots, n_i, m_i, \dots) \in \omega^\omega\) where \(n_i\) and \(m_i\) are the numbers played by I and II respectively at step i. If \(x \in A\), then I wins the game. Otherwise, II wins.

### Definition 6.5.1.

Given a set \(A \subseteq \omega^\omega\), I has a winning strategy \(\vec{\sigma} : \omega^{<\omega} \to \omega\) for the game \(G_A\) if for every \(g \in \omega^\omega\), the real \(\vec{\sigma} * g = (n_0, g(0), \dots, n_i, g(n_i), \dots) \in A\) where \(n_0 = \vec{\sigma}(0)\) and \(n_i = \vec{\sigma}(\langle n_0, g(0), \dots, n_{i-1}, g(i-1) \rangle)\) for \(i > 0\).

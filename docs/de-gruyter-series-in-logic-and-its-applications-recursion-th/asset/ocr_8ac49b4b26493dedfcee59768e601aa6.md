If \( F : 2^\omega \to 2^\omega \) is degree invariant, then it is order preserving if for any \( x \le_T y \), \( F(x) \le_T F(y) \).

**Theorem 7.4.2** (Slaman and Steel [142]). Assume ZF + AD + DC. Suppose that \( F : 2^\omega \to 2^\omega \) is order preserving and increasing on a cone. Then either (i) there is an < \( a_1 \), such that on a cone, \( F(x) =_T x^{(a)} \), or (ii) on a cone, \( F(x) >_T x^{(a)} \) for any \( a < a_1 \)).

Proof. Assume that \( F \) is increasing on the cone \( C \). If (ii) is not true for \( F \), then by Lemma 7.4.1 and Proposition 6.6.5, there exists an ordinal \( a < \omega_1 \) such that \( x^{(a)} \le_T F(x) \) on a cone \( C_1 \subseteq C \). Let \( \alpha_0 \) be the least such ordinal. Then \( F(x) >_T x^{(\beta)} \) for any \( \beta < \alpha_0 \). We may assume that \( \alpha_1^{>} > \alpha_0 \). Let \( x_0 \in C_1 \). By relativizing Theorem 7.3.2 to \( x_0 \), there is a \( y \ge_T x_0 \) such that \( y \in F(x_0) \equiv y^{(\alpha_0)} \). Since \( F \) is order preserving and increasing, \( F(y) \ge_T y \in F(x_0) \equiv_T y^{(\alpha_0)} \). Thus \( F(y) \equiv_T y^{(\alpha_0)} \). It follows that the set \( \{x \mid F(x) \equiv_T x^{(a_0)}\} \) is cofinal. By Proposition 6.6.6 again, \( F(x) \equiv_T x^{(a_0)} \) on a cone.

# 7.5 Pressdown functions

A function \( F : 2^\omega \to 2^\omega \) is pressdown on a cone \( C \) if \( F(x) <_T x \) for \( x \in C \). By Theorem 7.1.3, every uniformly degree invariant function that is pressdown on a cone is a constant on a cone.

**Theorem 7.5.1** (Slaman and Steel [142]). Assume ZF + AD + DC. If \( F \) is a degree invariant pressdown function on a cone, then \( F \) is constant on a cone.

Proof of Theorem 7.5.1. Suppose that \( F \) is a degree invariant pressdown function on a cone \( C = \{x \mid x \ge_T z\} \) for some z. By Proposition 6.6.5, there is an \( e_0 \) and a pointed tree T such that \( F(x) = \Phi^z_{e_0} \) for all \( x \in [T] \). By Lemma 7.1.5, we may assume that \( \Phi_{e_0} \) is a homeomorphism from [T] to a perfect set [S].

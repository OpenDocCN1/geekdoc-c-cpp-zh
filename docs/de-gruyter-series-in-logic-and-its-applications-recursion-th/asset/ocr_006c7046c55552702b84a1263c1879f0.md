**Theorem 3.3.4.** Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a transitive model of KP. Then for each $\beta < o(M)$, $L_\beta \in M$ and $(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M} = L_\beta$.

**Proof.** By Theorem 3.3.2, the formula “$x = L_\beta$” is $\Delta_1$-definable over $\mathcal{M}$. By the absoluteness of $\Delta_0$-formulas, $L_\beta \in M$ and $(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M} = L_\beta$.

**Corollary 3.3.5.** Suppose $\mathcal{M}$ is transitive, admissible and $\mathcal{M} \models \text{“V = L”}$. Then $M = L_a$ where $a = o(\mathcal{M})$.

**Theorem 3.3.6.** $a$ is admissible if and only if $(L_a, \in)$ is admissible.

**Proof.** If $(L_a, \in)$ is admissible, then $a$ is admissible.

If $a = o(\mathcal{M})$ for some admissible $\mathcal{M}$, then $(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M} = L_\beta$ for each $\beta < a$ by Theorem 3.3.4. Hence it suffices to show that $L_a = \bigcup_{\beta < a}(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M}$ is admissible. We verify that $\Delta_0$-collection holds in $L_a$ and leave the verification of other properties to the reader.

Suppose that $(L_a, \in) \models (\forall x \in a)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y)$. By the absoluteness of $\Delta_0$-formulas, for each $x \in a$, there is a (least) $\beta < a$ such that

$\mathcal{M} \models (\exists y \in \mathcal{E}) \varphi(x, y).$

Then Theorem 3.3.2 and Theorem 3.1.6 on $\Sigma$-collection imply that there is an $a_0 < a$ such that $a \in L_{a_0}$, and

$\mathcal{M} \models (\forall x \in a)(\exists \beta < a_0)(\exists y \in L_\beta)\varphi(x, y).$

The absoluteness of $\Delta_1$-formulas now gives

$(L_a, \in) \models (\forall x \in a)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y).$

Then if $y \in (L_a, \in) \models (\exists x \in a)\varphi(x, y)) \in L_{a_0+1}$ and is contained in $L_a$.

Obviously if $a = \omega$, then $L_a = \text{HF}$. Hence $L_a$ is the smallest admissible set (cf. Theorem 3.2.4). Note that if $a > \omega$ is admissible, then $\omega a = a$.

A fundamental property of $L$ is the following known as condensation:

**Theorem 3.3.7.** Suppose that $\beta > 0$ and $(X, \in) \prec_{\Sigma_1} (J_\beta, \in)$. Then $(X, \in) \cong (J_\gamma, \in)$ for some $\gamma \le \beta$. Moreover $\pi(x) \supseteq x$ for every $x \in X$.

**Proof.** Suppose $(X, \in) \prec_{\Sigma_1} (J_\beta, \in)$. Then $(X, \in)$ is wellfounded and satisfies the extensionality axiom. By Theorem 3.1.12, there is a transitive set $M$ such that

$\pi : (X, \in) \cong (M, \in).$

We claim that $M = J_\gamma$ where $\gamma = \sup(\pi[\delta]) \in \delta \cap \text{On}$.

Clearly $\langle M, \in \rangle \prec_{\Sigma_1}^{\Sigma_1} \langle J_\beta, \in \rangle$ and $M$ is transitive. By Proposition 3.3.3, there is a $\Sigma_0$-formula $\varphi(x, \delta, s)$ such that for any $x \in J_\delta$ and $\delta < \beta$, $(J_\beta, \in) \models \exists \exists \varphi(x, \delta, s)$ if and only if $x = J_\delta$. Since $M$ is transitive, by an induction on $\delta < \gamma$, we prove that $J_\delta \in M$ for any $\delta < \gamma$. Hence $J_\gamma \subseteq M$.

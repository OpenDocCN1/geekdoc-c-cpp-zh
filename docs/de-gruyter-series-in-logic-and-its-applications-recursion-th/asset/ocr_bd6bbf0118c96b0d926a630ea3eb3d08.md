# **Theorem 8.2.4.** $(\omega_1)^L = \omega_1$ if and only if the $\Pi^1_1$-inductive principle $\mathfrak{I}$ holds.

Proof. The "if" direction is immediate: Choose P(x, y) to express x <=_T y. Then P is cofinally progressive. Let A be a \Pi^1_1-set satisfying I for P, of <_1-height \omega_1. Then (\omega_1)^L = \omega_1.

For the other direction, suppose P is \Pi^1_1 and cofinally progressive. Then for any pair of reals x, z \in L, the set U_{x,z} = {y | y >=_T z >= P(x, y)} is a nonempty \Pi^1_1(x, z)-set. By Theorem 4.12, there is a y \in U_{x,z} \cap L. By Theorem 4.1.2 again, (L, \in) \models P is cofinally progressive.

By Theorem 8.2.3, there is a \Pi^1_1-set A such that (A)^L witnesses the correctness of the inductive principle I in L.

Since (\omega_1)^L = \omega_1, \mathfrak{I} (I) is true in V. Since the statement "x \in L^A_{\omega_1}" is \Pi^1_1 and (L, \in) \models (\forall x)(x \in A \to x \in L^A_{\omega_1}),

by the Shoenfield Absoluteness Lemma (Corollary 4.2.9), (V, \in) \models (\forall x)(x \in A \to x \in L^A_{\omega_1}). Hence A \subset L.

Let y \in A. Since A \subseteq L, y \in L. Then there exists an x \in L^A_{\omega_1} such that (L, \in) \models x codes the set {z | z \in A \wedge z <_L y} \wedge P(x, y).

Since A \subset L, x codes the set {z | z \in A \wedge z <_L y}. Since the relation P is \Pi^1_1 and x, y \in L, by the Shoenfield Absoluteness Lemma (Corollary 4.2.9) again, P(x, y) holds. Hence (ii) follows.

One may relativize the \Pi^1_1-inductive principle \mathfrak{I} to admit real parameters and obtain its boldface version \underline{\mathfrak{I}}. Then Theorem 8.2.4 may be generalized to state: Boldface \Pi^1_1-inductive principle \underline{\mathfrak{I}} fails if and only if there is no real x such that (\omega_1)^{L[x]} = \omega_1. This leads to the following conclusion.

# **Corollary 8.2.5.** The statement "ZFC + \Pi^1_1-inductive principle \mathfrak{I} is false" is consistent if and only if "ZFC+ There exists an inaccessible cardinal" is consistent.

Proof. If \mathfrak{I} fails, then (\omega_1)^{L[x]} < \omega_1 for all x, so that the latter is inaccessible in L. Conversely, suppose ZFC+ "There is an inaccessible cardinal" is consistent. Then by Proposition 6.2.9 (the Levy collapsing map), ZFC+ "\omega_1 is inaccessible in L^r" is consistent.

Now in a model M with such a property, \omega_1 > (\omega_1)^{L[x]} for all x. Hence \mathfrak{I} fails in M.

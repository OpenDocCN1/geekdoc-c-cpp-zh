Then \( C_1 \) is \( \Pi_1^1 \). By \( \text{MA}_{\aleph_1} \), Lemma 6.3.3 and Theorem 6.3.5, there is a real \( g \) such that for any \( \alpha < \aleph_1 \) and \( n \in \omega \),

+   1. \( g \cap \chi_{\alpha, 2n+1} \) is infinite if and only if \( n \notin \chi_\alpha \); and

+   2. \( g \cap \chi_{\alpha, 2n+2} \) is infinite if and only if \( n \in \chi_\alpha \).

Let \( h_0 \) be a function so that \( h_0(x, n) = i \) (if \( (\exists j)(i \) is the \( p_j^i \)-th element of \( x) \)). Note that \( h_0(x_\alpha, n) = \chi_{\alpha, n} \) and \( h_0(y_\alpha, n) = y_{\alpha, n} \).

Set \( h_1(x) = \{n \mid h_0(x, 2n + 1) \cap g\} = \text{co}\infty \) and \( h_2(x) = \{n \mid h_0(x, 2n + 2) \cap g\} = \text{co}\infty \).

Then for any \( x \in 2^\omega \), \( x \in B_0 \leftrightarrow h_1(x) \in C_0 \land h_2(h_1(x)) = x \),

showing that \( B \) is \( \Pi_1^1 \).

# Theorem 6.3.7 (Martin and Solovay [90]).

Assume \( \text{MA}_{\aleph_1} \). Then \( (\omega_1)^{[L[x]]} = \omega_1 \) for some \( x \in 2^\omega \) if and only if every subset of \( 2^\omega \) with cardinality \( \aleph_1 \) is \( \Pi_1^1 \).

# Proof.

Suppose that \( (\omega_1)^{[L[x]]} < \omega_1 \) for all \( x \in 2^\omega \). Let \( A = \{x_\alpha\}_{\alpha < \omega_1} \) be a maximal chain of Turing degrees. Then \( A \) has cardinality \( \aleph_1 \) and contains no perfect subset. It follows that for any real \( x \), \( A \) must contain a real not constructible in \( L[x] \). If \( A \) is \( \Pi_1^1(x_0) \) for some \( x_0 \), then by Lemma 4.3.2, \( A \) contains a perfect subset which is a contradiction.

Conversely suppose that \( (\omega_1)^{[L[x]]} = \omega_1 \) for some \( x \in 2^\omega \). By Lemma 6.3.6, every subset of \( 2^\omega \) of cardinality \( \aleph_1 \) is \( \Pi_1^1 \).

# Remark 6.3.8.

Theorem 6.3.7 may be viewed as a result that goes "beyond the limits" of recursion theory, in which typically a lightface version of a theorem is proved and then relativized to a boldface version. However, in this case there is no lightface version for Theorem 6.3.7.

An immediate consequence of Theorem 6.3.7 (see Exercise 6.3.3) is that if \( \text{MA}_{\aleph_1} \) holds, then \( (\omega_1)^{[L[x]]} = \omega_1 \) for some \( x \in 2^\omega \) if and only if every union of \( \aleph_1 \)-many Borel sets is \( \Sigma_2^1 \). In contrast to this, Harrington [44] proved that for any cardinal \( \kappa < 2^{\aleph_0} \), there is a model of ZFC in which any union of \( \kappa \)-many Borel sets is \( \Pi_2^1 \).

Without loss of generality, we may assume that for any splitting node $\alpha \in T$, $\Phi_e^{\alpha \upharpoonright 0}$ is compatible with $\Phi_{e_0}^1$. The plan is to find two reals $x_0, x_1 \le_T T$ in [T] such that $\Phi_{e_0}^{x_0} \oplus \Phi_{e_1}^{x_1} \ge_T T$. Since F is degree invariant and T is pointed, $F(x_0 \oplus x_1) \equiv_T F(x_0) \equiv_T F(x_0) \oplus F(x_1) \ge_T T \equiv_T x_0 \oplus x_1$. Then we have a contradiction to the assumption that $F(x) <_T x$ on C. The following lemma says that for $x \in [T]$, the growth rate of any function $h \le_T x$ is controlled by F(x).

# Lemma 7.5.2. Suppose that $x \in [T]$ and $h \le_T x$. For each e, let $l^e$ be a function recursive in $\Phi_{e_0}^x$ so that

$l^e(0) = 0$

and

$l^e(n + 1) = \sum_{i \le n} \min \{ m \mid \Phi_{e(i)}^{x_0 \upharpoonright m}(i) \downarrow \}.$

Then there is an e such that $l^e$ is total and $(\exists m)(\forall n \ge m)(h(l^e(n)) < l^e(n + 1))$.

Proof. Without loss of generality, we may assume that h is increasing. Suppose that the lemma is false. We construct a real $y \le_T x$ in [T] such that $\Phi_e^y, \tau \Phi_{e_0}^x$. By shrinking T if necessary, we may assume that $x \le_T T$. Since T is pointed, we have $y \equiv_T x$ but $\Phi_{e_0}^x \not\ge_T \Phi_{e_0}^y$, a contradiction.

The construction of o is by full approximation recursive in x. We shall construct an x-recursive sequence $\sigma_0 < \sigma_1 \cdots$ and let $y = \bigcup_{s < \omega} \sigma_s$. At any stage s, we may declare a number e to be discarded.

Let $\sigma_0 = 0$.

At stage s + 1, let $\tau > s$ be the immediate splitting node in T so that $\Phi_{e_0}^{\tau \upharpoonright 0}(k_s) \downarrow \neq \Phi_{e_0}^{\tau \upharpoonright 1}(k_s) \downarrow$ for some $k_s$. For i = 1, let $\tau_i > \tau$ be the next immediate splitting node in T so that $\Phi_{e_0}^{\tau \upharpoonright 0}(k_s) \downarrow \neq \Phi_{e_0}^{\tau \upharpoonright 1}(k_s) \downarrow$ for some $k_s^i > k_s$.

Search for the least e < s which has not been discarded and

$\Phi_e^{h(h(|\tau_0| + |\tau_1|))}(k_s) / h(|\tau_0| + |\tau_1|) \downarrow$ .

If there exists such an e, then let $\sigma_{s+1} = \tau^i$ where i is the least number such that

$\Phi_e^{k_s | h(|\tau_0| + |\tau_1|)}(k_s) / h(|\tau_0| + |\tau_1|) \downarrow \neq \Phi_{e_0}^{\tau_i}(k_s)$

and discard e. Otherwise, let $\sigma_{s+1} = \sigma_s$.

Clearly $y \le_T x$. Suppose that $\Phi_e^y = \Phi_{e_0}^{x_0}$ for some e. By the assumption, for any m there exists $n \ge m$ such that $h(l^e(n)) \ge l^e(n + 1)$. Let $s_0$ be a stage large enough so that no $e' \le e$ is discarded after s0. Then there is a $n > |s_{0}| + s_0$ such that $h(l^e(n)) \ge l^e(n + 1)$. Let $s_1$ be the largest stage $\ge s_0$ so that $k_{s_1} \le l^e(n)$. Then at stage $s_1 + 1$, either $k_0$ or $k_1$ is greater than $l^e(n)$. Hence $\Phi_{e_0}(k_{s_1}) > l^e(n)$. Since $k_{s+1} \le l^e(n + 1)$, we have $\Phi_{e_0}^{h(h(|\tau_0| + |\tau_1|))}(k_s) / h(|\tau_0| + |\tau_1|) \downarrow$ . In other words, e is discarded at stage $s_1 + 1$.

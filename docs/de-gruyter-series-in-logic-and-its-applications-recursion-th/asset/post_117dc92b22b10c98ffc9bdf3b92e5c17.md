
## 12.3 Lowness for randomness

### 12.3.1 General lowness notions

Given a notion \(\Gamma\) of randomness, we may relativize it to a real \(x\) to obtain \(\Gamma^x\). Define \(\text{Low}(\Gamma) = \{x \in 2^\omega \mid \Gamma \subseteq \Gamma^x\}\). Then every member of \(\text{Low}(\Gamma)\) is said to be low for \(\Gamma\).

One may consider a slightly stronger lowness notion. Since each \(\Gamma\) is defined in terms of \(\Gamma\)-tests, we may declare \(x\) to be low for \(\Gamma\)-tests if every \(\Gamma^x\) test covered (as a set) by a \(\Gamma\)-test. More details concerning these can be found in [111] and [24].

Hence if \(x \in \text{Low}(\Gamma)\) then it does not provide any information regarding randomness for \(\Gamma\). Nevertheless, the property of lowness seems somewhat mysterious since one has no clue on the complexity of members of \(\text{Low}(\Gamma)\). A very important and successful application of classical recursion theory is the characterization of the lowness property for various notions of randomness.

### 12.3.2 Lowness for Kurtz randomness

**Theorem 12.3.1** (Stephan, Yu [160] and Greenberg, Miller [38]). _For any real_ \(x\)_, the following are equivalent:_

- (i) \(x\) is low for Kurtz randomness;
- (ii) \(x\) is low for Kurtz tests;
- (iii) \(x\) is recursively dominated and non-DNR.

It is known that there are \(2^{\aleph_0}\) many recursively dominated and non-DNR reals. Hence Theorem 12.3.1 says that there is an abundance of reals which do not provide any information about Kurtz randomness.

### 12.3.3 Lowness for Schnorr randomness

**Definition 12.3.2.** A real \(x\) is _recursively traceable_ if for any \(f \le_T x\), there is a recursive function \(g : \omega \to \mathscr{P}_{<\omega}(2^{<\omega})\) such that for any \(e\), \(f(e) \in g(e)\) and \(|g(e)| \le e\).

It is not difficult to see that every recursively traceable real is recursively dominated and non-DNR. Moreover, the construction of a recursively traceable real is quite similar to that of a set of minimal Turing degree. They are both obtained using Sacks forcing with recursive perfect trees.

**Theorem 12.3.3** (Terwijn, Zambella [163] and Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan [67]). _For any real_ \(x\)_, the following are equivalent:_

- (i) \(x\) is low for Schnorr randomness;
- (ii) \(x\) is low for Schnorr tests;
- (iii) \(x\) is recursively traceable.

Hence there are again many reals that are low for Schnorr randomness.

### 12.3.4 Lowness for Martin-Löf randomness

**Definition 12.3.4.** Let \(\Gamma\) be a notion of randomness. A real \(x\) is a _base_ for \(\Gamma\) if there is a \(\Gamma^*\)-random \(y\) such that \(x \le_T y\).

The following result is one of the deepest in algorithmic randomness theory.

**Theorem 12.3.5** (Nies [110] and Hirschfeldt, Nies, Stephan [48]). _For any_ \(x\)_, the following are equivalent:_
(i) _\(x\) is K-trivial;_
(ii) _\(x\) is a base for Martin-Löf randomness;_
(iii) \((\exists c)(\forall n)(K(n) \le K^c(n) + c)\)_;_
(iv) _\(x\) is low for Martin-Löf tests;_
(v) _\(x\) is low for Martin-Löf randomness._

The concept of a randomness base is particularly important in the proof of Theorem 12.3.5. Note that one may view Martin-Löf randomness to be the weakest notion of randomness as it has only countably many bases (see [27]). Following the characterization in Theorem 12.3.5, Nies introduced a new reduction relation \(\le_{LR}\):

**Definition 12.3.6.** \(x \le_{LR} y\) if every \(y\)-Martin-Löf random real is \(x\)-Martin-Löf random.

Hence by Theorem 12.3.5, \(x \le_{LR} 0\) if and only if \(x\) is \(K\)-trivial. This relation is expressed in terms of Kolmogorov complexity as follows:

**Theorem 12.3.7** (Kjos-Hanssen, Miller, Solomon [66]). \(x \le_{LR} y\) _if and only if_
\((\exists c)(\forall n)(K^y(n) \le K^x(n) + c)\)_._

Combining Corollary 12.2.13 with van Lambalgen's Theorem (12.1.7) offers additional evidence for the randomness thesis:

**Proposition 12.3.8.** _For any two Martin-Löf random reals_ \(x\) _and_ \(y\)_,_ \(x \le_K y\) _implies_ \(y \le_{LR} x\)_._

### 12.3.5 Lowness for strong Martin-Löf randomness

**Theorem 12.3.9** (Downey et al. [23], Nies [111], and Kjos-Hanssen et al. [66]). _For any_ \(x\)_, the following are equivalent:_
(i) _\(x\) is K-trivial;_
(ii) _\(x\) is low for strong Martin-Löf test;_
(iii) _\(x\) is low for strong Martin-Löf randomness._

## 13 More on hyperarithmetic theory

### 13.1 Hyperarithmetic measure theory

The application of measure theory in recursion theory was initiated by Spector [155], Sacks [117] and Leeuw, Moore, Shannon, and Shapiro [19]. Randomness theory on the natural numbers may be viewed as such an application, while higher randomness theory is the union of measure theory with hyperarithmetic theory. In this chapter we discuss these connections to set the stage for higher randomness in the next chapter.

### 13.1.1 The measure of $\Pi_1^1$-sets

Lemma 13.1.1 (Sacks [120]). The set

\{(\varphi, p) \mid p \text{ is rational} \land \varphi \text{ is ranked with } x \text{ as its only free variable} \land \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) = \varphi\}) \ge p\}

is $\Pi_1^1$.

\{(\varphi, p) \mid p \text{ is rational} \land \varphi \text{ is ranked with } x \text{ as its only free variable} \land \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) = \varphi\}) \ge p\}

is $\Sigma_1$ over $(L_{\omega_1^{CK}}, \in)$. We apply Theorem 3.1.8 by performing induction on the rank of $\varphi$.

If $\varphi$ is atomic, then

\{(\varphi, p) \mid p \text{ is rational} \land \varphi\text{'s only free variable is } x \land \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) = \varphi\}) \ge p\}

is recursive. By induction on the complexity of $\varphi$, it is not difficult to see that

\{(\varphi, p) \mid p \text{ is rational} \land \varphi\text{'s only free variable is } x \land \varphi \text{ is of rank } 0 \land \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) = \varphi\}) \ge p\}

is arithmetical and hence $\Sigma_1$ over $(L_{\omega_1^{CK}}, \in)$. Now suppose that the set

Let \(\varphi(x, x^\beta)\) be a formula of rank at most \(\beta\) so that the only free variables are \(x\) and \(x^\beta\). Then \(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta)\) if and only if \(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv \varphi(x, x^\beta \mid \psi)\) for some formula \(\eta(n)\) with rank less than \(\beta\), where \(n\) is the only free variable of \(\psi\).

For simplicity, write \(\varphi(x, x^\beta \mid \psi)\) as \(\varphi(x, \psi)\). Then

\(\{(\lnot(\exists x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta), p) \mid \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta)\}) \ge p \wedge p \text{ is rational} \} = \{(\lnot(\exists x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta), p) \mid (\exists n \text{ of rank} < \beta) (n \text{ is the only free variable of } \psi) \wedge p \text{ is rational} \wedge \mu(\{x \mid (\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv \varphi(x, \psi)\}) \ge p \}.\)

Note that the set of formulas \(\varphi\) of rank \(< \beta\) where \(n\) is the only free variable belongs to \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}}\). Hence the set \(\{(\lnot(\exists x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta), p) \mid p \text{ is rational} \wedge \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta)\}) \ge p \}\) is \(\Sigma_1\) over \((L_{\omega_1^{CK}}, \in)\).

For formulas of the form \((\forall x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta)\) where \(n\) is the only free variable, note that uniformly in \((L_{\omega_1^{CK}}, \in)\) there is an \(\omega\)-enumeration of the set of such formulas. Fix such an enumeration \(\{\psi_i(n)\}_{i < \omega}\). Then

\(\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\forall x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta)\}) \ge p\)

if and only if

\(\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\forall i)\varphi(x, \psi_i)\}) \ge p\)

if and only if

\((\forall j)\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv \wedge_{i \le j} \varphi(x, \psi_i)\}) \ge p.\)

Hence \(\{(\forall x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta), p\} \mid p \text{ is rational} \land \mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\forall x^\beta)\varphi(x, x^\beta)\}) \ge p\}\) is \(\Sigma_1\) over \((L_{\omega_1^{CK}}, \in)\).

We leave it to the reader to verify the other cases. Note that the induction is uniform and hence the lemma follows.

**Corollary 13.1.2.** Let \(p > 0\) be rational. Suppose that \(\varphi(n, y)\) is a ranked formula whose only free unbounded set variable is \(y\). If for any \(n < \omega\), \(\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists y)\varphi(n, y)\}) \ge p\), then there is a \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\) such that \((\forall n)\mu(\{x \mid (\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists y^\beta)\varphi(n, y^\beta)\}) \ge p\).

**Proof.** Let \(m_0\) be fixed so that \(p > 2^{-m_0}\). By Lemma 13.1.1 and the assumption, there is a function \(f : \omega \times (\omega \setminus \{m_0\}) \to \omega_1^{CK}\) such that for every \(n\) and \(m > m_0\),

\(\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists y^{<m_0})\varphi(n, y^{<m_0})\}) \ge p \ge 2^{-m}.\)

Let \(g(n) = \bigcup_{m < \omega} f(n, m)\). By the admissibility of \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}}\), \(g\) is total in \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}}\). Hence for all \(n\), \(\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists y^{<g(n)})\varphi(n, y^{<g(n)})\}) \ge p\). Let \(\beta = \bigcup_{n < \omega} g(n)\). By admissibility again, \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\). Moreover, \((\forall n)\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv (\exists y^\beta)\varphi(n, y^\beta)\}) \ge p\).

**Theorem 13.1.3.** (Sacks [120]). Let \(p\) be rational. The set \(\{(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner, p) \mid p \text{ is rational.} \}\) is \(\Sigma_1^1 \wedge \varphi\)’s only free variable is \(x\) if \(\mu(\{x \mid \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \equiv \varphi\}) \ge p\}\) is \(\Pi_1^1\).

**Proof.** This follows immediately from Lemma 13.1.1 and Corollary 13.1.2.

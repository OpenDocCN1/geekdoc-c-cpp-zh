Define \( T = \{\sigma \in 2^{<\omega} \mid (\exists y \in B)(y \succ \sigma)\} \). Clearly \( B \subseteq [T] \). Since \( B \) is \( A_1^1 \), \( [T] \) is \( \Sigma_1^1 \). Since \( A \) is closed, we have \( [T] \subseteq A \). Also \( A \) is null implies that \( [T] \) is null as well. By Lemma 13.1.10 and a routine application of \( \Pi_1^1 \)-uniformization, there is a total \( A_1^1 \)-function \( f : \omega \to \text{HYP} \) such that for any \( k, f(k) \in \Sigma_1 \cap \text{HYP}, A_{f(k)} \subseteq 2^\omega \setminus [T] \) and \( \mu(A_{f(k)}) > 2^{-k} \). Let

```
\( C = \{z \mid (\forall k)(z_k \notin A_{f(k)})\} \).
```

Then \( C \) is a \( A_1^1 \)-closed nullset. However, \( x \in C \) and this is a contradiction.

# 14.1.2 Base of a cone of Kurtz random reals

From the proof of Theorem 14.1.3, one sees that every hyperdegree above that of \( \mathcal{O} \) contains a \( A_1^1 \)-Kurtz random real. This fails for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness. We say that a hyperdegree \( \mathbf{a} \) is a *base of a cone for T-Kurtz randomness* if for every hyperdegree \( \mathbf{b} \ge \mathbf{a} \), \( \mathbf{b} \) contains a \( T \)-Kurtz random real.

The hyperdegree of \( \mathcal{O} \) is a base of a cone for \( A_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness as proved in Theorem 14.1.3. Not every nonzero hyperdegree is a base of a cone for \( A_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness (see Lemma 14.6.1). Does there exist a base of a cone for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness? If so, such a base, say \( \mathbf{b} \), is not hyperarithmetically reducible to a \( \Pi_1^1 \)-singleton. This means that a hyperdegree which serves as a base must be fairly complicated.

**Lemma 14.1.5.** For any reals \( x \) and \( z \ge_T x' \), there is an \( x \)-Kurtz-random real \( y \equiv_T z \).

**Proof.** Let \( z \ge_T x' \) and fix an enumeration of \( x \)-t.e. open sets \( \{U_n^s\}_{n < \omega} \). We construct an increasing sequence \( \{\sigma_s\}_{1 \le s < \omega} \) of strings as follows.

+   Let \( \sigma_0 = 0 \).

+   Stage \( s + 1 \). Let \( l_0 = 0, l_1 = |\sigma_s| \), and \( l_{n+1} = 2^{l_n} \) for \( n > 1 \). For each such \( n \), let

```
\( A_n = \{\sigma \in 2^{h_{n-1}} \mid (\exists m < n)(\forall i)(\forall j)(l_m \le i, j < l_{m+1} \implies \sigma(i) = \sigma(j))\} \).
```

Then

```
\( |A_n| \le 2 \cdot 2^{-h_{n+1}} \).
```

In other words,

```
\( \mu(B_n = \bigcup \{[\sigma] \mid \sigma \ge \sigma_s \wedge \sigma \notin A_n\}) \ge 2^{-l_1} \cdot (1 - 2^{-h_{n+1} + l_{n+1}}) \).
```

Case 1. There is an \( m > l_1 + 1 \) such that \( |\{\sigma \ge \sigma_s \mid \sigma \in 2^m \cap [\sigma] \subseteq U_k^s\}| > 2^{m-l_1-1} \).

Let \( n = m + 1 \). Then \( l_{n+1} - 1 - l_n > 2 \) and \( l_n > m \). Then there is a \( \sigma \in 2^{l_{n-1}} \setminus A_n \) and a \( \tau \le \sigma \) such that \( [\tau] \subseteq U_k^s \) and \( \tau \ge 2^m \).

Let \( \sigma_{s+1} = \sigma(s)^{[l_{n-1}]} \).

Case 2. Otherwise. Let \( \sigma_{s+1} = \sigma_s^c(s)^{[l_{s-1}]} \).

This completes the construction at stage \( s + 1 \). Let \( U = \bigcup_s \sigma_s \). Obviously the construction is recursive in \( z \) and so \( y \ge_T z \). Moreover, if \( U_k^s \) is of measure 1, then Case 1 holds at stage \( s + 1 \). Hence \( y \) is \( x \)-Kurtz random.

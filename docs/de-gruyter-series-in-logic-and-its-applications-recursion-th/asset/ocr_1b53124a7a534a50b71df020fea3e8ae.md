# Substep 3 (Forcing a minimal cover). This is the only place where $z''$ is used.

Note that by induction on $n, T_n \le_T \oplus_{i<n} X_i$.

Finally, let $z = \bigcup_n \sigma_n$.

By the construction, $T_{n+1} \nleq_T \oplus_{i<n+1} X_i$ for every $n \in \omega$. If $\sigma_n^z$ is total and $\Phi_{e_{n+1}}^z \nleq \oplus_{i<n+1} X_i$, then we have Case (2) at Substep 3 of Step $n+1$. Then it is obvious that $z \le_T \Phi_{e_{n+1}}^z$. Hence $z$ is a minimal cover of $A$.

We show that $z'' \ge_T x$. This is proved by induction on $n$ that $x(n)$ may be $z''$-uniformly computed. To do this, we argue that the index $e_n$ is uniformly computed from $z''$.

Suppose we are at step $n+1$. By inductive hypothesis, assume that $z''$ recursively computes the index $e_n$. By the construction, we have $\Phi_{e_n}^z = T_n$ for all $y \in [T_n]$. Use $z$ to recursively find an initial segment of $z$ which is $R_1(T_n^{L_n(x_n)})$ or $L_1(T_n^{L_n(x_n)})$ for some $k_0$. In the first case, $x(n) = 0$. In the second case, $x(n) = 1$. Note that $\Phi_{k_0}^z$ is the perfect tree in Substep 1 of the construction. Now $z \succ$-recursively find $k_1$ such that $R_1((\Phi_{k_0}^z)^{L_{k_1}(x_n)}) \supset z$. Then $\Phi_{k_1}^z = \mathcal{T}(R_1((\Phi_{k_0}^z)^{L_{k_1}(x_n)}), x_n, T_{n+1,0}) = T_{n+1,1}$. Use $z''$ to decide which case $\Phi_{k_1}^z$ is in. In Case 1, we $z''$-recursively find the least $\sigma \in T_{n+1,1}$ with the required property. Let $S = T_0^{l_k}$. Then use $z$ to compute the $k_2$ such that $\Phi_{k_2}^z = S^R(S^{(l_k)})$. Then $e_{n+1} = k_2$ and $T_{n+1} = \Phi_{k_2}^z$. In Case 2, use $z$ to compute the $k_2$ such that $R_1(T_{n+1,1}^{L_{k_2}(x_n)}) \le z$. Then by the construction, $e_{n+1} = k_2$ and $T_{n+1} = \Phi_{k_2}^z$. Thus $z'' \ge_T x$.

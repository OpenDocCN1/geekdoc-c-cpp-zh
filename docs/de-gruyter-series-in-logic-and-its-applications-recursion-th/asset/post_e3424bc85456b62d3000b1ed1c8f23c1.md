
Lemma 7.1.5. There exists a perfect tree \( T_1 \subseteq T_0 \) such that \( T_1 \le_T T_0 \) and \( F \) is either injective or a constant on \( [T_1] \).

Proof. If there exists \( \sigma \in T_0 \) such that \( \Phi^\sigma_{e_0}(x) = \Phi^\sigma_{e_0}(y) \) for all \( x, y \in [T_0] \cap [\sigma] \), then let \( T_1 = T_0 \cap \{ \tau \in 2^{<\omega} \mid \tau \supset \sigma \} \). Obviously \( F \) is a constant on \( [T_1] \). Otherwise, \( T_0 \)-recursively finds a perfect subtree \( T_1 \subseteq T_0 \) such that \( F \) is injective on \( [T_1] \). \(\square\)

By Lemma 7.1.5, we may assume that \( F \) is injective on \( T_0 \). Let

```
\( S_0 = \{ \tau \in 2^{<\omega} \mid (\exists \sigma \in T_0)(\forall n \le |\tau|)(\tau(n) = \Phi^\sigma_{e_0}(n)) \} \).
```

Then \( S_0 \) is a \( T_0 \)-recursive perfect tree so that \( \Phi_{e_0} \) is a homeomorphism from \( [T_0] \) to \( [S_0] \).

Since \( T_0 \) is perfect, let \( p : 2^{<\omega} \to T_0 \) be an order-preserving, \( T_0 \)-recursive bijection. Then \( p \) may be viewed as a homeomorphism from \( 2^\omega \) to \( [T_0] \). Let \( \pi : 2^{<\omega} \to 2^{<\omega} \) be a recursive function such that for any \( \sigma \) and \( i < 2 \), \( p(\sigma^\frown i) = p(\sigma)^\frown i \) if \( \sigma^\frown i \neq \emptyset \) and \( \sigma(j) = 1 \) for some \( j < |\sigma| \); else \( \pi(\sigma^\frown i) = \sigma^\frown (1-i) \). Then \( \pi \) can be viewed as a homeomorphism from \( 2^\omega \) to \( 2^\omega \) by letting \( \pi(\alpha) = (\bigcup_n \pi(\alpha \upharpoonright n)) \). Let

```
\( p_0 = p \circ \pi \circ p^{-1} \).
```

Then \( p_0 \) is a homeomorphism from \( T_0 \) to \( T_0 \). We leave it to the reader to verify the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1.6. For any \( \sigma \), \( \{\pi^{(n)}(\alpha)\}_{n \in 2^{<\omega}} \) is a permutation of \( 2^{|\sigma|} \).

Let \( p_1 = F \circ p_0 \circ F^{-1} \) be a homeomorphism from \( [S_0] \) to \( [S_0] \) and fix an \( x_0 \in [T_0] \). Since \( T_0 \) is uniformly pointed, there is a pair \( (i_0, j_0) \) such that for any \( n \), \( p_1^{(n)}(x_0) \equiv p_1^{(n+1)}(x_0) \) via \( (i_0, j_0) \). Then \( F(p_0^{(n)}(x_0)) \equiv F(p_0^{(n+1)}(x_0)) \) via \( (i_1, j_1) = \ell((i_0, j_0)) \). Note that for any \( n \), \( F(p_0^{(n)}(x_0)) = p_1^{(n)}(F(x_0)) \). So the real \( \Phi_{e_0}^{p}(p_1^{(n)}(F(x_0))) \) is recursive in \( p_1^{(n)}(x_0) = F(x_0) \).

Given a perfect tree \( T \) and a number \( n \), we say that a finite tree \( S \subseteq T \) codes a \( 2^n \) copy of \( T \) if

- \( S \) is isomorphic to \( 2^n \);
- for any \( \tau \in T \setminus S \), there exists an end node \( \sigma \in S \) such that \( \tau \supset \sigma \).

Lemma 7.1.7. \( S_0 \) is recursive in \( F(x_0) \).

Proof. Note that we may view \( F : [T_0] \to [S_0] \) as a homeomorphism. Hence for any \( \sigma \), there is an \( m \) such that \( p^{-1}(F^{-1}((F(x_0) \upharpoonright m))) \) is of length \( 2^{|\sigma|} \). By Lemma 7.1.6, \( \{\pi^{(n)}(p^{-1}(F^{-1}((F(x_0) \upharpoonright m))))\}_{n \in 2^{<\omega}} \) is a permutation of \( 2^{|\sigma|} \). Since both \( F \) and \( p \) are homeomorphisms, the finite tree

```
\( S_0^{[\sigma]} = \{ \tau \mid (\exists n < 2^{|\sigma|})(\tau \le p_1^{(n)}(F(x_0) \upharpoonright m)) \} \).
```

codes a \( 2^{|\sigma|} \) copy of \( S_0 \).

Thus \( \sigma \in S_0 \) if and only if \( \sigma \in S_0^{[\sigma]} \). Obviously \( S_0^{[\sigma]} \) can be effectively computed from \( \{p_1^{(n)}(F(x_0))\}_{n \in 2^{<\omega}} \). Hence \( S_0 \le_T F(x_0) \). \(\square\)

This shows that \( S_0 \) is a pointed tree. Then there exists an \( x_1 \in [S_0] \) such that \( T_0 \oplus S_0 \le_T x_1 \). Since \( \Phi_{e_0} \) is a homeomorphism from \( T_0 \) to \( S_0 \), it is not difficult to see that the unique real \( y \in [T_0] \) such that \( \Phi_{e_0}^y = x_1 \) is recursive in \( x_1 \). In other words, \( F(y) = x_1 \ge_T y \), a contradiction.

## 7.2 Martin's conjecture for uniformly degree invariant functions

The results discussed in this section are due to Steel [159].

Lemma 7.2.1. Assume ZF + AD + DC. Then \( \le_M \) is a linear ordering on the collection of functions which are increasing and uniformly degree invariant.

Proof. Suppose that \( F \) and \( G \) are increasing uniformly degree invariant functions via \( t \) on a cone \( \{x \mid x \ge_T z_0\} \). Define a set \( A \subseteq \omega^\omega \) so that \( x_0 \oplus x_1 \in A \) if and only if either \( x_1^i \notin 2^\omega \) or

- \( x_0^i \in 2^\omega \);
- \( \Phi_{e_0}^{x_0^i} = x_1^i \) where \( x_0(0) = \langle e_0, n_0 \rangle \), and
- either \( \Phi_{e_1}^{x_1^i} \notin x_0^i \) or \( n_0 \in F(x_0^i) \leftrightarrow n_1 \notin G(x_1^i) \) where \( x_1(0) = \langle e_1, n_1 \rangle \).

Consider a two person game \( \mathcal{G}_A \) played by I and II on \( A \), with I playing \( \langle e_0, n_0 \rangle \) as the first move. Suppose that I has a winning strategy \( \hat{\sigma} \) for \( \mathcal{G}_A \). Fix a real \( y \ge_T \hat{\sigma} \oplus z_0 \oplus t \). By the Recursion Theorem relativized to \( y \), for any number \( m \), there is an \( e_1^m \) such that \( \Phi_{e_1^m} = (\delta(\overline{x_1^m})) \) where \( x_1^m = (\langle e_1^m, m \rangle)^\frown y \). Note that the function \( m \mapsto e_1^m \) is recursive. Let \( x_0^m = \delta(\overline{x_1^m}) \) and \( x_0^m(0) = \langle e_0^m, n_0^m \rangle \). Note that the function \( m \mapsto \langle e_0^m, n_0^m \rangle \) is \( y \)-recursive (actually a constant function). Since \( \hat{\sigma} \) is a winning strategy for I, \( \Phi_{e_0^m}^{x_0^m} = y \) and

\( n_0^m \in F(x_0^m) \leftrightarrow m \notin G(x_1^m) = G(y) \).

Note that \( x_1^m \equiv_T y \) via \( \langle e_0^m, e_1^m \rangle \), and so we have \( F(x_0^m) \equiv_T F(y) \) via \( \langle e_0^m, e_1^m \rangle = (i_0^m, i_1^m) \). Since \( F(y) \ge_T y \), \( t \le_T y \) and \( m \mapsto \langle e_0^m, e_1^m \rangle \) is \( y \)-recursive, the function \( m \mapsto (i_0^m, i_1^m) \) is \( F(y) \)-recursive. Then for any \( m \), we may \( F(y) \)-recursively find \( n_0^m \) and \( i_1^m \) such that \( \Phi_{i_1^m}^{F(y)}(n_0^m) = F(x_0^m)(n_0^m) = 1 - G(y)(m) \). Hence \( G(y) \le_T F(y) \) and so \( G \le_M F \).

If II has a winning strategy, then by a similar argument one shows that \( F \le_M G \).

Given \( x \) and \( y \), we say that \( x \equiv_M y \) via \( (e_0, e_1) \) if there are two recursive functions \( \Phi_{e_0} \) and \( \Phi_{e_1} \) such that for any \( n \),

- \( n \in y \Leftrightarrow \Phi_{e_0}(n) \downarrow \in x \); and
- \( n \in x \Leftrightarrow \Phi_{e_1}(n) \downarrow \in y \).

Lemma 7.2.2. Assume ZF+AD+DC. If \( F \) and \( G \) are increasing uniformly degree invariant functions via \( t \) such that \( F <_M G \), then \( F' \le_M G \).

Proof. Suppose that \( F <_M G \) on the cone \([x \mid x \ge_T z_0]\) but \( F' \not\le_M G \). Let \( p : 2^{<\omega} \to \omega \) be a recursive bijection and for any \( x \), let \( G_1(x) = y \) where

\( y = \{n \mid (\forall m)(p(G(x) \mid m) \neq n)\} \).

Then for each \( x \), \( G_1(x) \equiv_T G(x) \). Moreover, since \( G \) is uniformly degree invariant, so is \( G_1 \).

We apply the same method as that in Lemma 7.2.1. Define \( A \subseteq \omega^\omega \) such that \( x_0 \oplus x_1 \in A \) if and only if either \( x_1^t \notin 2^\omega \) or

- \( x_0^t \in 2^\omega \);
- \( \Phi_{e_1}^{x_0^t} = x_1^t \) where \( x_0(0) = \langle e_0, n_0 \rangle \), and
- either \( \Phi_{e_1}^{x_1^t} \neq x_0^t \) or \( n_0 \in F'(x_0^t) \Leftrightarrow n_1 \notin G_1(x_1^t) \), where \( x_1(0) = \langle e_1, n_1 \rangle \).

A game \( \mathcal{G}_A \) is played by the two players I and II. As in Lemma 7.2.1, if II has a winning strategy for \( \mathcal{G}_A \), then \( F' \le_M G_1 \) which is a contradiction.

Hence I has a winning strategy \( \vartheta \) for \( \mathcal{G}_A \). Fix \( y \ge_T \vartheta \oplus z_0 \oplus t \). By the Recursion Theorem relativized to \( y \), for each \( m \) there is an \( e_1^m \) such that \( \Phi_{e_1^m} = (\delta(x_1^m))' \) where \( x_1^m = (e_1^m, m)^\frown y \). Note that the function \( m \mapsto e_1^m \) is recursive. Let \( x_0^m = \delta(x_1^m) \) and \( x_0^m(0) = \langle e_0^m, n_0^m \rangle \). The function \( m \mapsto (e_0^m, n_0^m) \) is \( y \)-recursive. Since \( \vartheta \) is a winning strategy for I, \( \Phi_{e_0^m}^{x_1^m} = y \) and

\( n_0^m \in F'(x_0^m) \Leftrightarrow m \notin G_1(x_1^m) = G_1(y) \).

Note that \( x_0^m \equiv_T y \) via \( (e_0^m, e_1^m) \). We then have \( F(x_0^m) \equiv_T F(y) \) via \( t(e_0^m, e_1^m) = (i_0^m, i_1^m) \). Since \( t \le_T y \) and \( m \mapsto (e_0^m, e_1^m) \) is \( y \)-recursive, the function \( m \mapsto (i_0^m, i_1^m) \) is \( y \)-recursive as well. Hence there is a \( y \)-recursive function \( m \mapsto (j_0^m, j_1^m) \) such that \( F'(x_0^m) \equiv_T F(y) \) via \( (j_0^m, j_1^m) \). Thus for any \( m \), one may \( y \)-recursively find \( x_0^m \) and an \( j_1^m \) such that

\( F'(y)(\Phi_{j_1^m}(n_0^m)) = F'(x_0^m)(n_0^m) = 1 - G_1(y)(m) \).

Since \( F(y) \ge_T y \), the set \( E_1 = \{m \mid F'(y)(\Phi_{j_1^m}(n_0^m)) = 1\} \) is infinite and \( F(y) \)-r.e. Let \( E_2 \subseteq E_1 \) be infinite \( F(y) \)-recursive. Then for any \( m \in E_2 \), there is an \( n_m \) such that \( p^{-1}(m) = G_1(y) \downarrow n_m \). Thus \( G(y) \le_T F(y) \) and so \( G \le_M F \), contradicting the hypothesis.

We conclude that \( F' \le_M G \).

# ## 1.1.5 Relativization

As in classical recursion theory, all the results above may be relativized to a real \( x \). We use boldface symbols with tilde \( \Sigma_n^0, \Pi_n^0, \Delta_n^0, \Sigma_n^1, \Pi_n^1 \) and \( \Delta_n^1 \) to denote the corresponding sets (of numbers or reals) relativized to a real. A set is _projective_ if it belongs to \( \Pi_n^1 \) for some \( n \).

# ## 1.2 Ordinal notations

A key goal in descriptive set theory is to investigate properties of real numbers and sets of real numbers within the system ZFC. However, many statements about complexity classes above the \( \Pi_1^1 \)-level are independent of ZFC. Hence it is natural to study in greater depth properties of \( \Pi_1^1 \)-sets. Higher recursion theory provides an ideal platform as well as powerful tools for this purpose. In this section, we introduce the notion of a recursive ordinal and an ordinal notation which is at the heart of analysis of \( \Pi_1^1 \)-sets. The recursive ordinals allow one to carry out effective transfinite induction over the ordinal \( \omega_1^{CK} \) similar to what is done over \( \omega_1 \) for transfinite induction.

# ## 1.2.1 Kleene's O

Let \( \langle . \rangle : \omega \times \omega \to \omega \) be a recursive bijection. Define an arithmetical set \( P \subseteq 2^\omega \) such that \( x \in P \) if and only if \( x \) satisfies the following conditions:

+   - (i) \( (1, 2) \in x \);
+   - (ii) \( (\forall m)(\forall n)((m, n) \in x \to \langle n, 2^n \rangle \in x) \);
+   - (iii) \( (\forall e)((\forall n)(\Phi_e(n) \downarrow \land (\forall n)(\Phi_e(n), \Phi_e(n+1)) \in x) \to (\forall n)((\Phi_e(n), 3 \cdot 5^e \in x)) \);
+   - (iv) \( (\forall n)(\forall m)(\forall k)((n, m) \in x \land (m, k) \in x \to \langle n, k \rangle \in x) \).

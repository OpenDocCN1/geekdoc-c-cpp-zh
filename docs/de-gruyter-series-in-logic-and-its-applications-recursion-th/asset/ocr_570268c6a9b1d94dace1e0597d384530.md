It is immediate that $J_0 = \emptyset \in M$. If $\delta > 0$, then $\pi^{1+\delta}(\beta) < \beta$. Then $(J_\beta, \in) \models (\exists x)(\exists s)\varphi(x, \pi^{-1}(\delta), s)$ and so $(M, \in) \models (\exists x)(\exists s)\varphi(x, \delta, s)$. Let $x \in M$ and let $s \in M$ such that $(M, \in) \models \varphi(x, \delta, s)$. By the absoluteness of $\varphi(x, \delta, s)$, any admissible structure $\aleph$ containing $x, s$ and $\delta$ satisfies $\varphi(x, \delta, s)$. By Proposition 3.3.3, $J_\delta \ni x \in M$.

Furthermore, for any $x \in M$, there exists a $\delta < \beta$ such that $\pi^{-1}(x) \in J_\delta$. Hence $(J_\beta, \in) \models (\exists \delta)(\exists y)[(\exists s)\pi^{-1}(x) \in y \wedge \varphi(y, \delta, s)]$. Then $(M, \in) \models (\exists \delta)(\exists y)[(\exists s)x \in y \wedge \varphi(y, \delta, s)]$. By Proposition 3.3.3 again, $x \in J_\delta$ for some $\delta' < \gamma$. We conclude that $J_\gamma \supseteq M$ and hence $J_\gamma = M$.

Suppose $x <_{J_\beta} \pi(x)$ for some $x \in X$. Take $x_0$ to be the $<_{J_\beta}$-least such set. Then $x_0 \in J_\beta$ since $\pi(x_0) \in J_\beta$ and so there is an $x_1 \in X$ such that $\pi(x_1) = x_0$. If $x_0 <_{J_\beta} x_1$, then $\pi(x_0) <_{J_\beta} \pi(x_1) = x_0$ which contradicts the assumption that $x_0 <_{J_\beta} \pi(x_0)$. So $x_1 <_{J_\beta} \pi(x_1) = x_0$ which contradicts the choice of $x_0$ to be the $<_{J_\beta}$-least counterexample.

An immediate consequence of the Condensation Theorem (3.3.7) is that $V = L$ implies the Continuum Hypothesis. We leave the proof as an exercise.

# Corollary 3.3.8. $2^\omega \cap L = 2^\omega \cap L_\alpha$.

Another important tool in the theory of constructibility is the use of Skolem functions. A relation or function is $\Sigma_n(\aleph)$-definable if it is $\Sigma_n$-definable over $\aleph$ (possibly with parameters).

Definition 3.3.9. Given a structure $\mathfrak{M}$, a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function for $\aleph$ is a $\Sigma_n(\aleph)$-definable function $h : \omega \times M \to M$ with parameter $p \in M$ such that for any $A \subseteq M$ which is $\Sigma_n$-definable over $\aleph$ with parameters $p, x \in M$, there is an $i \in \omega$ satisfying $h(i, x) \in A$.

Thus a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function is a $\Sigma_n$-definable choice function for $\Sigma_n$-sets.

# Proposition 3.3.10. Suppose that $a > 0$ is admissible and $h$ is a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function for $(J_a, \in)$. Then

+   (i) for any $x \in J_a$, $x \in \text{Range}(h(\omega \times \{x\}))$ and $(\text{Range}(h(\omega \times \{x\})), \in) \prec_{\Sigma_n} (J_a, \in)$;

+   (ii) if $q \in J_a$ and $X \subseteq J_a$ is closed under ordered pairs, then $X \cup \{q\} \subseteq \text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\})))$ and $(\text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\}))), \in) \prec_{\Sigma_n} (J_a, \in)$.

Proof. Note that (i) is straightforward to verify and is a standard fact in model theory. For (ii), since $X$ is closed under ordered pairs, any finite sequence from $X$ used to define a finite sequence in $\text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\})))$ may be coded by a single parameter. Hence it is routine to show $(\text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\}))), \in) \prec_{\Sigma_n} (J_a, \in)$.

We remark that the existence of a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function is not a trivial fact. In model theory, defining a Skolem function for a structure $\mathfrak{M}$ requires a well-ordering of $M$. In the constructible universe $L$, however, there is a uniformly $\Sigma_1$-definable Skolem function.

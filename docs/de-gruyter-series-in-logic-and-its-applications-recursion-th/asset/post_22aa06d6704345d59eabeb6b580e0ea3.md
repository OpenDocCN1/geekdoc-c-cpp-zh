## 1.3 Effective transfinite induction

### 1.3.1 Recursion theorem

The motivation for introducing recursive ordinals is to perform effective transfinite induction. We make it precise in this section, by first recalling the following theorem famously known as the Recursion Theorem (also called the Kleene Fixed Point Theorem).

### Theorem 1.3.1 (Kleene).
Suppose $f : \omega \to \omega$ is recursive. Then $\Phi_{f(e)} = \Phi_e$ for some $e$.

Proof. By the s-m-n Theorem, there is a recursive function $g$ such that $\Phi_{g(e)} = \Phi_{f(e)}$ for each $e$. Let $i'$ be the index of the recursive function $f \circ g$. Then $\Phi_{i}(g(i)) = \Phi_{f(i)} = \Phi_{g(i)}$.

The Recursion Theorem is a powerful tool in the study of effective computability in classical recursion theory. In higher recursion theory, it is used for effective transfinite induction. The following proposition is the first illustration of such an application.

### Proposition 1.3.2 (Sacks [123]).
Let $<_R$ be an ordering relation on $\omega$ with a minimal element. Suppose $f$ is a recursive function such that for all $e \in \omega$: (i) For each $n$ in the field of $<_R$, $\Phi_e(n)$ is defined whenever $\Phi_{f(i)}(m)$ is defined for all $m <_R n$; (ii) $\Phi_e(e)$ is defined on every minimal element of $<_R$. Then there is an index $i$ such that $\Phi_i = \Phi_{f(i)}$ and $\Phi_i$ is defined on every wellfounded initial segment of $R$.

Proof. By Theorem 1.3.1, there is an $i$ such that $\Phi_i = \Phi_{f(i)}$. By (i), if $n$ is an $<_R$-minimal element, then $\Phi_i(n)$ is defined. By an easy induction and an application of (ii), $\Phi_i$ is defined on every wellfounded initial segment of $R$.

In particular, if $R$ is wellfounded then $\Phi_i$ is defined on the field of $R$. Note that no assumption is made on the complexity of $R$ in Proposition 1.3.2. As long as the recursive function $f$ exists, we may find such an index $i$. In general, some nice properties concerning $R$ are required to guarantee the existence of $f$.

Intuitively, $\Phi_e$ is a “one-step effective induction” operator over $\Phi_e$. For effective transfinite induction, one needs the assumption that $\Phi_e$ itself is produced by $f$ through another function $\Phi_{e'}$, i.e. $\Phi_e = \Phi_{f(e')}$. The Recursion Theorem says that this is possible. We shall see many applications of Proposition 1.3.2 to effective transfinite induction. In this chapter we give detailed verifications for some of these. In subsequent chapters, such details will be suppressed and left to the reader.

### 1.3.2 Operators on ordinal notations

We now define an operator $+_o$ on $\mathcal{O}$. In addition to requiring it to be closed in $\mathcal{O}$ (i.e. \(m \in \mathcal{O}\) and \(n \in \mathcal{O}\) implies \(m +_o n \in \mathcal{O}\)), we also require \(|m| + |n| = |m +_o n|\).

Let \(g\) be a recursive function such that

$$
\Phi_{g(e,m,n)}(k) = \Phi_e(m, \Phi_n(k))
$$

for all \(e, k, m, n\). Intuitively, \(g\) is a function to be used to handle induction at limit stage.

By the s-m-n Theorem, there is a recursive function \(h\) such that

$$
\Phi_{h(e)}(m, n) = \begin{cases} m & n = 1, \\ 2^{\Phi_e(m,k)} & n = 2^k, \\ 3 \cdot 5^{g(e,m,k)} & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 7 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

By the Recursion Theorem, there is a \(c\) such that \(\Phi_{h(c)} = \Phi_c\). Define

$$
m +_o n = \Phi_c(m, n).
$$

It is straightforward to verify that

$$
m +_o n = \begin{cases} m & n = 1, \\ 2^{m+k} & n = 2^k, \\ 3 \cdot 5^{c(m,k)} & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 7 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

Note that

$$
\Phi_{g(e,m,k)}(n) = \Phi_e(m, \Phi_k(n)) = m +_o \Phi_k(n)
$$

for all \(n\). Obviously \(+_o\) is a recursive function on \(\omega \times \omega\). The following is proved by induction on \(<_o\).

### Proposition 1.3.3.
The function \(+_o\) satisfies the following properties: For all \(m, n\),

- (i) \(m, n \in \mathcal{O} \Leftrightarrow m +_o n \in \mathcal{O}\).
- (ii) \(m, n \in \mathcal{O} \Longrightarrow |m +_o n| = |m| + |n|\).
- (iii) \(m, n \in \mathcal{O} \text{ and } n \neq 1 \Longrightarrow m <_o m +_o n\).
- (iv) \(m \in \mathcal{O} \text{ and } k <_o n \Leftrightarrow m +_o k <_o m +_o n\).
- (v) \(m \in \mathcal{O} \land n = k \in \mathcal{O} \Leftrightarrow m +_o n = m +_o k \in \mathcal{O}\).

An interesting application of effective transfinite induction is the following basic result.

### Theorem 1.3.4 (Kleene [71]).
There are two recursive functions $f$ and $g$ such that for all $n \in \omega$,

- (i) $W_{f(n)} = \{m \mid m <_\omega n\}$,
- (ii) $W_{g(n)} = \{(i, j) \mid i <_\omega j <_\omega n\}$.

Proof. (i) By the s-m-n Theorem, there are two recursive functions $p, q$ such that

$$
W_{p(e, m)} = \{m\} \cup W_{\phi_e(m)},
$$
$$
W_{q(e, m)} = \bigcup \{W_{\phi_i(\phi_e(n))} \mid n \in \omega\}.
$$

Fix $e_0$ such that $W_{e_0} = \emptyset$. Define a recursive function $h$ as follows:

$$
\Phi_{h(e)}(n) = \begin{cases} e_0 & n = 1, \\ p(e, k) & n = 2^k, \\ q(e, k) & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

By the Recursion Theorem, there is a constant $c$ such that $\Phi_{h(c)} = \Phi_c$. Set $f = \Phi_c$.

Then

$$
f(n) = \begin{cases} e_0 & n = 1, \\ p(c, k) & n = 2^k, \\ q(c, k) & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

Obviously $f$ is total since both $p$ and $q$ are total. Then

$$
W_{f(n)} = \begin{cases} \emptyset & n = 1, \\ \{k\} \cup W_{f(k)} & n = 2^k, \\ \bigcup \{W_{\phi_i(\phi_e(n))} \mid m \in \omega\} & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

An easy induction shows that $W_{f(n)} = \{m \mid m <_\omega n\}$ for each $n \in \omega$.

(ii) By the s-m-n Theorem, there are two functions $p$ and $q$ such that

$$
W_{p(e, m)} = \{(i, j) \mid i \in W_{f(m)}\} \cup W_{\phi_e(m)},
$$
$$
W_{q(e, m)} = \bigcup \{W_{\phi_i(\phi_e(n))} \mid n \in \omega\},
$$

where $f$ was defined in (i). Fix $e_0$ so that $W_{e_0} = \emptyset$. Let

$$
\Phi_{h(e)}(n) = \begin{cases} e_0 & n = 1, \\ p(e, k) & n = 2^k, \\ q(e, k) & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

By the Recursion Theorem, there is a constant $c$ such that $\Phi_{h(c)} = \Phi_c$. Set $g = \Phi_c$.

Hence

$$
g(n) = \begin{cases} e_0 & n = 1, \\ p(c, k) & n = 2^k, \\ q(c, k) & n = 3 \cdot 5^k, \\ 0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

Note that $g$ is total since both $p$ and $q$ are total. Then

$$
W_{g(n)} = \begin{cases} \emptyset & n=1, \\ \{\langle i, k \rangle \mid i <_O k\} \cup W_{g(k)} & n=2^k, \\ \bigcup \{W_{g(\phi_i(m))} \mid m \in \omega\} & n=3 \cdot 5^k, \\ W_0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

An easy induction shows that $W_{g(n)} = \{\langle i, j \rangle \mid i <_O j <_O n\}$ for $n \in O$.

Theorem 1.3.4 allows one to carry out transfinite induction uniformly for $e \in O$. Here we give two applications of Proposition 1.3.3.

### Lemma 1.3.5.
There is a recursive function $g$ such that for all $e$:

- (i) $g(e) \in O \Leftrightarrow W_e \subseteq \emptyset$.
- (ii) $g(e) \in O \leftrightarrow |n|_O < |g(e)|$ for every $n \in W_e$.

Proof. By the s-m-n Theorem, there is a recursive function $r$ such that $\Phi_{r(e)}(0) = 1$ and the range of $\Phi_{r(e)}$ is $(W_e \setminus \{0\}) \cup \{1\}$. By the s-m-n Theorem again, there is a recursive function $s$ such that $\Phi_{s(e)}(0) = 1$ and $(\forall n)(\Phi_{s(e)}(n+1) = \Phi_{s(e)}(n) \oplus 2^{\Phi_{r(e)}(n+1)})$. Define

$$
g(e) = 3 \cdot 5^{s(e)}.
$$

Note that if $W_e \subseteq \emptyset$, then $g(e) \in O$ by Proposition 1.3.3. If $g(e) \in O$, then $\Phi_{s(e)}(n) \in O$ for each $n$. Hence $W_e \subseteq O$ by Proposition 1.3.3.

Assume $g(e) \in O$ and $n \in W_e$. Then $n <_O \Phi_{s(e)}(m) + 2^n = \Phi_{s(e)}(m+1) <_O 3 \cdot 5^{s(e)} = g(e)$ where $m$ is the least number such that $\Phi_{r(e)}(m+1) = n$.

As a consequence of the proof of Lemma 1.3.5, we have

### Corollary 1.3.6.
There is a recursive function $g$ such that for all $e$, if $W_e \subseteq O$ and $<_O$ on $W_e$ is linear, then $g(e) \in O$ and $(\forall n)(n \in W_e \rightarrow n <_O g(e))$.

Corollary 1.3.6 may be viewed as a $\Sigma^0_1$-boundedness principle, i.e. there is an effective way to find an upper bound for any r.e. subset of $O$. Lemma 1.3.5 will be significantly strengthened in Theorem 1.5.5.


Suppose that $x$ is $\Pi^1_1$-semitraceable, $\omega_1^x = \omega_1^{ck,x}$, and $f \leq_h x$. Fix a $\Pi^1_1$-partial function $p$ for $f$ witnessing the semitraceability of $x$. Since $p$ is a $\Pi^1_1$-function, there is a recursive injection $h$ such that $p(m) = m \leftrightarrow h(m, m) \in O$.

Let $R(n, m)$ be a $\Pi^1_1(x)$-relation such that $R(n, m)$ if and only if there exists a $k$, where $m > k \geq n$, so that $f(k) = p(k)$. Then there is a total function $g$ uniformizing $R$ such that $g$ is $\Pi^1_1(x)$, and hence $\Delta^1_1(x)$. Thus for every $n$ there is an $m \in [g(n), g(g(n))]$ such that $f(m) = p(m)$. Let $g'(0) = g(0)$, and $g'(n + 1) = g(g'(n))$ for all $n < \omega$. Define a $\Pi^1_1(x)$-relation $S(n, m)$ such that $S(n, m)$ if and only if $m \in [g'(n), g'(n + 1))$ and $p(m) = f(m)$. Uniformizing $S$, we obtain a $\Delta^1_1(x)$-function $g''$.

Let $H = \{h(m, k) \mid k \in (\exists n)(g''(n) = m \land f(m) = k)\}$. Then $H$ is $\Delta^1_1(x)$ and since $\omega_1^x = \omega_1^{ck,x}$, $H \subseteq O_n$ for some $n \in O$. Since $O_n$ is $\Delta^1_1$, we may define a $\Delta^1_1$-function $\bar{f}$ by setting $\bar{f}(i) = j$ if $h(i, j) \in O_n$ and $\bar{f}(i) = 1$ otherwise. Then there are infinitely many $i$ such that $f(i) = \bar{f}(i)$.

The following clarifies the relationship between traceability and semitraceability.

## Proposition 13.3.11 (Kjos-Hanssen, Nies, Stephan and Yu [68]). Every $\Delta^1_1$-traceable real is $\Delta^1_1$-dominated and $\Delta^1_1$-semidominated.

Proof. Clearly every $\Delta^1_1$-traceable real is $\Delta^1_1$-dominated. Let $x$ be a $\Delta^1_1$-traceable real and let $f$ be a $\Delta^1_1(x)$-function. Define $g(n) = \langle f(2^n), f(2^n + 2), \dots, f(2^{n+1} - 1) \rangle$. Then there is a $\Delta^1_1$ trace $T$ for $g$ such that $|T|_n \leq n$ for all $n$.

For $2^n + 1 \leq m \leq 2^{n+1}$, let $\bar{f}[m]$ be the $(m - 2^n)$-th entry of the tuple which is the $(m - 2^n)$-th element of $T_n$ if there exists such a tuple, and let $\bar{f}(m) = 1$ otherwise. It is not difficult to see that for every $n$ there is at least one $m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1})$ such that $f(m) = \bar{f}(m)$.

## Corollary 13.3.12. A real $x$ is $\Delta^1_1$-traceable if and only if for every $\bar{y}$ hyperarithmetic in $x$, there is a hyperarithmetic function $f$ such that

$(\forall n)(\exists m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1}))(f(m) = \bar{f}(m))$.

## Proposition 13.3.13. For any $x$, the following are equivalent.

- (i) $x$ is $\Delta^1_1$-semidominated and $\Delta^1_1$-dominated;
- (ii) For every function $g \leq_h x$, there exist an increasing $\Delta^1_1$-function $f$ and a $\Delta^1_1$-function $F : \omega \to \mathscr{P}_{<\omega}(\omega)$ with $|F(n)| \leq n$ such that

$(\forall n)(\exists m \in [f(n), f(n+1)))(g(m) \in F(m))$.

Proof. (i) $\Rightarrow$ (ii): Immediate because $1 \leq n$.

(ii) $\Rightarrow$ (i). Suppose $\bar{g} \leq_h x$. Without loss of generality, assume $\bar{g}$ is nondecreasing. Let $f$ and $F$ be the corresponding $\Delta^1_1$-functions. Let $u(n) = \sum_{k \in [f(n)+1)} k_{k \in F(k)}$ and note that $u$ is a $\Delta^1_1$-function dominating $\bar{g}$. Let

$h(n) = \langle \bar{g}(2^n + 1), \bar{g}(2^n + 2), \dots, \bar{g}(2^{n+1} - 1) \rangle$. Then by assumption there are corresponding $\Delta^1_1$-functions $f_n$ and $F_n$. For every $n$ and $m \in [2^n, 2^{n+1})$, let $g(m) = \text{the } (m - 2^n)\text{-th entry of the } (m - 2^n)\text{-th element in } F_h(n) \text{ if such an } m \text{ exists, and let } g(m) = 1 \text{ otherwise. Then } g \text{ is a } \Delta^1_1\text{-function semitracing } \bar{g}$.

To separate $\Delta^1_1$-traceability from the conjunction of $\Delta^1_1$-semitraceability and $\Delta^1_1$-domination, one applies a modified Sacks forcing construction.

## Definition 13.3.14.

- (i) A $\Delta^1_1$-perfect tree $T \subseteq 2^{<\omega}$ *fully splits at n* if for every $\sigma \in T$ with $|\sigma| \in [2^n, 2^{n+1})$, both $\sigma 0$ and $\sigma 1$ are in $T$. Then $n$ is said to be a *fully splitting level* of $T$.
- (ii) A $\Delta^1_1$-perfect tree $T \subseteq 2^{<\omega}$ is *clumpy* if there are infinitely many $n$'s where $T$ fully splits at $n$.

Let $\mathbb{F} = \langle F, \le \rangle$ be the partial order of clumpy trees ordered under inclusion. Given a formula $\varphi$ in the language $\mathscr{L}(\omega_1^{ck}, X)$ of ramified analytical hierarchy (§ 5.1.1), define the forcing relation $T \Vdash \varphi$ as in Sacks forcing:

- If $\varphi$ is a ranked sentence and $(\forall x \in T)(\exists(\alpha \in O^{ck}, x) \Vdash \varphi)$, then $T \Vdash \varphi$;
- If $\varphi(y)$ is unranked and $T \Vdash \varphi(y \mid \psi)$ for some $\psi$ of rank at most $\beta$, then $T \Vdash (\exists y)^\beta \varphi(y^\alpha)$;
- If $T \Vdash (\exists y)^\beta \varphi(y^\alpha)$, then $T \Vdash (\exists y)\varphi(y)$;
- If $\varphi(n)$ is unranked and $T \Vdash \varphi(\bar{m})$ for some numeral $\bar{m}$, then $T \Vdash (\exists n)\varphi(n)$;
- If $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are unranked, $T \Vdash \varphi$ and $T \Vdash \psi$, then $T \Vdash \varphi \land \psi$;
- If $\varphi$ is unranked and $(\forall p)(P \subseteq T \rightarrow P \Vdash \varphi)$, then $T \Vdash \neg \varphi$.

By Theorem 5.1.1, we have the following.

## Lemma 13.3.15.

The set

\(\{ (T, \ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \mid T \text{ is a condition } \land \varphi \text{ is } \Sigma_1^1 \land T \Vdash \varphi \}\)

is $\Pi_1^1$.

## Lemma 13.3.16.

- (i) Let $\langle \varphi_i \rangle_{i < \omega}$ be a hyperarithmetic sequence of $\Sigma_1^1$-sentences. Suppose that for every $i$ and $Q \le T$, there exists an $R \le Q$ such that $R \Vdash \varphi_i$. Then there exists an $R \le T$ such that $R \Vdash \varphi_i$ for every $i$.
- (ii) $(\forall \psi)(\forall T)(\exists Q \le T)(Q \Vdash \psi \lor Q \Vdash \neg \psi)$.

Proof. Using the notation $P \Vdash n = \{ \tau \in 2^{<\omega} \mid \tau \in P \}$, define a $\Pi_1^1$-relation $\mathcal{R}$ by

\(\mathcal{R}(R, i, \sigma, P) \leftrightarrow (\sigma \in R \wedge P \subseteq R \wedge P \Vdash \varphi_i \wedge P \Vdash [\sigma] = \{ \tau \mid \tau < \sigma \} \wedge \log |\sigma| - 1 \text{ is the } i\text{-th fully splitting level of } R).\)

Then $\mathcal{R}$ is uniformized by a partial $\Pi_1^1$-function $F : F \times \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \rightarrow \mathbf{F}$. Using $F$, a hyperarithmetic family $\langle P_\sigma \mid \sigma \in 2^{<\omega} \rangle$ may be defined by recursion on $\sigma$. Let \( P_\alpha = T \). If \( \sigma \notin P_\alpha \), let \( P_\sigma \cap P_\alpha = \emptyset \). If \( \log |\sigma| - 1 \) is not a fully splitting level of \( P_\alpha \), set \( P_\sigma \cap P_\alpha = P_\alpha \). Otherwise, \( P_\sigma \cap P_\alpha = \emptyset, P_\sigma \cup P_\alpha \subseteq P_\alpha, P_\sigma \cap [\sigma], P_\sigma \cap [\sigma] \models [\sigma] = \{ \tau \mid \tau \prec \sigma \} \) and \( P_\sigma \cap P_\alpha \models \bigwedge_{i \le i} \psi_i \), where \( \log |\sigma| - 1 \) is the \( i \)-th fully splitting level of \( T \).

Let \( R = \cap_i \bigcup_{|g|=n} P_\alpha \). Then \( R \in \mathbf{F} \). It is routine to verify that \( R \models \varphi_i \) for every \( i \). The proof of (ii) is similar to the proof of Lemma 5.3.4.

A real \( x \) is generic if for every dense set \( \mathscr{D} \subseteq \mathbf{F} \) arithmetical in \( \mathcal{O} \), there is a \( T \in \mathscr{D} \) such that \( x \in [T] \). One can show as in the proof of Lemma 5.3.6 that for every sentence \( \varphi \) and generic \( x \),

\( \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \models \varphi \iff (\exists P)(x \in P \land P \models \varphi) \).

## Lemma 13.3.17.

- (i) \( \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \) satisfies \( \Delta_1^1 \)-comprehension. Hence \( \omega_1^x = \omega_1^{CK} \);
- (ii) \( x \) is \( \Delta_1^1 \)-dominated and \( \Delta_1^1 \)-semitraceable;
- (iii) \( x \) is not \( \Delta_1^1 \)-traceable.

Proof. The proof of (i) is the same as that of Lemma 5.3.7 and is left to the reader.

For (ii), by Proposition 13.3.13, it suffices to show that for every condition \( g \le_h x \), there exists an increasing \( \Delta_1^1 \)-function \( f \) and a \( \Delta_1^1 \)-function \( F : \omega \to \omega^\omega \) such that

- (1) \( |F(n)| \le n \);
- (2) every interval \( [f(n), f(n) + 1) \) captures an \( m \) with \( g(m) \in F(m) \).

Since \( g \le_h x \) and \( \omega_1^x = \omega_1^{CK} \), there is a ranked formula \( \varphi \) such that for every \( n \), \( g(n) = m \) if and only if \( \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \models \varphi(n, m) \). Hence there is a condition \( S \models (\forall n)(\exists m)\varphi(n, m) \). Let \( T \subseteq S \). As in the proof of Lemma 13.3.16, one can build a hyperarithmetic sequence \( \langle P_\sigma \rangle_{\sigma \in 2^{<\omega}} \) of conditions such that

\( P \models \varphi(0, [m_0, m_0 + 1)) \) for \( i \le 1 \)

where \( \sigma \in P_\alpha \) and \( \log |\sigma| - 1 \) is a fully splitting level of \( P_\alpha \). Let \( Q \) be as defined in the proof of Lemma 13.3.16. Let \( f \) be the \( \Delta_1^1 \)-function such that \( f(0) = 0 \), and \( f(n + 1) \) is the least number \( k > f(n) \) so that \( m_0 \) is defined for some \( \sigma \) with \( f(n) < |\sigma| < k \). Let \( F(n) = \{0\} \cup \{m_0 \mid |\sigma| = n\} \), and note that \( f \) is a \( \Delta_1^1 \)-function. Then

\( Q \models (\forall n)(|F(n)| \le n) \land (\forall n)(\exists m \in [f(n), f(n) + 1))(\exists i \in F(m))(\varphi(m, i)) \).

This implies that

\( Q \models (\exists F)(\exists f)[(\forall n)(|F(n)| \le n) \land (\forall n)(\exists m \in [f(n), f(n) + 1))(\exists i \in F(m))(\varphi(m, i))] \).

Since \( T \) is an arbitrary condition stronger than \( S \), one has

\( S \models (\exists F)(\exists f)[(\forall n)(|F(n)| \le n) \land (\forall n)(\exists m \in [f(n), f(n) + 1))(\exists i \in F(m))(\varphi(m, i))] \).

Since \( x \in S \),

\( \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \models (\exists F)(\exists f)[(\forall n)(|F(n)| \le n) \land \)
\( (\forall n)(\exists m \in [f(n), f(n) + 1))(\exists i \in F(m))(\varphi(m, i))] \).

Thus \( x \) is \( \Delta_1^1 \)-dominated and \( \Delta_1^1 \)-semitraceable.

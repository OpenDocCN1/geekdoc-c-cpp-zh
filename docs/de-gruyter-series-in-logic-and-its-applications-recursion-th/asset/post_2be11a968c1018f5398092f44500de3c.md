## 8.4 Martin's conjecture for \(\Pi^1_1\)-functions

### 8.4.1 \(\Pi^1_1\)-Martin's conjecture in \(L\)

Since Borel determinacy is a theorem of ZF + DC [93], the results in Chapter 7 hold under ZF + DC for \(\Delta^1_1\)-functions. Since Chapter 7 is concerned with total functions, by Proposition 11.8 (v) the results apply to total \(\Pi^1_1\)-functions as well. However, the situation changes when it comes to partial \(\Pi^1_1\)-functions. Throughout this section, a function \(F\) is said to be \(\Pi^1_1\) if its graph \(G^F\) is \(\Pi^1_1\). We prove that Martin's conjecture fails for certain \(\Pi^1_1\)-functions under the assumption \(V = L\). For a start, Lemma 7.2.1 says (under ZF + AD + DC) that \(\le_M\) is a linear ordering for increasing uniformly degree invariant functions. The conclusion fails if one assumes \(V = L\).

**Theorem 8.4.1.** Assume \(V = L\). There is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-uniformly degree invariant function \(F\) that is increasing and order preserving on a cone such that for all \(y\), there exist reals \(x_0, x_1 \ge_T y\) satisfying \(F(x_0) \equiv_T x_0\) and \(F(x_1) \ge_T 0^{(\omega)}\).

Proof. Assume \(V = L\). Define \(P(x, y)\) as

\(y \in L_{\omega_1^y} \wedge 0^{O^y} = (y)_0 \wedge (y)_1 = x \wedge (y)_2 \text{ is the } <_L\text{-least real such that } (y)_2 \not<_T (y)_0.\)

Since "\(0^{O^y} = (y)_0\)" is \(\Pi^1_1\), \(P\) is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-cofinally progressive relation. By Theorem 8.2.4, there is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-set \(A\) satisfying the prescribed properties in J.

By the definition of P, every real is Turing below some real in A. Moreover, P ensures that \(<_T\) is a well ordering and consistent with \(<_L\) on A.

Define \(F(x) = y\) if

- (1) \(y \in A\);
- (2) \(x \le_T y\);
- (3) \(x \not\ge_T 0 \Rightarrow (\forall n) x \not<_T ((y)_1)_n\).

Roughly speaking, \(y\) is the \(<_T\)-least real in \(A\) such that \(x \le_T y\).

By (3) and the property of \(A\), \(F\) is well defined. Furthermore, it is \(\Pi^1_1\), increasing, order preserving and uniformly degree invariant (\(x \equiv_T y\) implies \(F(x) = F(y)\)). Moreover, since \(F(x) = x\) for every \(x \in A\), we have \(F(x) \equiv_T x\) cofinally.

For every real \(s\), choose \(x_s \in A\) such that \(x_s \ge_T s\). Then for the \(<_T\)-least real \(y \in A\) above \(x_s\), there is a \(z\) coding \(\{x \mid x <_L y \wedge x \in A\} = \{x \mid x <_T y \wedge x \in A\}\) so that \(P(z, y)\) holds. Obviously \(x'_s \le_T z \le_T O^y\). By the definition of P,

\(O^{x'} \le_T O^{y'} \le_T \Sigma y,\)

and \((y)_1 = z\). Thus

\(\{((y)_1)_n \mid n \in \omega\} = \{r \mid r \le_T x \wedge r \in A\}.\)

Since \(x'_s \le_T r\) for all \(r \in A\) with \(r \le_T x'_s\), we have \(x'_s \le_T ((y)_1)_n\) for all \(n \in \omega\). Hence

\(F(x'_s) = y.\)

Thus the function \(F\) is \(M\)-incomparable with the function \(G\) where \(G(x) \equiv_T x'\) for every \(x\).

Assume ZF + DC + AD. By Lemma 7.14, if \(F\) is uniformly degree invariant and nonincreasing on a cone, then \(F(x) <_T x\) on a cone \(C = \{x \mid x \ge_T z_0\}\) for some \(z_0\). We show that this also fails for \(\Pi^1_1\)-functions under \(V = L\).

**Theorem 8.4.2.** Assume \(V = L\). There is a uniform degree invariant, nonincreasing \(\Pi^1_1\)-function \(F\) such that \(F(x) <_T x\) on a cone. In particular, \(F\) is not constant on a cone.

**Proof.** Let \(A\) be the \(\Pi^1_1\)-set as constructed in Theorem 8.4.1. Define \(F(x) = y\) if and only if there is a real \(z \equiv_T y'\) such that

- (1) \(z \in A\);
- (2) the degree of \(y\) is minimal;
- (3) \(y = O_e^x\) for some \(e\) such that for all \(i < e\), if the degree of \(\Phi_i^x\) is minimal then \((\Phi_i^x)' \# z\), and
- (4) \(x >_T 0 \Rightarrow (\forall n)(x \le_T ((z)_1)_n)\).

We leave it to the reader to verify that \(F\) is the desired counterexample.

By Lemma 7.2.4, ZF + AD + DC implies that \(\le_M\) is a well-ordering on the set of increasing uniform degree invariant functions. This again fails under the assumption \(V = L\).

**Theorem 8.4.3.** Assume \(V = L\). There is a sequence of uniform degree invariant, increasing, \(\Pi^1_1\)-functions \(\{f_n\}_{n < \omega}\) such that \(f_{n+1} <_M f_n\) for all \(n\). Thus \(<_M\) is not a prewellordering.

**Proof.** Define \(P(x, y)\) by

\(y \in L_{\omega_1^y} \wedge (y)_0 = x \wedge (y)_1 \text{ is the } <_L\text{-least such that}\)
\((y)_1 \le_T (y)_0 \wedge y \equiv_T (y)_0 \oplus (y)_1 \oplus (y)_2).\)

Clearly \(P(x, y)\) is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-cofinally progressive relation. By Theorem 8.2.4, there is a \(\Pi^1_1\)-set \(A\) with the property prescribed by P.

By the definition of P, every real is Turing below some element of \(A\) and \(<_T\) is a well-ordering consistent with \(<_L\) on \(A\).

It is not difficult to see that there is an arithmetical set \( W \subseteq \{(m, n, z) \mid m, n < \omega \land z \in 2^\omega\} \) such that \( (W_n^z)_{n < \omega} \), where \( W_n^z = \{m \mid (m, n, z) \in W\} \), is a sequence of \( z \)-r.e. sets satisfying \( z <_T W_{n+1}^z \leq_T W_n^z \) for each \( n \).

Let \( f_n(x) = y \) if there exists a \( z <_T y \) such that

- (i) \( z \in A \);
- (ii) \( z \geq_T x \);
- (iii) \( y = W_{n+1}^z \);
- (iv) \( x >_T 0 \Rightarrow (\forall m)(x \not\leq_T ((z)_0)_m) \).

Roughly speaking, \( y \) is \( W_{n+1}^z \) for the \( <_T \)-least \( z \) in \( A \) with \( z \geq_T x \).

Clearly \( \{f_n\}_{n < \omega} \) is a sequence of \( \Pi^1_1 \)-functions. Note that for any \( x <_T y \) in \( A \), \( x' \leq_T y \). Hence for every \( n \), \( f_n \) is degree invariant and increasing.

By (iv) and the property of \( A \), \( f_n \) is well defined. By the choice of \( \{W_n^z\}_{n < \omega} \), \( \{f_n\}_{n < \omega} \) is a \( <_M \)-descending sequence.

### 8.4.2 The consistency strength of \(\Pi^1_1\)-Martin's conjecture

Given that the results in Chapter 7 are established under AD which is essentially a large cardinal assumption, one would expect a direct connection between Martin's conjecture and large cardinals. The first level for such an investigation is clearly \( \Pi^1_1 \). We prove that if \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Martin's conjecture is true, then \( 0^\sharp \) exists.\(^2\) Define the Friedman set \( F^* \) as
\( F^* = \{x \mid (\forall a < \omega_1^x)(\forall a \subseteq a)(a \in L_{a^+_1} \rightarrow a \in L_{a+1}[x]) \} \)

It is not difficult to see that \( F^* \) is \( \Sigma^1_1 \).

**Lemma 8.4.4.** \( F^* \) is cofinal in the Turing degrees.

Proof. For any real \( z \), let
\( F(z) = \{x \oplus z \mid (\forall a < \omega_1^x)(\forall a \subseteq a)(a \in L_{a^+_1} \rightarrow a \in L_{a+1}[x \oplus z]) \} \).
Then \( F(z) \) is \( \Sigma^1_1(z) \) and nonempty. By Gandy's Basis Theorem (2.5.3) relativized to \( z \), there is a \( x \) such that \( \omega_1^{x \oplus z} = \omega_1^z \) and \( x \oplus z \in F(z) \). Then \( x \oplus z \in F^* \).

We will use the following result due to Harrington. A sketch of the proof is given in Exercises 8.4.3 to 8.4.8.

**Lemma 8.4.5** (Harrington [45]). If \( 0^\sharp \) does not exist, then \( \tilde{F}^* = 2^\omega \setminus F^* \) is cofinal in the Turing degrees.

**Theorem 8.4.6** (Chong, Wang and Yu [13]). If there is no uniformly order-preserving \( \Pi^1_1 \)-function \( G \) such that \( \{x \mid G(x) = 0^{0^{x'}}\} \) and \( \{x \mid G(x) = 0^{0^{0^{x'}}}\} \) are cofinal in the Turing degrees, then \( 0^\sharp \) exists.

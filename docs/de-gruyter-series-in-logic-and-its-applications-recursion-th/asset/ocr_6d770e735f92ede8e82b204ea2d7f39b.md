Since \( x \in [T_0] \cap [T_1] \),

\(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \vDash (\forall n)\varphi_0(x \upharpoonright n) \wedge (\forall n)\varphi_1(x \upharpoonright n).\)

By the genericity of \( x \) and the definition of forcing for ranked formulas, there is a condition \( T \) such that \( x \in [T] \) and

\( T \vDash (\forall n)\varphi_0(\dot{x} \upharpoonright n) \wedge (\forall n)\varphi_1(\dot{x} \upharpoonright n).\)

This means that

\((\forall z)(z \in [T] \Rightarrow \mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, z) \vDash (\forall n)\varphi_0(z \upharpoonright n) \wedge (\forall n)\varphi_1(z \upharpoonright n))\)

and so \( T \le T_0, T_1 \).

# **Lemma 5.5.6.** Suppose that \( x \) is Sacks generic. Then for any sentence \( \varphi \),

\(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \vDash \varphi \Rightarrow (\exists T)(x \in [T] \land T \Vdash \varphi).\)

Proof. If \( \varphi \) is ranked, then by the definition of forcing, \((\exists T)(x \in [T] \land T \Vdash \varphi)\) implies \(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \vDash \varphi\). Conversely suppose that \(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \vDash \varphi\). The genericity of \( x \) implies that there is a condition \( T \) such that \( x \in [T] \) and either \( T \Vdash \varphi \) or \( T \Vdash \neg \varphi \). Then by the definition of forcing again, \( T \Vdash \varphi \).

If \( \varphi \) is unranked, then one may prove the lemma by induction on the complexity of \( \varphi \) as was done in Theorem 5.24. The only difference with Cohen forcing is when \( \varphi \) is the conjunction of two sentences, but this is resolved by applying Lemma 5.5.35.

# **Lemma 5.5.7.** If \( x \) is Sacks generic, then \(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x)\) satisfies \(\Delta_1^1\)-comprehension and hence \(\omega_1^x = \omega_1^{CK}\).

Proof. Suppose that

\(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \vDash (\forall n)(\exists z)\varphi(n, z) \leftrightarrow (\forall z)\psi(n, z),\)

where both \(\varphi(n, z)\) and \(\psi(n, z)\) are arithmetical.

Thus

\(\mathfrak{A}(\omega_1^{CK}, x) \vDash (\forall n)(\exists z)(\varphi(n, z) \vee \neg \psi(n, z)).\)

Let \( T \) be a condition such that

\( T \Vdash (\forall n)(\exists z)(\varphi(n, z) \vee \psi(n, z)).\)

By the definition of forcing,

\((\forall S \le T)(\neg S \Vdash (\exists n)(\neg \exists z)(\varphi(n, z) \vee \neg \psi(n, z))).\)

Hence

\((\forall S \le T)(\forall n)(\neg S \Vdash \neg \exists z(\varphi(n, z) \vee \neg \psi(n, z))),\)

implying that

\((\forall P \le T)(\forall n)(\exists S \le P)(S \Vdash \exists z(\varphi(n, z) \vee \neg \psi(n, z))).\)

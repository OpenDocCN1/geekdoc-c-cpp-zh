
## Define

```
J_0[A] = \emptyset,
J_{\beta+1}[A] = \omega^\omega(J_\beta[A]),
J_\lambda[A] = \bigcup_{\beta<\lambda} J_\beta[A], \text{ if } \lambda \text{ is a limit ordinal.}
J[A] = \bigcup_\beta J_\beta[A]
```

If \( A = \emptyset \), then we write \( J \) for \( J[A] \). Intuitively and according to Jensen [55], \( J_{\beta+1} \) is the result of “stretching” the collection of the definable subsets of \( J_\beta \) upwards by \( \omega \)-levels in rank without adding new sets. The proof of the following proposition may be found in [22].

### Proposition 3.3.3. For all \(\beta\),

- \( J_\beta[A] \) is transitive.
- There is a \(\Sigma_1\)-formula \(\varphi(u, w)\) such that for any two ordinals \( v < \beta \), \( (J_\beta, \in) \models \varphi(v, x) \) if and only if \( x = J_v \);
- There is a \(\Sigma_1\)-formula \(\varphi_\prec(u, v)\) such that
  - (i) for any \(\beta\), \(\varphi_\prec(u, v)\) defines a well-ordering \( <_J \) on \( J_\beta \);
  - (ii) for any \( v > \beta \), if \( y \in J_\beta \) and \( (J_v, \in) \models \varphi_\prec(x, y) \), then \( x \in J_\beta \) and \( (J_\beta, \in) \models \varphi_\prec(x, y) \).
- Theorem 3.3.2 remains true for \( J_\beta \);
- \( J_{\beta+1}[A] \cap \mathscr{P}(J_\beta[A]) = \text{Def}^\omega(J_\beta[A]) \).
- \( L_\beta[A] \subseteq J_\beta[A] \subseteq L_{\omega\beta} \).
- \( J_\beta[A] = L_\beta[A] \) if and only if \( \alpha\beta = \beta \).
- \( L[A] = \bigcup_\beta J_\beta[A] \);
- Theorem 3.3.2 remains valid for \( J \).

The Gödel operators provide an algorithmic approach to the construction of \( L \). It reveals the “black box” hidden within the reference to definability. Based on Proposition 3.3.3, we may think of \( L \) as being obtained by an effective transfinite induction over the class of ordinals, analogous to the way hyperarithmetic sets are defined over \( \omega_1^{\text{CK}} \). This connection is useful as it suggests initial segments of \( L \) as models of computation in the general setting.

## 3.3.2 Condensation and Skolemization

If one works within a structure \( \mathfrak{M} \) and performs the stage-by-stage construction of \( L \), then one arrives at the same \( L_\beta \) for each \( \beta \in M \) if \( \mathfrak{M} \) satisfies a sufficiently strong condition such as admissibility. The following theorem formalizes this idea. Let \( (L_\beta)^\mathfrak{M} \) denote the \( L_\beta \) constructed in \( \mathfrak{M} \).

### Theorem 3.3.4. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be a transitive model of KP. Then for each $\beta < o(M)$, $L_\beta \in M$ and $(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M} = L_\beta$.

**Proof.** By Theorem 3.3.2, the formula “$x = L_\beta$” is $\Delta_1$-definable over $\mathcal{M}$. By the absoluteness of $\Delta_0$-formulas, $L_\beta \in M$ and $(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M} = L_\beta$.

### Corollary 3.3.5. Suppose $\mathcal{M}$ is transitive, admissible and $\mathcal{M} \models \text{“V = L”}$. Then $M = L_a$ where $a = o(\mathcal{M})$.

### Theorem 3.3.6. $a$ is admissible if and only if $(L_a, \in)$ is admissible.

**Proof.** If $(L_a, \in)$ is admissible, then $a$ is admissible.

If $a = o(\mathcal{M})$ for some admissible $\mathcal{M}$, then $(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M} = L_\beta$ for each $\beta < a$ by Theorem 3.3.4. Hence it suffices to show that $L_a = \bigcup_{\beta < a}(L_\beta)^\mathcal{M}$ is admissible. We verify that $\Delta_0$-collection holds in $L_a$ and leave the verification of other properties to the reader.

Suppose that $(L_a, \in) \models (\forall x \in a)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y)$. By the absoluteness of $\Delta_0$-formulas, for each $x \in a$, there is a (least) $\beta < a$ such that

$\mathcal{M} \models (\exists y \in \mathcal{E}) \varphi(x, y).$

Then Theorem 3.3.2 and Theorem 3.1.6 on $\Sigma$-collection imply that there is an $a_0 < a$ such that $a \in L_{a_0}$, and

$\mathcal{M} \models (\forall x \in a)(\exists \beta < a_0)(\exists y \in L_\beta)\varphi(x, y).$

The absoluteness of $\Delta_1$-formulas now gives

$(L_a, \in) \models (\forall x \in a)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y).$

Then if $y \in (L_a, \in) \models (\exists x \in a)\varphi(x, y)) \in L_{a_0+1}$ and is contained in $L_a$.

Obviously if $a = \omega$, then $L_a = \text{HF}$. Hence $L_a$ is the smallest admissible set (cf. Theorem 3.2.4). Note that if $a > \omega$ is admissible, then $\omega a = a$.

A fundamental property of $L$ is the following known as condensation:

### Theorem 3.3.7. Suppose that $\beta > 0$ and $(X, \in) \prec_{\Sigma_1} (J_\beta, \in)$. Then $(X, \in) \cong (J_\gamma, \in)$ for some $\gamma \le \beta$. Moreover $\pi(x) \supseteq x$ for every $x \in X$.

**Proof.** Suppose $(X, \in) \prec_{\Sigma_1} (J_\beta, \in)$. Then $(X, \in)$ is wellfounded and satisfies the extensionality axiom. By Theorem 3.1.12, there is a transitive set $M$ such that

$\pi : (X, \in) \cong (M, \in).$

We claim that $M = J_\gamma$ where $\gamma = \sup(\pi[\delta]) \in \delta \cap \text{On}$.

Clearly $\langle M, \in \rangle \prec_{\Sigma_1}^{\Sigma_1} \langle J_\beta, \in \rangle$ and $M$ is transitive. By Proposition 3.3.3, there is a $\Sigma_0$-formula $\varphi(x, \delta, s)$ such that for any $x \in J_\delta$ and $\delta < \beta$, $(J_\beta, \in) \models \exists \exists \varphi(x, \delta, s)$ if and only if $x = J_\delta$. Since $M$ is transitive, by an induction on $\delta < \gamma$, we prove that $J_\delta \in M$ for any $\delta < \gamma$. Hence $J_\gamma \subseteq M$.

It is immediate that $J_0 = \emptyset \in M$. If $\delta > 0$, then $\pi^{1+\delta}(\beta) < \beta$. Then $(J_\beta, \in) \models (\exists x)(\exists s)\varphi(x, \pi^{-1}(\delta), s)$ and so $(M, \in) \models (\exists x)(\exists s)\varphi(x, \delta, s)$. Let $x \in M$ and let $s \in M$ such that $(M, \in) \models \varphi(x, \delta, s)$. By the absoluteness of $\varphi(x, \delta, s)$, any admissible structure $\aleph$ containing $x, s$ and $\delta$ satisfies $\varphi(x, \delta, s)$. By Proposition 3.3.3, $J_\delta \ni x \in M$.

Furthermore, for any $x \in M$, there exists a $\delta < \beta$ such that $\pi^{-1}(x) \in J_\delta$. Hence $(J_\beta, \in) \models (\exists \delta)(\exists y)[(\exists s)\pi^{-1}(x) \in y \wedge \varphi(y, \delta, s)]$. Then $(M, \in) \models (\exists \delta)(\exists y)[(\exists s)x \in y \wedge \varphi(y, \delta, s)]$. By Proposition 3.3.3 again, $x \in J_\delta$ for some $\delta' < \gamma$. We conclude that $J_\gamma \supseteq M$ and hence $J_\gamma = M$.

Suppose $x <_{J_\beta} \pi(x)$ for some $x \in X$. Take $x_0$ to be the $<_{J_\beta}$-least such set. Then $x_0 \in J_\beta$ since $\pi(x_0) \in J_\beta$ and so there is an $x_1 \in X$ such that $\pi(x_1) = x_0$. If $x_0 <_{J_\beta} x_1$, then $\pi(x_0) <_{J_\beta} \pi(x_1) = x_0$ which contradicts the assumption that $x_0 <_{J_\beta} \pi(x_0)$. So $x_1 <_{J_\beta} \pi(x_1) = x_0$ which contradicts the choice of $x_0$ to be the $<_{J_\beta}$-least counterexample.

An immediate consequence of the Condensation Theorem (3.3.7) is that $V = L$ implies the Continuum Hypothesis. We leave the proof as an exercise.

### Corollary 3.3.8. $2^\omega \cap L = 2^\omega \cap L_\alpha$.

Another important tool in the theory of constructibility is the use of Skolem functions. A relation or function is $\Sigma_n(\aleph)$-definable if it is $\Sigma_n$-definable over $\aleph$ (possibly with parameters).

### Definition 3.3.9. Given a structure $\mathfrak{M}$, a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function for $\aleph$ is a $\Sigma_n(\aleph)$-definable function $h : \omega \times M \to M$ with parameter $p \in M$ such that for any $A \subseteq M$ which is $\Sigma_n$-definable over $\aleph$ with parameters $p, x \in M$, there is an $i \in \omega$ satisfying $h(i, x) \in A$.

Thus a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function is a $\Sigma_n$-definable choice function for $\Sigma_n$-sets.

### Proposition 3.3.10. Suppose that $a > 0$ is admissible and $h$ is a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function for $(J_a, \in)$. Then

- (i) for any $x \in J_a$, $x \in \text{Range}(h(\omega \times \{x\}))$ and $(\text{Range}(h(\omega \times \{x\})), \in) \prec_{\Sigma_n} (J_a, \in)$;
- (ii) if $q \in J_a$ and $X \subseteq J_a$ is closed under ordered pairs, then $X \cup \{q\} \subseteq \text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\})))$ and $(\text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\}))), \in) \prec_{\Sigma_n} (J_a, \in)$.

Proof. Note that (i) is straightforward to verify and is a standard fact in model theory. For (ii), since $X$ is closed under ordered pairs, any finite sequence from $X$ used to define a finite sequence in $\text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\})))$ may be coded by a single parameter. Hence it is routine to show $(\text{Range}(h(\omega \times (X \cup \{q\}))), \in) \prec_{\Sigma_n} (J_a, \in)$.

We remark that the existence of a $\Sigma_n$-Skolem function is not a trivial fact. In model theory, defining a Skolem function for a structure $\mathfrak{M}$ requires a well-ordering of $M$. In the constructible universe $L$, however, there is a uniformly $\Sigma_1$-definable Skolem function.

Then $z$ is $\Pi_1^1$. We leave it to the reader to verify that

$(\forall j \in \mathcal{O})((n \mid (j, n) \in z) = \Gamma(j))$.

Hence $\Gamma_{\omega^{CK}} = \{n \mid (\exists j \in \mathcal{O})((j, n) \in z)\}$ is a $\Pi_1^1$-real.

Let $i \in \mathcal{O}^* \setminus 0$. Define $D \subseteq \mathcal{O}^* \times 2^\omega$ to be a $\Sigma_1^1$-set such that $x \in D$ if and only if

$(\forall n)(\forall j)(j \in \mathcal{O} \cap 2^\omega \to ((j, n) \in z \leftrightarrow (j, n \in x)))$.

By Gandy's Basis Theorem (2.5.3), there is a $z^* \in D$ such that $\omega_1^{z^*} = \omega_1^{CK}$.

For $j \in \mathcal{O}^*$, let

$z_j^* = \{n \mid (\exists k)(k <_\mathcal{O} j \land (k, n) \in z^*)\}$.

Clearly $\Gamma_{\omega^{CK}} = \bigcup_{j \in \mathcal{O} \cap \mathcal{O}^*} z_j^*$. Moreover, for any $j_0 <_\mathcal{O} j_1 <_\mathcal{O} \dots t, z_{j_n}^* \subseteq z_j^*$.

Suppose that $n \in \Gamma(\omega^{CK})$. We claim that there is a $j \in \mathcal{O} \cap \mathcal{O}_1^*$ such that $n \in \Gamma(z_j^*) = \Gamma(\Gamma(j))$. Otherwise, since $\Gamma$ is monotonic, it must be the case that $n \notin \Gamma(z_j^*)$ for any $j \in \mathcal{O}_1^* \setminus 0$. Let

$j \in y_n \leftrightarrow n \in \Gamma(z_j^*)$.

Note that $z_j^* <_{h^*} z^*$ for every $j \in \mathcal{O}^*$. By Corollary 2.2.12, $y_n$ is $\Pi_1^1\{z^*\}$. By the assumption, $y_n = \mathcal{O}_1^* \cap \mathcal{O}$. Thus $y_n$ is also $\Sigma_1^1\{z^*\}$ and hence $\Delta_1^1\{z^*\}$. Obviously $\omega_1^{\mathcal{O}_1^* \cap \mathcal{O}} > \omega_1^{CK}$ and hence $\omega_1^{y_n} > \omega_1^{CK}$, contradicting the choice of $z^*$. We conclude that $\Gamma(\Gamma_{\omega^{CK}}) \subseteq \bigcup_{j \in \mathcal{O} \cap \mathcal{O}^*} \Gamma(j) = \bigcup_{j \in \mathcal{O} \cap \mathcal{O}^*} \Gamma[j] = \Gamma_{\omega^{CK}}$. $\square$

# 8.1.1 Inductively defining $\mathcal{O}$

As an illustration, we show how $\mathcal{O}$ may be defined inductively. Let $\Gamma$ be a function such that for any $x$,

$$
\Gamma(x)(n) = \begin{cases} 1 & n \in x \\ 1 & n = 1 \\ 1 & (\exists m \in x)(n = 2^m), \\ 1 & (\exists e)(\forall i)(n = 3 \cdot 5^e \cdot \Phi_i(e) \in x), \\ 0 & \text{Otherwise.} \end{cases}
$$

We leave the proof of the other proposition to the reader.

**Proposition 8.1.5.** Let $\Gamma$ be as defined above. Then $\Gamma_\infty = \Gamma_{\omega^{CK}} = \emptyset$.

Thus $\mathcal{O}$ is inductively defined by the arithmetical function $\Gamma$. Indeed, every $\Pi_1^1$-real is "generated" by a simple monotonic function $\Gamma$ (Exercise 8.1.3).

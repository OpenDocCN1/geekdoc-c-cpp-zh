
Define \( T = \{\sigma \in 2^{<\omega} \mid (\exists y \in B)(y \succ \sigma)\} \). Clearly \( B \subseteq [T] \). Since \( B \) is \( \Sigma_1^1 \), \( [T] \) is \( \Sigma_1^1 \). Since \( A \) is closed, we have \( [T] \subseteq A \). Also \( A \) is null implies that \( [T] \) is null as well. By Lemma 13.1.10 and a routine application of \( \Pi_1^1 \)-uniformization, there is a total \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-function \( f : \omega \to \text{HYP} \) such that for any \( k, f(k) \in \Sigma_1 \cap \text{HYP}, A_{f(k)} \subseteq 2^\omega \setminus [T] \) and \( \mu(A_{f(k)}) > 2^{-k} \). Let

```
\( C = \{z \mid (\forall k)(z_k \notin A_{f(k)})\} \).
```

Then \( C \) is a \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-closed nullset. However, \( x \in C \) and this is a contradiction.

## 14.1.2 Base of a cone of Kurtz random reals

From the proof of Theorem 14.1.3, one sees that every hyperdegree above that of \( \mathcal{O} \) contains a \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-Kurtz random real. This fails for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness. We say that a hyperdegree \( \mathbf{a} \) is a *base of a cone for T-Kurtz randomness* if for every hyperdegree \( \mathbf{b} \ge \mathbf{a} \), \( \mathbf{b} \) contains a \( T \)-Kurtz random real.

The hyperdegree of \( \mathcal{O} \) is a base of a cone for \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness as proved in Theorem 14.1.3. Not every nonzero hyperdegree is a base of a cone for \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness (see Lemma 14.6.1). Does there exist a base of a cone for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness? If so, such a base, say \( \mathbf{b} \), is not hyperarithmetically reducible to a \( \Pi_1^1 \)-singleton. This means that a hyperdegree which serves as a base must be fairly complicated.

**Lemma 14.1.5.** For any reals \( x \) and \( z \ge_T x' \), there is an \( x \)-Kurtz-random real \( y \equiv_T z \).

**Proof.** Let \( z \ge_T x' \) and fix an enumeration of \( x \)-t.e. open sets \( \{U_n^s\}_{n < \omega} \). We construct an increasing sequence \( \{\sigma_s\}_{1 \le s < \omega} \) of strings as follows.

- Let \( \sigma_0 = 0 \).

- Stage \( s + 1 \). Let \( l_0 = 0, l_1 = |\sigma_s| \), and \( l_{n+1} = 2^{l_n} \) for \( n > 1 \). For each such \( n \), let

```
\( A_n = \{\sigma \in 2^{l_{n-1}} \mid (\exists m < n)(\forall i)(\forall j)(l_m \le i, j < l_{m+1} \implies \sigma(i) = \sigma(j))\} \).
```

Then

```
\( |A_n| \le 2 \cdot 2^{-l_{n+1}} \).
```

In other words,

```
\( \mu(B_n = \bigcup \{[\sigma] \mid \sigma \ge \sigma_s \wedge \sigma \notin A_n\}) \ge 2^{-l_1} \cdot (1 - 2^{-l_{n+1} + l_{n+1}}) \).
```

Case 1. There is an \( m > l_1 + 1 \) such that \( |\{\sigma \ge \sigma_s \mid \sigma \in 2^m \cap [\sigma] \subseteq U_k^s\}| > 2^{m-l_1-1} \).

Let \( n = m + 1 \). Then \( l_{n+1} - 1 - l_n > 2 \) and \( l_n > m \). Then there is a \( \sigma \in 2^{l_{n-1}} \setminus A_n \) and a \( \tau \le \sigma \) such that \( [\tau] \subseteq U_k^s \) and \( \tau \ge 2^m \).

Let \( \sigma_{s+1} = \sigma(s)^{[l_{n-1}]} \).

Case 2. Otherwise. Let \( \sigma_{s+1} = \sigma_s^c(s)^{[l_{s-1}]} \).

This completes the construction at stage \( s + 1 \). Let \( U = \bigcup_s \sigma_s \). Obviously the construction is recursive in \( z \) and so \( y \ge_T z \). Moreover, if \( U_k^s \) is of measure 1, then Case 1 holds at stage \( s + 1 \). Hence \( y \) is \( x \)-Kurtz random.

Let \( l_0 = 0 \) and let \( l_n = 2^n \) for \( n > 0 \). To compute \( z(n) \) from \( y \), \( y \)-recursively find the \( n \)-th \( l_m \) such that for all \( i \), \( j \) with \( l_m \le i < j < l_{m+1} \), \( y(i) = y(j) \). Then \( z(n) = y(l_m) \).

We call \( A \subseteq \omega \times 2^\omega \) a universal \( \Pi_1^1 \)-closed set if (i) it is \( \Pi_1^1 \), (ii) for every \( n \), \( A_n = \{x \mid (n, x) \in A\} \) is a \( \Pi_1^1 \)-closed set, and (iii) every \( \Pi_1^1 \)-closed set is \( A_n \) for some \( n \). By Theorems 13.1.3 and 13.1.14, the real \( x_0 = \{n \mid \mu(A_n) = 0\} \) is \( \Sigma_1^1 \). Let

\( c = \{(n, \sigma) \mid n \in x_0 \wedge (\exists x)(n, x) \in A \wedge \sigma \prec x\} \subseteq \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \).

Then \( c \) may be viewed as a \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-real. Since every \( \Pi_1^1 \)-null closed set is \( \Pi_1^1(c) \), every \( c \)-Kurtz-random real is \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz random.

## Theorem 14.1.6. The hyperdegree of \( c \) is the base of a cone for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

Proof. Lemma 14.1.5 implies that the Turing degree of any \( y \ge_T c' \) contains a member which is \( c \)-Kurtz random and therefore \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz random. Thus \( c \) is a base for \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Kurtz randomness.

We analyze the complexity of \( c \). Since every \( \Pi_1^1 \)-singleton is recursive in \( c \), \( c \notin L_{\delta_1^1} \). Moreover, the set \( \{x \mid x \text{ is a } \Pi_1^1\text{-singleton}\} \) is \( \Delta_1^1(c) \). In other words, \( \{x \mid x \text{ is a } \Pi_1^1\text{-singleton}\} \in L_{\omega_1^{ck}}[c] \). Hence one has \( \omega_1^c > \delta_1^1 \). Applying the same argument as that in Proposition 4.4.4, the reals in \( L_{\delta_1^1} \) are precisely those that are \( \Delta_1^1 \) and so \( c \) is not \( \Delta_1^1 \). Moreover, since \( c \) is \( \Sigma_2^1 \), it is \( \Sigma_1^1 \) definable over \( L_{\delta_1^1} \) and hence \( c \in L_{\delta_1^1+1} \). Indeed, all \( \Sigma_1^1 \)-reals belong to \( L_{\delta_1^1+1} \). Thus,

\( c \) has the largest hyperdegree among all \( \Sigma_2^1 \) reals.

## 14.2 \( \Delta_1^1 \) and \( \Pi_1^1 \)-Martin-Löf randomness

### 14.2.1 \( \Delta_1^1 \)-randomness

Definition 14.2.1. Let \( \Gamma \) be a definable class of sets of reals. We say that \( x \) is \( \Gamma \)-random if it does not belong to any \( \Gamma \)-nullset.

In this section, we study the key properties of \( \Delta^1_1 \)-randomness.

Proposition 14.2.2. Suppose that \( A \) is a \( \Delta^1_1 \)-nullset. There is a \( \Delta^1_1 \)-set \( V \subseteq \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \) such that \( (\forall n)(\mu(V_n) = 2^{-n}) \) and \( A \subseteq \bigcap_{n<\omega} V_n \), where \( V_n = \{\sigma \mid (n, \sigma) \in V\} \) is a \( \Delta^1_1 \)-open set.

Proof. As before, \( \{A_s\} \) is the collection of sets of reals generated by Borel codes \( z \) (§2.7.1). Since \( A \) is a \( \Delta^1_1 \)-nullset, by Lemma 13.11.10 there is a \( \Delta^1_1 \)-function \( f : \omega \to \Sigma_1 \cap \text{HYP} \) such that for every \( n < \omega \),

- \( \mu(A_{f(n)}) < 2^{-n} \), and
- \( A \subseteq A_{f(n)} \)

Then \( A_{f(n)} = [U_n] \) for some \( \Delta^1_1 \)-set \( U_n \subseteq 2^{<\omega} \). It now suffices to show that \( \{U_n\}_{n<\omega} \) may be expanded to a \( \Delta^1_1 \)-collection \( \{V_n\}_{n<\omega} \) such that \( U_n \subseteq V_n \) and \( \mu(V_n) = 2^{-n} \). Since the set \( U = \{(n, \sigma) \mid \sigma \in U_n\} \) is \( \Delta^1_1 \), there is an \( m \in \Theta \) such that \( U \le_T H_m \). Note that the set \( \{(x, e, p) \in 2^\omega \times \omega \times \mathbf{Q} \mid \mu(\{o\} \mid (\exists i)(\Phi_e^i(i) = o\})) > p\} \) is r.e. Then by a standard technique in classical randomness theory relativized to \( H_{2^m} \), there is a \( V \subseteq \omega \times 2^{<\omega} \) recursive in \( H_{2^m} \) such that for every \( n \), \( \mu(V_n) = 2^{-n} \) and \( U_n \subseteq V_n \). Then \( A \subseteq \bigcap_{n<\omega} V_n \).

By Proposition 14.2.2, \( \Delta^1_1 \)-randomness may be viewed as a higher analog of Schnorr randomness.

Proposition 14.2.3 (Martin-Löf [97]). The set of \( \Delta^1_1 \)-random reals is \( \Sigma^1_1 \) but not \( \Delta^1_1 \).

Proof. Define a \( \Sigma^1_1 \)-set

```
\( A = \{x \mid (\forall e)(\forall n)((m \in \Theta)(\mu m[\{i(\{o\} \mid \exists i)(\Phi_e^{H_n}(\langle m, i \rangle) = o\})) \le 2^{-m}) \to (\exists m)(\forall i)(x \not\prec \Phi_e^{H_n}(\langle m, i \rangle))).\)
```

By Proposition 14.2.2, \( A \) is precisely the set of \( \Delta^1_1 \)-random reals. Since \( A \) does not contain a hyperarithmetic member, by Theorem 13.13.13, it is not \( \Delta^1_1 \).

### 14.2.2 \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Martin-Löf randomness

A sequence of open sets \( \{U_n\}_{n<\omega} \) is a Martin-Löf test (ML test) if \( \mu(U_n) < 2^{-n} \) for all \( n \). Given a class \( \Gamma \) of sets of reals (e.g., \( \Pi^1_1 \)), \( \{U_n\}_{n<\omega} \) is a \( \Gamma \)-ML test if \( \{(n, \sigma) \mid \sigma \in 2^{<\omega} \wedge [\sigma] \in U_n\} \in \Gamma \).

Definition 14.2.4. Let \( \Gamma \) be a class of sets of reals. Then \( x \) is \( \Gamma \)-ML random if \( x \notin \bigcap_{n<\omega} U_n \) for any \( \Gamma \) ML test \( \{U_n\}_{n<\omega} \).

Proposition 14.2.5 (Jorhth and Nies [51]). There is a universal \( \Pi^1_1 \)-ML test \( \{U_n\}_{n<\omega} \), i.e., for every \( \Pi^1_1 \)-ML test \( \{V_n\}_{n<\omega} \), \( \bigcap_{n<\omega} V_n \subseteq \bigcap_{n<\omega} U_n \).


Proof. If \(0^\sharp\) does not exist, then by Lemmas 8.4.4 and 8.4.5, both \(F^*\) and \(\tilde{F}^*\) are cofinal. Let \(P(x, y)\) be an arithmetical predicate defined as

\(x \in \tilde{F}^* \leftrightarrow (\forall y)P(x, y)\).

### Claim 1. If \(x \le_T y\) are such that \(x \in F^*\) and \(y \in \tilde{F}^*\), then \(O^x \le_h y\).

Proof. As \(y \notin F^*\), there are \(a < \omega_1^x\) and \(a \le \omega\) with \(a \in L_{a_1^x}[L_{a+1}(y)]\). Then \(a \notin L_{a+1}[x]\) and \(\omega_1^x \le a < \omega_1^y\). As \(x \le_T y\), \(O^x <_h y\).

By Claim 1, if \(x \le_T y\) are such that \(x \in F^*\) and \(y \in \tilde{F}^*\), then \(O^{O^x} \le_T O^y\).

- (1) \((y)_0 = 0^{O^x} \land x \in F^* \text{ or } (y)_0 = 1^{O^{O^x}} \land (\exists v \le_T y)(y)_0 \neg P(x, v)\),
- (2) \((y)_1 = O^x\),
- (3) \(\Phi_e^x\) is partial \(\Rightarrow (y)_{e+2} = 0\),
- (4) \(u = \Phi_e^x\) is total \(\Rightarrow\)
  - (i) \((y)_0(0) = 0 \land (\exists v \le_T (y)_1)\neg P(u, v) \land (y)_{e+2} = 1^{O^{0^{y(e_i)}}}\) where \(i\) is the least index so that \(O^{y(e_i)} = O^{0^{y(e_i)}}\), or
  - (ii) \((y)_0(0) = 1 \land (\exists v \le_T (y)_1)\neg P(u, v) \land (y)_{e+2} = 0^{O^{O^x}}\), or
  - (iii) \((\forall v \le_T (y)_1)P(u, v) \land (y)_{e+2} = 0^{O^{O^x}}\).

It is straightforward to verify that \(G\) is \(\Pi^1_1\).

### Claim 2. If \(x \in F^*\) then \(G(x) \equiv_T O^{O^{O^x}}\).

Proof. Clearly \(x \in F^* \Rightarrow O^{O^{O^x}} \le_T G(x)\) and \((G(x))_0 \oplus (G(x))_1 \le_T O^{O^{O^x}}\). Given \(e < \omega\), \(O^{O^{O^x}}\) can uniformly decide whether \(\Phi_e^x\) is total. Suppose that \(\Phi_e^x\) is total. To calculate \((G(x))_{e+2}(n)\) we need to check clauses (4 ii, 4 iii) above. Now the predicate \((\forall v \le_T (y)_1)P(u, v)\) is \(\Delta_1^1(O^x)\), and hence recursive in \(O^{O^x}\). Once this predicate is decided, \(O^{O^{O^x}}\) will be able to complete the calculation with the use of two recursive functions \(f, g\) such that \(u = \Phi_e^x \Rightarrow O^{O^x} = \Phi_{f(e)}^{O^{O^{O^x}}}\) and \(u = \Phi_e^w \Rightarrow O^{O^{O^w}} = \Phi_{g(e)}^{O^{O^{O^x}}}\).

### Claim 3. If \(x \in \tilde{F}^*\) then \(G(x) \equiv_T O^{O^x}\).

Proof. This is similar to the proof of Claim 2, except for the final step in the calculation of \(((G(x))_{e+2}(n))\). Now \(O^{O^x}\) can decide whether (4 i) or (4 iii) holds, as in the above claim. If (4 iii) holds, then the calculation is again similar to the above. Suppose that (4 i) holds. Then \(u \in F^*\). By Claim 1, \(O^x \le_h x\) and thus \(O^{O^x} \le_T O^x = (G(x))_1\). Now the index \(i\) prescribed in (4) may be found uniformly via a \(\Pi_1^1(O^x)\) procedure. Hence \(O^{O^x}\) computes \((G(x))_{e+2}(n)\) uniformly.

It follows from the last two claims that \(G\) is degree invariant. Moreover, \(G\) preserves \(\le_T\) by Claim 1.

To show that \(G\) is uniformly order preserving, let \(h\) be a recursive function such that \((\forall x, y, e)(x = \Phi_e^y \Rightarrow O^x = \Phi_{h(e)}^{O^y})\). In addition, let \(s\) be another recursive function with

\((\forall y', x, y, e, i)(x = \Phi_e^{y'} \rightarrow \Phi_i^x = \Phi_{s(i)}^y)\).

Suppose that \(x = 0_e^\omega\). Then

- (1) \((G(y))_0 = (G(y))_{i+2}\),
- (2) \((G(y))_1 = 0_{(h(e))}^{(G(y))}\), and
- (3) \((G(y))_{i+2} = (G(y))_{s(i)+2}\).

Hence G is as desired.

## 9 Independence results in recursion theory

### 9.1 Independent sets of Turing degrees

### 9.1.1 Locally countable partial ordering

**Definition 9.1.1.** A partially ordered set \((\mathbf{P}, \le_P)\) is locally countable if for any \(p \in \mathbf{P}\), \(|\{q \mid q \le_P p\}| \le \aleph_0\).

**Definition 9.1.2.** A function \(f : (\mathbf{P}, \le_P) \to (\mathbf{Q}, \le_Q)\) is an embedding if \((\forall p_0, p_1 \in \mathbf{P}) (p_0 \le_P p_1 \Leftrightarrow f(p_0) \le_Q f(p_1))\).

Obviously \((\mathscr{D}, \le_T)\), the partial ordering of the Turing degrees, is locally countable. Sacks made the following conjecture.

**Conjecture 9.1.3** (Sacks [117]). Every locally countable partial ordering on \(2^{\aleph_0}\) is embeddable into \((\mathscr{D}, \le_T)\).

A positive solution is known assuming the Continuum Hypothesis:

**Theorem 9.1.4** (Sacks [117]). Assuming ZFC + CH. Every locally countable partially ordered set \((2^{\aleph_0}, \le_P)\) is embeddable into \((\mathscr{D}, \le_T)\).

### 9.1.2 Independent sets of Turing degrees

As a "test question" for Conjecture 9.1.3, Sacks introduced the notion of independent sets and conjectured that every maximal independent set of Turing degrees has size \(2^{\aleph_0}\).

**Definition 9.1.5.** Let \((\mathbf{P}, \le_P)\) be a partial ordering. \(X \subseteq \mathbf{P}\) is independent if for any finite subset \(Y\) of \(X\) and any \(p \in X \backslash Y\), there is a \(q \in \mathbf{P}\) such that \(p \le_P q \wedge (\forall r \in Y)(r \le_P q)\).

**Theorem 9.1.6** (Sacks [117]). Assume ZFC + CH. Every maximal independent set of Turing degrees has size \(2^{\aleph_0}\).

However, the conclusion of Theorem 9.1.6 is independent of ZFC:

**Theorem 9.1.7** (Groszek and Slaman [40]). There is a model \(\mathbb{N} \vDash \text{ZFC} + \neg\text{CH}\) with a maximal independent set of Turing degrees of size \(\aleph_1\).

The rest of this section is devoted to a proof of Theorem 9.1.7. We begin with a ground model \(\mathbb{M} \vDash \text{ZFC} + \text{GCH}\). Let \(\mathbb{P} = (P, \le) = \Pi_{\alpha < \omega_1} \mathbb{S}_\alpha\) be product Sacks forcing with countable support, where \(\mathbb{S}_\alpha\) is Sacks forcing for \(\alpha < \omega_1\). Every P-generic set \(G\) may be viewed as an \((\omega_1)^\mathbb{M}\)-sequence of reals \(\langle G_\alpha \rangle_{\alpha \in (\omega_1)^\mathbb{M}}\) where \(G_\alpha = \{i \mid (\alpha, i) \in G\}\). It is not difficult to see that the Turing degrees of \(\langle G_\alpha \rangle_{\alpha \in (\omega_1)^\mathbb{M}}\) form an independent set.

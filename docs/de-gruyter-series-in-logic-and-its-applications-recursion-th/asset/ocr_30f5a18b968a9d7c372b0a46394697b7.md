Hence there are again many reals that are low for Schnorr randomness.

# ## 12.3.4 Lowness for Martin-Löf randomness

**Definition 12.3.4.** Let \(\Gamma\) be a notion of randomness. A real \(x\) is a _base_ for \(\Gamma\) if there is a \(\Gamma^*\)-random \(y\) such that \(x \le_T y\).

The following result is one of the deepest in algorithmic randomness theory.

**Theorem 12.3.5** (Nies [110] and Hirschfeldt, Nies, Stephan [48])**.** _For any_ \(x\)_, the following are equivalent:_
(i) _\(x\) is K-trivial;_
(ii) _\(x\) is a base for Martin-Löf randomness;_
(iii) \((\exists c)(\forall n)(K(n) \le K^c(n) + c)\)_;_
(iv) _\(x\) is low for Martin-Löf tests;_
(v) _\(x\) is low for Martin-Löf randomness._

The concept of a randomness base is particularly important in the proof of Theorem 12.3.5. Note that one may view Martin-Löf randomness to be the weakest notion of randomness as it has only countably many bases (see [27]). Following the characterization in Theorem 12.3.5, Nies introduced a new reduction relation \(\le_{LR}\):

**Definition 12.3.6.** \(x \le_{LR} y\) if every \(y\)-Martin-Löf random real is \(x\)-Martin-Löf random.

Hence by Theorem 12.3.5, \(x \le_{LR} 0\) if and only if \(x\) is \(K\)-trivial. This relation is expressed in terms of Kolmogorov complexity as follows:

**Theorem 12.3.7** (Kjos-Hanssen, Miller, Solomon [66])**.** \(x \le_{LR} y\) _if and only if_
\((\exists c)(\forall n)(K^y(n) \le K^x(n) + c)\)_._

Combining Corollary 12.2.13 with van Lambalgen's Theorem (12.1.7) offers additional evidence for the randomness thesis:

**Proposition 12.3.8.** _For any two Martin-Löf random reals_ \(x\) _and_ \(y\)_,_ \(x \le_K y\) _implies_ \(y \le_{LR} x\)_._

# ## 12.3.5 Lowness for strong Martin-Löf randomness

**Theorem 12.3.9** (Downey et al. [23], Nies [111], and Kjos-Hanssen et al. [66])**.** _For any_ \(x\)_, the following are equivalent:_
(i) _\(x\) is K-trivial;_
(ii) _\(x\) is low for strong Martin-Löf test;_
(iii) _\(x\) is low for strong Martin-Löf randomness._

It is not difficult to see that there is an arithmetical set \( W \subseteq \{(m, n, z) \mid m, n < \omega \land z \in 2^\omega\} \) such that \( (W_n^z)_{n < \omega} \), where \( W_n^z = \{m \mid (m, n, z) \in W\} \), is a sequence of \( z \)-r.e. sets satisfying \( z <_T W_{n+1}^z \leq_T W_n^z \) for each \( n \).

Let \( F_n(x) = y \) if there exists a \( z <_T y \) such that

+   (i) \( z \in A \);
+   (ii) \( z \geq_T x \);
+   (iii) \( y = W_{n+1}^z \);
+   (iv) \( x >_T 0 \Rightarrow (\forall m)(x \not\leq_T (z)_0)_m \).

Roughly speaking, \( y \) is \( W_{n+1}^z \) for the \( <_T \)-least \( z \) in \( A \) with \( z \geq_T x \).

Clearly \( \{f_n\}_{n < \omega} \) is a sequence of \( \Pi^1_1 \)-functions. Note that for any \( x <_T y \) in \( A \), \( x' \leq_T y \). Hence for every \( n \), \( F_n \) is degree invariant and increasing.

By (iv) and the property of \( A \), \( F \) is well defined. By the choice of \( \{W_n^z\}_{n < \omega}, \{f_n\}_{n < \omega} \) is a \( <_M \)-descending sequence.

# ## 8.4.2 The consistency strength of \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Martin's conjecture

Given that the results in Chapter 7 are established under AD which is essentially a large cardinal assumption, one would expect a direct connection between Martin's conjecture and large cardinals. The first level for such an investigation is clearly \( \Pi^1_1 \). We prove that if \( \Pi^1_1 \)-Martin's conjecture is true, then \( 0^\sharp \) exists.\(^2\) Define the Friedman set \( F^* \) as
\( F^* = \{x \mid (\forall a < \omega_1^x)(\forall a \subseteq a)(a \in L_{a^+_1} \rightarrow a \in L_{a+1}[x]) \} \)

It is not difficult to see that \( F^* \) is \( \Sigma^1_1 \).

**Lemma 8.4.4.** \( F^* \) is cofinal in the Turing degrees.

Proof. For any real \( z \), let
\( F(z) = \{x \oplus z \mid (\forall a < \omega_1^x)(\forall a \subseteq a)(a \in L_{a^+_1} \rightarrow a \in L_{a+1}[x \oplus z]) \} \).
Then \( F(z) \) is \( \Sigma^1_1(z) \) and nonempty. By Gandy's Basis Theorem (2.5.3) relativized to \( z \), there is a \( x \) such that \( a_1^{x \oplus z} = a_1^z \) and \( x \oplus z \in F(z) \). Then \( x \not\geq_T z \in F \).

We will use the following result due to Harrington. A sketch of the proof is given in Exercises 8.4.3 to 8.4.8.

**Lemma 8.4.5** (Harrington [45]). If \( 0^\sharp \) does not exist, then \( \tilde{F} = 2^\omega \setminus F \) is cofinal in the Turing degrees.

**Theorem 8.4.6** (Chong, Wang and Yu [13]). If there is no uniformly order-preserving \( \Pi^1_1 \)-function \( G \) such that \( \{x \mid G(x) = 0^{0^{x'}}\} \) and \( \{x \mid G(x) = 0^{0^{0^{x'}}}\} \) are cofinal in the Turing degrees, then \( 0^\sharp \) exists.

+   (i) Range($h_p$) $\supseteq y_0 \cup \{\infty\}$;

+   (ii) for any $m, f((\exists z^{>n})(\varphi(n, z^{>n}) \lor 
+   eg \psi(n, z^{>n})) < \gamma_{m+1}$ and for any $n$ and $p_n \le p$ in $L_{\gamma_{m+1}}$, there exists a $q_n \le p_n$ with $q_n \in L_{\gamma_{m+1}}$ such that $q_n \Vdash (\exists z^{>n+1})(\varphi(n, z^{>n+1}) \lor 
+   eg \psi(n, z^{>n+1}))$, and

+   (iii) for each $m$, $\gamma_{m+1} = \omega \gamma_{m+1}$.

Let $eta = igcup_{m < \omega} \gamma_m = \omega eta$. Then $eta < \alpha$. We claim that

$p \Vdash (\forall n)((\exists z^eta)(\varphi(n, z^eta) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^eta))$.

The forcing relation is equivalent to

$(\forall n)(\forall q \le p)(\exists r \le q)(q \Vdash (\exists z^eta)(\varphi(n, z^eta) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^eta))).$

Given $n$ and $q \le p$, let $q'$ be a condition such that $t'_{q'} = t_q$ and $h_{q'}(\alpha) = h_q(\alpha)$ for $h_q(\alpha) < eta$ and $h_{q'}(\alpha) = \infty$ otherwise. By (i), $q' \le p$ and $q' \in L_{\gamma_m}$ for some $m$. By (ii), there is a condition $r' \in L_p$ such that $r' \le q'$ and

$r' \Vdash (\exists z^{\gamma_m})(\varphi(n, z^{\gamma_m}) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^{\gamma_m})).$

By (iii), Ret$(\omegaeta, q, q')$ holds and

$\omega \cdot f((\exists z^{\gamma_m})(\varphi(n, z^{\gamma_m}) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^{\gamma_m}))) < eta.$

By Lemma 5.4.5 and Lemma 5.4.6, there is a condition $r \le q$ such that

$r \Vdash (\exists z^eta)(\varphi(n, z^{\gamma_m}) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^{\gamma_m})).$

Hence

$r \Vdash (\exists z^eta)(\varphi(n, z^eta) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^eta)).$

By Lemma 5.4.10,

$\mathfrak{A}(\alpha, T) \Vdash (\forall n)(\exists z^eta)(\varphi(n, z^eta) \lor 
eg \psi(n, z^eta)).$

Let $y$ be defined such that $y \in \dot{y}$ if and only if $\mathfrak{A}(\alpha, T) \Vdash (\exists z^eta)\varphi(n, z^eta)$. Then $y \in \mathfrak{A}(\alpha, T)$ and thus $\mathfrak{A}(\alpha^{<\omega}, T)$ satisfies $\Delta_1^\xi$-comprehension. $\square$

In summary, we have the following:

# **Theorem 5.4.12** (Sacks [122]). For any countable admissible ordinal $\alpha > \omega$, there is a real $x$ such that $\omega_1^x = \alpha$.

# **Remark 5.4.13.** In the literature, there are several proofs of Theorem 5.4.12. Sacks's original proof was by the method of perfect set forcing and provided a stronger conclusion. The second proof, due to Friedman and Jensen [31], was by an application of the Barwise Compactness Theorem [3]. The third proof, which remains unpublished, was due to Jensen [54] and uses a class of Levy collapse operations which may be viewed as a variant of Steel forcing.

# ## 4.1.2 A constructible basis theorem

Theorem 4.1.2 (Gupasari [42]). If \( A \subseteq 2^\omega \) is a nonempty \( \Pi^1_1 \)-set, then there is an \( x \in A \) such that \( x \in L_{\omega_1^x} \).

Proof. Let T be the tree presentation for A and (x, f) its leftmost path. We claim that \( x \in L_{\omega_1^x} \). By Lemma 4.1.1, \( f \in L_{\omega_1^x}[x] \). Hence there is an ordinal \( \alpha_0 < \omega_1^x \) such that Rang(f) = \( \alpha_0 \). We define tree \( T^{\alpha_0} \) by replacing \( \omega_1 \) with \( \alpha_0 \) in T everywhere. Obviously \( T^{\alpha_0} \in L_{\omega_1}[x] \) for some \( \gamma_0 < \omega_1^x \) and (x, f) is an infinite path in \( T^{\alpha_0} \). Moreover, for any \( (\sigma, p) < (x \upharpoonright |\sigma|, f \upharpoonright \text{Dom}(p)) \), there is no infinite path in \( T^{\alpha_0}_{(\sigma, p)} = (\{\sigma', \tau' \in T^{\alpha_0} \mid (\sigma', p') > (\sigma, p)\} \). Otherwise, any such infinite path would also be in T and to the left of (x, f), which contradicts the choice of (x, f) in T. Then for any \( (\sigma, p) < (x \upharpoonright |\sigma|, f \upharpoonright \text{Dom}(p)) \), there exist ordinals \( \alpha_{(\sigma, p)} < \omega_1^x \), \( \beta_{(\sigma, p)} < \alpha_{(\sigma, p)} \) and a function \( g_{(\sigma, p)} \in L_{\alpha_{(\sigma, p)}} \) such that \( g_{(\sigma, p)} \) is an order-preserving function from \( T^{\alpha_0}_{(\sigma, p)} \) to \( \beta_{(\sigma, p)} \). Moreover, the function from \( (\sigma, p) < (x \upharpoonright |\sigma|, f \upharpoonright \text{Dom}(p)) \) to \( \alpha_{(\sigma, p)} < \omega_1^x \) is \( \Sigma_1 \) in \( L_{\omega_1^x}[x] \). Since the set \( \{(\sigma, p), T^{\alpha_0}_{(\sigma, p)} \mid (\sigma, p) < (x \upharpoonright |\sigma|, f \upharpoonright \text{Dom}(p))\} \) is an element of \( L_{\omega_1^x}[x] \), there is a \( \gamma < \omega_1^x \) that bounds any \( \alpha_{(\sigma, p)} \) where \( (\sigma, p) < (x \upharpoonright |\sigma|, f \upharpoonright \text{Dom}(p)) \). We may assume that \( \gamma > \alpha_0 \). Now working in \( L_\gamma \), we define an infinite path as follows: \( (o_0, p_0) = 0 \). \( (i_{i+1}, p_{i+1}) > (o_i, p_i) \) in \( T^{\alpha_0} \) is of length i + 1 so that (1) for all \( (\sigma, p) < (o_{i+1}, p_{i+1}) \) not extending \( (o_{i+1}, p_{i+1}) \), there is an order-preserving function \( g_{(\sigma, p)} \in L_\gamma \) from \( T^{\alpha_0}_{(\sigma, p)} \) into an ordinal \( \beta_{(\sigma, p)} < \gamma \), and (2) there is no order-preserving function \( g \in L_\gamma \) from \( T^{\alpha_0}_{(o_{i+1}, p_{i+1})} \) into any ordinal \( \beta < \gamma \). By the assumption of \( \gamma \), we have \( \bigcup_{i < \omega} (o_i, p_i) = (x, f) \). Clearly the sequence \( \{(i_i, o_i, p_i)\}_{i < \omega} \) is an element of \( L_{\gamma + \omega} \). Hence \( (x, f) \in L_{\gamma + \omega} \subseteq L_{\omega_1^x} \), and so \( x \in L_{\omega_1^x} \).

We have as an immediate consequence of Theorem 4.1.2,

Corollary 4.1.3. If x is a \( \Pi^1_1 \)-singleton, then \( x \in L_{\omega_1^x} \).

One may also introduce the notion of strong \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML randomness by considering reals that avoid all generalized \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML tests. Then Proposition 14.4.8 says that strong \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML randomness is different from \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML randomness. Note that every generalized \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML test \(\{U_n\}_{n \in \omega}\) is a member of \(L\), and by Corollary 13.17, \((L, \epsilon) \vDash \mu(\cap_{n \in \omega} U_n) = 0\). Hence there is a real in \(L\) which is strongly \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML random. It follows that if the set of constructive reals is null, then strong \(\Sigma^1_1\)-ML randomness is strictly weaker than \(\Sigma^1_1\)-randomness.

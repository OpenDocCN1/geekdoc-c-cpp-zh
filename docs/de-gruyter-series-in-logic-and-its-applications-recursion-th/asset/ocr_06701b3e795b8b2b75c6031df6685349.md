straightforward to verify that pairing, union and the \(\Delta_0\)-comprehension axioms hold in \(\mathcal{M}_1\). We prove \(\mathcal{M}_1 = \Delta_0\)-collection.

Suppose that \(\varphi(u, v)\) is a \(\Delta_0\)-formula and \(a \in M_1\) such that

\(\langle M_1, E \rangle = (\forall x Ea)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y).\)

Since \(\varphi(u, v)\) is \(\Delta_0\) and \(M_1\) is an E-closed subset of M, we have

\(\langle M, E \rangle = (\forall x Ea)(\exists y)\varphi(x, y).\)

Now \(\langle M, E \rangle = \text{KP}\) implies that there is a \(b \in M\) such that

\(\langle M, E \rangle = (\forall x Ea)(\exists y E b)\varphi(x, y).\)

By Lemma 3.5.3, there is a \(b_0\) such that \(\text{rk}(b_0) = a_0\) is the least ordinal in \(\mathcal{M}\) to satisfy the collection axiom for \(\varphi\). If \(a_0 \in M_1\), then by Lemma 3.5.3, \(b_0 \in M_1\) and we are done. Otherwise, \(a_0 \in M \setminus M_1\). But then there is a nonstandard \(a_1 E a_0\) such that for every \(x E a\), there is a \(y E b_0\) in \(M_1\) with \(\text{rk}(y) E a_1\) and \(\langle M, E \rangle \models \varphi(x, y)\). Note that \(\varphi(x, y) \land \text{rk}(y) E a_1\) is a \(\Sigma\)-formula and

\(\langle M, E \rangle = (\forall x Ea)(\exists y)(\varphi(x, y) \land \text{rk}(y) E a_1).\)

By the \(\Sigma\)-collection Theorem (3.1.6), there is a \(b_1 \in M\) such that

\(\langle M, E \rangle = (\forall x Ea)(\exists y E b_1)\varphi(x, y) \land \text{rk}(y) E a_1).\)

Define

\(c = [y \mid y E b_1 \land \langle M, E \rangle \models \text{rk}(y) E a_1 \land \exists x E a (\varphi(x, y))].\)

By Theorem 3.1.6, \(c \in M\).

Then \(\text{rk}(c) E a_0\) and \(a_0 = \text{rk}(b_0)\). Since \(a_0\) is the least ordinal in \(\mathcal{M}\) to satisfy the collection axiom for \(\varphi\), we have a contradiction. \(\square\)

# **Proposition 3.5.5.** Let \(\mathcal{M} \models \text{KP}\) be an \(\omega\)-model. Then \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}} \in M\).

Proof. Let \(M_1 \le M\) be the standard part of M. By Lemma 3.5.4, \(\langle M_1, E \rangle = \text{KP}\). Hence \(\langle M_1, E \rangle\) is also an \(\omega\)-model of KP. Since \(\omega_1^u(u) \in M_1\), by \(\Delta_0\)-separation every recursive set belongs to \(\langle M_1, E \rangle\). By \(\Sigma\)-induction, \(\omega_1^{CK} \in M_1\). Let \(\pi : \langle M_1, E \rangle \to \langle N, \in \rangle\) be the collapsing map. Then \(\langle N, \in \rangle\) is admissible so that \(\omega_1^{CK} \le N\). By Theorem 3.3.4, \(L_{\omega_1^{CK}} \in N\). We prove by induction on \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\) that \(\pi^{-1} L_{\omega_1^{CK}}\) is the identity map.

Note that by Proposition 3.3.3 there exists a \(\Delta_1\)-formula \(\varphi(u, v)\) such that for any ordinal \(\beta < \omega_1^{CK}\), \(\langle N, \in \rangle = \varphi(\beta, x)\) if and only if \(x = J_\beta\). Moreover,

\(\langle N, \in \rangle = \varphi(\beta, J_\beta) \land (\forall x E J_\beta)(\exists y \in \beta)(\forall y)(x \in y \leftrightarrow \varphi(y, y)).\)

We prove by induction that \(\pi^{-1}(J_\gamma) = J_\gamma\) for every \(\gamma < \omega_1^{CK}\).

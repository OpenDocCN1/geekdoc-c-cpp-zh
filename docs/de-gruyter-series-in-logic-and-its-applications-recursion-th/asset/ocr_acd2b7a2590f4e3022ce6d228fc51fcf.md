# 5.4.3 Exercises

Exercise 5.4.1. Prove that for any x with \( x \notin L_{\omega_1^x} \), there is a y such that \( y \ge_h x \), \( y \notin_h x \) and \( \omega_1^x = \omega_1^y \).

Exercise 5.4.2. Prove that \( x \in L_{\omega_1^x} \) if and only if for all y, \( \omega_1^y \ge \omega_1^x \) implies \( y \ge_h x \).

Exercise 5.4.3. Let \( \alpha_n \) be the n-th admissible ordinal. Then \( \bigcup_{n<\omega} \alpha_n \) is not admissible.

Exercise 5.4.4 (Chong [11]). Suppose that \( y > \omega \) is an ordinal so that \( J_{y+1} \backslash J_y \cap 2^\omega \neq \emptyset \). Then for any admissible ordinal \( \alpha < \gamma^+ \), where \( \gamma^+ \) is the least admissible ordinal greater than y, there is an x \( \in J_y \backslash J_y \) such that \( \omega_1^x = \alpha \).

Exercise 5.4.5 (Jensen [54]). Suppose that \( \alpha \) is a countable limit of admissible ordinals. Then there is a set \( A \subseteq \alpha \) so that for any admissible \( \beta < \alpha \), \(\langle L_\beta[A], \epsilon, A \cap L_\beta[A] \rangle\) is admissible and \(\langle L_\alpha[A], \epsilon, A \cap L_\beta[A] \rangle \models \text{\texttt{"\beta is countable"}}\).

Exercise 5.4.6 (Jensen [54]). Suppose that \( \omega < \alpha_0 < \cdots < \alpha_n \) is a finite sequence of countable admissible ordinals. Prove that there is an x such that for any \( i \le n \), \( \alpha_i \) is the i + 1-th countable admissible ordinal in L[x].

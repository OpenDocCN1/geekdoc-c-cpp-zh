
Proof of Claim 2. Suppose not. We obtain a winning strategy $\tilde{\sigma}$ for I playing $G_A$ as follows:

By Claim 1, there is an $m$ such that no hyperarithmetic function $f \in C$ exists for which $f(m)$ is defined. Let $\tilde{\sigma}(0) = m$. Note that by (iii), for any hyperarithmetic $f \in C$ and number $j$, $f(m^j)$ is undefined.

By induction on $k$, for any play $(i_0, j_0, \dots, i_k)$ at step $k$ by I following $\tilde{\sigma}$, there is no hyperarithmetic $f \in C$ such that $f((i_0, j_0, \dots, i_k))$ is defined. Then for any $j_k$ and any hyperarithmetic $f \in C$, $f((i_0, j_0, \dots, i_k, j_k))$ is undefined. By Claim 1, there is a $i_{k+1}$ for which there is no hyperarithmetic function $f_{i_{k+1}} \in C$ so that $f_{i_{k+1}}((i_0, j_0, \dots, i_k, j_k, i_{k+1}))$ is defined. Let $\tilde{\sigma}((i_0, j_0, \dots, i_k, j_k)) = i_{k+1}$. Then $\tilde{\sigma}$ is a winning strategy for I, contradicting the assumption.

Thus there is a hyperarithmetic function $g \in C$ such that $g(0) \in \mathcal{O}$. We define a hyperarithmetic strategy $\tilde{\tau}_g$ for II as follows: For each $n$, let $\tilde{\tau}_g(n) = m$ where $g(m) <_{\mathcal{O}} g(0)$. For each $k$ and $a \in \omega^{<\omega}$ with even length, let $\tilde{\tau}_g(a * k) = m$ where $(a * k * m) <_{\mathcal{O}} g(a)$ (if it exists).

We claim that $\tilde{\tau}_g$ is II's winning strategy. To see this, for any $h \in \omega^\omega, g(h * \tilde{\tau}_g \upharpoonright 2k + 2) <_{\mathcal{O}} g(h * \tilde{\tau}_g \upharpoonright 2k)$ for any $k$ with $g(h * \tilde{\tau}_g \upharpoonright 2k) > 1$. Since $g(0) \in \mathcal{O}$, there is a $k$ such that $g(h * \tilde{\tau}_g \upharpoonright 2k) = 1$. Thus $h * \tilde{\tau}_g \in \omega^\omega \setminus A$.

A general basis theorem for the analytical hierarchy is the following Third Periodicity Theorem due to Moschovakis.

## Theorem 6.6.2 (Moschovakis [107]).

- (i) Suppose that every $\Sigma^1_{2n}$-set is determined. If I has a winning strategy for $G_A$ where $A$ is $\Sigma^1_{2n}$, then I has a $\Delta^1_{2n+1}$-winning strategy.

- (ii) Suppose that every $\Delta^1_{2n}$-set is determined. If I has a winning strategy for $G_A$ where $A$ is $\Pi^1_{2n+1}$, then I has a $\Delta^1_{2n+2}$-winning strategy.

## 6.6.2 Cones of Turing degrees

A set $\mathbf{A}$ of Turing degrees is a cone if there exists a $\mathbf{z}$ such that $\mathbf{A} = \{\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{x} \ge \mathbf{z}\}$. We say that $\mathbf{z}$ is the base of $\mathbf{A}$.

### Proposition 6.6.3. Assume ZF + AD. For any set $\mathbf{A}$ of Turing degrees, either $\mathbf{A}$ or its complement contains a cone of Turing degrees.

### Proof.

Let $A = (\mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{x} \in \mathbf{A})$. Suppose that I has a winning strategy $\tilde{\sigma}$ for $G_A$. Then for any $g \ge_T \tilde{\sigma}, h * g \equiv_T g$. Thus $g \in A$. Hence $\mathbf{A}$ contains a cone of Turing degrees.

If II has a winning strategy $\tilde{\tau}$ for $G_A$, then by the same argument, the complement of $\mathbf{A}$ contains a cone of Turing degrees.

Although the proof of Proposition 6.6.3 looks straightforward, it reveals the remarkable fact that the global structure of the Turing degrees may be sensitive to set-theoretic assumptions.

### Definition 6.6.4.

- \( A \in \omega^\omega \) is cofinal if for every \( x \) there is a \( y \in A \) such that \( y \ge_T x \).
- A tree \( T \in \omega^{<\omega} \) is pointed if \( T \) is perfect and for any \( x \in [T] \), \( x \ge_T T \).
- A pointed tree \( T \) is uniformly pointed if there exists an \( e \) such that for any \( x \in [T] \), \( T = \Phi^e_x \).

### Proposition 6.6.5 (Martin [91]).

ZF + DC + AD implies that every cofinal set of reals has a uniformly pointed subtree.

### Proof.

Suppose that \( A \subseteq \omega^\omega \) is cofinal. Let \( B \subseteq \omega^\omega \) be such that \( z = x \oplus y \in B \) if and only if either

- \( x \notin_T y \); or
- \( x \le_T y \) and \( y \notin A \).

By AD, \( G_B \) is determined. Suppose that II has a winning strategy \( \dot{\sigma} \). Let \( y \in A \) so that \( y \ge_T \dot{\tau} \). Then \( \dot{\sigma} * y = y \oplus x \) for some \( x \le_T y \). Hence \( \dot{\sigma} * y \notin B \).

Thus II has a winning strategy \( \dot{\tau} \). Let \( T_0 \subseteq 2^{<\omega} \) be a uniformly pointed tree such that \( T_0 \models \dot{\tau} \) (see Exercise 6.6.1). Let \( T_1 \) be defined such that \( (n_0, \dots, n_i) \in T_1 \) if and only if there exists a finite string \( (m_0, \dots, m_i) \in T_0 \) such that \( (m_0, n_0, \dots, m_i, n_i) \) is a play by II at step \( i \) following \( \dot{\tau} \). Clearly \( T_1 \le_T \dot{\tau} \). Since \( \dot{\tau} \) is II’s winning strategy, for any \( y \in [T_1] \), there is an \( x \in [T_0] \) such that \( y = x \oplus \dot{\tau} \) and \( \dot{\tau} * x \le_T y \). Moreover \( T_1 \) is a perfect tree: Every \( \tau = (m_0, \dots, n_i) \in T_1 \) that is the image of a \( \sigma = (m_0, \dots, m_i) \in T_0 \) (i.e. a play by II of \( (m_0, n_0, \dots, m_i, n_i) \) at step \( i \) following \( \dot{\tau} \)) has a pair of (immediate) incompatible extensions \( \tau_1 \) and \( \tau_2 \) in \( T_1 \) so that there exist \( x_1, x_2 >_T x \) with \( x_1 * \dot{\tau} >_T \tau_1 \) and \( x_2 * \dot{\tau} >_T \tau_2 \), since otherwise there is a unique \( y \) such that for any \( x >_T \sigma \) in \( [T_0] \), \( x \le_T \tau * \dot{\tau} = y \) and this would imply that there are uncountably many reals recursive in \( y \), which is a contradiction. We conclude that \( T_1 \) is a pointed subtree of \( A \).

We follow the idea in [58] to construct a subtree \( T_2 \) of \( T_1 \) so that \( T_2 \) is uniformly pointed. Define a sequence of trees \( T_1 = T_1^{-1} \supseteq T_1^0 \supseteq T_1^1 \dots \) which are recursive in \( T_1 \) as follows:

### Stage e:

Case (I). There is an \( n \) such that \( \Phi_e^e(n) \downarrow T_1(n) \) for some \( \sigma \in T_e^{<e-1} \). Let \( T_e^e = \{ \tau \mid \tau \in T_1^{e-1} \wedge (\tau \ge_T \sigma \lor \tau \subseteq \sigma) \} \) and proceed to the next stage.

Case (II). Otherwise.

Subcase (i) \( (\forall n)(\forall \sigma \in T_e^{<e-1})(\exists \tau > \sigma)(\tau \in T_e^{<e-1} \wedge \Phi_e^\tau(n)[|\tau|]) \). Then it is not difficult to define a \( T_1 \)-recursive perfect tree \( T_e^e \subseteq T_1^{e-1} \) such that for every \( x \in [T_e^e] \), we have \( \Phi_e^x = T_1 \). In this case, terminate the construction since \( T_1^e \le_T T_1 \) and \( T_1^e \) is a uniformly pointed tree.

Subcase (ii) Otherwise. Then there exist \(n_0\) and \(a_0 \in T_1^{<i}\) such that for every \(\tau \ge \sigma_0\) in \(T_1^{<i-1}\), \(\Phi_e^\sigma(n)[|\tau|] \downarrow\). Let \(T_1^i = \{ \tau \mid \tau \in T_1^{<i-1} \wedge (\tau \ge \sigma_0 \lor \tau \le \sigma) \}\). Proceed to the next stage.

If the construction does not terminate at any stage \(e\), then there is a real \(x \in \cap_e [T_1^e]\). By the definition of the sequence \(\{T_1^i\}\), we have \(\Phi_e^\sigma \neq T_1\), which contradicts the fact that \(T_1\) is a pointed tree. Thus the construction must terminate at some stage \(e\) where Subcase (i) holds. Let \(T_2 = T_1^e\).

## 6.6.4 Turing determinacy

### Definition 6.6.6.

- Turing determinacy (TD) states that any set of Turing degrees either contains or is disjoint from a cone.
- If \(\Gamma\) is a class of sets of Turing degrees, then \(\Gamma\)-Turing determinacy states that every set in \(\Gamma\) either contains or is disjoint from a cone.

Turing determinacy plays a significant role in descriptive set theory, as shown by the following theorems which are stated without proof:

### Theorem 6.6.7 (Harrington [45]).

ZFC+\(\Sigma_1^1\)-determinacy is consistent if and only if ZFC+\(\Sigma_1^1\)-Turing determinacy is consistent.

### Theorem 6.6.8 (Woodin).

- (i) For any \(n \ge 2\), ZFC + \(\Sigma_n^1\)-determinacy is consistent if and only if ZFC + \(\Sigma_n^1\)-Turing determinacy is consistent.

- (ii) ZF + DC + AD is consistent if and only if ZF + DC + TD is consistent.

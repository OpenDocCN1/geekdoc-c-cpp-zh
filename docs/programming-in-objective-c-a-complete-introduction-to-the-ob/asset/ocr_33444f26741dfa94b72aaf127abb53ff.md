In the table, a leading zero in front of an integer constant indicates the constant is in octal (base 8) notation, whereas a leading 0x (or 0X) indicates it is in hexadecimal (base 16) notation. A number like 0x1p3 represents a hexadecimal floating constant. Don't worry about these formats, they are just summarized in the table for completeness. Further, suffixes like f, L (l), u (U), and LL (LL) can also be used to explicitly express constants as float's, unsigned, and long long, respectively.

# Arithmetic Expressions

In Objective-C, as in virtually all programming languages, the plus sign (+) is used to add two values, the minus sign (-) is used to subtract two values, the asterisk (*) is used to multiply two values, and the slash (/) is used to divide two values. These operators are known as binary arithmetic operators because they operate on two values or terms.

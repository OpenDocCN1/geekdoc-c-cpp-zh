# 7.1.1 Twilight Deletions

In this chapter, we focus on deletions that are hard to discover because of their length. As mentioned earlier, short deletions as well as long deletions are no longer fundamental difficulties, or even become part of best-practice workflows. Mid-sized deletions, which we refer to as “NGS twilight deletions”, have become substantial computational challenges after 2009. Most recent advances have made their discovery possible in population-scale practice (9, 10, 14) – earlier approaches had pointed out theoretical ways, but remained slower by orders of magnitude (15). Evidence of this is the fact that catalogs of deletions resulting from projects (1), where References (9, 10) have been used, contain comprehensive amounts of such twilight deletions, with excellent validation rates. This is stark contrast to related projects (in particular the 1000 Genomes Project (2)).

In this chapter, we review discovery and genotyping of mid-sized deletions has been difficult and explain the techniques by which this became possible. The organization of this chapter is as follows:

+   - In Section 7.2, we provide the necessary notation.
- In Section 7.3, we give the formal definition of “twilight zone” deletions. We briefly revisit the different approaches for deletion discovery in resequencing studies, and outline our pitfalls when it comes to discovering mid-sized (“twilight zone”) deletions.
- In Section 7.5, we present a novel maximum likelihood approach for genotyping deletions which achieves highly favorable performance rates on twilight zone indels.
- In Section 7.6, we evaluate a comprehensive selection of state-of-the-art tools on NGS reads from a genome containing rare variants (Venter's genome (16)), where NGS reads are simulated by means of the Assemblathon (17) read simulator and current NGS technology (Illumina HiSeq and MiSeq reads).
- In Section 7.7, we discuss all results presented and point out challenges that are still open.

# 9.1 INTRODUCTION

DNA methylation is one of the most important epigenetic mechanisms that ensures the maintenance and inheritance of gene-expression programs in mammalian cells (1). DNA methylation primarily involves the methylation of the 5th carbon on residues (5-mC) in a CpG dinucleotide. Three highly conserved DNA methyltransferase (DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B), which complex together in vivo, establish and maintain the DNA methylation landscape within the human genome (2). DNMT1 has a high affinity toward hemi-methylated DNA and is the key maintenance methyltransferase in mammals. Epigenetic alterations are at least common as, if not more than, in normal cells, cancer cells exhibit global hypomethylation in repeat elements and pericentromeric regions and simultaneously hypermethylation in normally protected CGIs (5, 6). Aberrant DNA methylation changes can also cause a number of human diseases such as developmental diseases (ICF syndrome, Prader-Willi, and Angel syndromes), aging-related diseases (e.g., Alzheimer’s), and complex trait diseases (7–11). Therefore, it is very important to understand how methylation patterns are established and maintained during normal development.

Recently, it was discovered that 5-hydroxymethylosine (5-hmC), an intermediate to be abundant in human and mouse brains, as well as in embryonic cells (12, 13). Ten-ele translocation (Tet) family of DNA hydroxylases including Tet1, 2, and 3 can catalyze the oxidation of 5-mC to 5-hmC (12, 13). Subsequently, 5-hmC can be further oxidized by Tet enzymes to 5-formylcytosine (5-IC) and 5-carboxycytosine (5-caC) (14, 15). The oxidation pathway from 5-mC to 5-hmC and 5-caC was proposed as a plausible mechanism of DNA glycosylase (TDG) (14). It was demonstrated that TET1 is expressed in embryonic stem (ES) cells and TET2 regulates cell commitment by modulating the balance between self-renewal and differentiation (17). While TET3 is important in regulating the global DNA methylation of zygotes (18). Although the biological function of 5-hmC is not fully understood, it has been implied in transcriptional regulation (19, 20). Loss of 5-hmC has been reported as an epigenetic hallmark of melanoma, with diagnostic and prognostic implications (21). In addition, TET proteins are frequently altered by genetic mutation or gene translocations in human leukemia.

A variety of methods are available for analyzing gene-specific as genome-wide DNA methylation patterns (Figure 9.1). Almost all of the methods utilize three basic approaches: (i) digest unmethylated DNA with methylation-sensitive restriction enzymes; (ii) use antimethylcytosine antibodies or methyl-binding domain (MBD) proteins to enrich methylated DNA; and (iii) bisulfite treatment that can convert unmethylated cytosine into uracil leaving methylated cytosine unchanged. Several excellent results of the genome-wide DNA methylation analysis are already available evolved during the last two decades from using low-throughput PCR and gel-based platforms to the current high-throughput microarray and next-generation sequencing (NGS) technologies.

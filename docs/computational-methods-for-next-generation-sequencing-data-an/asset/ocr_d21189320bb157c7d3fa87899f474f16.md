# 16.1 INTRA-HOST VIRUS POPULATIONS

Viruses are pathogens that cause infectious diseases (1). A single virus particle, called virion, exhibits its genetic material as a single- or double-stranded linear or circular DNA or RNA molecule in one or multiple copies. A virion alone cannot reproduce itself as. For reproduction, a virus depends on infecting cells of an organism. RNA viruses are of particular interest, as their viral replication enzymes have a low fidelity and produce error-prone copies of the genome, including single point mutations and recombination. This high error rate, limits the genome size in the range of 10³–10⁸ base pairs (bp), which is much smaller than bacterial or eukaryotic genomes. Beyond a certain error rate threshold, mutational meltdown would lead to a virus population that is not able to preserve the genotype with the highest fitness, where fitness rate and genome size are anticorrelated (2). A genome is protected by a protein coat and many viruses are protected by an additional envelope. Receptors on the outside surface are used to attach and fuse with host cells. The viral life cycle, from infection of a cell to the release of new virions, is very short and is completed in a few days. This leads to a vast amount of virions in each infected host. The swarm of viruses evolves through mutation and natural selection. It is subject to the host’s immune pressure and possibly antiviral therapy.

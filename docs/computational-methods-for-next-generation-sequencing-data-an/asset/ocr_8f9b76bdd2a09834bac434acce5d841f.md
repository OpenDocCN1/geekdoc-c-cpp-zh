The last step is CNVs calling. At first, segments with a mean RD signal deviating by least a quarter from genomic RD signal are selected, and regions with p-value by a t-test less than 0.05 is called. Experiment results on data from the 1000 Genomes Project (reads length 36) show that CNator is able to achieve high sensitivity (86-96%) with false-discovery rate 3-20%, and high resolution in breakpoint discovery (< 200 bp in 90% of cases with high sequencing coverage). However, CNator is sensitive to read length, and for constant coverage, as read length increases, less reads will be generated, which will degrade the sensitivity. Also, longer reads are more difficult to be fully mapped around the breakpoints. Thus, it is harder for CNator to call small CNVs. Therefore, RD-based approach is useful for CNVs discovery, but still has its limitations.

# 8.2.2.2 BreakDancer is one of the first RP-based computational approaches. It is designed for the genome-wide detection of five types of structural variants, including deletions, insertions, inversions, and intachromosomal and interchromosomal translocations. It takes paired-reads as input. If both single-ended and paired-ended reads are given, only the paired-ended ones will be used for further analysis.

First, BreakDancer tallies the anomalous read pairs. Types of structural variations have different anomalous patterns on paired-reads, as explained earlier. According to these different patterns, algorithm tries to locate a set of anomalous regions and identify connected clusters. Different SV types are considered. Each region should have more anomalous read pairs than expected by chance. This allows BreakDancer to filter some false variants due to sequencing and mapping errors.

A structural variant is then considered to derive from a set of regions, each of which connects via another anomalous read pair. For example, BreakDancer uses a preset threshold of at least two anomalous read pairs. Compared to average coverage, the more supporting the region(s), the stronger statistical signals are obtained. BreakDancer applies a statistical test, called confidence score estimation, to quantify the underlying error probabilities of each potential structural variant. This test assumes that the genomic locations of each type structural variant follows a uniform distribution. For each pair of adjacent sites, the probability of harboring a structural variant follows binomial distributions with each component representing one of the insert types. On the scale of genome-wide, the probability of multiple structural variants locating at a particular site follows mixture of Poisson distribution.

In practice, BreakDancer suggests to define three types of inserts: long, medium, and short that are measured according to preset thresholds. The identification process is to find the regions that contain significantly more inserts and insertions from regions that contain significantly short inserts via the Poisson test.

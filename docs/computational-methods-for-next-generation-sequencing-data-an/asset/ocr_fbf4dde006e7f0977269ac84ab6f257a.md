However, many clinically relevant viruses, including human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) and hepatitis C virus (HCV), undergo recombination with high frequency. # 16.1.2 Fitness

If a virus has a high error rate, it follows an evolutionarily stable strategy (7) to generate a population of hapotypes with approximately equal fitness instead of one hapotype being the fittest (Figure 16.2). Occupation of such a broad fitness landscape is survival of the flattest (8). It is a survival strategy. The evolutionary trajectory of the viral infection depends not on the characteristics of the fittest but also on the whole population.

Natural selection also improve the changes of survival in changing environments. For example, a low-frequency hapotype that hephitylates an appropriate resistance mutation could gain an advantage under treatment. Then, the mass of the quasispecies shifts to the new master sequence and a new hapotype distribution is created. This survival strategy improves changes of adaption to environmental changes, such as treatment switches.

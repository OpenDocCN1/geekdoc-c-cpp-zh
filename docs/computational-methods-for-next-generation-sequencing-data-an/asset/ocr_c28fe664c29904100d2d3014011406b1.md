# COMPUTATIONAL APPROACHES FOR FINDING LONG INSERTIONS AND DELETERS

Figure 8.3 shows the region of the long deletion and Figure 8.4 shows the region of the long insertion.

From Figure 8.3, we can see that the coverage at reference: 250,001–251,000 (the deletion) is only half of that at other coordinates (without SVs). This is because only one copy of genome of the sample has the sequence from 250,001–251,000, and we sequenced it with 30x. The regions without SVs are sequenced with 60x coverage, 30x for each copy. So one signature of long deletion when using NGS results is that the coverage of the deletion is smaller compared with regions without SVs. Ideally, for organisms with two copies of genome, if the deletion is heterozygous (in both copies of genome) then the coverage is about 0. This is the read depth (RD) signature. For real data, the RD signature can suffer from several aspects. Reads may be generated not as evenly as the simulated data. Coverage at different locations of the same copy of genome can fluctuate. Coverage from the two copies of genome at the same location can be different. Sequencing and mapping errors may add false-positive reads to the region and remove true-positive reads from the region. Also, not every set has high coverage. Therefore, it may happen that the coverage drop at the location of deletion cannot be reliably detected from the background fluctuation of coverage. Moreover, the number of copies of genomes is not necessarily two. Some organisms have more.

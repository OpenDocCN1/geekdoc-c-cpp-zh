Another well-known fact affecting genotyping is the sequencing of multiple copies of the same fragment produced during the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) amplification step (23). This problem creates unexpectedly a high number of calls in the regions of the genome where the multiplexed fragment was extracted. PCR duplicated fragments are likely to produce false-negative calls in heterozygous sites. A common strategy to reduce the effects of PCR amplification is to identify clusters of different reads starting and ending at exactly the same genomic location and keeping a small maximum m of alignments per cluster. This ensures that only up to m copies of a fragment multiplied by PCR amplification will be taken into account for SNP discovery. Smaller values of m increase the specificity at the cost of sensitivity (19).

**7.5.1.1 Split-Read Evidence** Let us first consider the case of no alignment uncertainty. If read alignments are correct, then they precisely reflect the differences between the donor and the reference.

Let $D$ be the genotyped and $A$ be an alignment, for example, $A$, stretches across the breakpoints of $D$. Under the assumption of no alignment uncertainty, we obtain that $A$ stems from a chromosomal copy that is affected by $D$ and only if $w_a$ and $z_a$ agree with the left and right breakpoints of $D$. If the split disagrees with the deletion breakpoints or there is no split, the read behind $A$ stems from a chromosomal copy that is not affected by the deletion with probability one.

Let $A$ be an alignment with a split/gap that agrees with $D$. By the above considerations, the read behind $A$ stems from a chromosomal copy that is affected by $D$. By Bayes' formula, $P(G_i \mid A^+) \propto P(A^+ \mid G_i)$. First, $P(A^+ \mid G_i) = \frac{1}{2}(P(A^+ \mid G_i) + P(A^+ \mid G_j))$, which reflects that if one randomly selects one of the two chromosomal copies, only one of which is affected by $D$, and then generates the read from it. The *case of $B$ being an alignment in disagreement with $D$* is treated analogously, with $P(B^+ \mid G_2) = 0$. Transforming this into a posterior probability yields

```
$P(G_i \mid A^+) \doteq \begin{cases} 2 & i=2 \ 1 & i=1 \ 0 & i=0 \end{cases} \text{ and } P(G_i \mid B^+) \doteq egin{cases} 0 & i=2 \ 1 & i=1 \ 2 & i=0 \ \end{cases} \quad (7.7)$
```

# 2.2 BACKGROUND

We live in a world dominated by microorganisms (1). Interconnected members of this invisible microbial majority form distributed metabolic networks linking genomic and phenotypic expression from single cells to earth systems (2, 3). Such networks are continuously changing through mutation, gene transfer, and habitat selection creating redundant and resilient microbial modules driving matter and energy conversion in natural and engineered ecosystems (4–8). Plurality sequencing, also known as environmental genomics, ecological genomics, or metagenomics, generates vast amounts of environmental sequence information with the potential to transform our worldview (4, 9–11) and enable technological and therapeutic innovations through perception and manipulation of these networks (12–18).

Although progeny and single-cell sequencing technologies are rapidly expanding our capacity to generate environmental sequence information (19–22), there are a number of computational and analytical challenges that limit knowledge creation and translation (23–25). Collins and colleagues agree that similar problems are common to many areas of modern experimental research: “Data comes in all scales and shapes, covering large international experiments; cross-laboratory, single-laboratory, and individual observations; and potentially individuals lives. The discipline and scale of individual experiments and their data rates make the use of tools a formidable problem!” (26). In particular, computational and visual analytical bottlenecks present powerful limits to integration across different levels of biological information (DNA, RNA, protein, and scalable comparisons between replicate samples or communities. While good progress has been made in developing tools to integrate, user-friendly softwares that integrate novel statistical and distributed computing techniques and effective structures across multiple hierarchical levels are needed to fully realize the power and promote of environmental sequence information.

Several services integrate environmental sequence analysis and information storage. The Metagenome Rapid Annotation using Subsystem Technology (MG-RAST http://metagenomics.mpdr.org/) is a web-based portal using the SEED annotation framework for functional genes and subsystems that provides annotation and comparative analysis services (27). The Integrated Genomes and Metagenomes (IMG/M, http://img.jie.doe.gov/m/) (28, 29) and Community Infrastructure for Advanced Microbial Ecology Research and Analysis (CAMERA http://camera.caliti2.net/) (30) archive data sets provide annotation and comparative analysis services for selected sequencing projects.

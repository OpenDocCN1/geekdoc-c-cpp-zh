First, it measures the Pearson or Spearman's correlation coefficient for each pair of samples and generates a heat map to cluster samples based on a matrix of the correlation coefficients. Second, PCA is performed and plotted using the first and second components. Finally, BSPipe conducts hierarchical clustering and k-means to identify differential methylation in an unsupervised manner.

Differential methylation among groups BSPipe identifies DMRs in descriptive way by methylation between two samples or groups. It also assesses the significance of DMRs between two groups using statistical tests such as Student t, Wilcoxon, and Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests, and three or more groups using ANOVA. BSPipe generates adjusted p-values for multiple comparisons, and diagnostic plots such as histogram plots of p-values and adjusted p-values, scatter plots between two samples, and a volcano plot heat map for DMRs.

External programs and parallel execution BSPipe prepares the input of and runs programs as QDM and BSmooth to enable user's complementary analysis. To take advantage of a multiple computer or a cluster computer, BSPipe coupled with grid engines, for example, sun grid engine, through a configuration file. It splits input files in FASTQ format into many small files and then submits a job for each split file to perform sequence mapping to methylation quantification. The methylation of CpGs is merged. BSPipe also supports annotation and visualization of DMRs as depicted in the next section.

# 9.4 CONCLUSION

Enrichment-based and bisulfite-based approaches described in this chapter have successfully profiled DNA methylation and identified genome-wide methylation, for example, References 18, 97–99. As analysis results, sequence mapping and methylation profile and differential methylation of CpGs, tiling windows, or genomic features are stored in various formats as BAM, Wiggle, GFF, and BED, which can be visualized on the UCSC genome browser (68), gbrowser (69), IGV (70), or IG (71).

Identified DMRs are annotated with genomic features as genes, CGIs (CpG Islands), noncoding RNAs, and repeats in the NCBI and UCSC databases. Specifically, a gene is regarded as having upstream, exon, intron, and downstream components. Users can set the length of the upstream and downstream parts of genes. Coding exons are further classified into 5' UTR, 5' CDS, middle CDS, 3' CDS, and 3' UTR. Furthermore, the annotation distinguishes the strands, as is, categories for each strand, and then, and then merges them into bi-upstream, upstream, bi-downstream, downstream, and the categories for gene body. In the comparison with CpG islands (CGIs), DMRs are stratified into north CpG shelves and shores, inside CGIs, and south shores. For each of the other features as repeats and noncoding RNAs, a DMR is classified as an inside or outside feature. The annotation results can be summarized in tables and pie charts.

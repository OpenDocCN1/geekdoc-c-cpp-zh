The measure $v$ represents the ratio of samples in an OR, or reliability of methylation patterns. To merge adjacent ORs with the same methylation pattern, the algorithm chooses an OR with the maximal $v$ and merges adjacent ORs if their distance is greater than 200 bp or the $v$ values of the adjacent OR are greater than 0.5. The methylation status of a merged region is determined by $u_1$ and $u_2$, which are the $u$ values of hotspot and adjacent ORs: CMR if $u_1=1$ and $u_2=1$, CUMR if $u_1=-1$ and $u_2=-1$, and DMR. DMRs are divided into hypo- or hypermethylated regions based on $u_1, u_2>0$ or $u_1, u_2<0$.

To quantify the methylation difference of identified regions, CpG_MPS used modified Shannon entropy to decode CpGs in QDMR (94) with the average methylation of CpGs in the identified regions. The lower entropy of DMRs represents the larger methylation change among samples, as described in QDMR. However, the entropy of CMRs and CUMRs are differently interpreted, as is, the higher entropy represents the most frequent methylation. CpG_MPS applied to determine the threshold of entropy for significant DMRs, CMRs, or CMRRs.

9.3.2.4 BSmooth BSmooth has proposed a detection method for differential regions using a smoothing technique, which smooths the methylation of a CpG by averaging the methylation of CpGs in a window. BSmooth estimates methylation profile for a window with =2 kbp and =70 CpGs using a local likelihood smoother (96), which is suitable for binary data with a non-additive Gaussian error model and is implemented in R package “locfit”.

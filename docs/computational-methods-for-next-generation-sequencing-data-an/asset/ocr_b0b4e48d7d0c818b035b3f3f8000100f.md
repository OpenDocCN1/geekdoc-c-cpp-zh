In addition to point mutations, our simulation model produces recombinant genomes by first constructing the genotype (i.e., sequence of SNVs) for the population. A random walk is performed over this genotype as specified number of times. Any crossover that occurs represents a new recombination between the “left” and “right” original genomes. Recombinations are independently produced, and no control is imposed over the number and length of the recombination. This model produces highly recombinant data on average, posing challenges and can be used to assess assembly accuracy. Simulation model incorporates mutation into the process by selecting a population and nucleotide-swap at random. Simulation results (Figure 4,7) suggest that our method can accurately assess viral population in the presence of recombinants and point mutations, maintaining PPP of 100%.

Finally, we evaluate population quantification accuracy, which is the accuracy of our method in prediction abundances of the assembled variants. Taking the results from VGA on 10 real HIV clones with 50,000 reads and 2 x 100 bp, the JSD is 2.93e-05 for the power law and 0.001 for the uniform-based populations. This already small measure only decreases as the size of the input grows.

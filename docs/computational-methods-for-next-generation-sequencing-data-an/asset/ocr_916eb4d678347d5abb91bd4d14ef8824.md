9.2.2.2 MEDIPS Similar to Batan, MEDIPS (65, 72) defining coupling factors as the local CpG density by counting the number of CpGs for a given position. Let $p$ be a given position, and $d$ the size of the library (fragment) chosen for the sequencing (Figure 9.6). The objective is to count the number of CpGs in $[p-d, p+d]$. Several ways have been proposed: count, linear, exp, log, and custom. While count counts the number of CpGs, the other weigh CpGs based on the distance to $p$ using linear, exponential, or logarithmic function. Custom allows user-defined weights, for example, Batan's coupling factors. Coupling factors are precomputed for a genome. Since mammalian genomes are big, fixed-size (say 50 bp) are used for efficiency, where $p$ is the center of a bin. The authors of MEDIPS have tested the different weighting functions and library sizes with 50 bp by measuring the correlation of the methylation to local CpG density using bisulfite sequencing (73). For each of about 300 genomic loci of length 50-500 bp in the bisulfite sequencing, they computed the mean scores and the mean factors, and measured a Pearson correlation coefficient. The best parameter setting is use $l=700$ and count; this gives the best negative correlation as expected in mammalian cells: regions with low CpG density are highly methylated while those with high CpG density are mainly unmethylated.

To make the read count comparable among samples, it is normalized in RPM (Reads Per Million), which is the read count when the read count at position $i$ and $n$ represents the total number of reads mapped. Now we have $rpm = c * 10^6/n$ where $c$ represents the read count. The goal is to adjust the RPM value by the coupling factor, that is, CpG density. To this end, MEDIPS visually inspected a calibration plot where the $x$-and $y$-axis represent coupling factors and read counts, respectively. As shown in Figure 9.6, while the M axis shows no dependency, the grey line called a calibration curve shows a linear relationship for the low range of coupling factor. High range of coupling factor has low read count because regions with high CpG density are mainly unmethylated in mammalian cells. Pelluzzo al. (75) confirmed the density for the high range of coupling factor in an experiment of fully methylated samples using MedIP-ChIP data. Based on this observation, MEDIPS fits a linear regression model to calculate the methylation score with CpG density. A coupling factor with $n$ genomic bins, $cf = (cf_1, ..., cf_n)$, the algorithm finds the first local maximum of coupling factors, which is denoted as $cf^m$ and performs a linear regression using the least square approach to $cf^m = (cf_1^m, ..., cf_n^m)$ and $rpm^m = (rpm_1, ..., rpm_n) : rpm = \alpha * cf + eta$ where $\alpha$ and $eta$ are the theoretical slope and intercept and $\epsilon$ is an error term. The parameters are adjusted, RPM values are calculated using $rms = \log(2rpm/(\alpha+eta))$. Figure 9.7 shows large changes between RPM and RMS for two samples. To compare any region of interest (ROI), especially for different lengths, MEDIPS calculates an methylation score of ROI by the mean of rms values divided by the mean of coupling factors.

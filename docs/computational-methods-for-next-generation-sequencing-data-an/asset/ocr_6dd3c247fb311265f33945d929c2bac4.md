We allow an incorrect assignment of reads to the samples *in silico contamination*. The average percentage of samples without silico contamination reads from 100% to 98.13% for $T = 15$, from 100% to 96.13% for $T = 25$, and from 100% to 93.8% for $T = 35$ (Figure 3.6a); the percentage of silico contaminated samples increases with the total number of samples. In silico contaminants constitute a small minority within contaminated samples: an average of 0.163% of all reads for $T=15$, 0.5545% for $T=25$, and 0.892% for $T = 35$ (Figure 3.6b).

Root square error of the frequency estimates of deconvoluted haplotypes is in order 0.031–0.107% for $T=15$, 0.025–0.139% for $T=25$, and 0.028–0.174% for $T=35$; this is an increasing function of the number of samples (Figure 3.7).

According to all measures considered earlier, the accuracy of the samples deconvolution is affected by the number of allowed samples per pool. The algorithm is more accurate for smaller pools although the accuracy remains high even for larger pools.

This has not been widespread in RNA-seq analyses due to cost considerations, but the capacity of the sequencing platforms has increased to the point where sufficient depth of coverage is easily reached, so investing in replicate samples is becoming more common. Obviously this is most important when the primary interest is the discovery of differentially expressed (DE) genes. This issue has been discussed in depth (12) and it is being realized that replicates are as important to an analysis as sequencing depth, which reaches a point of diminishing returns, and addition of replicates can increase the power to find differentially expressed genes (36).

Apart from cost, there are a variety of variables to consider in deciding how much sequence is necessary, depending on the overall objective of the analysis. A major determinant is the organism that is being sequenced; the capacity of transcription from a 3 Gb human genome are different from that of the 12 Mb cerevisiae genome in terms of gene sizes, exon-intron structure, information abundance, and the extent of gene expansions. As the output of the current technology is that any output from current machines will be overkilled for most non-mammalian organisms, we will only consider this in terms of mammalian genomes.

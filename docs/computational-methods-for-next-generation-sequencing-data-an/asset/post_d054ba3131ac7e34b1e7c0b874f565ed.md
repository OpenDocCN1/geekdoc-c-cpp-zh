## INTRODUCTION

A major goal of transcriptomic analysis is to identify and characterize genes, as RNA-seq has been shown to capture a range of expression spanning five orders of magnitude. However, read coverage is not uniform even within the same transcript due to biases in library preparation and sequencing. Furthermore, each read may be assigned to several genes in a family that share similar sequences or to several isoforms of the same gene. Biological phenomena such as overlapping and nested genes and trans-splicing increase the difficulty of assembly. Lastly, analyzing the large volume of data in an RNA-seq experiment demands tools that are scalable and efficient.

There are two classes of transcript assembly: *de novo*, which assemble based solely on sequence overlap, and genome-based, which first align reads to a reference genome and then assemble the overlapping alignments. Genome-based methods are generally more accurate. However, in the absence of a genome sequence or if the genome is highly fragmented, *de novo* assembly can be effectively used to determine a representative set of transcripts, although the assembly process is more complex and error prone. We review the two approaches, specific methods and design principles, in the following sections.

## 11.2 DE NOVO ASSEMBLY

This class of methods assembly based on the overlap of their sequences. Intuitively, if the end sequence of one read is similar to the start of another, this relationship establishes an order between the two reads within the originating sequence.

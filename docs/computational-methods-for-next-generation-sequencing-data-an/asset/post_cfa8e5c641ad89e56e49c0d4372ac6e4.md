In the upper part of the twilight zone (see Table 7.3), internal segment size-based and hybrid approaches have clear advantages over (split-read) alignment-based approaches because the recall rates of alignment-based approaches drop to zero or the precision suffers. Among alignment-based approaches, PINDEL has the best recall on longer deletions, at least at sufficiently high precision, and therefore makes a valuable contribution in those ranges. Note that among internal segment size-based approaches, VariationHunter puts clear emphasis on recall, which comes at the expense of inaccurate annotations, as indicated by very strict precision. The only approaches that achieve high recall across all ranges of the twilight zone are CLEVER and MATE-COWER. The real weaknesses are heterogeneous deletions of length 30–50bp, however, none of the other tools achieve better recall on HiSeq data. In this category, one has to step up from HiSeq in combination with BWA-MEM. When comparing HiSeq with MiSeq data, an immediate observation is that alignment-based approaches tend to achieve higher recall on MiSeq data, at the cost of lower precision. Internal segment size-based approaches often incur deigible losses on MiSeq data, which is likely due to the longer average segment size for the MiSeq data.

## 7.4 Genotyping

See Tables 7.4 and 7.5 for the following. We evaluated state-of-the-art protocols for genotyping. We ran UnifiedGenoty (UG) and HaplotypeCaller (HC) on both BWA-MEM and GSNAP algorithms to produce genotyping calls. We also ran MATE-CLEVER in mode. It becomes evident in an overall statement, MATE-CLEVER is the most favorable approach for genotyping deletions longer than 30 bp, both at sufficiently high recall (see "homozyg correct" in Table 7.4) and good precision (see "heterozyg, correct" in Table 7.5). Usually, MATE-CLEVER predicts homozygosity in about 90% and heterozygosity in about 80% of cases. While MATE-CLEVER is seemingly the most favorable overall, the other approaches have partial strengths. Notably, both BWA-HC and GSNAP-HC achieve better rates than MATE-CLEVER on homozygous deletions of 30–49bp, in terms of recall and precision. When comparing UG with HC, one observes that HC leads to considerable increases in recall over UG, while UG is generally the most conservative method for genotyping deletions longer than 50bp.

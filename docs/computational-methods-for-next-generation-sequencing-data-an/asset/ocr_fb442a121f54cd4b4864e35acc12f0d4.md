To model experimental variability, QDMR assumed that the distribution of methylation values in a region follows a normal distribution with mean close to zero and an unknown standard deviation (SD). First, it chose 0.07 for SD, which allows methylation variation between 43 and 57 in 68% of the samples, between 36 and 64 in 95%, and between 29 and 71 in 99%. Second, simulated 5000 uniformly methylated regions across samples and computed entropy $H_Q$ for each region. Third, it determined the entropy value at p-value of 0.05 (one-sided) as a threshold of the distribution of 5000 entropies. It repeated this process 10 times and used the mean of the 10 samples as the final threshold for DMR identification.

# 9.3.2 Identification of CpG Methylation Patterns of Genomic Regions (**CpG_MPs**)

The tool *CpG_MPs* searches hot spots of differential methylation using hot spot extension and Shannon entropy. It measures beta values of CpGs with 5 or more reads and divides a CpG into four categories based on its base score: *unmethylated* for [0.0,0.3], *partially unmethylated* for (0.3,0.5], *partially methylated* for (0.5,0.7), and *methylated* for [0.7,1). Next, it searches for unmethylated and methylated hot spots that have at least n consecutively unmethylated and methylated hot spots, respectively. Then, it extends the unmethylated hotspots towards both ends until reaching the partially methylated or methylated CpG. Similarly, it extends methylated hotspots. It combines two extended hotspots with the same region if the difference is less than 200 bp and computes the mean and standard deviation of the beta values of CpGs for each methylated or unmethylated region (finally merged, extended hotspots) as shown in Figure 9.14a.

## 7.5.1.2 Internal Segment-Based Evidence

Internal segment-based evidence is provided by evaluating the empirical statistics on fragment length inherent to the library read stems. We develop this part in view of fragment length statistics being approximately Gaussian. However, this can be easily generalized to arbitrary statistics. Let $D$ be the deletion to be genotyped and $C(D)$ be its centerpoint. Let $R$ be the read that has alignment to A where $x_A < C(D) < y_A$ that is, the alignment interval of $A$ contains the centerpoint of the breakpoints of $D$. Note that a centerpoint-oriented selection leads to a balanced is in choosing equal amounts of reads to provide evidence for and against the deletion, as outlined in Reference 10. Let $\mu$ and $\sigma$ be mean and standard deviation of the internal segment size distribution of the library $R$ stems from. So, internal segment length, as a random variable $X$, is distributed as the normal distribution $X \sim N_{\mu, \sigma}$ for the library under consideration. There are two cases: first, alignments $A$ whose reads stem from a chromosomal copy that is not affected by $D$, and second alignments $B$ whose reads stem from a chromosomal copy that is affected by $D$. We obtain

```
$I(A) \sim N_{\mu, \sigma}^A$ and $I(B) \sim N_{\mu+L, \sigma}^B$,
```

where the case reflects that the alignment contains the deletion of length L. Refer to Figure 7.7 for an illustration. We compute that $P(A^+ | G_0) \sim N_{\mu, \sigma}^A(I(A))$ and $P(A^+ | G_2) \sim N_{\mu+L, \sigma}^B(I(A))$ as appropriate densities for the cases of no variant and a homozygous variant.

Let $Z := N_{\mu, \sigma}^A(I(A)) + N_{\mu+L, \sigma}^B(I(A))$. In analogy for the split-read case, we arrive at

```
$P(G_i | A^+) := \frac{1}{2} \cdot N_{\mu, \sigma}^A(I(A)) \quad i = 1 \\
\frac{1}{2} \cdot N_{\mu+L, \sigma}^B(I(A)) \quad i = 0$
```

as an appropriate probability distribution for reads whose alignments span the breakpoints of deletions by their internal segments.

## 7.6 RESULTS

### 7.6.1 Data Set

We downloaded all variant annotations for Craig Venter's genome from the HuRef database (16). We generated a diploid genome using these annotations, as per the procedure described in Reference 10 to generate a 'father' genome, which results in a genome that is realistic in terms of both variants and zygosity status of variants. Note that direct use of the annotations results in a genome with an unrealistic ratio of heterozygous to homozygous deletions (vastly overrated homozygous indels), which is likely due to the difficulties in determining the zygosity status of insertions and deletions during the original assembly stage. We have resolved these issues such that the ratio of heterozygous and homozygous deletions is realistic.

Subsequently, we simulated reads for each copy, using Simseq, the read simulator of the Assemblathon (17), at 15x coverage, which results in 30x coverage overall. We opted to simulate reads according to two prevalent and most recent Illumina protocols: HiSeq and MiSeq, where mean and standard deviation for the internal segment were 112 and 15 (HiSeq) and 250 and 15 (MiSeq), respectively.

Table 7.1 gives an overview of the used tools. We aimed at selecting state-of-the-art tools from different categories. Furthermore, we included four standard read mappers in the analysis: Bowtie2, BWA (MEM), GSNAP, and Stampy. Although these tools do not explicitly target deletion discovery, they have some capabilities of mapping reads across deletions. For these tools, we extracted all deletions that were contained in two or more read alignments to compile a set of predictions.

- Strict precision, which is the fraction of calls where the centerpoint of breakpoints deviates by not than 20bp and the length by not than 10bp.

- We also evaluate the caslets in terms of Recall (hom.) and Recall (het.), which are the fractions of true homozygous and heterozygous deletions that were correctly discovered, according to the criteria for relaxed precision.

## 10.1 INTRODUCTION

DNA methylation is the addition of a methyl group at the carbon-5 residue of DNA cytines. In mammals, DNA methylation is observed mainly at CpG dinucleotides (CpG islands). In stem cells and plants, it is also extensively found in the context of CpHpG where H is A, T, or C.

In mammals, methylation is first established de novo by methyltransferases Dnmt3a and Dnmt3b during early development and gametogenesis. It is [maintained by Dnmt1] during replication.

DNA methylation has been associated with gene expression, imprinting, silencing, embryonic development, and cancer. Various techniques are available to profile methylation, either genome-wide or targeted at specific regions. In general, these techniques are divided into two categories: (i) enrichment based and (ii) bisulfite-based methods. In the first category enrich genomic regions with high methylation using antibody or methylation status-sensitive endonuclease by selection; then, enriched regions are subject to hybridization via microarrays or by sequencing. Typical methods in this category include MeD-Seq, MBD-Seq, and CHARM. Readers are referred to the review for an introduction to these methods and their relative advantages and drawbacks. The second group of bisulfite-conversion methods is particularly attractive for methylome profiling because these methods have enabled scientists to produce methylation maps at single-base resolution. Bisulfite-conversion methods involve the following steps:

- 1. chemical conversion of non-methylated cytosines to uracils by treating DNA with sodium bisulfite
- 2. PCR amplification (that changes uracils to thymines),
- 3. sequencing size-selected DNA fragments

Initially, the sequencing of bisulfite-treated DNA fragments was carried out via the Sanger method, but the cost of this procedure restricted bisulfite-conversion methods to small-scale analysis. Recently, progress in next-generation sequencing technology made it possible to determine the methylation status of a whole genome (BS-seq). In eukaryotes, it was first used for relatively small genomes, such as that of Arabidopsis thaliana (5, 6) and reduced representation of large genomes (7, 8). It is now practical to profile large genomes such as human and mammalian genomes (9, 10).

Compared to enrichment-based methods, BS-seq proves to be the most informative approach. It allows the examination of methylation status at single-base resolution, whereas enrichment-based methods only give average profile over a region.

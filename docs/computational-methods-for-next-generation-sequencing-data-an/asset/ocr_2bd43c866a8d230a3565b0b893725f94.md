# COMPUTATIONAL METHODS FOR NGS DATA ANALYSIS

In the following, we assume that a total of $M$ bootstrap samples is generated for each of the compared conditions. We experimented with two approaches for pairing the FPKMs estimated from the two sets of bootstrap samples. The “matching” approach, a random one-to-one mapping is created between the $M$ estimates of first condition and the $M$ estimates of the second condition. This results in $M$ pairs of FPKM estimates. In the “all” approach, $M^2$ pairs of FPKM estimates are generated by pairing each FPKM estimate for first condition with each FPKM estimate for second condition. When pairing FPKM estimate $a_i$ for the first condition with FPKM estimate $b_j$ for the second condition, we use $a_i/b_j$ as an estimate for the fold change in the gene expression level between the two conditions. The “matching” approach results in $N=M$ fold change estimates, while the “all” approach results in $N=M^2$ fold change estimates.

The IsoDE for differential expression uses two user-specified parameters, namely the minimum fold change and the minimum bootstrap support $b$. For a given threshold $f$ (typically selected based on biological considerations), we calculate the percentage of fold estimates that are equal to or higher than $f$ when testing for overexpression, respectively, to lower than $1/f$ when testing for underexp. If this percentage is higher than the minimum bootstrap support $b$, otherwise the gene is classified as nondifferentially expressed (non-DE). The actual bootstrap support for fold threshold $f$ and the minimum fold change with bootstrap support of at least $b$, are included in the IsoDE output to allow the user to easily increase the stringency of the DE test.

As discussed in the results section, varying the bootstrap support $b$ allows us to achieve a smooth trade-off between sensitivity and specificity for a fixed fold change $f$ (see.g., Figure 14.3). Since different trade-offs may be desirable in different biological contexts, no $b$ is universally applicable. In our experiments, we computed $b$ using a simple binomial model for the distribution of fold estimates and a significance level $\alpha=0.05$. Specifically, we assume that under the null the fold changes from bootstrap estimates are likely to be greater or smaller than $f$. We then compute $b$ as $x_{min}/N$, where $x_{min}=\min\{x:P(X\geq x)\leq\alpha\}$ and $X$ is a binomial random variable denoting the number of successes in $N$ independent Bernoulli trials with success probability of $0.5$. For convenience, a calculator for computing the bootstrap support needed to achieve a desired significance level (the possible number) of bootstrap samples for each condition has been made available online (see Availability).

The number $M$ of bootstrap samples is another parameter that the users of IsoDE must specify. As discussed in the results section, computing the bootstrap support for all genes takes negligible time, and the overall running time of IsoDE is dominated by the time to implement the $2M$ IsoEM runs. Hence, the overall run-times grow linearly with $M$. Experimental results suggest that the “all” pairing approach produces highly accurate results with relatively small values of $M$ (e.g., $M=20$), and thus results in run-times, independent of the number of replicates.

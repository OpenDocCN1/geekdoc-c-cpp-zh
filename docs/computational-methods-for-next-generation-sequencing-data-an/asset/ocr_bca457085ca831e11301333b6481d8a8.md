Conversely, de novo approaches are favored when there is no no genome sequence available or when the genome is fragmented or of low quality. Specific scenarios such as the presence of trans-spliced genes, in polymorphic regions not included in the reference genome, gene fusion or rearrangement events characteristic of cancer samples are also well addressed with de novo techniques. As a drawback, de novo assembly is computationally more challenging and significantly more sensitive to sequencing errors. It is also more prone to overclustering of paralogs and producing chimeric constructs. Given the mix of strategies employed by genome and transcriptome analysis projects, both approaches are likely to be needed and will be widely used in the foreseeable future. Transcript reconstruction methods and their mathematical foundations need to continually adapt to provide more accurate solutions and to adapt to the characteristics and biases of the evolving sequencing technologies.

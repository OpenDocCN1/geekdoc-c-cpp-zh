# 17.2 BACKGROUND

# 17.2.1 Infectious Bronchitis Virus

RNA viruses are causing a significant burden on the health and productivity of culturally important animals since the rapid evolution of RNA viruses within infected hosts is coupled in this context with frequent transmissions between animals, due to the typically high animal density in production environments. Poultry farms are particularly susceptible to viral infections, which cause significant economic losses worldwide in terms of impaired growth, reduced egg production and quality, and even mortality.

In the United States with infections with viral strains of Newcastle disease and highly pathogenic influenza, IBV is considered the biggest cause of economic loss. It infects the domestic fowl (1). Apart causing respiratory disease, it may also replicate at many non-respiratory surfaces. First discovered in the United States in 1930, IBV has undergone exponential population growth (2) and is now distributed worldwide, with dozens of circulating serotypes that may differ by as much as 25% in the amino acid sequence of the variable region of the spike glycoprotein.

As serotypes cross-protect, chicken may get infected multiple times during their lifetime. IBV infection of broilers retards growth while in layers drops down egg production. Young chickens may directly from IBV infection, but a greater number due to secondary bacterial infections. Vaccination, most commonly with attenuated vaccines, is short-lived and have to be revived multiple times during their lifespan, with different serotypes.

Several IBV strains can co-circulate in a region, creating conditions for recombination between strains and resulting in complex evolution and epidemiology (3). Cloning and sequencing have been used to show that IBV variants exist in infected poultry (4) as well as in widely used commercial attenuated vaccines (5). Furthermore, McKinley et al. (5) have shown that attenuated vaccines in vivo selection following vaccination and can infect contact-exposed chickens. With significant field persistence of Arkansas-type vaccine viruses (6), this reinforces the evidence that infectious IBV strains can emerge from the evolution of attenuated vaccines in commercial flocks (7).

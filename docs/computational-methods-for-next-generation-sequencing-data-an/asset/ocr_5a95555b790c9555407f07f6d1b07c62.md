The adaptor-ligated DNA is then treated with bisulfite to convert unmethylated cytosines to uracil. After PCR amplification of the bisulfite-treated DNA, the library is sequenced by NGS. The WGBS-seq approach is capable of measuring the methylation status of more than 90% of cytosines in human genomes (3). WGBS is instrumental in the identification of CpG methylation in human ES cells and the identification of large partially methylated domains (up to megabases in length) in cancer genomes. However, the cost of WGBS approach is still quite expensive. In this regard, RRBS (reduced representation bisulfite sequencing) is a cost-effective method for sub-genome scale DNA methylation analysis. For RRBS, the high molecular genomic DNA is first digested with a methylation-insensitive restriction enzyme (MspI). A small fraction of the DNA (40–220 bp) is then extracted. After ligation to the methylated adaptors, the library is treated with bisulfite, amplified by PCR, and sequenced by NGS (37). Overall 8–14% of CpG sites in the human and mouse genome can be analyzed by RRBS. However, MspI restriction sites are biased distributed toward CpG regions (CpG island); therefore, more than 80% of CpG island and overall half of promoters in the human and mouse genomes can be surveyed by RRBS. One of the limitations is that the CpG sites analyzed by RRBS highly depend on the MspI restriction sites (CCGG) in the human genome. CpG sites outside of the MspI fragment will be analyzed by this method. Various targeted bisulfite sequencing approaches such as bisulfite padlock (38, 39), hybrid solution capture (40), multiplexed PCR (41) have been developed for analyzing DNA methylation patterns in specific gene of interest. However, most of these methods involve complex protocols and the upfront cost for synthesizing probes and primers is relatively high, and therefore hinder the adoption of these approaches (42).

Bisulfite treatment cannot differentiate 5-mC from 5hmC because it converts 5hmC to cytosine-5-methylsulfonate (CMS). CMS is read as “C” during sequencing. Booth et al. developed a novel method called “OxBS-seq,” utilizes perruthenate (Kru04) selectively oxidizing 5hmC to 5-fC. The subsequent bisulfite treatment converts 5-fc to uracil, unlike unmethylated cytosine. By subtracting out an OxBS-seq data from regular bisulfite results, Ox-seq can generate high-resolution 5-hmC map in samples (45). TAB-seq, another recently developed method, takes a different approach for single-nucleotide 5-hmC mapping (46). TAB-seq is based on the fact that Tet proteins can convert 5-mC to 5-hmC (12, 13), but also 5-hmC to 5-cAC, which behaves like unmethylated cytosines during bisulfite conversion. TAB-seq converts 5-hmC to 5-gC using β-GT in order to prevent the 5-hmC from being oxidized by Tet recombinant enzyme.

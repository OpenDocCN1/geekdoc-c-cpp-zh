From the de novo consensus, VGA assembled 32 full-length viral genomes that differ from each other in 2145 SNVs. Among known HIV sequences, Gag/Pol is closest to the de novo consensus. Each of the 32 full-length viral genomes do not contain stop codons inside two known coding regions of Gag/Pol of 1520 bbp and 1820 bbp, respectively. Alternatively, when VGA was applied to all 15M uncorrected reads, 57 distinct viral genomes are assembled among which 36 contain stop codons in the two coding regions. This shows that a regular sequencing protocol is unsuitable for viral genome reconstruction.

# 4.7 COMPARISON OF ALIGNMENT ON ERROR-CORRECTED READS

When the high-fidelity sequencing protocol was applied on real HIV data, 3M error-corrected reads were obtained. To investigate the effect of the aligner on mapping statistics, we mapped the reads to Gag/Pol sequence using such alignment tools as Mosaik v2.2 (30), Burrows-Wheeler Alignment (BWA) v0.7.12 (36), and Bowtie v2.2.5 (37) with default parameters. Mosaik is a tool for mapping second- third-generation sequencing reads to a reference genome. Mosaik can align reads generated by all the major sequencing technologies. It employs a clustering strategy coupled with the Smith-Waterman algorithm, which allows to capture mismatches as short insertions and deletions. BWA tool is based on backward search with Burrows-Wheeler transform to efficiently align low-divergent sequences against a reference sequence such as the human genome. Bowtie2 is the fastest of the three tools. It was developed for aligning sequencing reads and Bowtie2 indexes the genome with an FM index (38) (based on Burrows-Wheeler transform) to keep its memory footprint for the human genome, its memory footprint is typically around 3.2GB of RAM. Bowtie2 supports backed, local, and paired-end alignment modes.

Overall, mapping tools were consistent in placing the reads onto the viral genome. however, they differ in number of mapped reads. BWA could map totally 56% of the reads were mapped with both ends and 8% with only one end. Mosaik mapped 46% of all reads, out of which 36% were both ends and 10% were one end. Finally, Bowtie2 mapped 42% of the reads where 32% were both ends and 10% were one end. BWA produced more overlapping alignments (when both ends have common regions) - there was 11% overlapped paired-end reads while Mosaik resulted in 7.5% and Bowtie2 resulted in 7%. On the other hand, several overlapped reads disagree each other in Mosaik and Bowtie2 mappings, while about 5% of the overlapped reads in BWA had matches in common regions. BWA also produced the most soft clipping alignments. If we consider a soft clipping region of 10nt, then 6% of all reads were mapped as soft clipped in BWA, 0.3% in Mosaik, and no one read in Bowtie2.

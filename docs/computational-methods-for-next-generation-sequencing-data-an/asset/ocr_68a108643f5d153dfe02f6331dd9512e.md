# 9.1 INTRODUCTION

DNA methylation is one of the most important epigenetic mechanisms that ensures the maintenance and inheritance of gene-expression programs in mammalian cells (1). DNA methylation primarily involves the methylation of the 5th carbon on residues (5-mC) in a CpG dinucleotide. Three highly conserved DNA methyltransferase (DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B), which complex together in vivo, establish and maintain the DNA methylation landscape within the human genome (2). DNMT1 has a high affinity toward hemi-methylated DNA and is the key maintenance methyltransferase in mammals.

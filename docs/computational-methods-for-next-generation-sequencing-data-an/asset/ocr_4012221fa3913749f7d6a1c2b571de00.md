# 7.2.1 Alignments

We write $A(R)=(A_1,A_2)$ for an alignment of read $R=(R_1,R_2)$ against the reference. We use $x_A,y_A$ for the rightmost reference position of the alignment of the left end, the leftmost position of the alignment of the right end. We write $I(A) := y_A - x_A - 1$ for the length of the alignment interval of $A$.

# 7.2.2 Gaps/Splits

Alignments $A=(A_1,A_2)$ can be mapped, where gaps indicate insertions or deletions. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that each alignment is affected by at most one gap – note that NGS alignments containing two gaps are extremely rare. For notational simplicity, we will assume in the examples and explanations to follow that $A_1$ displays a gap. We write $u_g$ for the reference position that precedes the gap, and $z_g$ for the reference position that immediately follows the gap. In turn, we refer to the position in the read that precedes the gap as $u_A$ and the position in the read that follows the gap as $v_A$; that, reference number $\mathcal{G}[u_g]$ aligns with $R[u_A]$ and $\mathcal{G}[z_g]$ aligns with $R[v_A]$. Depending on whether $z_g = u_A+1$ (see alignment D in Figure 7.1) or $u_g = v_A+1$ (see alignment C), the gap indicates an insertion in the donor genome (where the inserted sequence is $R[u_A+1, v_A-1]$) or a deletion in the donor genome (where the deleted sequence is $\mathcal{G}[u_A+1, z_g-1]$.

# 7.2.3 Deletions

Let $D_L$ and $D_R$ be the reference coordinates of the left and right breakpoint of a deletion $D$. That is, nucleotides from (and including) position $D_L$ all (including) position $D_R$, which together form the sequence $\mathcal{G}[D_L, D_R]$, are missing in the donor genome. We define $C(D) := \frac{D_L+D_R}{2}$ be the center of $D$ (which need not be an integer) and $L(D) := D_R-D_L+1$ be the length of the deletion. We parameterize the deletion $D=(C(D), L(D))$ by its center $C(D)$ and its length $L(D)$.

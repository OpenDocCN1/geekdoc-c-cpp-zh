Most of the early NGS error correction algorithms assumed sequencing errors are distributed randomly, which led to the conclusion that erroneous reads have low frequencies, and therefore, can be easily filtered using an appropriate frequency cutoff. The-cutoff filtering is still the most widely used approach for NGS error correction. However, subsequent studies have shown that the assumption of the random error distribution is not always correct. In particular, error rate is strongly affected by position in the sequence (with probability of error increasing at the read's end), length of a homopolymer or sequence read, and the spatial localization in PT plates (8, 15, 16). As a result, erroneous reads can have frequencies that are only comparable to such haplotypes (Figure 15.2), which significantly impacts the use of cutoffs.

Differences in error rates and types among NGS technologies, all studies agreeing that sequencing errors pose a serious problem, becomes especially challenging for heterogeneous viral samples. The emphasis was on obtaining a consensus sequence of an intrabrast viral population, and in many studies this is still the main goal of using NGS. However, recent studies show that individual intrahost variants of highly heterogeneous viruses substantially differ in their properties and roles, with many variants being often responsible for immune escape, drug resistance, and viral transmission (17–22).

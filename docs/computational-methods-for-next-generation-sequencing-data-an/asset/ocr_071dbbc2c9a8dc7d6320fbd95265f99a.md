ET was successful in detection of correct haplotypes in 10 mixtures and 10 of 14 single-clone samples, but failed to discriminate one false haplotype in single-clone samples. KEC was correct for 13 of 14 single-clone samples (with 2 false haplotypes not being removed in sample) and 9 of 10 mixtures (with the exception of the M5 mixture, where it failed to detect low-frequency variants), kGEM detected all true haplotypes in all samples, but failed to discriminate some false haplotypes in 9 of 14 single-clone samples and in all 10 mixtures. SHORAH found all correct haplotypes in all single-clone samples and producing a large number of false haplotypes and a significant divergence of estimated and estimated frequencies of haplotypes.

Thus, all four algorithms successfully found true haplotypes in all single-clone samples and detected most trueotypes in mixtures, with ET and kGEM being the most sensitive and KEC being the least. The specificity of SHORAH is lower than other algorithms, with KEC and kGEM.

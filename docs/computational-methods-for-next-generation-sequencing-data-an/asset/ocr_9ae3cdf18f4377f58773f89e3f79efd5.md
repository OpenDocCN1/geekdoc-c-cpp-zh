# 13.1 INTRODUCTION

As the amount of data on RNA splicing increases rapidly due to advance in high-throughput sequencing, alternative splicing has become one of the most important mechanisms to study in nonmodel organisms. It is crucial to a variety of biological functions, including sex determination, neural biology, courtship, immunity, chromatin remodeling, growth and development, tissue assembly, and tissue organization (1–5).

# 13.1.1 Alternative Splicing

In higher organisms, there is usually more than one to splice an RNA to produce an mRNA (see Figure 13.1). In human, it has been estimated that more than 70% of genes have alternative splicing (6), with over hundreds of variants in highly alternatively spliced genes (7). Due to alternative splicing, the number of protein variants is much larger than the number of genes. Thus, alternative splicing is a very important regulatory mechanism.

## 9.3.2 Identification of CpG Methylation Patterns of Genomic Regions (**CpG_MPs**)

The tool *CpG_MPs* searches hot spots of differential methylation using hot spot extension and Shannon entropy. It measures beta values of CpGs with 5 or more reads and divides a CpG into four categories based on its base score: *unmethylated* for [0.0,0.3], *partially unmethylated* for (0.3,0.5], *partially methylated* for (0.5,0.7), and *methylated* for [0.7,1). Next, it searches for unmethylated and methylated hot spots that have at least n consecutively unmethylated and methylated hot spots, respectively. Then, it extends the unmethylated hotspots towards both ends until reaching the partially methylated or methylated CpG. Similarly, it extends methylated hotspots. It combines two extended hotspots with the same region if the difference is less than 200 bp and computes the mean and standard deviation of the beta values of CpGs for each methylated or unmethylated region (finally merged, extended hotspots) as shown in Figure 9.14a.

To identify DMRs, CMRs (Conservatively Methylated Regions), and CUMRs (Conservatively Unmethylated Regions) among multiple samples, *CpG_MPs* defined two measures for an OR (Overlapping Region) among methylated or unmethylated regions for two or more samples: $u = (n_m - n_u)/(n_m + n_u)$ and $v = (n_m + n_u)/n_m$ where $n_m$ and $n_u$ represent the number of samples with methylated and unmethylated regions, respectively, and $N$ represents the number of samples

The measure $v$ represents the ratio of samples in an OR, or reliability of methylation patterns. To merge adjacent ORs with the same methylation pattern, the algorithm chooses an OR with the maximal $v$ and merges adjacent ORs if their distance is greater than 200 bp or the $v$ values of the adjacent OR are greater than 0.5. The methylation status of a merged region is determined by $u_1$ and $u_2$, which are the $u$ values of adjacent ORs: CMR if $u_1=1$ and $u_2=1$, CUMR if $u_1=-1$ and $u_2=-1$, and DMR. DMRs are divided into hypo- or hypermethylated regions based on $u_1, u_2>0$ or $u_1, u_2<0$.

To quantify the methylation difference of identified regions, CpG_MPs used modified Shannon entropy to decode CpGs in QDMR (94) with the average methylation of CpGs in the identified regions. The lower entropy of DMRs represents the larger methylation change among samples, as described in QDMR. However the entropy of CMRs and CUMRs are differently interpreted, as is, the higher entropy represents the most frequent methylation. CpG_MPs applied to determine the threshold of entropy for significant DMRs, CMRs, or CMRRs.

## 9.3.2.4 BSmooth

BSmooth has proposed a detection method for differential regions using a smoothing technique, which smooths the methylation of a CpG by averaging the methylation of CpGs in a window. BSmooth estimates methylation profile for a window with 2 kbp and 70 CpGs using a local likelihood smoother (96), which is suitable for binary data with a non-additive Gaussian model and is implemented in R package “locfit”. Bsmooth applied a signal-to-noise statistic (SNS) to identify DMRs between groups of samples and take into account biological variation. For a window, the SNS is defined as $(\mu_T - \mu_C)/(\sigma_T + \sigma_C)$ where $\mu_T$ and $\sigma_T$ ($u_C$ and $\sigma_C$) represent the mean and standard deviation, respectively, of the treatment (control groups). Furthermore, the denominator was approximated by $\sigma \times \sqrt{1/n_1 + 1/n_2}$ where $n_1$ and $n_2$ represent the number of samples in treatment and control groups, respectively. $\sigma$ is a location-dependent standard deviation to represent biological variation across samples in a window and was estimated by smoothing using a running mean with a window size of 10 the standard deviation, was computed empirically across groups and floored at their 75 percent. DMRs are determined if their SNS is greater than $c$ or less than $-c$ where $c$ is a cutoff based on the marginal empirical distribution. It was recommended to use only CpGs with some coverage in all samples to detect DMRs with three more CpGs, average methylation difference = 0.1, and at least one CpG every 300 bp.

The above-described algorithm was modified for cancer data because of its heterogeneity, cancer cells are highly variable while normal cells have similar methylation. The algorithm was also modified for detecting large hypomethylated regions in cancer as reported by Hansen et al. (36). To these ends, the window size of a DMR was increased to at least 50 kbp wide, and the cutoff for SNS was decreased. These modifications led to including more CpGs into DMRs and detecting large-scale changes by smoothing large, wider windows.

CLEVER clusters all read alignments. To achieve low enough run-times in practice, CLEVER makes use of a highly engineered implementation of a max-clique technique as underlying clustering algorithm.

CLEVER solves this problem by collecting all read alignments into a read alignment graph, where each node represents a read alignment and edges indicate that overlapping alignments are likely to reflect identical alleles. Maximal cliques represent maximal groups of read alignments of all which reflect the same allele (the right panel of Figure 1 Reference 9). If there are indel alleles in the donor genome, the max-cliques reflecting such deviate from the internal size statistics. If sufficiently many reads participate in a max-clique, they give rise to significant signals when reflecting only relatively small indels, as revealed by common multiple-sample Z-tests. The statistical model of CLEVER further allows to address that read alignments can be ambiguous due to repetitive sequence and corrects for multiple testing, thereby keeping control of the false discovery rate.

Key to success in enumerating all max-cliques is a bitwector implementation of a max-clique enumeration algorithm that exploits the particular structure of read alignment graphs. See References 9 and 28 for details and corresponding run-time analyses.

# 7.4.2 MATE-CLEVER

While CLEVER discovers mid-sized deletions, the accuracy at both high recall and precision (see Section 7.6) of the predicted breakpoints suffers from the usual defaults that are common to segment size-based approaches. MATE-CLEVER, as a hybrid approach, aims at curing this and discovers deletions not only at high recall and precision but at high accuracy of their breakpoints (see Figure 7.5 for an illustration of MATE-CLEVER).

The workflow of MATE-CLEVER (10) is that of a common hybrid approach where step 2 makes use of CLEVER in the first step. Subsequently, in step 3, it makes use of a novel split-read aligner, LASER (29), which has been particularly trimmed to compute highly accurate split-read alignments reflecting larger gaps also. The outputs of MATE-CLEVER are mid-sized (and long-sized) deletions, discovered by CLEVER, where breakpoints are corrected and therefore highly accurate.

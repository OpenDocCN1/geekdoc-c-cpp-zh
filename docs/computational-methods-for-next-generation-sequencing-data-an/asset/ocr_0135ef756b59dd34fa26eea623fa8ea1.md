The read depth of a particular segment is significantly different from the global read depth if the p-value of a one sample t-test is less than 0.05. The read depths of two neighbor segments are significantly different if the p-value of a two-sample t-test is less than 0.01 for correction of testing multiple hypotheses. For small segments (less than 15 bins), they are also cataloged as different if their difference is more than $2^{H_b}$, which is the bandwidth corresponding to the average read depth of the union of the two segments tested. The bandwidth is increased in powers until it reaches 128, which is enough to call CNVs in human samples.

Once a partition of the genome is complete using the algorithm explained earlier, CNVs are segments in which the average read depth deviates by at least $rac{1}{p}$ from the average read depth, where $p$ is the normal density of the sequenced organism. Significance is assessed by a one-sample t-test and p-values are corrected for testing of multiple hypotheses.

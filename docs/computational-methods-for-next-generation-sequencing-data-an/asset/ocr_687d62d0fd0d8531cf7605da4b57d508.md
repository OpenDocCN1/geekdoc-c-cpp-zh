# 10.2 THE PROBLEM OF MAPPING BS-TREATATED READS

The first step in methylation analysis with BS-seq is mapping bisulfite-treated sequenced reads (hereafter called *reads*) back to the reference genome. In BS-reads mapping, potential matches of T in a read to C in a reference must be allowed in the usual mapping of C in a read to C in a reference. Moreover, BS-mapping must take into account distinct types of bisulfite libraries: the first type, *directional*, produced sequences that are bisulfite-converted versions of two original genomic strands (9) and the second type, *nonirectional*, yields that correspond to four possible strands resulting from PCR steps (5). Figure 10.1 shows the effect of BS conversion and subsequent PCR amplification. There are four strands of PCR: PCR⁺ PCR⁻, PCR*, and PCRᶜ. In directional bisulfite libraries, the addition of special adapters before bisulfite treatment ensures that only PCR⁺ and PCR⁻ corresponding to the original genomic strands are sequenced. The nondirectional libraries result in sequencing all four strands of PCR amplification.

Recalling that sequencing proceeds from the 5′-end toward the 3′-end. For paired-end reads, to produce the 3′-end of a strand with Illumina sequencing instruments, the 5′ end of the reverse-complement of the strand is sequenced. Figure 10.1 shows the paired-end sequenced reads resulting from all PCR strands. In single-end sequencing, only 5′ mates shown on Figure 10.1 are accurate. The order of mates in paired-end reads is essential for correct BS-mapping. We illustrate this by discussing the mapping approaches, in which sequenced reads are mapped directly to the positive strand of the genome, DS⁺ With directional bisulfite libraries, when the 5′ mate maps to DS+, only T-C mismatches are legal (i.e., T in a read is allowed to map onto C in the genome). For paired-end reads, the reverse-complement of the corresponding 3′ mate is mapped onto DS+ with T-C mapping, indicating the unmethylated status of the cytosine. For reverse-complement of the 5′ mate maps onto DS+, only A-G mismatches must be allowed indicating the unmethylated cytosine on DS+, the base pair corresponding to G on DS+ the corresponding 3′ mate in paired-end reads maps directly onto DS+ while A-G mismatches. It is important to emphasize that only one kind of BS-mismatches (either T-C or A-G, but not both) must be allowed per single alignment of a read to a reference; moreover, in paired-end alignment, mates must have the same kind of mismatches.

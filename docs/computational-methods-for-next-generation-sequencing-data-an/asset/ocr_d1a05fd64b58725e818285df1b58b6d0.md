# 16.2.6 Quaspecies Assembly Performance

Performance of quaspecies assembly depends on the many factors of the input sample and the sequencing data set. The amplicon layout needs to be designed for the underlying assembly problem. Sufficient overlap of neighboring amplicons is necessary to enable read assembly across amplicons.

The heterogeneity of the underlying population including the haplotype distribution and the spatial heterogeneity along the genome, the read length, and the error rate of the sequencing technology are closely coupled to the haplotype reconstruction. That haplotypes differ by an amount that is close to the error rate of the sequencing technology are challenging to reconstruct. Even if there is enough diversity to distinguish between haplotypes, the distribution over the genome has to be approximately round. If homogeneous regions are longer than the average read length, haplotype phasing is limited to regions with heterogeneity (Figure 16.7).

Solutions to overcome homogeneous regions are either long PacBio (Figure 16.7d) or paired-end reads (Figure 16.7c) that contain a sufficiently large insert fragment size to bridge conserved regions. Another strategy is to fix haplotype frequencies of the flanking regions. In fact, short reads cannot be compensated for by a higher coverage (45). Reconstruction of low-frequency haplotypes demands an ultradeep sequencing approach with a uniform coverage.

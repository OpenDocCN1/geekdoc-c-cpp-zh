In the upper part of the twilight zone (see Table 7.3), internal segment size-based and hybrid approaches have clear advantages over (split-read) alignment-based approaches because the recall rates of alignment-based approaches drop to zero or the precision considerably suffers. Among the alignment-based approaches, PEL has the best recall on longer deletions, at least at sufficiently high precision, and therefore makes a valuable contribution in those ranges. The only approaches that achieve high recall across all size ranges of the twilight zone are CLEVER and MATE-CLEVER. The real weaknesses are heterogeneous deletions of length 30–50bp, however, none of the other tools achieve better recall on HiSeq data. In this category, one has to step up from HiSeq in combination with BWA-MEM. On HiSeq data, heterogeneous deletions of 30–50bp can still be considered a weak spot for all approaches, with CLEVER achieving 61% recall, as the best performance.

When comparing HiSeq with MiSeq data, an immediate observation is that alignment-based approaches tend to achieve higher recall on MiSeq data, at the cost of lower precision. Internal segment size-based approaches often incur non-negligible losses on MiSeq data, which is likely due to the longer average internal segment size for the MiSeq data.

# 7.6.4 Genotyping

See Tables 7.4 and 7.5 for the following. We evaluated five state-of-the-art protocols for genotyping deletions. We ran the UnifiedGenoty (UG) and the Haplotype-Caller (HC) on both BWA-MEM and GSNAP algorithms to produce genotyping calls. We also ran MATE-CLEVER in genotyping mode, as outlined in Section 7.5.1.

It becomes evident that in an overall statement, MATE-CLEVER is the most favorable approach when to genotyping deletions longer than 30 bp, both at sufficiently high recall (see "# Calls") and good precision (see column "homozyg, correct" in Table 7.4 and "heterozyg, correct" in Table 7.5). Usually, MATE-CLEVER predicts homozygosity in about 90% and heterozygosity in about 80% of the cases.

While MATE-CLEVER seemingly is the most favorable approach overall, the other approaches have partial strengths. Notably, both BWA-HC and GSNAP-HC achieve better performance rates than MATE-CLEVER on homozygous deletions of 30–49bp, in terms of both recall and precision. It remains to add, that the value of this remains somewhat unclear because these tools do not achieve similar good rates on heterozygous deletions.

When comparing UG with HC, one observes that HC leads to considerable increases in terms of recall over UG, while incurring losses in terms of genotyping processing.

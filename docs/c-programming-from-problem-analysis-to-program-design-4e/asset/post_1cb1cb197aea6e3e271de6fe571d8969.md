## Deploying to a Device

If you have a Windows Phone 8, you can deploy, or send, your app to your phone. Once you have deployed an application to a phone device, it is stored on the device for later use. You are limited to 10 of your own applications on your phone at one time. You can also send compiled versions of your applications to other registered developers.

To deploy to your phone, your phone has to be unlocked for debugging and testing Windows Phone applications. In order to do this, you have to have a Windows Phone Developer Account. This account enables you to not only test your application, but also publish your application to the Windows Phone Store. A Developer account is free to students who have a DreamSpark account. DreamSpark accounts are also free (https://www.dreamspark.com/), but there is a requirement that you have to verify your student status when you create a DreamSpark account. One way to verify student status is to enter your school e-mail address (domain) when you set up the account. If you don’t have a DreamSpark account, there’s a charge associated with developer accounts.

You register for a Windows Phone Developer Account at the Windows Phone Developer Center (http://dev.windowsphone.com). Once you have a Windows Phone Developer Account, there is still one more step. You have to register your phone or device as a developer device. As a student, you can only register or unlock one device for free. In order to register the phone, the phone must be physically connected to a Windows 8 PC and you have to have a current developer account and know your Microsoft ID and password associated with your developer account. You connect your phone to your computer using a USB cable. Launch the **Windows Phone Developer Registration** app.

> **NOTE**
>
> Find the **Windows Phone Developer Registration** app from the Start screen by right-clicking near the bottom-right corner and selecting All apps. Windows Phone Developer Registration is listed under the **Windows Phone SDK 8.0**.

Once the phone is registered, you can then deploy applications to it. Instead of selecting one of the emulator options, you select **Device** from the dropdown list tied to the **Start** icon, as illustrated in Figure 15-43.

## Coding Standards

Use an appropriate classification suffix such as lbl for Label or btn for Button so that you can differentiate between the control objects and take advantage of IntelliSense during development.

ASPX files should not contain any inline server-side scripting. ASPX files should only contain the visual elements of the page such as the XHTML and static text. The programming logic for the page should be contained in a separate code-behind file. Code-behind files should contain the programming logic for the event handlers.

Name controls that will be used in the code-behind file.

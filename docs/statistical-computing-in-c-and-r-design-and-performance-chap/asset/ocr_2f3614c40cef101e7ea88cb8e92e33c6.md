# 7.5 Least squares

Suppose that we have an $n 	imes 1$ vector $y$ that is to be approximated by a linear combination of the columns of an $n 	imes p$ real matrix $X$. That is, the objective is to find a $p$-vector $b$ such that $XB$ provides a "estimator" for $y$ in sense. In statistics the $y$ typically represents observed values for a random variable and $X$ correspond to observed or preset values of $p$ "predictor". For problems of this nature one can expect $n > p$ and that $n > p$ has the case that will be addressed here.

$Xb = y$

is overdetermined and generally has no solution. Instead, a least-squares solution can be sought by attempting to minimize $|Xb|$ over all $b \in \mathbb{R}^p$ with $b$ chosen as the minimizer with smallest Euclidean norm. This process turns out to be equivalent to looking for a minimum norm solution to the normal equations

$X^TX = X^Ty$

which puts us back in the framework of Section 7.3 with $A = X^T$ and $c = X^Ty$ that $X^TX$ is nonsingular, equivalently, $X$ has rank $p$. More generally, for analysis of variance and other problems arising from the general linear model the rank $p$ assumption may not hold and some additional work is needed.

At this juncture it is useful to introduce the concept of a generalized inverse for a matrix as part of our linear algebra repertoire. There are actually several ways to define such quantities, but for our purposes suffices to consider only the Moore-Penrose generalized inverse (e.g., Ben-Israel and Greville 2003). For an $n 	imes p$ matrix $X$, its Moore-Penrose generalized inverse is the unique $X^\dagger$ that satisfies the Penrose equations

```
$XX^\dagger = X$
$X^\dagger XX^\dagger = X^\dagger$
$(XX^\dagger)^T = XX^\dagger$
$(X^\dagger X)^T = X^\dagger$
```

The next theorem gives the claim regarding the Moore-Penrose inverse and the least-squares solution of an over-determined system. Its proof can be found in Ben-Israel and Greville (2003, Chapter 3).

**Theorem 7.3.** Let $X$ be an $n 	imes p$ matrix of rank $r$ with $n > p \geq r$. The vector

```
$b = X^\dagger y$
```

is the minimizer of $\|y - Xb\|$ that has minimal norm in

```
$||b|| = \inf { \theta : ||X^T\theta|| = ||y||}$
```

The SVD can be used to provide a computable characterization of the solution in (7.35). To see this, $X = UDV^T$ be the singular-value decomposition of $X$ with $U = [u_1, \dots, u_n]$ and $V = [v_1, \dots, v_p]$, respectively, $n 	imes n$ and $p 	imes p$ orthogonal matrices and $D = \{d_{ij}\}$ an $n 	imes p$ matrix where only nonzero entries are the square roots of the noneigenvalues $\lambda_i^2, i=1, \dots, r$ of $X^TX$. We now claim that

```
$X^\dagger = VD^TU^T$
```

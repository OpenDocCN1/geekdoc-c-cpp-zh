### Algorithm 7.3 Power method

```
$v^{(0)} = Av/\|Av\|; \lambda^{(0)} = (v^{(0)})^T Av^{(0)}, niter = 0, change = \infty$
while change > \delta and niter < itMax do
  niter := niter + 1
  v^{(n)} := Av^{(n-1)}/\|Av^{(n-1)}\|;
  \lambda^{(n)} := (v^{(n)})^T Av^{(n)}
  change := |\lambda^{(n)} - \lambda^{(n-1)}|;
end
return v^{(n)}, \lambda^{(n)}$
```

Algorithm 7.3 successfully applies $A$ to the vector $v$, renormalizes and evaluates the ratio of quadratic forms in $A$ that provides the changes in $\lambda_i$ from successive iterations. If the magnitude of change falls below the specified tolerance $\delta$, execution is terminated. The value of change is initialized at $\infty$ so that the *while* loop will begin.

Up to this point only the largest eigenvalue has been considered. For example, let $v_1, \lambda_1$ be the approximations to $v_1, \lambda_1$ that are returned by the power method recursion for the largest eigenvalue. then a new matrix $A = A - \lambda_1v_1v_1^T$

**Theorem 7.1.** Let $v$ be a $p$-vector with $(v, v_1) \neq 0$ and let $v^{(k)}, \lambda^{(k)}$ be the approximations to $v_1, \lambda_1$ that are returned by the $k$th step of the power iteration. As $k \to \infty, v^{(k)} \to \pm v_1$ in Euclidean norm and $|\lambda^{(k)} - \lambda_1| \to 0$.

*Proof.* Note that $v^{(1)} = Av^{(1)}/\|Av\|$.

```
$v^{(2)} = \frac{Av^{(1)}}{\|Av\|} = \frac{A^2v}{\|Av\|}$
```

By induction one concludes that on the $k$th step of the recursion

```
$\lambda^{(k)} = A^k v/\|A^k v\|$
```

Since $\lambda^{(k)} = (v^{(k)})^T A^k v_1$, this gives

$$ \lambda_1 - \lambda^{(k)} = \lambda_1 \left[ 1 - \frac{1 + \sum_{j=2}^{2k} (\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^2 (a_j/a_1)^2}{1 + \sum_{j=2}^{2k} (\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^2 (a_j/a_1)^2} \right] $$

which converges to 0 at the rate $(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^{2k}$.

To establish convergence of $v^{(k)}$, first observe that $a$ is sign in eigenvectors; i.e., both $v_1$ and $-v_1$ are eigenvectors for $\lambda_1$. The vectors $v^{(k)}$ will converge to one of these depending on the sign of $a_1$. The convergence is to $v_1$ if $a_1 > 0$ and to $-v_1$ otherwise. For suppose that $a_1 > 0$ the other case can be handled analytically.

Then,

$$\langle v^{(k)}, v_1 \rangle = \frac{\langle v_1, A^k v \rangle}{\|A^k v\|}$$

$$= \frac{a_1 \lambda_1^k}{a_1^2 \lambda_1^{2k} + \sum_{j=2}^k \lambda_j^{2k} a_j^2}}{\left(1 + \sum_{j=2}^k (\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^2 (a_j/a_1)^2\right)^{1/2}}$$

Thus, $\|v^{(k)} - v_1\|^2 = 2 - 2\langle v^{(k)}, v_1 \rangle$, converges to zero at rate $(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^{2k}$ and the theorem is proved.

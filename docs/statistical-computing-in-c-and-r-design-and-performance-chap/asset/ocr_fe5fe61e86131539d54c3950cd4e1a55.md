It remains only to compute the chi-square statistic.

> $$\frac{\sum (Obs[i] - E[i])^2}{E[i]}$ $

where Obs[i] are the observed frequencies of the K population elements or categories and E[i] are the expected frequencies for each category under the null hypothesis. The focus is on largely categories; i.e., given any draw from the population, the chance an object will be from any of the categories is 1/K.
Our formula for the chi-square statistic is appropriate for drawing with replacement but must be alerted for situations where draws are conducted without replacement. Specifically, if n independent draws are conducted where k elements are selected without replacement, Joe (1993) shows that

> $$\chi^2 = \frac{K-1}{K-k} \frac{(Obs[i]-n/K)^2}{n/K}$

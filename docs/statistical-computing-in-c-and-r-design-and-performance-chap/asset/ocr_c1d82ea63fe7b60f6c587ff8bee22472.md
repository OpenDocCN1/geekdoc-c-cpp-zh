# 4.7 Generating random normals

Given the importance of the normal distribution to statistics it is no surprise that a number of methods have been developed for the generation of normal deviates. Notable examples include those proposed in Box and Müller (1958), Ahrens and Dieter (1973) and Kinderman and Ramage (1976). We will focus our attention on the Box-Muller approach. The basic result as stated in Box and Müller (1958) is if $U_1$ and $U_2$ are random variables with a uniform distribution of $[0, 1]$ then

```
$Z_1 = \sqrt{-2\ln(U_1)}\cos(2\pi U_2)$ \(4.16)
$Z_2 = \sqrt{-2\ln(U_1)}\sin(2\pi U_2)$ \(4.17)
```

are independent normal normal variables (Exercise 4.34). This has the implication that pairs of normals can be generated from pairs of uniforms through the simple transformations in (4.16)-(4.17). A polar coordinates perspective leads to yet another method that employs the accept-reject method while using of trigonometric functions.

**Theorem 4.6.** Let $(U_1, U_2)$ have a uniform distribution over the unit circle: i.e., the $(U_1, U_2)$ density is

```
$f(u_1, u_2) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{4\pi}, & -1 \le u_1, u_2, u_1^2 + u_2^2 \le 1, \ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}$
```

Then, $R = U_1^2 + U_2^2$ and $V = U_1\sqrt{R}$,

```
$Z_1 = \sqrt{-2\ln(R)}V,$ \(4.18)
$Z_2 = \sqrt{-2\ln(R)}\sqrt{1-V^2}$ \(4.19)
```

are independent standard normal random variables.

Proof. The proof is relatively straightforward, but somewhat tedious. The result will be established in two steps. First, it will be shown that the bivariate variable $(U_1, U_2)$ is uniformly distributed over the unit circle, $R$ and $V$ are independent with $R$ having a uniform distribution on $[0, 1]$.

To accomplish our first objective, let $r = u_1^2+u_2^2$ and take $v = u_1/\sqrt{r}$. This gives $u_1=rv$ and $v=\sqrt{r(1-v^2)}$ from which the Jacobian is found to be $(1-v^2)^{-1/2}/2$. The joint $(R, V)$ density can be written as

```
$f(r, v) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{4\pi}(1-v^2), & 0 \le r \le 1, -1 \le v \le 1, \ 0, & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}$
```

which proves that $R$ is uniformly distributed on $[0, 1]$ and independent of $V$.

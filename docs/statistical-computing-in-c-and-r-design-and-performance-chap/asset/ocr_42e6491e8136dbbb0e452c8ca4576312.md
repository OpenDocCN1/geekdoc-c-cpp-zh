A variant of the generalized linear feedback shift register is the twisted feedback
register generator (e.g., Matsumoto and Kurura 1992). In these cases of vectors length
r are produced using

```
$v_i = (Ax_{i-p}) \oplus v_{i-p-q} \quad (4.8)$
```

With an A r r matrix having all 0 and 1 entries. This approach can produce generators of
periods as long as $2^r-1$ is where $t^p+t^q+1$ is primitive modulo 2 and the characteristic
polynomial of A is irreducible. In this respect the generators realize the potential of shift
register methods. Matsumoto and Kurura (1992) suggest using

$A^T = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \ 0 & 0 & 1 & \dots & 0 \ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \ 0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & 1 \ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 & \dots & b_r \end{pmatrix}$

with $b = (b_1, \dots, b_r)^T$ a vector of zeros and provide specific choices for $b$ that
produce the maximum period. The Mersenne shift register developed by Matsumoto and Takuji
(1998) is a popular variation of the twisted shift register generator with period
$2^{19937}-1$. The original Mersenne twisted has been modified in various ways (e.g.,
Saito and Matsumoto 2006) to improve its speed and ease of initialization.

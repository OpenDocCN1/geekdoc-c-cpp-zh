Generally, for sufficiently large value $\lambda^{(k)}$ and vector $v^{(k)}$ returned at the $kth iteration of the power method algorithm provides numerical approximations to the largest eigenvalue of $A$ and its associated eigenvector. Upper bounds for the size of the approximation errors for both the eigenvalues and eigenvectors are obtained as a by-product.

**Theorem 7.1.** Let $v$ be a $p$-vector with $(v, v_1) \neq 0$ and let $v^{(k)}, \lambda^{(k)}$ be the approximations to $v_1, \lambda_1$ that are returned by the $kth$ step of the power method iteration. As $k \to \infty, v^{(k)} \to \pm v_1$ in Euclidean norm and $|\lambda^{(k)} - \lambda_1| \to 0$.

*Proof.* Note that $v^{(1)} = Av^{(1)}/||Av||$.

```
$v^{(2)} = \frac{Av^{(1)}}{||Av||} = \frac{A^2v}{||Av||}$
```

By induction one concludes that on the $kth$ step of the recursion

```
$\lambda^{(k)} = A^k v/||A^k v||$
```

Since $\lambda^{(k)} = (v^{(k)})^T A^k v_1$, this gives

```
$\lambda_1 - \lambda^{(k)} = \lambda_1 \left[ 1 - \frac{1 + \sum_{j=2}^{2k+1} (a_j/\lambda_1)^2 (a_j/a_1)^2}{1 + \sum_{j=2}^{2k} (\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^2 (a_j/\a_1)^2} \right]$
```

which converges to 0 at the rate $(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^{2k}$.

To establish convergence of $v^{(k)}$, first observe that $a$ is sign in eigenvectors; i.e., both $v_1$ and $-v_1$ are eigenvectors for $\lambda_1$. The vectors $v^{(k)}$ will converge to one of these depending on the sign of $a_1$ in (7.23). The convergence is to $v_1$ if $a_1 > 0$ and to $-v_1$ otherwise. For suppose that $a_1 > 0$ the other case can be handled analytically.

Then,

```
$$\langle v^{(k)}, v_1 \rangle = \frac{\langle v_1, A^k v \rangle}{||A^k v||}$
```

```
$= \frac{a_1 \lambda_1^k}{a_1^2 \lambda_1^{2k} + \sum_{j=2}^k \lambda_j^2 \lambda_k^2 a_j^2}}{\left\1 + \sum_{j=2}^k (\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^2 (a_j/a_1)^2\\}^{1/2}}$
```

Thus, $||v^{(k)} - v_1||^2 = 2 - 2\langle v^{(k)}, v_1 \rangle$, converges to zero at rate $(\lambda_2/\lambda_1)^{2k}$ and the theorem is proved

Another useful function for avoiding explicit R loops is outer. The name derives from its application to compute product of vectors or matrices. Given vectors a and b of dimensions n and m, respectively, their outer product is defined as the n x m matrix whose (i,j) is the product of the ith entry of a and the jth entry of b. The outer product of two vectors in R is written as a%ob%. For example, the 10 multiplication table may be generated by invoking the outer product operator

```
1:10%1:10
```

In fact, %% is a wrapper for the more general function outer that has prototype outer(X, Y, FUN)

with X and Y typically vector or to catch an analog of %% on the X and Y objects where multiplication is replaced by the operation specified in FUN. Thus the output of outer is a matrix of length(X) x length(Y) generating elements of the FUN(X[i], Y[j]). By choosing X=Y=(1:10) and FUN = "*" reproduces the multiplication table that was previously obtained via %%.

In Listing 5.3 an array of index pairs for the columns of the data matrix was created that was used to determine which means were to be compared. An alternative spin on our mean comparison demonstrates how outer provides a means of bypassing explicit construction of such an index array.

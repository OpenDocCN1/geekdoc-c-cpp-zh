You can easily learn more about what the candidate knows by asking questions and discussing problem-solving approaches than you can by posing a single puzzle that may happen to fall outside the candidate's experience.

If you're a candidate, try not to be panicked or offended by puzzle questions. Make sure you understand the problem and give it your best shot. Remember, failing to solve a problem doesn't necessarily mean you've failed the interview.

You can find a huge number of puzzle questions online searching for “interview puzzle questions.” Read a bunch and get a feel for the kinds of interviews and the kinds of approaches required to solve them. If you don't face these sorts of puzzles in an interview, they can be interesting and fun, so you won't have wasted your time.

However, don't forget to work on the other parts of your interview skills. Brush up on your algorithms, database design, architecture, project management, and other relevant skills. Last but least, don't forget to get a good book or two about how to prepare for interviews more generally.

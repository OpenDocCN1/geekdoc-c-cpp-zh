+   4. In this case, general A never receives an acknowledgment. Therefore, he assumes he didn't send enough the first time around, and he sends another with more messages. General B receives some of these messages and sends a new batch of acknowledgments (possibly including this time). Eventually the generals will go through rounds that general A will receive an acknowledgment.

After the first batch of messages, if general A doesn't receive acknowledgment (when he sent them, he didn't know $P_{AB}$ so he didn't know how many to send), he assumes general B didn't receive one. So he sends another, larger batch.

Similarly, if general B doesn't receive a reply to the acknowledgments (when he sent them, he didn't know $P_{BA}$ so he didn't know how many to send), he assumes general A didn't receive one. So he sends another, larger batch.

Eventually general A sends enough messages for general B to receive and calculate $P_{AB}$. After that, general B will eventually send enough acknowledgments for general A to receive and calculate $P_{BA}$. Finally, general A will eventually send enough replies to give general B the value $P_{BA}$, and both generals will know their probabilities.

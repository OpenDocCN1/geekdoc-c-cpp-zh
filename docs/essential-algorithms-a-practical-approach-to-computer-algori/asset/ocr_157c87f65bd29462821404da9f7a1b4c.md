> **Great GCDs**

The key to Euclid's algorithm is that GCD(A, B) = GCD(B, A Mod).
To understand why this is, consider the definition of the modulus operator.
If the remainder R = A Mod B, A = m × B + R for some integer m. If g is the GCD of A and B, and g divides m, it must also divide R. Because g divides A and B evenly and A = m × B + R, g must divide R evenly. Because g divides m × B, it must also divide R evenly.
This proves that g divides A and R. To say g = GCD(B, R) you still need to know that g is the largest integer that divides B and R. If g divides B and R, Then g also divides m × B + R. But A = m × B + R, so g divides A as well. This means that g is not GCD(A, B). This contradicts the assumption that g = GCD(A, B).

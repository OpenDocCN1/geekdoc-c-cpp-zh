If one of the node's siblings holds more than K values, you can merge the node with one of its siblings to make a node that holds 2 × K values.
For example, consider the tree shown at the top of Figure 11-15. Suppose you want to delete the value 12. The leaf node and its siblings all hold K values, so you cannot redistribute values. Instead, you can merge the leaf with one of its siblings, as shown at the bottom of the figure.

![](bbox=[0.135, 0.523, 0.638, 0.743])

Figure 11-15: Sometimes when you delete a value from a B-tree node, you must merge the node with a sibling.

When you merge two nodes, the parent node may not hold K values. In that case you must repeat the process with the parent node, either rearranging values among that node's siblings or merging that node with one of its siblings.
In the worst case, the merge travels all the way up the tree to the root, where it causes a root merge. This is the only way a B-tree grows shorter.

### Problem specification
While shopping for her dream car, Sydney Green has noticed that many dealers are offering buyers of either a large rebate or an extremely low financing rate, much lower than the rate Sydney would pay for financing the car through her local credit union. Sydney is not sure whether to take the lower financing rate from the dealer or take the rebate and finance the car through her credit union. She wants a program that will calculate a monthly car payment using these scenarios. The formula involves the amount, interest rate, and term (number of payments). As shown below are two examples that use the formula to calculate a periodic payment. Example 1 calculates the annual payment for a $9000 loan for three years at 5%. The annual payment rounded to the nearest cent is $3304.88. In other words, if you would borrow $9000 for three years, you would need to make three annual payments of $3304.88 to pay off the loan. Example 2 calculates the monthly payment for a $12,000 loan for five years at 6%. To pay off this loan, you would need to make 60 payments of $231.99. When calculating a monthly payment, you must convert the annual interest rate to a monthly interest rate by dividing the rate by 12. You also need to convert the term from years to months. This is accomplished by multiplying the number of years by 12. (When you apply for a loan, the lender typically quotes you an annual interest and expresses the term in years.)

# Posttest Loops

Recall that a repetition cycle is either a pretest loop or a posttest loop. The difference between both types of loops pertains to when the loop's condition is evaluated. Unlike a pretest loop's condition, which is evaluated before the instructions within the loop are processed, a posttest loop's condition is evaluated after the instructions loop. As a result, the instructions in a posttest loop will always be processed at least once, whereas the instructions in a pretest loop may never be processed. You learned about pretest loops in Chapter 7. You will learn about posttest loops in this chapter. Although pretest loops are the most commonly used, it is essential to understand the way posttest loops work. You may encounter a situation where a posttest loop is the better choice. Or, you may encounter a posttest loop in another program's code.

The problem specification and algorithms shown in Figure 8-1 help you identify the difference between pretest and posttest loops. Algorithm 1 contains a pretest loop, and Algorithm 2 contains a posttest loop. The purpose of each loop is to position Robin directly from her bedroom door. Compare the first and last lines in the loop. More specifically, notice the location of the loop's condition. In the pretest loop, the condition appears in the first line, indicating that Robin should evaluate before she follows the instructions. In the posttest loop, the condition appears in the last line, indicating that Robin should evaluate after the instructions. The pretest loop in Algorithm 1 will work when Robin is zero or more steps away from her bedroom door. The posttest loop in Algorithm 2, will work only when Robin is at least one step away from the door.

Robin is standing in her hallway facing the bedroom door. The door, which may or not be closed, an unknown number of steps away from her. Robin wants to go inside her bedroom.

```
Algorithm 1 – pretest loop
repeat while (you are not directly in front of the bedroom door)
 walk forward
end repeat
if (the bedroom door is closed)
 open the bedroom door
end if
 walk forward

Algorithm 2 – posttest loop
repeat
 walk forward
 repeat while (you are not directly in front of the bedroom door)
if (the bedroom door is closed)
 open the bedroom door
end if
 walk forward
```

> Pretest and posttest loops are also called top-driven and bottom-driven, respectively.

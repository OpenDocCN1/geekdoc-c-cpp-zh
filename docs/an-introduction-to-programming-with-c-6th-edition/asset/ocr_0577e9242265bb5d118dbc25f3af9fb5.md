1-2 An example of the selection structure

1-3 shows another example of the selection structure. In this example, Robin is holding either a red or yellow balloon, and she is facing two boxes that are located five steps away from her. One of the boxes is yellow, and the other is red. Your task is to instruct Robin to drop the balloon into the appropriate box: the yellow balloon in the yellow box, and the red balloon in the red box. To write an algorithm to accomplish the task, you need to use four additional instructions from Robin’s instruction set: if (the balloon is red), else, drop the balloon in the red box, and drop the balloon in the yellow box. The additional instructions allow Robin to make a decision about the color of the balloon she is holding and take the appropriate action based on that. 1--3 shows the problem specification along with an illustration of the problem. It also shows an algorithm that solves the problem. Unlike the structure from 1-2, which requires Robin to take a specific action when the structure’s condition evaluates to true, the selection structure 1-3 requires her to take one action when the condition evaluates to true, a different action when it evaluates to false. In other words, the selection structure in 1-3 has both a true path and a false path. The else instruction marks the beginning of the false path instructions. Notice that the drop the balloon in the red box and drop the balloon in the yellow box instructions are indented within their respective respects.

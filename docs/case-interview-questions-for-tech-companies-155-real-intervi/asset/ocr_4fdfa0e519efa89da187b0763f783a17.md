# Providing Feedback

Give me an example of when you had to provide feedback.

Things to Consider

+   - Can you think of a time when you:
  - Confronted someone when others wouldn't have courage to do so
  - Provided feedback in an extraordinary, elegant way?

Common Mistakes

+   - Offering an example where the feedback was ordinary. For instance, "I suggested to my colleague that we are starting meeting later so that we can accommodate colleagues joining from ten time zones."
- Giving an example where poorly received feedback had limited or no consequences. For example, "I gave feedback that we do a cooking class, the team voted to participate in a glassblowing instead."

# Answer

CANDIDATE: When I first worked at Dell, I was the newest member on the staff. A co-worker, who had worked for Dell for 30 years, told me that he "knew the ropes" and that I would have "wait my turn in line."

I was taken aback by his brusque approach. Unfortunately, the drama didn net end on the first day. He would never look me in the eye during meetings, and I found that he routinely left me off important emails.

I wanted to discuss the issue with him, but he never gave me the chance. I imagined that he felt I was young and naive. I didn't feel good dismissed; it hurt my sense of self-worth. Ultimately, I decided to take the high road and prove myself. I worked hard, acted professionally, and learned quickly.

After about two months, he surprised me by inviting me to have coffee. He told me that he had underestimated me and he was impressed that I tried hard to fit and prove myself.

At that moment, I wanted to seek revenge and tell him that he was the worst person I've ever worked with. However, I decided to take the high road again. I told him that I appreciated how he recognized my effort, and I told him that it was difficult adjusting to a new environment, especially team members like him appeared to be unwelcoming.

He told me he was impressed by candness and willingness to give feedback. He later told my manager he was impressed that I was willing to confront people and tell them bad news. He thought it was remarkable. He felt others would have scuttle the awkward feelings and choose to instead talk behind his back.

My boss shared this feedback with me and later included this positive email in my six-month performance review.

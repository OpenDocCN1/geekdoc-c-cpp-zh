# Expense Variance

If you projected a $500M expense and the variance came in $1M, what are some of the explanations for why that is happening? Be prepared to give more than three scenarios.

Things to Consider

+   - Is it a positive or negative variance?
- Did you brainstorm all potential reasons and most importantly, did you brainstorm the reasons that the interviewer is thinking of?

Common Mistakes

+   - Getting scared by the term "variance"
- Not mentioning that forecast could have been $1M below forecast

# Answer

CANDIDATE: Here are some scenarios I can think of:

Reasons why expenses are $1M below forecasted

+   - Accounting mistake
- Team did not get a chance to spend all of the money it intended
- Team put budgeted an overgenerous buffer, in case actual came in higher than expected

Reasons why expenses are $1M above forecasted

+   - We needed more than something than we originally thought, thus increasing our cost.
- Some of the stuff came broken, and we had to replace them.
- The price for the things we needed went up between the projection and when we purchased them.
- We forgot to calculate some of the expenses, like shipping.
- We had to give a raise or recruited new people. We didn't spend more to cover up for it.
- Something went wrong during this time and we had to spend more to cover for it.
- This took longer than expected, and so our expenses went up as a result.

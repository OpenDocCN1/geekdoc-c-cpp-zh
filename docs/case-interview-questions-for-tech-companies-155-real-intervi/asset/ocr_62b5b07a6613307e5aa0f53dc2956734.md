+   * Require candidates to have a methodical approach
* Typically call for number crunching
* Expect candidates to make a recommendation, affecting the target goal in an effective and logical way

However, unlike case questions, tech case questions are more likely to have the following characteristics:

+   * **Involve real-world scenarios.** To maintain client privacy, consulting cases often use fictitious names and scenarios. Tech firms are more likely to use actual problems, making case questions easier to anticipate.
* **Ambiguous.** Tech companies use questions that are less specific and more open-ended, similar to the broad strategic questions that CEOs face day-to-day.
* **Require tech domain-expertise.** Consulting firms don't expect candidates have industry-specific knowledge, but tech firms do.
* **Dearth of background information.** Unlike consulting, tech cases rarely, if ever, have background information to share with the candidate. There is no interview guide, and the interviewer usually do not exhibits to reveal to the interviewee.
* **Unlikely to provide help, or other assistance.** Consulting interviews often help candidates with unknown assumptions. Tech companies are less helpful in this regard. Sometimes, they don't know or don't want to reveal confidential information. Other times, they withhold assumptions so they can assess a candidate's judgment.

# What the book is about and how to use it

This book includes over 150 practice problems along with sample answers. The questions are organized by question type; there are different question categories including product design, marketing, operations, finance and business development.

The best candidates are attempting the problems on their own and comparing their answers with the sample. I would strongly recommend that you do the same.

To reinforce that concept, I created blank pages for the first 10 questions. After that, I hope you continue to do problems and first review the sample answer.

And one more tip: do you research the types of questions you're likely to encounter; pick and choose the sample questions that are most similar to what you think you're likely to get at the actual interview.

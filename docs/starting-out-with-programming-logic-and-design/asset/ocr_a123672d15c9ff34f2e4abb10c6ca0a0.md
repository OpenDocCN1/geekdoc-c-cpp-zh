# 3.2 Defining and Calling a Module

Just as important understanding how modules work is understanding how to design a modularized program. Programmers commonly use a technique known as top-down design to break down an algorithm into modules. The process of top-down design is performed in the following manner:

+   - The overall task that to perform is broken down into a series of subtasks.
- Each of the subtasks is examined to determine whether it can be broken down into more subtasks. The step is repeated until no more subtasks can be identified.
- Once all of the subtasks have been identified, they are written in code.

This process is called top-down design because the programmer begins looking at the top level of tasks that must be performed, and breaks down those tasks to lower levels of tasks.

> NOTE: The top-down design process is sometimes called stepwise refinement.

# Hierarchy Charts

Flowcharts are good tools for graphically depicting the flow of logic inside a module, but they do not have a visual representation of the relationships between modules. Programmers commonly use hierarchy charts for this purpose. A hierarchy chart, which is also known as a structure chart, shows boxes that represent each module in a program. The boxes are connected in a way that illustrates their relationship to one another. Figure 3-7 shows an example of a hierarchy chart for a paying program.

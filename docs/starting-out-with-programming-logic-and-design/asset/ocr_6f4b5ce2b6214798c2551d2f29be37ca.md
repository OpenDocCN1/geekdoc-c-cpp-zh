# Combining Structures

You cannot use decision structures alone to create a complete program. You use a decision structure to handle any part of a program that needs to test a condition and condition. For parts of a program you need to use other structures. For example, Figure 4-2 shows a complete flowchart that combines a decision structure with two sequence structures.(Figure 4-2 is not a flowchart of a computer algorithm, but of a human action.)

The flowchart in the figure starts with a sequence structure. Assuming you have an outdoor thermometer in your window, the first step is Go to the window, and the next step is Read thermometer. A decision structure appears next, testing the condition Cold outside. If this is true, the Wear a coat is performed. Another sequence structure appears next. The Open the door is performed, followed by Go outside.

Quite often, structures must be nested inside of other structures. For example, look at the partial flowchart in Figure 4-3. It shows a decision structure with a sequence structure inside it. (Once again, this is a flowchart showing human actions, not programs.) The decision structure tests the condition Cold outside. If that condition is true, the steps in the sequence structure are executed.

+   - **Step 2:** {12, 16, 30, 11, 24} 35 {51, 56, 45}
+   - **Step 3:** {12, 16, 11, 30, 24} 35 {51, 45, 56}
+   - **Step 4:** {12, 16, 11, 24, 30} 35 {51, 45, 56}

Therefore the new list with partition is obtained as follows.

+   - **Step 1:** {[12, 16, 11, 24] 30} 35 {[51, 45] 56}
+   - **Step 2:** {[11, 12, 16, 24] 30} 35 {[45, 51] 56}

Hence you obtain a list:

11, 12, 16, 24, 30, 35, 45, 51, 56.

# 9.10.1. Radix Sort

The radix sort can be declared as the most efficient sorting methods available. It is generally applicable for 2 digit numbers but the concept holds good even for 3 digit numbers though the examples dealt with here are only using two digit numbers.

This version of the sorting technique is pretty simple and involves in dividing every element into two parts. The right digit or the digit in the tens place is called most significant digit (MSD) and digit in the units place is called least significant digit (LSD). Example: in the number 40 the LSD and the MSD are demarcated as follows:

MSD: 4 LSD: 0

The whole sorting process is carried out on the basis of sorting the LSD and MSD. The whole radix sort process is two step procedure where the sorting in the first step is based on lsd and sorting in second is based on msd.

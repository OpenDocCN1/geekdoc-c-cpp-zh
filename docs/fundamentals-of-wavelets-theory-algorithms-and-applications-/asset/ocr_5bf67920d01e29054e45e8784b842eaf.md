```
\hat{F}(\omega) = h \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} f(kh) e^{-j k \omega h}
= \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z}} \hat{f}\left(\frac{\omega h + 2\pi k}{h}\right)
= \hat{f}(\omega) + \sum_{k \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}} \hat{f}\left(\omega + \frac{2\pi k}{h}\right)
(3.79)
```

Hence \(\hat{F}(\omega)\) contains \(\hat{f}(\omega)\) plus infinitely many copies of \(\hat{f}(\omega)\) shifted along the \(\omega\)-axis. In order for \(\hat{f}(\omega)\) to be disjointed with its copies, the amount of shift, \(2\pi/h\), must be at least \(2 \Omega\) (see Figure 3.9):

```
\frac{2\pi}{h} \ge 2\Omega, \quad h \le \frac{\pi}{\Omega}.
(3.80)
```

(4.13) \(\hat{g}_a(\omega) = e^{-a\omega^2}; \quad \alpha > 0.\)

The window property of \(g_a(t)\) can be computed using the formulas in Section 4.1 to give \(t^* = \omega^* = 0, \Delta g_a = \sqrt{\alpha}\) and \(\Delta \hat{g}_a = 1/(2\sqrt{\alpha})\). Observe that \(\Delta g_a \Delta \hat{g}_a = 0.5\) attains the lower bound of the uncertainty principle.

# 4.2.3 Time-Frequency Window

Let us consider the window function \(\phi(t)\) in (4.7). If \(t^*\) is the center and \(\Delta_\phi\) the radius of the window function, then (4.7) gives the information of the function \(f(t)\) in the time window.

(4.14) \([t^* + b - \Delta_\phi, t^* + b + \Delta_\phi]\).

To derive the corresponding window in the frequency domain, apply Parseval's identity (3.41) to (4.7). We have

(4.15) \(\begin{aligned} G_\phi f(b, \xi) &= \int_{-\infty}^\infty f(t) \overline{\phi(t-b)} e^{-i\xi t} dt \\ &= \frac{1}{2\pi} e^{-i\xi b} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \hat{f}(\omega) \overline{\hat{\phi}(\omega - \xi)} e^{ib\omega} d\omega \end{aligned}\)

(4.16) \(= e^{-i\xi t^*} [\hat{f}(\omega) \overline{\hat{\phi}(\omega - \xi)}]^\vee (b),\)

where the symbol V represents the inverse Fourier transform. Observe that (4.15) has a form similar to (4.7).

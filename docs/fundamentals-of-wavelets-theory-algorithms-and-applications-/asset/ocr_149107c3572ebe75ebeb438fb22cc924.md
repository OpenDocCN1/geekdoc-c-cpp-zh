\( p_M(t) = \frac{1}{T} \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} p(\tau) \sum_{k=-M}^{M} e^{jk\omega_0(t-\tau)} d\tau \quad = \frac{1}{T} \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} p(\tau) \left[ \frac{\sin \left( M + \frac{1}{2} \right) (t-\tau) \omega_0}{\sin \frac{1}{2} (t-\tau) \omega_0} \right] d\tau, \quad \text{(3.90)} \)

which is the convolution between the original periodic function with the Fourier series kernel discussed in Section 3.2.2. We can easily see that the oscillatory characteristic \( K_N \) is carried into the partial sum. If \( p(t) \) is a rectangular pulse train or a periodic function with jump discontinuities, the partial Fourier series will exhibit oscillation around the discontinuities. This is known as the *Gibb's phenomenon*. The percentage of overshoot remains constant regardless of the number of terms taken for the approximation. As \( M \rightarrow \infty \), the sum converges to the midpoint at the discontinuity [4].

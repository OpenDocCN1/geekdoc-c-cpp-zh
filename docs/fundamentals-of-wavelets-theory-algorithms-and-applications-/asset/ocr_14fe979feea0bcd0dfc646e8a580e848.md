Observe that the function \(\phi(t)\) is represented as a superposition of a scaled and translated version of itself, and hence the term *scaling function*. More precisely, \( A_0 \) is generated by \(\{\phi(\cdot - k) : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}\), and in general, \( A_s \) by \(\{\phi_{ks} : k, s \in \mathbb{Z}\}\). Consequently, we have the following two obvious results:

```
(5.1) \( x(t) \in A_s \Leftrightarrow x(2t) \in A_{s+1} \)
```

```
(5.2) \( x(t) \in A_s \Leftrightarrow x(t + 2^{-s}) \in A_s \).
```

For each \( s \), since \( A_s \) is a proper subspace of \( A_{s+1} \), there is some space left in \( A_{s+1} \), called \( W_s \), which when combined with \( A_s \) gives us \( A_{s+1} \). This space \(\{W_s\}\) is called the *wavelet subspace* and is complementary to \( A_s \) in \( A_{s+1} \), meaning

```
(5.3) \( A_s \cap W_s = \{0\}, s \in \mathbb{Z}, \text{ and } \)
```

```
(5.4) \( A_s \oplus W_s = A_{s+1} \).
```

With the condition (5.3), the summation in (5.4) is referred to as a direct sum, and the decomposition in (5.4) as a *direct-sum decomposition*.

To make use of the two-scale relations for processing, a signal must be in one of these nested approximation subspaces. One way to meeting this requirement is by projecting the signal into one of the $A_s$ for some $s$. This is particularly important if one only knows the sampled values of the signal at $x(t=k/2^s, k \in \mathbb{Z})$ for some large value of $s$.

Assuming that the signal $x(t)$ is not in the approximation $A_s$, we wish to find $x_s(t) \in A_s$ such that

(7.24) 
$$x(t) \equiv x_s(t) = \sum_k a_{k,s} \phi_{k,s}(t) = \sum_k a_{k,s} \phi(2^s t - k)$$

where $a_{k,s}$ are the scaling function coefficients to be computed from the signal samples. We will show how one can determine $a_{k,s}$ from the sample data $x(t=k/2^s)$ using the orthogonal projection of $x(t)$ on to the $A_s$ space.

Since $A_s$ is a subspace of $L^2$ and $x(t) \in L^2$, we consider $x_s(t)$ as the orthogonal projection of $x(t)$ onto the $A_s$ subspace. Then, $x(t) - x_s(t)$ is orthogonal to $A_s$, and therefore orthogonal to the basis function $\phi$.

(7.25)

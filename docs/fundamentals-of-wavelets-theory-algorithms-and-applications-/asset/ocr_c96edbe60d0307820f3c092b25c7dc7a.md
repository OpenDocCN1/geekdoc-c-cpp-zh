# 3.9.1 Discrete Fourier Basis and Discrete Fourier Series

For a given periodic sequence with periodicity $N$, we have

(3.91) $f_p(n+mN) = f_p(n), \quad m \in \mathbb{Z}$.

The Fourier basis for this periodic sequence has only $N$ basis functions, namely,

(3.92) $\mathbf{e}_k(n) = e^{\frac{j2\pi}{N}kn}, \quad k=0, 1, \dots, N-1$.

We can easily show the periodicity of the basis set

(3.93)
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{e}_{k+N} &= e^{\frac{j2\pi}{N}(k+N)n} \n&= e_k \cdot e^{j2\pi n} \n&= e_k
\end{aligned}
$$

since $e^{j2\pi n} = 1$ for integer $n$. Therefore, the expansion of $f_p(n)$ is in the form of

(3.94) $f_p(n) = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \alpha_k \mathbf{e}_k(n)$

2. We divide the spectrum into 32 nonoverlapping bands with equal width. From each band, we compute the average energy contained in the coefficients. The feature element becomes

(9.25) \( v_i^{(ff)} = \frac{1}{n_i} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} s_{i,j}^2, \quad i = 1, 2, \dots, 32, \text{ and } n_i = 128, \quad \forall i, \)

where \( s_{i,j}^2 \) is the \( j \)th FFT coefficient in the \( i \)th subband. Consequently the feature vector become

(9.26) \( \mathbf{v}^{(ff)} = \{ v_1^{(ff)} \, v_2^{(ff)} \, \dots \, v_{13}^{(ff)} \}^t \).

After the feature vectors have been obtained, we apply the feature vector normalization to separate the vectors farther apart to improve performance of recognition. These vectors are used to train an ANN. There are three hidden neurons in this ANN. Details of construction and training of the ANN are beyond the scope of this text, and we refer the interested reader to Ref. 6.


Let us recall the formulation of the multiresolution analysis, in which we have the approximation subspace as an orthogonal sum of the wavelet subspaces

(7.40)
\[
\mathbf{A}_M = \oplus_{s=-M+1}^{M'} \mathbf{W}_s + \mathbf{A}_{-M'}
= \mathbf{W}_{M-1} \oplus \mathbf{W}_{M-2} \oplus \dots \oplus \mathbf{W}_{-M'+1} \oplus \mathbf{A}_{-M'}
\]

for any positive integer \( M' \). Consequently, any good approximant \( x_M \in \mathcal{A}_M \) of a given function \( x \in L^2 \) (for sufficiently large \( M \)) has a unique (orthogonal) decomposition

(7.41)
\[
x_M = \sum_{n=1}^{M'} y_{M-n} + x_{-M+M'},
\]

where \( x_s \in \mathcal{A}_s \) and \( y_s \in \mathcal{W}_s \). Since \( \phi_m \) and \( \tilde{\phi}_m \) generate the same MRA while \( \psi_m \) and \( \tilde{\psi}_m \) generate the same wavelet subspace (a property not possessed by biorthogonal scaling functions and wavelets that are not semiorthogonal), we write

(7.42)
\[
\begin{cases}
x_s(t) = \sum_k a_{k,s} \phi(2^s t - k) = \sum_k \tilde{a}_{k,s} \tilde{\phi}(2^s t - k); \\
y_s(t) = \sum_k w_{k,s} \psi(2^s t - k) = \sum_k \tilde{w}_{k,s} \tilde{\psi}(2^s t - k),
\end{cases}
\]

for each \( s \in \mathbb{Z} \). We have not included the normalization factor \( 2^{s/2} \) in order to simplify the implementation.

If we apply the decomposition formula (7.36) to the scaling function coefficients, we have

(7.43)
\[
\begin{cases} a_{k,s} = \sum_{\ell} h_0[2k-\ell] a_{\ell, s+1}; \\ w_{s,k} = \sum_{\ell} h_1[2k-\ell] a_{\ell, s+1}, \end{cases}
\]

Since sequences \(\{h_0[k]\}\) and \(\{h_1[k]\}\) are infinitely long for semiorthogonal setting, it will be more efficient to use sequences \(\{g_0[k]\}\) and \(\{g_1[k]\}\) instead. This change of sequences is valid from the duality principle, which states that \(\{g_0[k], g_1[k]\}\) and \(\{h_0[k], h_1[k]\}\) can be interchanged, in the sense that

(7.44)
\[
\begin{cases} \frac{1}{2} g_0[k] \leftrightarrow h_0[-k]; \\ \frac{1}{2} g_1[k] \leftrightarrow h_1[-k], \end{cases}
\]

when \(\phi_m\) and \(\psi_m\) are replaced by \(\tilde{\phi}_m\) and \(\tilde{\psi}_m\). With the application of duality principle, we have

(7.45)
\[
\begin{cases} \tilde{a}_{k,s} = \sum_{\ell} g_0[\ell-2k] \tilde{a}_{\ell, s+1}; \\ \tilde{w}_{k,s} = \sum_{\ell} g_1[\ell-2k] \tilde{a}_{\ell, s+1}. \end{cases}
\]

However, to take advantage of the duality principle, we need to transform the coefficients \(\{a_{k,s}\}\) to \(\{\tilde{a}_{k,s}\}\). We recall that both \(\phi\) and \(\tilde{\phi}\) generates the same \(A_s\) space so that \(\phi\) can be represented by a series of \(\tilde{\phi}\)

(7.46)
\[
\phi(t) = \sum_k r_k \tilde{\phi}(t-k)
\]

for some sequence \(\{r_k\}\). We observe that this change-of-bases sequence is a finite sequence, if the scaling function has compact support. Indeed, by the definition of the dual, we have

(7.47)
\[
r_k = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi(t) \phi(t-k) dt.
\]

Therefore, at the original scale of approximation, with \(s = M\), an application of (7.46) yields

(7.48)
\[
\tilde{a}_{k,M} = \sum_l r_{k-l} a_{l,M}.
\]

which is an FIR operation. Observe that if we take splines as scaling functions—that is, \(\phi(t) = N_m(t)\), then \(r_k = N_{2m}(m - k); k = 0, \pm 1, ..., \pm m - 1\) [1]. As we have seen in previous discussions, the sequences \(\{g_0[k]\}\) and \(\{g_1[k]\}\) in the decomposition algorithm are finite sequences.

We can summarize our computation scheme as in Figure 7.11. The computation of \(\tilde{w}'_{k,s}, s = M - 1, ..., M - M'\) using \(a_M\) as the input sequence requires \(2M'\) FIR filters.

The importance of the coefficients \(\tilde{w}_{k,s}\) is that they constitute the CWT of \(x_M\) relative to the analyzing wavelet \(\psi_m\) at certain dyadic points—namely

(7.49)
\[
\tilde{w}_{k,s} = 2^{\frac{s}{2}}(W_{\psi} x_M)(\frac{k}{2^s}, \frac{1}{2^s}), \quad M - M' \le s < M, \quad k \in \mathbb{Z}.
\]

## 7.6 SIGNAL RECONSTRUCTION IN SEMIORTHOGONAL SUBSPACES

The algorithm described in Section 7.4 concerns the recovery of the original data. In that case, the original data are the set of scaling function coefficients \(\{a_M\}\) at the highest resolution. Since the original input signal is an analog function \(x(t) = x_M(t) = \sum a_{M,\ell} \phi(2^M t - \ell)\), it is necessary to recover the signal by performing the summation. Recall that the decomposition algorithm discussed in Section 7.5 produces the spline and wavelet coefficients in the dual spaces—namely (\(\{\tilde{a}_{k,s}\}, \{\tilde{w}_{k,s}\}\)). To use finite length two-scale sequences for the reconstruction, we must express the coefficients in dual spaces in terms of (\(\{a_{k,s}\}, \{w_{k,s}\}\)) in the spline and wavelet spaces. In addition, if the user needs to see the signal component at any intermediate steps in the decomposition, he or she would have to use the dual spline and dual wavelet series. In both cases, one can simplify the problem by a change of basis that maps the dual sequences back to the original space [2]. Since the sequences do not depend on the scale, the second subscript of the coefficients can be arbitrary. Such sequences are applicable to mapping between any two different scales.

### 7.6.1 Change of Basis for Spline Functions

Our objective is to write

(7.50)
\[
s(t) = \sum_k \tilde{a}_k \tilde{N}_m(t-k) = \sum_k \bar{a}_k N_m(t-k)
\]

By taking the Fourier transform of (7.50), we get

(7.51)
\[
\bar{A}(e^{j\omega}) \hat{\tilde{N}}_m(\omega) = A(e^{j\omega}) \hat{N}_m(\omega)
\]

where, as usual, the hat over a function implies its Fourier transform and \(\bar{A}(e^{j\omega})\) are the symbols of \(\{a_k\}\) and \(\{\tilde{a}_k\}\) respectively, defined as

(7.52)
\[
\tilde{A}(e^{j\omega}) := \sum_k \tilde{a}_k e^{jk\omega}, \quad A(e^{j\omega}) := \sum_k a_k e^{jk\omega}.
\]

The dual scaling function \(\tilde{N}_m\) is given by

(7.53)
\[
\hat{N}_m(\omega) = \frac{\hat{\tilde{N}}_m(\omega)}{E_{N_m}(z^2)}, \quad z = e^{j\omega/2}
\]

where \(E_{N_m}(z^2) = |\hat{\tilde{N}}_m(\omega + 2\pi k)|^2 \neq 0\) for almost all \(\omega\) since \(\{N_m(\cdot - k)\}\) is a stable or Riesz basis of \(A_0\). As discussed in Chapter 6, \(E_{N_m}(\omega)\) is the Euler-Frobenius Laurent series and is given by

(7.54)
\[
E_{N_m}(z^2) = |\hat{\tilde{N}}_m(\omega + 2\pi k)|^2 = \sum_{k=-m+1}^{m-1} N_{2m}(m+k) z^{2k}.
\]

It is clear that by multiplying (7.54) by \(z^{m-1}\), we can get a polynomial of degree \(2m-1\) in \(z\). The last equality in (7.54) is a consequence of the relation

(7.55)
\[
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} |\hat{f}(\omega + 2\pi k)|^2 = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \left\{ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(t+k) \overline{f(t)} dt \right\} e^{jk\omega}.
\]

Proof for (7.55).

Using Parseval identity, we have

\[
F(t) := \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(\tau+t)\overline{f(\tau)}d\tau
= \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |\hat{f}(\omega)|^2 e^{-j\omega t} d\omega
= \frac{1}{2\pi} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{2k\pi}^{2\pi(k+1)} |\hat{f}(\omega)|^2 e^{-j\omega t} d\omega
= \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{0}^{2\pi} \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} |\hat{f}(\omega + 2k\pi)|^2 e^{-j\omega t} d\omega.
\]
(7.56)

It is clear that \( F(\cdot) \) is a Fourier coefficient of a \( 2\pi \)-periodic function \( \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} |\hat{f}(\omega + 2k\pi)|^2 \). With this relation (7.55) follows directly. It is easy to show that

(7.57)
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} N_m(t+k)\overline{N_m(t)}dt = N_{2m}(m+k)
\]

with \( supp \ N_{2m}(t) = [0, 2m] \).

Combining (7.51), (7.53), and (7.54) and taking the inverse Fourier transform, we get

(7.58)
\[
a_k = (\{\tilde{a}_n\} * p[n])(k)
\]

where

(7.59)
\[
\frac{1}{E_{N_m}(z)} = \sum_k p[k] z^{-k}, \quad |z| = 1.
\]

It can be shown that

(7.60)
\[
p[k] = u_m \sum_{i=1}^{p_m} \Lambda_i \lambda_i^{k+p_m}, \quad k \ge 0;
\]

where

(7.61)
\[
\Lambda_i = \frac{1}{\lambda_i \prod_{j=1, j \neq i}^{2p_m} (\lambda_i - \lambda_j)}
\]

and \(\lambda_i : i = 1, \dots, 2p_m\) are the roots of (7.54) with \(|\lambda_i| < 1\) and \(\hat{\lambda}_i \hat{\lambda}_{2p_m+1-i} = 1\) for \(i = 1, \dots, p_m\). Here \(u_m = (2m-1)!\) and \(p_m = m-1\). Observe from (7.54) and (7.59) that

(7.62)
\[
\sum_k p[k] = \frac{1}{E_{N_m}(1)} = \sum_{k=-m+1}^{m-1} \frac{1}{N_{2m}(m+k)} = 1,
\]

where the last equality is a consequence of the partition of unity property of cardinal \(B\)-splines, described in Chapter 5.

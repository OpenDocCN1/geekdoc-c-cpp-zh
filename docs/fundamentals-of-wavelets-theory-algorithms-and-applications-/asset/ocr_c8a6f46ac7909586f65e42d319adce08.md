oscillation is not required for the window function in STFT. Compared with the definition of STFT in (4.7), the wavelet $\psi_{b,a}(t)$ takes the place of $\phi_{b,\xi}$. Hence a wavelet also behaves like a window function. The behavior and measures of wavelet windows are discussed in more detail in Section 4.5.2.

# 4.5.1 Inverse Wavelet Transform

Since the purpose of the inverse transform is to reconstruct the original function from its integral wavelet transform, it involves a two-dimensional integration over the scale parameter $a > 0$ and the translation parameter $b$. The expression for the inverse wavelet transform is

\( f(t) = \frac{1}{C_\psi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} db \int_0^{\infty} \frac{1}{a^2} [W_\psi f(b,a)] \psi_{b,a}(t) da, \)
(4.36)

where $C_\psi$ is a constant that depends on the choice of wavelet and is given by

\( C_\psi = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{|\hat{\psi}(\omega)|^2}{|\omega|} d\omega < \infty, \)
(4.37)

The condition (4.37), known as *admissibility condition*, restricts the class of functions that can be wavelets. In particular, it implies that all wavelets must have $\hat{\psi}(0) = 0$ [see (4.34)] to make the left-hand side of (4.37) a finite number. For a proof of (4.36) readers may refer to [2, Chap. 2].

\( H_1(n) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & \text{for } n=0 \\ \frac{-1}{\sqrt{2}} & \text{for } n=1 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \quad \text{(2.44)}\)

The odd translates of \( H_1(n) \) forms the complementary space so that any real sequence can be represented by the Haar basis.

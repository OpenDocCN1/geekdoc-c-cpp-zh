```
$$\mathbf{W}_f(t, \omega) = \sum_{k=1}^m \mathbf{W}_{f_k}(t, \omega) + \sum_{k=1}^m \sum_{l=1, l \neq k}^m \mathbf{W}_{f_k, f_l}(t, \omega),$$
(4.77)
```

where \(\mathbf{W}_{f_k}(t, \omega)\) is called the auto-term of the WVD, while \(\mathbf{W}_{f_k, f_l}(t, \omega)\) is a cross-term defined by

```
$$\mathbf{W}_{f_k, f_l}(t, \omega) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_k \left(t + \frac{\tau}{2}\right) \overline{f_l \left(t - \frac{\tau}{2}\right)} e^{-j\omega\tau} d\tau.$$
(4.78)
```

These cross-terms of the WVD are also called the interference terms and represent the cross-coupling of energy between two components of a multicomponent signal. These interference terms are undesirable in most signal-processing applications and much research effort has been devoted to reduce the contribution of these terms. We must remember that these cross-terms [6, 7] are necessary for perfect reconstruction of the signal. In signal detection and identification applications, we are interested in discovering only those signal components that have significant energy. The cross-terms are rendered unimportant since reconstruction of the signal is not necessary.

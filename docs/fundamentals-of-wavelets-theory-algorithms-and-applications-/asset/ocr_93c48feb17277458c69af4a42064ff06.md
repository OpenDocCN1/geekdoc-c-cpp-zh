(2.18) \(\langle \phi_k, \tilde{\phi}_t \rangle = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \phi_k(t) \overline{\tilde{\phi}_t(t)} dt = \delta_{k,t}.\)

the set \(\{\tilde{\phi}_k(t)\}_{k \in \mathbb{Z}}\) is called the dual basis of \(\{\phi_k(t)\}_{k \in \mathbb{Z}}\).

We may expand a function \(g(t)\) in terms of the biorthogonal basis

\(g(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} d_k \phi_k(t),\)

and obtain the coefficients by

(2.19) \(d_n = \langle g, \tilde{\phi}_n \rangle\)

(2.20) \(= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} g(t) \overline{\tilde{\phi}_n(t)} dt.\)

On the other hand, we may expand the function \(g(t)\) in terms of the dual basis

(2.21) \(g(t) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \tilde{d}_k \tilde{\phi}_k(t),\)

and obtain the dual coefficients \(\tilde{d}_k\) by

(2.22) \(\tilde{d}_i = \langle g, \phi_i \rangle\)

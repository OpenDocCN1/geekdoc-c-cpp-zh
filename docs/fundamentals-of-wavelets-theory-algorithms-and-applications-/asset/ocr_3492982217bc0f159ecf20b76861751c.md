(6.43) \(\langle \phi_{SH}(t-\ell), \phi_{SH}(t) \rangle = \delta_{0, \ell}\)

is cumbersome. Here it is rather easy to show that the Riesz bounds are 1; that is,

(6.44) \(\sum_k \left| \hat{\phi}_{SH}(\omega + 2\pi k) \right|^2 = 1.\)

The sequence \(\{g_0[k]\}\) can be obtained from the two-scale relation

(6.45) \(\frac{1}{2} \sum_k g_0[k] e^{-jk\omega/2} = \frac{\hat{\phi}_{SH}(\omega)}{\hat{\phi}_{SH}(\omega/2)}.\)

Since the left-hand side of the expression is a 4\(\pi\)-periodic function, we need a 4\(\pi\)-periodic extension of the right-hand side. In other words, \(G_0(z)\) is nothing but a 4\(\pi\)-periodic extension of \(\hat{\phi}_{SH}(\omega)\) (see Figure 6.4)

(6.46) \(G_0(z) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_\ell g_0[\ell] \exp\left(-j \frac{\omega \ell}{2}\right) = \sum_\ell \hat{\phi}_{SH}(\omega + 4\pi \ell).\)

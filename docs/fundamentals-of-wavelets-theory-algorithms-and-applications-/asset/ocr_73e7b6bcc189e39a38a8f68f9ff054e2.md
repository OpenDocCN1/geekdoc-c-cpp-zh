Using \( M=2 \) as an example, we write

\( x'(n) = y(n)_{\uparrow 2} \)
\( = \begin{cases} y(n/2), & \text{for } n \text{ even} \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} \)
(7.15)

In matrix form, we have

\( \begin{bmatrix} \vdots \\ x'(-2) \\ x'(-1) \\ x'(0) \\ x'(1) \\ x'(2) \\ x'(3) \\ x'(4) \\ \vdots \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \vdots \\ y(-1) \\ 0 \\ y(0) \\ 0 \\ y(1) \\ 0 \\ y(2) \\ \vdots \end{bmatrix} \)
(7.16)

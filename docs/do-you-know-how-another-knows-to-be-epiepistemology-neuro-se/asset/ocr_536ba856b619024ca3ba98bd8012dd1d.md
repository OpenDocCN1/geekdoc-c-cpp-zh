In other words, the question WHY culls a shift in thinking to the logical level above WHY, i.e.:

Y MAKES X.

This shift in logical levels is not cognitively contrived. It is a function of the grammar of A that evinces it. Like WHY, “make” is a multi-ordinal term. It opens a near infinity of sentences in which it has a place.

At the Freedom Seminar, we test this relationship in real time. We ask a student to come up to help us. We ask the question: “Why are you attending this seminar?” Let us say his answer is XYZ. Then we ask the class to look at him. We then ask: “Is it true-to-say that XYZ is MAKING you attend this seminar.” Without exception, ever since the offering of this seminar from 1983, every registrant has replied: “Yes” and at the same time nodded their head. To get a verbal affirmation with a co-analog confirmation is what is known as a congruent answer. It would have been very different if he vocalized, yes whilst shaking his head. This would be an incongruent answer. In our culture, the ideal is to get congruent answers, either in the positive or negative.

For all events and things, we are anchored to wondering what MAKES them happen or to produce them. Implicit to this is our concern about the quality of the doing of the MAKE.

Additionally, our automatic tendency is to find:

+   - What MAKEs a person be the way he is?
+   - What MADE a person do what he did?

Once you ask these questions, you logically lift from the person all accountability and responsibility for his actions. This is so, because whatever it is that MAKES him do it, is the variable that is responsible, not the person. On this path, we are now on the road of the unsane.

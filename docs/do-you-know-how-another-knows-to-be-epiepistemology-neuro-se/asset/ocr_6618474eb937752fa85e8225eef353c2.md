One more time, we review the universal metrics that apply to WELL-FORMEDNESS and by implicate extension to ILL-FORMEDNESS. They all concern the verbs of life:

+   (a). there must be free choice to do
+   (b). the doing is to be impeccable
+   (c). it is useful and worthwhile
+   (d). it is to be ethical, entailing no harm to self or other
+   (e). it is to be done with some love and compassion
+   (f). it must help the person to rise in his awareness

For a behaviour to be WELL-FORMED, it has to fulfil the above metrics. If the behaviour fails to do so then it is ILL-FORMED. If you set the above metrics to a smoker or alcoholic, you will have the answer as to whether their behaviour is well-formed or ill-formed.

The matters that relate to WELL-FORMEDNESS and ILL-FORMEDNESS are vast. Clearly, we cannot examine all these considerations here. So, we limit our examination to how they apply to human beliefs.

But, very few people are aware of the unique linguistic function of the question WHY. Its job is to get the JUSTIFICATION of the action that is described by the verb in any sentence. As you know every grammatical English sentence must have a verb. Therefore, every sentence may be hounded by a WHY. For this I can ask you: “Why are you picking your nose?” or “Why do you dress that way?” The odd thing is that people suppress the fitting answer, which is:

> "Mind your own business!"

> "What has it got to do with you?"

The bias to use WHY for every sentence, has atrophied our ability to use those questions that have functions that apply to what we really want to know. Thus, if you find your best friend’s son has stabbed his father, you have every legitimate basis to ask for his JUSTIFICATION for what he did: “Why have you stabbed your father?” However, your best friend’s son has returned from a three-week trip to Shanghai; and you ask him: “Why did you spend three weeks in Shanghai?” This is a futile question. What you really want to know is: “What did you do in Shanghai?” We remind you of the declaration of Virginia Satir that:

> "WHY is a mind fucking question."

Recently a friend told us of a family they know well. They have a son who is five years old and has taken to asking WHY for everything. They went through a phase in which they felt quite homicidal in response to his WHYs. The current result is that he has pinned them to the point of desperation. Their answer to him is: “Just because IT IS.” Which unfortunately is no answer at all. At no stage was he told NOT to use WHY; nor was he taught to use the other available interrogatives.

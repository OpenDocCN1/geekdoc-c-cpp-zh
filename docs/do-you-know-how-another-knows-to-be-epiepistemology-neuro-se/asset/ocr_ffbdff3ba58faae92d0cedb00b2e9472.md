In our clinical work, we have met with untold numbers of clients who have confirmed to us, through the NSP therapeutic manoeuvre known as the Quadrant Search, QS that: Yes! I am a PERFECTIONIST⁹. Of course, they have no awareness of what is at issue with this self-confessed ontology. In the graded shift along the scale from “I am just a simple human being” to “I am a perfectionist” is the corresponding shift from humility to hubris.

The problem for any person who says: I am a perfectionist, is the instinctive and intuitive evidence that he is human. For this he cannot escape the unconscious nagging that he might err. For this, he is doomed to a life of worry and anxiety that what he is to do might not be perfect. For some, when the task is a critical matter, they may even skid off into panic that what they do might be a fiasco or not good enough. They know they live amid people who fumble, stumble, bumble and bungle. If they are supervising the work of juniors they are invariably in a continuous state of tension, worry, anxiety and fear that they might fail their juniors.

They also can be so swiftly frustrated and quick to anger. So, it might not surprise you that they smoke cigarettes, pot or in some cases snort cocaine. It is known as a quick medical fix for tensions, frustration, and anger.

As perfectionists, if they bungle or fail, they will lie or put the blame on someone else. Of course, neither of these are ontologically well-formed in their semantics. Do you get the feeling we all live in a bit of a crazy world, and this crazy world is spawned out of some crazy logic?

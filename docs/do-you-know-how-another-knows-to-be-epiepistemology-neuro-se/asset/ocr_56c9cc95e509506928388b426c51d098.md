These above examples are the evidence as to how we naturally think – mathematically. If we refer to the equation for a circumference and its radius, it is about a modus ponens:

C = 2 π r.

If a radius has any real value, then the circumference will have its value by a factor of 2 π. How then is it that children have such problems with mathematics? The answer is that if they think by C→E, they will have problems. Reared and groomed in the language of A, they cannot see how the value of r makes the value of c. The child cannot see it because r does not make c. It is simply not there to see. The child will press on in this errant way with all other mathematical equations with disastrous results. He is doomed to have very poor marks in mathematics.

In the vernacular, the usual is to drop the “then” of the modus ponens. So rather than saying:

If you put on one of your usual wonderful parties, then, all your friends will be happy.

the line is:

If you put on one of your usual wonderful parties, all your friends will be happy.

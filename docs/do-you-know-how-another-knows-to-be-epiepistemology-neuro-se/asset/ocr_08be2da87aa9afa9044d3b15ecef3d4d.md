No matter what the behaviour is, you can apply the metrics of WELL-FORMEDNESS to determine if what people do is WELL-FORMED or not.

There is a story of a man who was to follow the path taken by those of the Jain faith in India. When the time came for him to leave there was a great celebration with family and friends. During this event, the Jain man gives one factory to his son and another to his daughter and son-in-law. He gives the title of his home and lands and all money, stock and shares to his wife. Literally, he now has nothing.

The next morning, when the household is still asleep, he awakens. With a begging bowl and a walking staff, he goes out of his home and never returns. Now he wanders all over India. He does not own a garment or shoes. He treads the path of utter penury. Long before he reaches a village, the villagers know he is coming. With utter respect, the Jain holy man is welcomed and given food and shelter. His only way of acknowledgement is to give to them his best Bodhisattvatic teaching out of his learnings by his wandering experiences; and the villagers and children, in rapture, listen and they learn.

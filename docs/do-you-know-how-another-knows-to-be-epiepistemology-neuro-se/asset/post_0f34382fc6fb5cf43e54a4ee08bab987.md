At the heart of WELL-FORMEDNESS and ILL-FORMEDNESS is how we satisfy the question as to whether what we think, say, feel and do is **fitting**? There is a critical distinction between what is WELL-FORMED versus what is RIGHT. However, in rare conditions, what is WELL-FORMED or RIGHT may be indistinguishable. Rather than talk about it we will illustrate the distinction by an example. During a recession, a wife brought her husband to see me. He was an excellent carpenter with a very good reputation. He was working between 75 to 90 hours a week, whilst others were on part time, had no business or were laid off.

Under these conditions, his wife, *de facto*, had become a single mother with her two children. She saw him off early after his breakfast each morning. The children never saw him. When he came home late, tired and exhausted, he would sit down before the TV and she would bring his meal. She told me: *Many a time I would come to see how he was in the living room. His meal was only partially eaten. He would be fast asleep. I would take away his tray of food. Then I would come back, put a pillow under his head, lift his feet up on the chesterfield and put a blanket over him. I would put out the lights and go upstairs to bed.* This was her complaint when she “dragged” him to see me:

> We do not have a life together!

His response was: Dr., these are hard times. Many are without work. I have to make what I can and when I can for my family. What could I say to him? I, however, continued to see the wife. It was supportive therapy; and she was truly thankful for it.

> No!

All of us are driven by our idiosyncratic and uniquely personal conclusions with respect to what for us is RIGHT to do. Yet, by this case example, at a deeper level, this man had no choice but to agree. From his anchored innate, heritable program of lawfulness and semantic well-formedness, he was forced to admit that what he had decided to do for his holiday was not well-formed; it was ill-fitting to exclude his wife and his children.

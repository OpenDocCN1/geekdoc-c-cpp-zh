For the answer, we go back to the basic propositions of the study of language, Linguistics9. This was what was put to us in 1979 when we took our first steps into the field of Linguistics:

+   (a). when we hear a person speak, we hear the sounds of his oral articulations. These sounds are known as surface structures, SS, of language. If we do not understand them, e.g. Nee how ma? they are known as phonemes. If we understand them, i.e. we grasp their semantic forms, they are then known as morphemes. The distinction between the two is whether we have the Transformational Grammar of the language that is being spoken. The above phonemes are the Mandarin words for the sentence: How are you? These words are phonemes for you: Apah marcham. Mari seeni. They are Malay morphemes. They stand for: What is happening? Come here.

+   (b). all morphemes by Deep Structure, DS will be taken down to Deep Linguistic Structure, DLS.

The above takes this description:

Surface Structure, SS
↓ DS
Deep Linguistic Structure, DLS

Then Bandler and Grinder proposed that the morphemes had to be taken down to the Sensory Data Base, SDB of:

+   Vision, V, Audition, A, Kinaesthetics, K, Olfaction, O Gustation, G

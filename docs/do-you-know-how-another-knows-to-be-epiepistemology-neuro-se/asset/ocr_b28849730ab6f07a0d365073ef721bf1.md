If you intend to spend a holiday for two weeks in Hokkaido, Japan, you will surely be in a perturbation. There are these problems:

+   (a). when is the best time to go?
+   (b). which airline to travel in?
+   (c). is it possible to visit neighbouring countries?
+   (d). questions re visa requirements?
+   (e). questions re currencies?
+   (f). what to wear?
+   (g). which luggage to use?
+   etc.

When you are about to take a nap and you are just about to doze off, there is a sharp sound. Suddenly you are awake. Now, you are in a perturbation because you are not quite sure of the nature of the sound. Quickly you reflect on it. Was it a gun shot, a sharp cry, a car engine backfire, a motor cycle engine backfire, a car crash, a part of a falling building and so forth? Until you come to your decision as to what it probably might be, you will remain in your perturbation of confusion and bewilderment.

There are two kinds of perturbations:

+   (a). a disruption of a person’s initial condition
+   (b). a sharp disturbance on one’s current existential state.

Think of the man to whom suddenly comes the thought that his brother, that he is in business with, may be swindling him. Think of a man who becomes suspicious that his best friend and his wife may be having an affair! Now both will be in a state of perturbation.

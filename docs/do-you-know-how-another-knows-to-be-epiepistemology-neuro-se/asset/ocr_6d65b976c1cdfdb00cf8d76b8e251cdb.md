To be able to consider the structure of one language of a definite structure, we must produce another language of a different structure in which the structure of the first can be analysed.

Alfred Korzybski: Science and Sanity, AN INTRODUCTION TO NON-ARISTOTELIAN SYSTEMS AND GENERAL SEMANTICS, The International Non-Aristotelian Library Publishing Company Sixth Reprinting, 1980, page 56.

Then in S&S we found the corroboration that Bertrand Russel had intuited upon the importance of a metalanguage:

> This verifies the contention of Bertrand Russell made in 1922 that there is a ‘possibility’ that ‘every language has . . . a structure concerning which, in the language, nothing can be said, but that there may be another language dealing with the structure of the first language, and having itself a new structure’. What Russell calls a ‘possibility’ becomes a fact once a system of different structure is built.

Alfred Korzybski: Science and Sanity, AN INTRODUCTION TO NON-ARISTOTELIAN SYSTEMS AND GENERAL SEMANTICS, The International Non-Aristotelian Library Publishing Company 1980 Sixth Reprinting page xxiii -xxiv.

> Eventually we realized that this state of affairs was directly linked to the hierarchical structure of all language, communication, learning, etc. As we pointed out in Chapter 1, to express or explain something requires a shift to one logical level above what is to be expressed or explained. No explaining can be accomplished on the same level; a metalanguage has to be used, but this metalanguage is not necessarily available.

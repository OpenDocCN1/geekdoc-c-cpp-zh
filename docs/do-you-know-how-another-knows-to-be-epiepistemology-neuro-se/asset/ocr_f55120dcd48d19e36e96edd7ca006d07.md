Then one day, they could stand and walk along the branches of their trees, even run along them. In time, they came down to explore the earth. At some point, perhaps by scavenging was their discovery of eating meat. Thus, they found that meat was a far richer source of protein than leaves. So, they began their pursuit and hunt for game on the plains, hills and valleys. In this context, they cornered their prey and applied the lethal use of rocks to hurl at their prey to kill them. Here was the evidence and proof to our ancestors of a canon of the Aristotelian system of Cause and Effect, A:

> Stone we throw, will kill.
> We *caused* the death of our prey.

Then they found the way to break stone with stone from which came fragments that had very sharp edges. The best of stones was obsidian, also known as volcanic glass. Strike it with a stone or antler, and it breaks off. The edge of the fragment has the sharpness of a surgeon’s knife. Such stones sharpened wooden sticks to form spears. These stone fragments could be fashioned into arrow heads. The next significant step for our ancestors was to discover how to create fire; and with this came the fragrance of burnt meat. It ended their days of dining on *meat tartare*.

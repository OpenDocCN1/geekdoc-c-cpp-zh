From these categories come the answers that will satisfy the enquirer. These nouns are multiordinal terms. They have an application in any possible context in life. You only need to think of a situation and you can manufacture a question that uses any of the above nouns. Let us take a simple example: At a meal, a guest inadvertently eructates a flatulence, a.k.a. burping. For this event, you may ask: What is your explanation/excuse/etc. for burping?

As for the question, WHY, let us consider this example. You see me constantly picking my nose. It is a mystery to you. So, you ask:

> "Why are you always picking your nose, Dennis?"

I reply honestly:

> "It is a habit that I have."

This is the reason, explanation, rationalization, justification, and excuse which you accept without further question; but do you really have the answer?

However, unconsciously and automatically we are anchored to use WHY to satisfy its linguistic function. What is its linguistic function? It is to seek the:

# JUSTIFICATION

for any verb. This is how it is a multi-ordinal term. There is no English sentence in which you may not apply WHY to its verb. This is manifest in a court trial when a legal counsel repeatedly demands an answer to his WHY before a hapless plaintiff.

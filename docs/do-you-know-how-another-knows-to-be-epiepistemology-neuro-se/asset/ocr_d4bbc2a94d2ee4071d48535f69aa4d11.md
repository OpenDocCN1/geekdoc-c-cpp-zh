The multiordinality of terms is the fundamental mechanism of the full conditionality of human semantic reactions⁹; it eliminates an unbelievable number of the old animalistic blockages and is fundamental for sanity.

> Alfred Korzybski: Science and Sanity, AN INTRODUCTION TO NON-ARISTOTELIAN SYSTEMS AND GENERAL SEMANTICS, The International Non-Aristotelian Library Publishing Company, Fourth Edition, Sixth printing 1980, page 14 - 15.

Briefly, let us recap by way of other examples, the multiordinal term: yes. As a term, it has an application in a vast number of semantic contexts. It has only one rider, namely that you agree with the semantics of that context. In turn, this is equally true for the term: no. It has an application in an incredibly vast number of semantic contexts.

If you give it some thought you will recall saying “Yes” to a vast number of invitations and events, things, people and decisions and this includes a McDonald’s hamburger. It included “Yes” in your last vote in the General Election in Canada, the shoes you bought, the film you elected to see and the new restaurant that was recommended to you.

⁹ human semantic reactions: When Korzybski wrote Science and Sanity, it was the fashion of the time to think of human behaviour as being similar to the knee-jerk reflex. A stimulus/tap on the patella tendon and VOILÀ, stat, the reaction of the flaccid leg popping straight out and forward. This behaviour, indeed, required no internal processing ors. We are not robots or dogs. For this, we now recommend that Korzybski’s abbreviation for human behaviour as s.r., semantic reaction, change to human semantic response, s.r., i.e. no bold from s.r.

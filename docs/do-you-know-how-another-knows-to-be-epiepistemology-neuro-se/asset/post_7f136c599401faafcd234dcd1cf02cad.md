## Fourth Logical Holon of A:

### WHY?

In our initial steps to explore A, we had to begin somewhere. It seemed to be the most fitting thing to start with the question WHY. Every person uses this question with facility and unconscious automaticity. As it turns out, in our time, the first question of the Catechism of the Catholic Faith was:

Why did God make you?

WHY is the dominant question in Religion, Law, Politics, Science, Business, Commerce and Journalism. It is also, the dominant question in all social interactive exchanges in life. It is most certainly the most used question by children.

WHY is the fulcrum upon which the entire holarchy of A, spins and turns, and for which:

- (a). ontologically our lives similarly twirl and pirouette
- (b). the affairs of all semantic domains of the entire world swirls and churns.

It is so because we often do not understand people, things and events. When this is so, we ask WHY to get the:

- (a). EXPLANATIONS
- (b). REASONS
- (c). RATIONALIZATIONS
- (d). JUSTIFICATIONS
- (e). EXCUSES

From these categories come the answers that will satisfy the enquirer. These nouns are multiordinal terms. They have an application in any possible context in life. You only need to think of a situation and you can manufacture a question that uses any of the above nouns. Let us take a simple example: At a meal, a guest inadvertently eructates a flatulence, a.k.a. burping. For this event, you may ask: What is your explanation/excuse/etc. for burping?

As for the question, WHY, let us consider this example. You see me constantly picking my nose. It is a mystery to you. So, you ask:

> "Why are you always picking your nose, Dennis?"

I reply honestly:

> "It is a habit that I have."

This is the reason, explanation, rationalization, justification, and excuse which you accept without further question; but do you really have the answer?

However, unconsciously and automatically we are anchored to use WHY to satisfy its linguistic function. What is its linguistic function? It is to seek the:

for any verb. This is how it is a multi-ordinal term. There is no English sentence in which you may not apply WHY to its verb. This is manifest in a court trial when a legal counsel repeatedly demands an answer to his WHY before a hapless plaintiff.

In this respect, let us now clarify the linguistic functions of the other interrogatives of the English language, to wit:

- (a). HOW seeks information about the method.
- (b). WHO is about information re persons.
- (c). WHAT is about information re things.

- (e). WHERE is about location.
- (f). WHICH is about choice/options.
- (g). WHOM is identity from a general set.
- (h). WHOSE is about a specific identity.

All of us are habituated to using WHY. We accept and accommodate to its consequences with the result that we have no cognitive awareness of them. Consider this question:

In our Law and Culture, the only place we know where you may lawfully and legitimately ask, “WHY” is in a Court of Law. ## Third Linguistic Holon:

### Y makes X

Across the span of one’s life, there are literally untold myriads of things that one does not understand which may include, for us:

> "WHY are you reading this book?"

Let us suppose your answer is:

> "I bought it to read because it was powerfully recommended to me to do so."

With this answer, I now understand WHY you are reading this book. However, with it I also know that:

> The powerful recommendation to read the book MADE you buy the book.

In other words, the question WHY calls a shift in thinking to the logical level above WHY, i.e.:

Y MAKES X.

This shift in logical levels is not cognitively contrived. It is a function of the grammar of A that evinces it. Like WHY, “make” is a multi-ordinal term. It opens a near infinity of sentences in which it has a place.

At the Freedom Seminar, we test this relationship in real time. We ask a student to come up to help us. We ask the question: “Why are you attending this seminar?” Let us say his answer is XYZ. Then we ask the class to look at him. We then ask: “Is it true-to-say that XYZ is MAKING you attend this seminar.” Without exception, ever since the offering of this seminar from 1983, every registrant has replied: “Yes” and at the same time nodded their head. To get a verbal affirmation with a co-analog confirmation is what is known as a congruent answer. It would have been very different if he vocalized, yes whilst shaking his head. This would be an incongruent answer. In our culture, the ideal is to get congruent answers, either in the positive or negative.

For all events and things, we are anchored to wondering what MAKES them happen or to produce them. Implicit to this is our concern about the quality of the doing of the MAKE.

Additionally, our automatic tendency is to find:

- What MAKEs a person be the way he is?
- What MADE a person do what he did?

Once you ask these questions, you logically lift from the person all accountability and responsibility for his actions. This is so, because whatever it is that MAKES him do it, is the variable that is responsible, not the person. On this path, we are now on the road of the unsane.

## Second Linguistic Holon:

## CAUSE and EFFECT

At the logical level, Y makes X, one is functionally at CAUSE and EFFECT, a.k.a. C→E. So, if we ask: Is it true that the powerful recommendation to read the book CAUSED you to buy the book? The answer is inevitably a congruent affirmative.

On earth and in the universe, is an infinity of phenomena. Our embedded logic compels us to find their CAUSES. This is the *modus operandi* of Science. However, in life people search for what CAUSES another to do what he has done. In doing so, they are looking for the noun by which the doer need not be accountable or responsible for his actions and consequences. The noun is hailed as the initiator and doer of the deed. This kind of thinking is nonsense.

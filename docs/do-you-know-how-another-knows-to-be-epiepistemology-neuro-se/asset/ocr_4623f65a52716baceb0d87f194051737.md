Then the idea of language structure, LS, came to us from our read of the works on Artificial Intelligence, AI, in the 1970s. What is a LS? It is the title of a semantic domain. In it are all the possible files about a given subject. The files have all possible sentences and words *apropos* to it. Put simply, the LS will have the entire vocabulary of the subject including its vernacular slangs and so forth. All material in a person’s LS is coded in binary language. Any LS is a component of its Holarchy. This means that our Applied Linguistics is the study of how we use our LSs in accord with the grammar of our language. The following are examples of LSs:

+   (a). The Canadian Constitution
+   (b). Yorkshire Pudding
+   (c). The Virgin Mary
+   (d). Niagara-on-the-Lake
+   (e). Euthanasia.

Consider this LS: *The Vienna State Opera Ball*. If you were the organizer of this event can you just imagine the sheer volume of sub-LSs that you will be using to actualize this event?

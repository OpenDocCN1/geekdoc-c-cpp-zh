In this, we shall always thank John Grinder5 for his teaching to us, this truth, about the English Language. It is impossible to generate an English sentence that is perfectly well-formed.

What is a well-formed English sentence? It is one that contains the entire body of relevant semantic information that the speaker of the sentence wishes to convey. Since this cannot be achieved, then by its nature all English sentences are

+   (a). semantically ill-formed
+   (b). incomplete
+   (c). ambiguous.

It is because of this that legal documents are as lengthy as they are; but no matter how lengthy they may be, they will never carry the entire intended body of information and meaning. There will always be a flaw in them. This offers to any lawyer the test as to how excellent he is to serve his client’s interest. His job is to find the flaw in the document and to exploit it to the utmost for his client’s sake.

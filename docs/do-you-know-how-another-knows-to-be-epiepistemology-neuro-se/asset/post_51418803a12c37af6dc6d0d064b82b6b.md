## A Note of Clarification

When Einstein’s paper on his Theory of Special Relativity, TSR, was published in 1905, he did not hold an august professional chair in a famed German University. He was just a factor in the Swiss Patent Office as an examiner of patents.

The scientific establishment of his time was very guarded towards his paper; and this was especially more so when his paper had a stunning conclusion that light did not travel in straight lines. This was a violation of Euclid’s finding that light travels in straight lines!

It is the blessed stance of Science that, you can come up with any theory of your choice with whatever conclusion it takes you to. However, what is required of you is that you must devise a legitimate test that carries the results that prove the conclusion you hold; after that, a second centre must do the same test and it must have the same results that prove your conclusion¹.

It was only after WW I that Astrophysicist Arthur Eddington could do the test, which proved the accuracy of Einstein’s conclusion, that light does NOT travel in straight lines. As it turned out, it was not just a second centre, but a slew of them, that did the same test and got the same test results — namely, light does not travel in straight lines.

We now know that we cannot totally rely on our common sense. We cannot, because it was common sense that told us, once upon a time that:

- (a). the world is flat
- (b). the sun rises in the east and sets in the west
- (c). the sun goes around the earth
- (d). we are at the centre of the universe
- (e). we are the only sentient life forms in the universe

All these conclusions and others have been shown to be untrue-to-fact. However, we think that there are still four common sense conclusions that are lurking about, to wit that:

- (a). WHY is a cogent and rational question to use in life
- (b). there is Cause and Effect
- (c). there is possibly no God
- (d). we have never been visited by extra-terrestrials

It is our view, that you may rely on your common sense only if, whatever it apprehends and comprehends, is an item that is within a Lorentzian space/time frame²; and you, firstly, can devise a test to verify it and secondly, somewhere else in the world the same test is applied and gets the same results.

There are claims that cannot be real-to-life:

- (a). I will love you till the end of time.
- (b). This will be forever.
- (c). This will go on into eternity

In this work, we shall do our best to ensure that what we put before you, in the form of a claim, conclusion, evaluation and assertion, has a test and/or the evidence for it. It is our view that, especially today, it is very improper to make claims or assertions without anything to support them. Thus, to assert that there is an INNER POWER in us, when by our lived experience there is no evidence for it, puts this claim in the fantasy land of rah, rah, rah! It is untenable to do such a thing. The claim needs to be corroborated by some test or evidence. Put simply: Where is the proof? Without it, as George R. R. Martin of Game of Thrones says:

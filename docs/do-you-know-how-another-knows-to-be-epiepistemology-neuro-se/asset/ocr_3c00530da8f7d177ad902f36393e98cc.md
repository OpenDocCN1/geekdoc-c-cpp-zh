Historically, we know that in the middle Ages the albino’s self-flagellation was the preferred method of choice for all Christians to punish themselves for their sins; and it had the blessing and imprimatur of the Church; it truly and certainly was a religiously fashionable thing to do. The flesh was weak. The logic was that by punishing it, it would be strengthened by blood and pain. Generally, it was felt in the middle Ages that the body needed to be punished. The aim was to deny earthly pleasures to the body. This included the wearing of a hair shirt; and to live a life in the wilderness such as a hermit in the desert. What we are describing here are the visceral ontologies that flow from medieval Christian orthodox thinking.³

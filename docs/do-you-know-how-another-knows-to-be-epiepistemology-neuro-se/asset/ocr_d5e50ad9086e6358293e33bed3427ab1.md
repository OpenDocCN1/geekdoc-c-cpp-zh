If Einstein was alive, this is what we would have shared with him. With his logical mind, he would have seen that what we have stated here is indeed true, that God does not play dice. It is only those who play dice that know-in-experience the balance between the incredible thrill, fear and challenge as well as the fun of it, versus the peril, sharp edge of the danger and the pain of loss. Put more formally, in Divine Order he would have seen the inevitable and the logical corollary of chance and the improbable. Then, he would have offered his contribution to the field of Quantum Physics. What would have been the consequences of him doing so, we shall of course now never know.

With the certainty of the death of every great soul is the loss of all the chances and improbabilities of their future creations. And this is a fundamental Law of Physics.

When John Lennon was murdered, we lost all the chances and improbabilities of the great music that he could have created for us.

At one level, we live in the age of Cybernetics and Geometrodynamics. Cybernetics opens our understanding to the wondrous world of systems and of systems within systems and of the flow of information in them and between them. Geometrodynamics has opened our eyes to the wonders and the incredible terrors of the heavens⁹. In this, we are singularly privileged to be able to see some of them.

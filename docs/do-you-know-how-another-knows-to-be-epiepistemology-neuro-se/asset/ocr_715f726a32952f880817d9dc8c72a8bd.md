Consider these next five examples:

+   (a). Governor General of Canada
+   (b). Prime Minister of Canada
+   (c). Chief of Police
+   (d). Air Commodore of Air Canada
+   (e). Head Nurse of ICU.

The respective office holders cited above, all have this in common. They have POWER over their subordinates. This condition is inevitable because the grammar of any hierarchical system requires this. Those who are under those above them are to OBEY; and this is for the good of the operation of the system.

However, it is open to abuse. This is certainly true in the world of C→E. It is for this that a hierarchical system risks losing the potential creative contribution of subordinates. In the world of NSP we are sure that no hierarchical system can afford such a loss.

It is essential to have a meta system in which one can be aware of such possibilities; and a system of penalties be clearly delineated for such violations. Currently, the corrective mechanism to abuse is by whistleblowing. And wisely, the English have legalized it.

To have people OBEY you is heady wine; and this can warp a person’s ontology. In A, the sceptre of power is the:

+   (a). ability and facility to blame the other
+   (b). the power to sack the underling

He, who is blamed, will feel diminished and inferior. If he is flexible, he will learn to back down well and artistically. If he is rigid and inflexible, he will be sacked.

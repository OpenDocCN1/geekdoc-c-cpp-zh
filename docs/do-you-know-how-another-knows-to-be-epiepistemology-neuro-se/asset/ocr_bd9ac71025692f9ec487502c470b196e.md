> "it may apply to all verbal levels and in each particular case may have different content or meanings and so no single content or meaning."

As an example, take the word structure:

+   (a). This structure will hold 100,000 tons of crude oil.
+   (b). This structure will collapse if that beam gives way.
+   (c). This structure is so rigid that if you break it, it will mean your death.
+   (d). How many structures do you hope to own?
+   (e). If you keep with more modifications, the structure will not look as it was originally intended.

The word can be applied to a statement about the first statement or the next and becomes an abstraction of it. There is a whole range of multioridinal terms of which we cite here a few: good, fine, love, excellent, gracious, ungracious, kind, fast, more, less and so forth.

Life is an instant of time. That instant of time is but one of an infinity of possible unique life situations.

The existence and scope of multioridinal terms means that our sentiency can expand into the semantics of an infinity of contexts. Theoretically, it means we escape the restrictions of the semantic fixations that can apply to us. It opens the possibility for us to have free will to have choices, whereas animals are fixated on what they want at that moment in time.

Here is another example of a way of being that held across centuries. The fate of a samurai warrior was to die for his master. It was the noblest and the greatest thing he could do. It was also the cultural orthodoxy of an ancient Roman that if he was dishonoured he was to take his life. So, he would. If an ancient Mandarin failed the Emperor of China, he would have to die. And so, he would end his life.

Times have changed. Today, we have high ministers who bungle in how their ministries spend and spend to the point where they cannot even account **for a lost dollar** let alone billions. Auditor Generals, federally and provincially in Canada, have severely critiqued them for their dishonourable fiduciary management. To date, not one has been brought to court. We wonder what has happened that, in these times, we have denied the place of honour in our orthodoxy with respect to our political and secular lives.

Our military men and women are dispersed into various battlefields in the world today. The operative presupposition is that they, if necessary, are to die with honour for their country. If this is so, then how is it that when our high politicians ruin our country into penury and debt, they do not pay the price for their dishonour to the country?

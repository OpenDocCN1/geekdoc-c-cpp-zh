The reality is that, at every moment of our lives we are *being*. We are not “I”-ing. In our *being*, is our *existing* and *existing* is to *be*. This is what is actual-to-life and true-to-fact. The greatest of life’s mysteries is *our being*. Its study is the basis of the disciplines of Ontology and Epistemology. From moment to moment and from place to place, we simply have no surety, guarantee or certainty as to exactly how *we will be*.

It is to this unreal thing, this “I” that we put on it, its *importance*, *esteem* and *ego*, to our *thresholds of eminence and magnitude*.

What is interesting however, is our most current research finding. To be able to live and function well in life, these *fictive variables*, SI, SE and Ego have to be extant in a person, ideally, within their semantically well-formed limits on the human body. This means that SI, SE and Ego cannot be ablated in a person if he is to be able to live his life well. When they are inoperant in a person it constitutes a discrete clinical condition. Thus, their absence leaves the person incomplete or un-whole. With this there are negative consequences as to how a person lives and what he can achieve, accomplish and fulfill in his life. Is this condition listed in DSM-5? The answer is NO!

The displacement of SI, SE and Ego is the result of critical traumatic experiences - physical, sexual, verbal, emotional and psychological. When they are displaced, it leaves an analog marker in its place. In the workshop on the therapeutic algorithm to restore SI, SE and Ego in a person the student will be taught where to look for the analog markers, how to repair the SI, SE and Ego and how to restore them to their body sites.

We cannot conclude our dissertation here without observing that the term “me” is also another nominalization of *to be* or *being*. Our current view is that semantically, “I” and “me” are synonymous terms.

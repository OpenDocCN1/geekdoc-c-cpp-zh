Another multioridinal term is love. Thus, for certain engineers, the Spitfire of WW II fame, was a thing that they fell in love with and so too did its pilots. On the other hand, you can love a classical Sunday pork roast with its fine crackling. Suddenly, you are aware of the innumerable things, persons, places and times you have loved; it even included your teddy bear. We can even say we love to love.

Hate is also a multi-ordinal term. So, let it be a given that we all hate a man like Hitler. However, I was at a dinner with Jennifer at the Boathouse Restaurant in Naples, Florida. There was one male in a party of three women in which he and he alone erupted into the most irritating ingratiating laughter, repeatedly that was my misfortune to have to listen to. It was the first time that I ever came close to hating the annoyance of a person’s laugh. Now it becomes clear to you of the things you might have hated in your life.

We have already stated that each of us can say any number of possible English sentences. This statement confirms that each of us has the power of infinity; to back it we have to have an infinite vocabulary. However, we do not! It is the vocabulary of multioridinal terms that underpins our infinite power in language and by it we can be infinite in our linguistics. What we are saying is that if we can generate any one of an infinite number of sentences, then it follows that we can think in any infinite number of ways. In turn, we can generate any one of an infinite number of ontologies. This is a wonderful ability that we have, however, it can lead to confusion, misunderstandings and arguments.

People with mental disorders, seem to lose this ability to generate any one of an infinite number of ontologies. They are stuck in a semantically ill-formed/malformed one.

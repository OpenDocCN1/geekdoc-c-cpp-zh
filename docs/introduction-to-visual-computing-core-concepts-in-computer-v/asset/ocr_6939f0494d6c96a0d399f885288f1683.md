# Fun Facts

The largest non-digital photographs in the world are made by stitching smaller images together. The largest single non-digital photograph captured in the world is of a control tower and runways at the US Marine Corps Air Station in El Toro, Orange County, California. It measures 32 feet high and 11 feet wide. It was taken in a decommissioned jet hangar, which was turned into a giant pinhole camera. The film was a 32′ × 111′ piece of white fabric covered in 20 gallons of light-sensitive emulsion. The fabric was exposed to the outside image for 35 minutes. Print washing the image was done with fire hoses connected to two fire hydrants.

The longest photographic negative in the world is 129 feet and was created by Esteban Pastorino Diaz in March, 2015. The negative is of a panorama of major streets in Buenos Aires, Argentina. The images were captured by the slit camera (a camera that captures 1 pixel wide columns at a time while panning from left to right), which was mounted to the roof of a moving car.

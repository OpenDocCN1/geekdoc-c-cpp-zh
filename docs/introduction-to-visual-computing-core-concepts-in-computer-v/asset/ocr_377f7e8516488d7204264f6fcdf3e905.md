# 10.4.2 Multi-Spectral Imaging

Multi-spectral imaging is a technique that addresses the issue of a limited 2D color gamut of any 3-primary color reproduction system. It is evident that the triangular 2D gamut formed by 3-primaries can never capture the entire 2D color gamut of the human eye. In fact, as shown in Figure 10.3, most color reproduction systems do not have highly saturated primaries thereby restricting the 2D gamut of 3-primary systems even further. This is due to a fundamental physical limitation. Saturated primaries are achieved by narrow band primaries which are light inefficient since they filter out most of the light in a scene retaining only a very narrow band. Therefore, saturated primaries require addressing this fundamental trade off between light efficiency and a larger 2D color gamut.

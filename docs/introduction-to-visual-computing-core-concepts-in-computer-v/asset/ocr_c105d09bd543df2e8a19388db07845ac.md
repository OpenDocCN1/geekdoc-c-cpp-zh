However, in some cases hue-preservation may not be applicable. One such example is that of histogram matching. Histogram matching is a technique that allows us to impart the look and feel of one image onto another which is only possible by modification of both hue and luminance. Consider two images $I_h$ and $I_g$ with two different histograms $h$ and $g$ respectively. The goal of histogram matching is to make these two histograms identical. To achieve this, we first find the corresponding cumulative distribution functions $H$ and $G$ respectively. Next, for an input $x_i$ in $I_g$, we map it to an input $x_j$ such that $G(x_i)=H(x_j)$.

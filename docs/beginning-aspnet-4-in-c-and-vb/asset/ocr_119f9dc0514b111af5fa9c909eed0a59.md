# ## 2.3 Lorentz Transformations

We now derive the coordinate transformations between inertial frames moving with constant relative velocity. Following Einstein's postulates, the transformation must be linear and preserve the speed of light.

Consider two inertial frames S and S', with S' moving along the x-axis with speed v. The required transformations are the Lorentz transformations:

```
$$\begin{aligned} x' &= \gamma (x - vt) \\ t' &= \gamma \left( t - \frac{vx}{c^2} \right) \end{aligned}$$
```

where $\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$ is the Lorentz factor. These expressions replace the Galilean transformation at high velocities.

+   - The origin of S' corresponds to the worldline $x = vt$.
+   - The transformation reduces to the identity when $v = 0$.
+   - The spacetime interval $s^2 = c^2 t^2 - x^2$ is invariant.

The inverse transformation is obtained by replacing v with -v, which is consistent with the symmetry of relative motion.

```
$$\begin{aligned} x &= \gamma (x' + vt') \\ t &= \gamma \left( t' + \frac{vx'}{c^2} \right) \end{aligned}$$
```

> > It is often said that the Lorentz transformation mixes space and time; the precise relationship nevertheless preserves a universal speed c.

In the non-relativistic limit, $v \ll c$, the factor $\gamma$ approaches unity and the Lorentz transformations reduce to the familiar Galilean form.

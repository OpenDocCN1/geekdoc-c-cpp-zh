# ## A Survey on Large Language Models from General Purpose to Domain-Specific Applications

This paper provides a comprehensive survey of large language models (LLMs) transitioning from general-purpose systems to domain-specific applications. We systematically review the evolution, key techniques, and emerging trends in adapting LLMs to specialized fields such as healthcare, finance, education, and legal domains.

We categorize domain-specific LLMs into three main paradigms: (1) fine-tuning based approaches, (2) retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and (3) hybrid architectures that integrate external knowledge bases with structured reasoning. Each paradigm offers distinct trade-offs between computational efficiency, knowledge accuracy, and deployment flexibility.

Our analysis covers over 100 representative models and benchmarks across 10 major domains. We highlight critical challenges including catastrophic forgetting during domain adaptation, evaluation inconsistency, and the increasing demand for explainable and accountable AI systems in high-stakes applications.

+   **Key contributions:**
+   
+   - A systematic taxonomy of domain-specific LLM adaptation methods
+   - A comparative analysis of 20+ benchmark datasets across 10 domains
+   - An in-depth discussion of open problems and future research directions

## TAKE-HOME ASSESSMENTS

- **1.** Two major forces determine the rise or fall of a hot air balloon, a lifting force and a drag force due to gravity. A formula for the force \(F\) in pounds on a spherical balloon is given by

  \[
  F=\frac{4}{3}\pi bR^3-4\pi wR^2-c,
  \]

  where \(R\) is the radius in feet of the balloon, \(b\) is the lifting force in pounds per cubic foot, \(w\) is the drag force in pounds per square foot, and \(c\) is the weight in pounds of the equipment including the basket and its occupants.

  - **a.** Assuming that \(w\) and \(c\) are both positive numbers, explain why the last two terms in the formula for \(F\) are preceded by minus signs.
  - **b.** The balloon will rise or fall depending on whether \(F\) is positive or negative. Ignore the weight of the equipment, that is, assume \(c=0\). What is the minimum radius \(R\) of the balloon in terms of \(b\) and \(w\) that will keep it steady (neither rising nor falling)? Show or explain your work.
  - **c.** Suppose that for a certain balloon, \(b=0.1\,\text{lb/ft}^3\) and \(w=0.2\,\text{lb/ft}^2\). What minimum radius of the balloon is required to lift equipment weighing \(c=750\) pounds?

- **2.** Find three real number or nonreal complex number solutions to the equation

  \[
  x^3-2x^2+26x=0.
  \]

- **3.** Determine a possible function rule for the following graph. The scales on both axes are one. The \(x\)-intercepts appear to be \(\frac{1}{2}\) and 2. Explain how the characteristics of the graph help you to determine this function rule.

  ![](img/524030daad8f78e8d291a7f96ed9b8cb_56_0.png)

  Function Rule: ______________________________

### Suggested Solutions

- **1.**

  - **a.** These terms represent forces that pull the balloon downward.
  - **b.** Solve \(\frac{4}{3}\pi bR^3 - 4\pi wR^2 = 4\pi R^2\left(\frac{bR}{3}-w\right)=0\). Since \(4\pi R^2>0\), it follows that \(R=\frac{3w}{b}\).
  - **c.** Solve \(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3(0.1)-4\pi R^2(0.2)-750\ge0\). Finding the \(x\)-intercept of the graph shows that the minimum radius of the balloon is approximately 14.51 ft.

- **2.** Since \(x^3-2x^2+26x=x(x^2-2x+26)\), we know that \(x=0\) is one solution to the equation. The other solutions will be solutions to \(x^2-2x+26=0\). They are \(1\pm5i\). So, the three solutions are \(x=0\), \(x=1+5i\), and \(x=1-5i\).

- **3.** The zeros are at \(x=2\) and \(x=0.5\), so the numerator could be \((x-2)(2x-1)\). The horizontal asymptote is \(y=1\), so the degrees of the numerator and denominator are equal and their leading coefficients are equal. There are vertical asymptotes at \(x=1\) and \(x=-0.5\), so the denominator could be \((x-1)(2x+1)\). The equation \(y=\frac{(x-2)(2x-1)}{(x-1)(2x+1)}\) is a good choice, and it can be verified by checking other points on the graph.

## PROJECT: Volume and Pressure

### Purpose

In this unit and in previous ones, you have used algebraic models in a variety of real-world applications. In this project, you will explore such an application involving volume and pressure, two important variables in physics.

### Directions

As the piston of an engine rotates, it compresses and decompresses the air in the chamber. A compressed air and fuel mixture produces greater forces than an uncompressed mixture when the gas is ignited by the spark plug. To model the compression of a piston, this project will explore the relationship between the volume and pressure for a 60-cubic centimeter (cc) syringe attached to a pressure sensor. There is no outlet for the air inside the syringe.

- **1.** Using a syringe and pressure sensor, gather your own data that will help you determine the relationship between the volume of air in the syringe and the pressure. If you cannot gather your own data, the table on the right gives the pressure readings for a syringe at 5-cc increments. The additional 3 cc of volume is to account for the volume of air in the connector tube. Make a scatterplot of the \((volume, pressure)\) data.

- **2.** What do you expect to happen to the pressure as the volume continues to decrease? What does this tell you about the graph of the \((volume, pressure)\) data?

- **6.** Write a report that includes your complete responses to Items 1–5 above. In your report, include a comparison of your relationship in Item 5 to Boyle’s Law. Also, include a summary in which you discuss how the ideas in each of the above parts fit together.

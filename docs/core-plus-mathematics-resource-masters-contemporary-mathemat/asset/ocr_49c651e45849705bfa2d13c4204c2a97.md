# ## PROJECT

# # Volume and Pressure

# ### Purpose

In this unit and in previous ones, you have used algebraic models in a variety of real-world applications. In this project, you will explore such an application involving volume and pressure, two important variables in physics.

# ### Directions

As the piston of an engine rotates, it compresses and decompresses the air in the chamber. A compressed air and fuel mixture produces greater forces than an uncompressed mixture when the gas is ignited by the spark plug. To model the compression of a piston, this project will explore the relationship between the volume and pressure for a 60-cubic centimeter (cc) syringe attached to a pressure sensor. There is no outlet for the air inside the syringe.

+   1. Using a syringe and pressure sensor, gather your own data that will help you determine the relationship between the volume of air in the syringe and the pressure. If you cannot gather your own data, the table on the right gives the pressure readings for a syringe at 5-cc increments. The additional 3 cc of volume is to account for the volume of air in the connector tube. Make a scatterplot of the *(*volume, pressure*)* data.
+   
+   2. What do you expect to happen to the pressure as the volume continues to decrease? What does this tell you about the graph of the *(*volume, pressure*)* data?

### Abstract

Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is a common method for aligning two sequences. It is widely used in audio alignment, bioinformatics, and other fields. Traditional DTW requires \(O(N^2)\) time and space, which limits its application to large-scale data. This paper proposes a new algorithm that reduces the time complexity to \(O(N \log N)\) while maintaining high accuracy. We evaluate our algorithm on music alignment tasks and demonstrate its superiority over existing methods.

### 1. Introduction

The alignment of time series data is a fundamental problem in many domains. For example, in music information retrieval, aligning a performance to a score enables applications such as score following and automatic accompaniment. The most widely used approach is Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), which computes an optimal alignment between two sequences under certain constraints. However, the classic DTW algorithm has a quadratic time and space complexity, making it impractical for long sequences.

Recent works have attempted to speed up DTW by using approximations or parallel computing. These methods often sacrifice accuracy or rely on specific hardware. In contrast, our algorithm provides an exact solution with significantly lower complexity. The main idea is to use a divide-and-conquer strategy combined with fast distance computation.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the classic DTW algorithm and its variants. Section 3 presents our proposed algorithm in detail. Section 4 describes the experimental setup and results. Section 5 concludes the paper and discusses future work.

### 2. Preliminaries

Let \(X = (x_1, x_2, ..., x_n)\) and \(Y = (y_1, y_2, ..., y_m)\) be two sequences. A warping path \(P = (p_1, p_2, ..., p_k)\) is a sequence of index pairs \(p_t = (i_t, j_t)\) that satisfies the following conditions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{(1)}&\quad p_1 = (1,1),\quad p_k = (n,m) \\
\text{(2)}&\quad 0 \le i_{t+1}-i_t \le 1 \quad \text{and} \quad 0 \le j_{t+1}-j_t \le 1 \\
\text{(3)}&\quad \text{No two consecutive steps are both diagonal.}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Example 7.5.4** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $[16, 8, 4]$ self-dual doubly-even binary code. From Example 7.3.3, its weight distribution is given by $A_0(\mathcal{C}) = A_{16}(\mathcal{C}) = 1$, $A_4(\mathcal{C}) = A_{12}(\mathcal{C}) = 28$, and $A_8(\mathcal{C}) = 198$. In the notation of Theorem 7.5.2, $S = \{4, 8, 12, 16\}$ and $s = 4$. Hence the covering radius of $\mathcal{C}$ is at most 4. Suppose that $\mathbf{v}$ is a coset leader of a coset of $\mathcal{C}$ of weight 4. (Note that we do not know if such a coset leader exists.) Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the $[16, 9, 4]$ linear code generated by $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathbf{v}$; thus $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{C} \cup (\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C})$. $\mathcal{D}$ is an even code, and $\mathcal{D}^\perp \subset \mathcal{C}$. Let $A_i = A_i(\mathcal{D}^\perp)$ and $A_i^\perp = A_i(\mathcal{D})$. Since $\mathcal{D}$ is even, $\mathcal{D}^\perp$ contains the all-one vector. Thus we know $A_0 = A_{16} = 1$ and $A_i = 0$ for all other $i \in$ except possibly $i = 4, 8,$ or $12$ as $\mathcal{D}^\perp \subset \mathcal{C}$. Thus using the first three equations from (7.8), we obtain

+   1 + A_4 + A_8 + A_{12} + 1 = 2^7.
+   4A_4 + 8A_8 + 12A_{12} + 16 = 2^6 \cdot 16,
+   16A_4 + 64A_8 + 144A_{12} + 256 = 2^5 \cdot 16 \cdot 17.

Solving produces $A_4 = A_{12} = 12$ and $A_8 = 102$. As $\mathcal{D}$ contains the all-one vector, $A_i^\perp = A_{16-i}^\perp$. As $\mathcal{D}$ is even with minimum weight 4, $A_1^\perp = 0$ for $i$ odd, and hence we only need $A_4^\perp$, $A_8^\perp$, and $A_{12}^\perp$ to determine the weight distribution of $\mathcal{D}$ completely. Using $(M_3)$, $(K)$, or $(P_1)$ we discover that $A_4^\perp = 44$, $A_8^\perp = 64$, and $A_{12}^\perp = 294$. The weight distribution of the coset $\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}$ is $A_i(\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}) = A_i(\mathcal{D}) - A_i(\mathcal{C})$. So the weight distribution of a coset of weight 4 is uniquely determined to be:

| Weight | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of vectors | 16 | 64 | 64 | 96 | 16 |

Notice that we still do not know if such a coset exists.

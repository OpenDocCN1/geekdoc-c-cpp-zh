# 9.4 The Balance Principle and the shadow

We construct a new code \( C'' \) of length \( n+2 \) by adjoining two components to \( C_0^\perp \) where

\( C'' = \{c00 \mid c \in C_0\} \cup \{c11 \mid c \in C_2\} \cup \{c10 \mid c \in C_1\} \cup \{c01 \mid c \in C_3\} \). (9.6)

Clearly, \( C'' \) is an \( (n+2, (n/2)+1) \) code. By Exercise 517, \( C'' \) is self-dual.

Consider first the case \( n \equiv 2 \pmod 8 \). Let \( c_1 \in C_1 \). Then \( c_1 + c_3 \in C_2 \) by Exercise 9.4.4 implying that \( \text{wt}(c_1 + c_3) \equiv 2 \pmod 4 \). But \( \text{wt}(c_1 + c_3) = \text{wt}(c_1) + \text{wt}(c_3) - 2\text{wt}(c_1 \cap c_3) \equiv \text{wt}(c_1) + \text{wt}(c_3) \pmod 4 \) as \( c_1 C_3 \). Since both \( \text{wt}(c_1) \) and \( \text{wt}(c_3) \) are odd, \( \text{wt}(c_1) \equiv \text{wt}(c_3) \pmod 4 \). As \( C'' \) cannot be Type II since \( n+2 \equiv 4 \pmod 8 \), \( \text{wt}(c_1) = \text{wt}(c_3) \equiv 1 \pmod 4 \), proving (iii) in this case.

Now suppose \( n \equiv 6 \pmod 8 \). Let \( D = \{c00 \mid c \in C_0\} \cup \{c11 \mid c \in C_2\} \), which is doubly even. Clearly, if \( \mathcal{D}' = \mathcal{D} \cup (\mathcal{D} + d) \) where \( d = 00 \cdots 011 \), \( \mathcal{D}' \) is a Type I code. Also as the all-one vector of length \( n \) is in \( C_2 \), the all-one vector of length \( n+2 \) is in \( \mathcal{D} \). By Exercise 526, \( \mathcal{D} \) is contained in only one Type I and two Type II codes of length \( n+2 \), implying that \( C'' \) must be Type II. Hence if \( c \in C_1 \cup C_3 \), then \( \text{wt}(c) \equiv 3 \equiv n/2 \pmod 4 \), proving (iii) in this case also.

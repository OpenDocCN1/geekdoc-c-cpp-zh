
Proof: Let \( G \) be a generator matrix for \( \mathcal{C} \). By Lemma 7.10.8 and its proof, there exists an \( r \times k \) matrix \( A \) of rank \( r \) such that \( U = \{ \mathbf{y} \in \text{col}(G) \mid A\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0} \} \) where \( \text{span}(U) \in \mathcal{F}_{k, k-r} \) and \( d_r(C) = n - m(U) \). Furthermore, \( AG \) generates an \( r \)-dimensional subcode \( \mathcal{D} \) of \( \mathcal{C} \). By Exercise 431 there are \( t = (q^r - 1)/(q - 1) \) (r-1)-dimensional subcodes \( V_1, V_2, \dots, V_t \) of \( \mathcal{D} \). To each \( V_i \) is associated an \( (r - 1) \times k \) matrix \( A_i \) of rank \( r - 1 \) such that \( A_i G \) is a generator matrix of \( V_i \). Letting \( U_i = \{ \mathbf{y} \in \text{col}(G) \mid A_i \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0} \} \), we see that \( \text{span}(U_i) \in \mathcal{F}_{k, k-r+1} \) as in the proof of Lemma 7.10.8. Also \( U \subset U_i \), as a column of \( AG \) is \( \mathbf{0} \) implying that the same column of \( A_i G \) is \( \mathbf{0} \) because \( V_i \) is a subcode of \( \mathcal{D} \). Conversely, if \( U' \subset \text{col}(G) \) where \( U \subseteq U' \) and \( \text{span}(U') \in \mathcal{F}_{k, k-r+1} \), there is an \( (r - 1) \times k \) matrix \( A' \) of rank \( r - 1 \) such that \( U' = \{ \mathbf{y} \in \text{col}(G) \mid A' \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0} \} \). As \( U \subseteq U' \), \( \mathcal{D} = \text{span}(U) \subseteq \text{span}(U') \). So \( A' G \) generates an \( (r - 1) \)-dimensional subspace, say \( V_i \), of \( \mathcal{D} \) and hence \( U' = U_i \).

\( d_{r-1}(C) \le n - m(U_i) \quad \text{for } 1 \le i \le t. \)

and thus

\( d_r(C) - d_{r-1}(C) \ge n - m(U) - [n - m(U_i)] = m(U_i \setminus U) \quad \text{for } 1 \le i \le t. \)

Every column of \( G \) not in \( U \) is in exactly one \( U_i \) because \( U \) together with this column, plus possibly other columns, spans a subspace of \( \mathcal{F}_{k, k-r+1} \) by the previous paragraph. Therefore

\( t(d_r(C) - d_{r-1}(C)) \ge \sum_{i=1}^t m(U_i \setminus U) = n - m(U) = d_r(C). \)

and the lemma follows.

## Exercise 431

Prove that there are \( t = (q^r - 1)/(q - 1) \) (r-1)-dimensional subcodes of an \( r \)-dimensional code over \( \mathbb{F}_q \).

## Theorem 7.10.10 (Generalized Griesmer Bound)

Let \( \mathcal{C} \) be an \( [n, k] \) code over \( \mathbb{F}_q \). Then for \( 1 \le r \le k \),

\( n \ge d_r(C) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^r-1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil. \)

**Proof:** If \( r = k \), the inequality reduces to the obvious assertion \( n \ge d_k(C) \); see Theorem 7.10.1. Now assume that \( r < k \). Without loss of generality, using Theorem 7.10.1(ii), we may assume that \( \mathcal{C} \) has a generator matrix \( G \) of the form

\( G = \begin{bmatrix} G_1 & O \\ G_2 & G_3 \end{bmatrix}. \)

where \( G_1 \) is an \( r \times d_r(C) \) matrix of rank \( r \) and \( G_3 \) is a \( (k-r) \times (n-d_r(C)) \) matrix. The matrix \( [G_2 \ G_3] \) has rank \( k-r \). If \( G_3 \) has rank less than \( k-r \), there is a nonzero codeword \( \mathbf{x} \) in the code generated by \( [G_2 \ G_3] \), which is zero on the last \( n-d_r(C) \) coordinates, contradicting \( d_r(C) < d_{r+1}(C) \) from Theorem 7.10.1. Therefore \( G_3 \) has rank \( k-r \) and generates an \( [n-d_r(C), k-r] \) code \( C_3 \).

Let \( \mathbf{c} = c_1 \cdots c_n \in C \) where \( w((c_{d_r(C)+1} \cdots c_n)) = a > 0 \). The subcode of \( \mathcal{C} \) generated by \( \mathbf{c} \) and the first \( r \) rows of \( G \) is an \( (r+1) \)-dimensional subcode \( \mathcal{C} \) with support size \( a + d_r(C) \). So \( a \ge d_{r+1}(C) - d_r(C) \) implying that the minimum weight of \( C_3 \) is at least \( d_{r+1}(C) - d_r(C) \), by choosing the above \( c \) so that \( wt((c_{d_r(C)+1} \cdots c_n)) \) has minimum weight in \( C_3 \). By the Griesmer Bound applied to \( C_3 \),

\( n - d_r(C) \ge \sum_{i=0}^{k-r-1} \left\lceil \frac{d_{r+1}(C) - d_r(C)}{q^i} \right\rceil = \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{d_{r+1}(C) - d_r(C)}{q^{i-1}} \right\rceil. \)

By Lemma 7.10.9, \( (q^{k-1} - 1)d_r(C) \le (q^{k-1} - q)d_{r+1}(C) \). Using this,

\( d_{r+1}(C) - d_r(C) \ge \left( 1 - \frac{q^{k-1} - q}{q^{k-1} - 1} \right) d_{r+1}(C) = \frac{q-1}{q^{k-1} - 1} d_{r+1}(C). \)

Therefore,

\( n - d_r(C) \ge \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^{i-1}(q^{k-1} - 1)} d_{r+1}(C) \right\rceil. \)

But again using Lemma 7.10.9,

\( n - d_r(C) \ge \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^{i-1}(q^{k-1} - q)} d_r(C) \right\rceil = \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^r - 1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil. \) \(\square\)

The Griesmer Bound is the case \( r = 1 \) of the Generalized Griesmer Bound. We now show that if a code meets the Griesmer Bound, it also meets the Generalized Griesmer Bound for all \( r \), and its weight hierarchy is uniquely determined; the binary case of this result is found in [128]. To simplify the notation we let

\( b_r = d_r(C) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^r - 1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil \text{ for } 1 \le r \le k. \)

The Generalized Griesmer Bound then asserts that \( n \ge b_r \), for \( 1 \le r \le k \). We first show that \( b_{r+1} \ge b_r \), and determine when \( b_{r+1} = b_r \).

## Lemma 7.10.11

Let \( 1 \le r < k \). Then \( b_{r+1} \ge b_r \). Furthermore, \( b_{r+1} = b_r \) if and only if both of the following hold:

- (i) \( d_{r+1}(C) = \lceil (q^{k-1} - 1)d_r(C) / (q^{k-1} - q) \rceil \), and
- (ii) \( \lceil (q - 1)d_{r+1}(C) / (q^i(q^{r+1} - 1)) \rceil = \lceil (q - 1)d_r(C) / (q^{i+1}(q^r - 1)) \rceil \) for \( 1 \le i \le k - r - 1 \).

**Proof:** Lemma 7.10.9 implies that

\( d_{r+1}(C) \ge \left\lceil \frac{q^{k-1} - 1}{q^{k-1} - q} d_r(C) \right\rceil \) (7.19)

and

\( \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^{r+1} - 1)} d_{r+1}(C) \right\rceil \ge \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^{r+1} - q)} d_r(C) \right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^{i+1}(q^r - 1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil \) (7.20)

By (7.19) and (7.20),

```
\begin{aligned}
b_{r+1} &= d_{r+1}(C) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r-1} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^{r+1}-1)} d_{r+1}(C) \right\rceil \\
&\ge \left\lceil \frac{q^{k-1}-1}{q^{k-1}-q} d_r(C) \right\rceil + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r-1} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^{r+1}-q)} d_r(C) \right\rceil \\
&= \left\lceil d_r(C) + \frac{q-1}{q(q^r-1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil + \sum_{i=2}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^r-1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil \\
&= d_r(C) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^r-1)} d_r(C) \right\rceil = b_r.
\end{aligned}
```

Clearly, \( b_{r+1} = b_r \) if and only if equality holds in (7.19) and in (7.20) when \( 1 \le i \le k-r-1 \).

## Theorem 7.10.12

Let \(\mathcal{C}\) be an \([n, k, d]\) code over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) meeting the Griesmer Bound. Then:

- (i) \(\mathcal{C}\) meets the Generalized Griesmer Bound for all \(r\) with \(1 \le r \le k\), and
- (ii) \(d_r(\mathcal{C}) = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} \lceil d/q^i \rceil\) for \(1 \le r \le k\).

**Proof:** By the Generalized Griesmer Bound \(n \ge b_k\); using Lemma 7.10.11,

\(n \ge b_k \ge b_{k-1} \ge \cdots \ge b_1\).

As \(\mathcal{C}\) meets the Griesmer Bound, \(b_1 = n\). Therefore \(b_r = n\) for \(1 \le r \le k\), giving (i).

Note that (ii) holds when \(r=1\), since \(d_1(\mathcal{C}) = d\). Assume \(1 < r \le k\). By Lemma 7.10.11,

\(\left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^r(q^r-1)} d_r(\mathcal{C}) \right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^{r+1}(q^{r-1}-1)} d_{r-1}(\mathcal{C}) \right\rceil,\)

for \(1 < s < k\) and \(1 \le i \le k-s\). Applying this inductively for \(s=r, r-1, \dots, 2\), we have

\(\left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^r(q^r-1)} d_r(\mathcal{C}) \right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q(q-1)} d_1(\mathcal{C}) \right\rceil = \left\lceil \frac{d}{q^{r+1}} \right\rceil,\)

for \(1 \le i \le k-r\), as \(d(\mathcal{C}) = d\) by Theorem 7.10.1. Since \(b_r = n\), as \(\mathcal{C}\) meets the Griesmer Bound, we have

\(\sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{d}{q^i} \right\rceil = n = d_r(\mathcal{C}) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{q-1}{q^i(q^r-1)} d_r(\mathcal{C}) \right\rceil = d_r(\mathcal{C}) + \sum_{i=1}^{k-r} \left\lceil \frac{d}{q^{i+r-1}} \right\rceil.\)

So

\(d_r(\mathcal{C}) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \left\lceil \frac{d}{q^i} \right\rceil - \sum_{i=r}^{k-1} \left\lceil \frac{d}{q^{i+r-1}} \right\rceil = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} \left\lceil \frac{d}{q^i} \right\rceil,\)

and thus (ii) holds.

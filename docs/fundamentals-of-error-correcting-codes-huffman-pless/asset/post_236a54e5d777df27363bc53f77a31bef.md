## Theorem 6.4.12 Let $\mathcal{D}_1$ and $\mathcal{D}_2$ be a pair of odd-like duadic codes of length $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Assume that there is a solution $\gamma \in \mathbb{F}_q$ to (6.11). The following hold: (i) If $\mu_{-1}$ gives the splitting for $\mathcal{D}_1$ and $\mathcal{D}_2$, then $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_1$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_2$ are self-dual. (ii) If $\mathcal{D}_1\mu_{-1} = \mathcal{D}_1$, then $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_1$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_2$ are duals of each other.

Proof: Let $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$ be the pair of even-like duadic codes associated to $\mathcal{D}_1$ and $\mathcal{D}_2$. We have the following two observations: (a) $\widetilde{j(x)}$ is orthogonal to itself. (b) $\widetilde{j(x)}$ is orthogonal to $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}$.

The first holds because as a vector $\widetilde{j(x)}$ is $(1/n, 1/n \dots, 1/n, -\gamma) \in \mathbb{F}_q^{n+1}$. By the choice of $\gamma$, $\widetilde{j(x)}$ is orthogonal to itself. The second holds because $\mathcal{C}_i$ is even-like and the extended coordinate in $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_i$ is always 0.

Assume that $\mu_{-1}$ gives the splitting of $\mathcal{D}_1$ and $\mathcal{D}_2$. Then $\mathcal{C}_1\mu_{-1} = \mathcal{C}_2$. By Theorem 6.4.2, $\mathcal{C}_1 = \mathcal{D}_1^+$; also $\mathcal{C}_i$ is self-orthogonal by Theorem 6.4.1. As the extension of $\mathcal{C}_1$ is trivial, $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_1$ is self-orthogonal. From (a) and (b) the code spanned by $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_1$ and $\widetilde{j(x)}$ is self-orthogonal. By Theorem 6.1.3(v) and (ix), this self-orthogonal code is $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_1$, and so is self-dual. Analogously, $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_2$ is self-dual proving (i).

Now assume that $\mathcal{D}_1\mu_{-1} = \mathcal{D}_1$. Then $\mathcal{C}_1\mu_{-1} = \mathcal{C}_1$. By Theorem 6.4.3, $\mathcal{C}_2 = \mathcal{D}_1^\perp$ and hence $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_2$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_1$ are orthogonal to each other by Theorem 6.1.3(vi). From (a) and (b) the codes spanned by $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_1$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_2$ and by $\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_1$ and $\widetilde{j(x)}$ are orthogonal to each other. By Theorem 6.1.3(v) and (ix), these codes are $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_1$ and $\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_2$ of dimension $(n+1)/2$; hence they are duals of each other.

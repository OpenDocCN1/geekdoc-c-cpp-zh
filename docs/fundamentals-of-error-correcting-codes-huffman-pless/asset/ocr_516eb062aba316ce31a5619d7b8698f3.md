Let $\alpha$ be a primitive element of $\mathbb{F}_{2^m}$. By Theorem 4.4.3 we can view

$$H = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \alpha & \alpha^2 & \cdots & \alpha^{n-1} \\ 1 & \alpha^3 & \alpha^6 & \cdots & \alpha^{3(n-1)} \\ 1 & \alpha^5 & \alpha^{10} & \cdots & \alpha^{5(n-1)} \end{bmatrix}$$

as a parity check matrix for $\mathcal{B}(2, m)$. Let $y(x)$ be a polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]/(x^{2^{m-1}}-1)$. In Section 5.4.1 we defined the syndrome $S_i$ of $y(x)$ as $y(\alpha^i)$. By Exercise 295, we have

$$H \mathbf{y}^T = \begin{bmatrix} S_1 \\ S_3 \\ S_5 \end{bmatrix}, \quad (11.9)$$

where $\mathbf{y}$ is the vector in $\mathbb{F}_2^{2^{m-1}}$ associated to $y(x)$. By Theorem 1.11.5 and Exercise 295(b), there is a one-to-one correspondence between syndromes and cosets. Thus it suffices to show that for any pair of syndromes $S_1$ and $S_3$, there is a vector $\mathbf{y}$ with $\text{wt}(\mathbf{y}) \le 3$ that satisfies (11.9). Let $X_i$ be the location numbers of $\mathbf{y}$; see Section 5.4. Thus, by (5.8), we only need to show that for any $S_1$ and $S_3$ in $\mathbb{F}_{2^m}$, there exists $X_1, X_2,$ and $X_3$ in $\mathbb{F}_{2^m}$ such that

$$S_1 = X_1 + X_2 + X_3, \quad S_3 = X_1^3 + X_2^3 + X_3^3. \quad (11.10)$$

If we let $z_i = X_i + S_1$, then by Exercise 639 we obtain the equivalent system

$$0 = z_1 + z_2 + z_3, \quad s = z_1^3 + z_2^3 + z_3^3, \quad (11.11)$$

where $s = S_1^3 + S_3$. If $s = 0, z_1 = z_2 = z_3 = 0$ is a solution of (11.11). So assume that $s \neq 0$. Substituting $z_3 = z_1 + z_2$ from the first equation in (11.11) into the second equation gives

$$z_1^2 z_2 + z_1 z_2^2 = s.$$

We will find a solution with $z_2 \neq 0$, in which case if we set $z = z_1/z_2$ we must solve

$$z^2 + z + \frac{s}{z_2^3} = 0, \quad (11.12)$$

which is of the form examined in Lemma 11.3.4. Thus by the lemma we must find a $z_2$ such that $\text{Tr}_m(s/z_2^3) = 0$.


Recall that an $[n, k]$ generalized Reed-Solomon code over $\mathbb{F}_q$ is defined by

$$GRS_k(\gamma, v) = \{(v_0 f(\gamma_0), v_1 f(\gamma_1), \dots, v_{n-1} f(\gamma_{n-1})) \mid f \in \mathcal{P}_k\},$$

where $\gamma = (\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{n-1})$ is an $n$-tuple of distinct elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$, $v = (v_0, v_1, \dots, v_{n-1})$ is an $n$-tuple of nonzero elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$, and $\mathcal{P}_k$ is the set of polynomials in $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$ of degree $k-1$ or less including the zero polynomial. Suppose that $c = c_0 c_1 \dots c_{n-1} \in GRS_k(\gamma, v)$ is sent and $y' = y'_0 y'_1 \dots y'_{n-1} = c + e$ is received. Then there is a unique $f \in \mathcal{P}_k$ such that $c_i = v_i f(\gamma_i)$ for $0 \le i \le n-1$. We can find $e$ if we can determine the polynomial $f$. Let $\mathcal{A} = \{(\gamma_0, y_0), (\gamma_1, y_1), \dots, (\gamma_{n-1}, y_{n-1})\}$ where $y_i = y'_i / v_i$. Suppose for a moment that no errors occurred in the transmission of $c$. Then $y_i = c_i / v_i = f(\gamma_i)$ for $0 \le i \le n-1$. In particular, all points of $\mathcal{A}$ lie on the polynomial $p(x, y) = y - f(x)$. Now suppose that errors occur. Varying slightly our terminology from earlier, define an _error locator polynomial_ $\Lambda(x, y)$ to be any polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ such that $\Lambda(\gamma_i, y_i) = 0$ for all $i$ such that $y_i \neq c_i / v_i$. Since $y_i - f(\gamma_i) = 0$ if $y_i = c_i / v_i$, all points of $\mathcal{A}$ lie on the polynomial $p(x, y) = \Lambda(x, y)(y - f(x))$. The basic idea of the Sudan-Guruswami Algorithm is to find a polynomial $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ where each element of $\mathcal{A}$ is a root with a certain multiplicity and then find the factors of that polynomial of the form $y - f(x)$. Further restrictions on $p(x, y)$ are imposed to guarantee the error-correcting capability of the algorithm.

### The Sudan-Guruswami Decoding Algorithm for the $[n, k, n-k+1]$ code $GRS_k(\gamma, v)$

- I. Fix a positive integer $m$. Pick $\delta$ to be the smallest positive integer to satisfy
  
  $$\frac{nm(m+1)}{2} < N_{k-1}(\delta). \tag{5.21}$$

  Recall that $N_{k-1}(\delta)$ is the number of monomials $x^i y^j$ whose $(1, k-1)$-weighted degree is $\delta$ or less. Set

  $$t = \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{m} \right\rfloor + 1.$$

- II. Construct a nonzero polynomial $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ such that each element of $\mathcal{A}$ is a root of $p(x, y)$ of multiplicity at least $m$ and $p(x, y)$ has $(1, k-1)$-weighted degree at most $\delta$.

- III. Find all factors of $p(x, y)$ of the form $y - f(x)$ where $f(x) \in \mathcal{P}_k$ and $f(\gamma_i) = y_i$ for at least $t$ values of $i$. For each such $f$ produce the corresponding codeword in $GRS_k(\gamma, v)$.

We must verify that this algorithm works and give a bound on the number of errors that it can correct. The following three lemmas are needed.

### Lemma 5.4.15
Let $(\alpha, \beta) \in \mathbb{F}_q^2$ be a root of $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ of multiplicity $m$ or more. If $f(x)$ is a polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$ such that $f(\alpha) = \beta$, then $g(x) = p(x, f(x)) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x]$ is divisible by $(x - \alpha)^m$.

**Proof:** Let $f_1(x) = f(x + \alpha) - \beta$. By our hypothesis, $f_1(0) = 0$, and so $f_1(x) = x f_2(x)$ for some polynomial $f_2(x) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x]$. Define $g_1(x) = p(x + \alpha, f_1(x) + \beta)$. Since $(\alpha, \beta)$ is a root of $p(x, y)$ of multiplicity $m$ or more, $p(x + \alpha, y + \beta)$ has no monomial of degree less than $m$. Setting $y = f_1(x) = x f_2(x)$ in $p(x + \alpha, y + \beta)$ shows that $g_1(x)$ is divisible by $x^m$, which implies that $g_1(x - \alpha)$ is divisible by $(x - \alpha)^m$. However, $g_1(x - \alpha) = p(x, f_1(x - \alpha) + \beta) = p(x, f(x)) = g(x)$, showing that $g(x)$ is divisible by $(x - \alpha)^m$.

### Lemma 5.4.16
Fix positive integers $m, t, \text{and } \delta$ such that $mt > \delta$. Let $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ be a polynomial such that $(\gamma_i, y_i)$ is a root of $p(x, y)$ of multiplicity at least $m$ for $i \le n - 1$. Furthermore, assume that $p(x, y)$ has $(1, k - 1)$-weighted degree at most $\delta$. Let $f(x) \in \mathcal{P}_k$ where $y_i = f(\gamma_i)$ for at least $t$ values of $i$ with $0 \le i \le n - 1$. Then $y - f(x)$ divides $p(x, y)$.

**Proof:** Let $g(x) = p(x, f(x))$. As $p(x, y)$ has $(1, k - 1)$-weighted degree at most $\delta$, $g(x)$ is either the zero polynomial or a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $\delta$. Assume $g(x)$ is nonzero. Let $S = \{i \mid 0 \le i \le n - 1, f(\gamma_i) = y_i\}$. By Lemma 5.4.15, $(x - \gamma_i)^m$ divides $g(x)$ for $i \in S$. As the $\gamma_i$s are distinct, $h(x) = \prod_{i \in S} (x - \gamma_i)^m$ divides $g(x)$. Since $h(x)$ has degree at least $mt > \delta$ and $\delta$ is the maximum degree of $g(x)$, we have a contradiction if $g(x)$ is nonzero. Thus $g(x)$ is the zero polynomial, which implies that $y = f(x)$ is a root of $p(x, y)$ viewed as a polynomial in $y$ over the field of rational functions in $x$. By the Division Algorithm, $y - f(x)$ is a factor of this polynomial.

### Lemma 5.4.17
Let $p(x, y) = \sum_j \sum_t p_{j,t} x^j y^t \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$. Suppose that $(\alpha, \beta) \in \mathbb{F}_q^2$ and that $p'(x, y) = \sum_a \sum_b p'_{a,b} x^a y^b = p(x + \alpha, y + \beta)$. Then

$$p'_{a,b} = \sum_{j \ge a} \sum_{t \ge b} \binom{j}{a} \binom{t}{b} \alpha^{j-a} \beta^{t-b} p_{j,t}.$$

**Proof:** We have

$$p'(x, y) = \sum_j \sum_t p_{j,t} (x + \alpha)^j (y + \beta)^t$$

$$= \sum_j \sum_t p_{j,t} \sum_{a=0}^j \binom{j}{a} x^a \alpha^{j-a} \sum_{b=0}^t \binom{t}{b} y^b \beta^{t-b}.$$

Clearly, the coefficient $p'_{a,b}$ of $x^a y^b$ is as claimed.

We are now in a position to verify the Sudan-Guruswami Algorithm and give the error bound for which the algorithm is valid.

### Theorem 5.4.18
The Sudan-Guruswami Decoding Algorithm applied to the $[n, k, n - k + 1]$ code $GRS_k(\gamma, v)$ will produce all codewords within Hamming distance $e$ or less of a received vector where $e = n - \lfloor \delta/m \rfloor - 1$.

**Proof:** We first must verify that the polynomial $p(x, y)$ from Step II actually exists. For $p(x, y)$ to exist, $p(x + \gamma_i, y + y_i)$ must have no terms of degree less than $m$ for $0 \le i \le n - 1$. By Lemma 5.4.17, this is accomplished if for each $i$ with $0 \le i \le n - 1$,

$$\sum_{j \ge a} \sum_{t \ge b} \binom{j}{a} \binom{t}{b} \gamma_i^{j-a} y_i^{t-b} p_{j,t} = 0 \quad \text{for all } a \ge 0, b \ge 0 \text{ with } a + b < m. \quad (5.22)$$

For each $i$, there are $(m(m + 1))/2$ equations in (5.22) since the set $\{(a, b) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 \mid a \ge 0, b \ge 0, a + b < m\}$ has size $(m(m + 1))/2$; hence there are a total of $(nm(m + 1))/2$ homogeneous linear equations in the unknown coefficients $p_{j,t}$. Since we wish to produce a nontrivial polynomial of $(1, k - 1)$-weighted degree at most $\delta$, there are a total of $N_{k-1}(\delta)$ unknown coefficients $p_{j,t}$ in this system of $(nm(m + 1))/2$ homogeneous linear equations. As there are fewer equations than unknowns by (5.21), a nontrivial solution exists and Step II can be completed. By our choice of $t$ in Step I, $mt > \delta$. If $f(x) \in \mathcal{P}_k$ is a polynomial with $f(\gamma_i) = y_i$ for at least $t$ values of $i$, by Lemma 5.4.16, $y - f(x)$ is a factor of $p(x, y)$. Thus Step III of the algorithm will produce all codewords at distance $e = n - t = n - \lfloor \delta/m \rfloor - 1$ or less from the received vector.

As Step I requires computation of $N_{k-1}(\delta)$, the next lemma proves useful.

### Lemma 5.4.19
Let $s$ and $\delta$ be positive integers. Then $N_s(\delta) = \left( \delta + 1 - \frac{s}{2} \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{s} \right\rfloor \right) \left( \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{s} \right\rfloor + 1 \right) \ge \frac{\delta(\delta + 2)}{2s}$.

**Proof:** By definition, $N_s(\delta) = \sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor \delta/s \rfloor} (\delta + 1 - is) = (\delta + 1) \left( \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{s} \right\rfloor + 1 \right) - \frac{s}{2} \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{s} \right\rfloor \left( \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{s} \right\rfloor + 1 \right) \ge \left( \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{s} \right\rfloor + 1 \right) \left( \delta + 1 - \frac{\delta}{2} \right) \ge \frac{\delta}{s} \cdot \frac{\delta + 2}{2}$.

The result follows.

### Example 5.4.20
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $[15, 6, 10]$ Reed-Solomon code over $\mathbb{F}_{16}$. The Peterson-Gorenstein-Zierler Decoding Algorithm can correct up to four errors. If we choose $m = 2$ in the Sudan-Guruswami Decoding Algorithm, then $(nm(m + 1))/2 = 45$ and the smallest value of $\delta$ for which $45 < N_s(\delta)$ is $\delta = 18$, in which case $N_s(18) = 46$ by Lemma 5.4.19. Then $t = \lfloor \delta/m \rfloor + 1 = 10$ and by Theorem 5.4.18, the Sudan-Guruswami Algorithm can correct $15 - 10 = 5$ errors.

where supp(x) denotes the support of x. We count the number of elements in X in two different ways. If tw(x) = j with x \in C^\perp, there are \binom{n-j}{v-j} sets I of size v \ge j that contain supp(x). Thus |X| = \sum_{|I|=v} A_j^\perp(I), and the result follows by equating the two counts.

In our second lemma, we consider again the q^k \times n matrix M whose rows are the codewords of C, and we let M(I) denote the q^k \times |I| submatrix of M consisting of the columns of M indexed by I. Note that each row of M(I) is a codeword of C^l; however, each codeword of C^l may be repeated several times.

# **Lemma 7.1.2** Each codeword of C^\perp occurs exactly q^{k-k_l} times as a row of M(I), where k_l is the dimension of C^l.

**Proof:** The map f : C \to C^T given by f(x) = x^T (i.e. the puncturing map) is linear and surjective. So its kernel has dimension k - k_l and the result follows.

The verification of the n + 1 equations of (7.3) is completed by counting the v-tuples of zeros in the rows of M for each 0 \le v \le n in two different ways.

# **Theorem 7.1.13** The equations of (7.3) are satisfied by the weight distributions of an [n, k] code C over F_q and its dual.

**Proof:** Let N_v be the number of v-tuples of zeros (not necessarily consecutive) in the rows of M for 0 \le v \le n. Clearly, a row of M of weight j has \binom{n-j}{v} v-tuples of zeros. Thus

N_v = \sum_{j=0}^{n-v} \binom{n-j}{v} A_j.

which is the left-hand side of (7.3). By Lemma 7.1.1, the right-hand side of (7.3) is

q^{k-v} \sum_{j=0}^{v} \binom{n-j}{n-v} A_j^\perp = q^{k-v} \sum_{j=0}^{v} \sum_{|I|=v} A_j^\perp(I) = q^{k-v} \sum_{|I|=v} \sum_{j=0}^{v} A_j^\perp(I).

But \sum_{j=0}^{v} A_j^\perp(I) = q^{k-k_l}, as (C^\perp)^T is a [v, v-k_l] code. Therefore

q^{k-v} \sum_{j=0}^{v} \binom{n-j}{n-v} A_j^\perp = q^{k-v} \sum_{|I|=v} q^{v-k_l} = \sum_{|I|=v} q^{k-k_l}.

By Lemma 7.1.2, q^{k-k_l} is the number of zero rows of M(I), which is the number of |I|-tuples of zeros in the coordinate positions I in M. Thus \sum_{|I|=v} q^{k-k_l} = N_v. Equating the two counts for N_v completes the proof.

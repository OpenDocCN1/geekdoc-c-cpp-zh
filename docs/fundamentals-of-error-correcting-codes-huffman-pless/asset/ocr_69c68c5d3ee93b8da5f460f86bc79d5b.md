(v) If $d$ is odd, then

$$A_2(n, d) \le \frac{2^n}{\sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i} + \frac{\binom{n}{t} \left( \frac{n-t}{t+1} - \frac{n-t}{t+1} \right)}{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{t+1} \right\rfloor}}.$$ (2.13)

**Proof:** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $(n, M, d)$ code over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Notice that $t$ is the packing radius of $\mathcal{C}$; $d = 2t + 1$ if $d$ is odd and $d = 2t + 2$ if $d$ is even. So the spheres of radius $t$ centered at codewords are disjoint. The vectors in these spheres are precisely the vectors in $\mathbb{F}_q^n$ that are distance $t$ or less from $\mathcal{C}$. We will count these vectors together with those vectors at distance $t + 1$ from $\mathcal{C}$ and use this count to obtain our bounds. To that end let $\mathcal{N}$ be the vectors at distance $t + 1$ from $\mathcal{C}$; so $\mathcal{N} = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n \mid d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \}$. Let $|\mathcal{N}|$ denote the size of $\mathcal{N}$. Therefore,

$$M \sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i} (q-1)^i + |\mathcal{N}| \le q^n.$$ (2.14)

as the summation on the left-hand side counts the vectors in the spheres of radius $t$ centered at codewords; see (1.11). Our bounds will emerge after we obtain a lower bound on $|\mathcal{N}|$.

Let $\mathcal{X} = \{ (\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) \in \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{N} \mid d(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \}$. To get the lower bound on $|\mathcal{N}|$ we obtain lower and upper estimates for $|\mathcal{X}|$.

We first obtain a lower estimate on $|\mathcal{X}|$. Let $\mathcal{X}_c = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N} \mid (\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) \in \mathcal{X} \}$. Then

$$|\mathcal{X}| = \sum_{\mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}} |\mathcal{X}_c|.$$ (2.15)

Fix $\mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}$. Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n$ be a vector at distance $t + 1$ from $\mathbf{c}$ so that $wt(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) = t + 1$. There are exactly

$$\binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1}$$

such vectors $\mathbf{x}$ because they are obtained by freely changing any $t + 1$ coordinates of $\mathbf{c}$. Some of these lie in $\mathcal{X}_c$ and some do not. Because $wt(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) = t + 1$, $d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) \le t + 1$. Let $\mathbf{c}' \in \mathcal{C}$ with $\mathbf{c}' \neq \mathbf{c}$. Then by the triangle inequality of Theorem 1.4.1, $d(\mathbf{c}', \mathbf{c}) = wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}) \le wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) + wt(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) = wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) + t + 1$, implying

$$d - t - 1 \le wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}).$$ (2.16)

If $d = 2t + 2$, $wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) \ge t + 1$ yielding $d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1$ since $\mathbf{c}' \in \mathcal{C}$ was arbitrary and we saw previously that $d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) \le t + 1$. Therefore all such $\mathbf{x}$ lie in $\mathcal{X}_c$ giving

$$|\mathcal{X}_c| = \binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1},$$

hence

$$|\mathcal{X}| = M \binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1} \quad \text{if } d = 2t + 2.$$ (2.17)

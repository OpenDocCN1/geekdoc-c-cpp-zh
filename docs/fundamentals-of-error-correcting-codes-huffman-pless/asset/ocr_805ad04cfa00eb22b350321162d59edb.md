# Exercise 413 Show that for codes over $\mathbb{F}_4$, $W_C(x, y) = W_C^L(y, x, x, x)$. $\diamond$

The Lee weight enumerator is sometimes used for codes over fields of characteristic $p \neq 2$. For such fields, let $\mathbb{F}_q = \{a_0 = 0, a_1, \dots, a_{r-1}, -a_1, \dots, -a_r\}$ where $r = (q-1)/2$. The Lee weight enumerator of $\mathcal{C}$ is the polynomial

```
$W_C^L(x_0, x_1, \dots, x_{q-1}) = \sum_{c \in C} x_0^{k(c)} \prod_{i=1}^r x_{a_i}^{(c)_i + (-c)_i}$.
```

The Lee weight enumerator reduces the number of variables in the complete weight enumerator essentially by half. If $q > 3$, then $W_C(x_0, x_0) = W_C^L(x_0, x_1)$. There is also a relationship between the Lee weight enumerator of $\mathcal{C}$ and the Lee weight enumerator of $C^\perp$, which is obtained by equating $x_a$ and $x_{-a}$ in the MacWilliams identity for the complete weight enumerator.

MacWilliams identities are established for other families of codes and other types of weight enumerators in [291].

For (i), \( a \in N_p \), then \( (Q(x) + 2N(x))\mu_a = N(x) + 2Q(x) \). Theorem 4.3.13(i) (whose proof is still valid for cyclic codes over \( \mathbb{Z}_4 \)) implies that \( \mathcal{D}_1\mu_a = \mathcal{D}_2 \). Similarly, if \( a \in Q_p \), then \( (Q(x) + 2N(x))\mu_a = Q(x) + 2N(x) \) and \( (N(x) + 2Q(x))\mu_a = N(x) + 2Q(x) \), implying \( \mathcal{D}_1\mu_a = \mathcal{D}_1 \). The parts of (i) involving \( C_i \) are similar.

Since \( p = -1 \pmod 8 \), \( \bar{j}(x) = 3 \sum_{i=0}^{p-1} x^i = 3 + 3Q(x) + 3N(x) \). Thus \( (Q(x) + 2N(x))(N(x) + 2Q(x)) = (Q(x) + 2N(x))(\bar{j}(x) + 1 - (Q(x) + 2N(x))) = (Q(x) + 2N(x))(1 - Q(x) - 2N(x)) + Q(x) + 2N(x)) = (Q(x) + 2N(x))(1 - Q(x) - 2N(x)) + Q(x) + 2N(x)) = ... \) proving part (ii).

Using \( (Q(x) + 2N(x))(N(x) + 2Q(x)) = \bar{j}(x) \), for (iii) we have \( (1 - N(x) + 2Q(x)) \times (1 - Q(x) + 2N(x)) = 1 - N(x) + 2Q(x) - Q(x) + N(x)(2N(x) + N(x) + 2Q(x))(Q(x) + 2N(x)) = 1 + N(x) + Q(x) + \bar{j}(x) = 0 \) proving that \( C_1 \cap C_2 = \{0\} \). Since \( (1 - N(x) + 2Q(x))(1 - Q(x) + 2N(x)) = 0 \), \( C_1 + C_2 \) has generating idempotent \( 1 - N(x) + 2Q(x) + 1 - Q(x) + 2N(x) = 2 + N(x) + Q(x) = 1 - \bar{j}(x) = 1 - \bar{j}(x)\mu_{-1} \) as \( \bar{j}(x)\mu_{-1} = \bar{j}(x) \). By Lemma 12.3.23, \( C_1 + C_2 = \langle \bar{j}(x) \rangle^+ \), completing (iii).

For (iv), we observe that \( \mathcal{D}_1 = \mathcal{D}_2|_1 = |\mathcal{D}_1|/|\mathcal{D}_2| \cap \mathcal{D}_2| \). By (i), \( |\mathcal{D}_1| = |\mathcal{D}_2| \), and by (ii), \( |\mathcal{D}_1| = 4^p \) and \( |\mathcal{D}_1 \cap \mathcal{D}_2| = 4 \). Thus \( \mathcal{D}_1 \) and \( \mathcal{D}_2 \) have size \( 4^{p+1/2} \); this is also their type as each has an idempotent generator (as opposed to a generator that is the sum of an idempotent and twice an idempotent; see Theorem 12.3.22 and Exercise 724). The remainder of (iv) is similar using (i) and (iii).

By (ii), \( \bar{j}(x) \in \mathcal{D}_2 \) implying that \( (N(x) + 2Q(x))\bar{j}(x) = \bar{j}(x) \) as \( N(x) + 2Q(x) \) is the multiplicative identity of \( \mathcal{D}_2 \). By Lemma 12.3.23, the generating idempotent for \( C_1 + \langle \bar{j}(x) \rangle \) is \( 1 - N(x) + 2Q(x) + \bar{j}(x) - (1 - N(x) + 2Q(x))\bar{j}(x) = 1 - N(x) + 2Q(x) + \bar{j}(x) - (\bar{j}(x) - \bar{j}(x)) = Q(x) + 2N(x) \), proving that \( C_1 + \langle \bar{j}(x) \rangle = \mathcal{D}_1 \). Similarly, \( C_2 + \langle \bar{j}(x) \rangle = \mathcal{D}_2 \), completing (v).

Finally, by Lemma 12.3.23, the generating idempotent for \( C_1^+ \) is \( 1 - (1 - N(x) + 2Q(x))\mu_{-1} = N(x)\mu_{-1} + 2Q(x)\mu_{-1} \). By Lemma 6.2.4, \( -1 \in N_p \) for \( p \equiv -1 \pmod 8 \). Hence \( N(x)\mu_{-1} = Q(x) \) and \( Q(x)\mu_{-1} = N(x) \). Therefore the generating idempotent for \( C_1^+ \) is \( Q(x) + 2N(x) \), verifying that \( C_1^+ = \mathcal{D}_2 \). This also implies \( C_2^+ \) is self-orthogonal as \( C_2 \subseteq \mathcal{D}_1 \) from (v), completing (vi).

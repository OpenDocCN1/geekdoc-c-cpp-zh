# ## 3.6 Field automorphisms

The field automorphisms of $\mathbb{F}_q$ form a group under function composition. We can describe this group completely.

Recall that an automorphism $\sigma$ of $\mathbb{F}_q$ is a bijection $\sigma : \mathbb{F}_q \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_q$ such that $\sigma(\alpha + \beta) = \sigma(\alpha) + \sigma(\beta)$ and $\sigma(\alpha\beta) = \sigma(\alpha)\sigma(\beta)$ for all $\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{F}_q$. Define $\sigma_p : \mathbb{F}_q \rightarrow \mathbb{F}_q$ by $\sigma_p(\alpha) = \alpha^p$ for all $\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_q$.

1 In Section 1.7, where we used field automorphisms to define equivalence, the field automorphism $\sigma$ acted on the right $x \mapsto (x)\sigma$, because the nominal maps act most naturally on the right. Here we have $\sigma$ act on the left $x \mapsto \sigma(x)$, because it is probably more natural to the reader. They are interchangeable because two field automorphisms $\sigma$ and $\tau$ commute by Theorem 3.6.1, implying that the right action $((x)\sigma)\tau = ((x)\tau)\sigma$ agrees with the left action $(\sigma(\tau(x))) = (\tau(\sigma(x)))$.

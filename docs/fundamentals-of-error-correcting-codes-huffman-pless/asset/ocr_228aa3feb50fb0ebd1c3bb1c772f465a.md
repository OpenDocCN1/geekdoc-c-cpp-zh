# **Theorem 4.3.13** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a cyclic code of length $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with generating idempotent $e(x)$. Let $a$ be an integer with $\gcd(a, n) = 1$. Then: (i) $\mathcal{C}_{\mu_a} = \{e(x)\mu_a\}$ and $e(x)\mu_a$ is the generating idempotent of the cyclic code $\mathcal{C}_{\mu_a}$, and (ii) $e(x)\mu_a = e(x)$ and $\mu_a \in \text{PAut}(\mathcal{C})$.

Proof: Using Theorem 4.3.12(iii), $\mathcal{C}_{\mu_a} = \{(e(x)f(x))\mu_a \mid f(x) \in \mathcal{R}_n\} = \{e(x)\mu_a \times f(x)\mu_a \mid f(x)\mu_a \in \mathcal{R}_n\} = \{e(x)\mu_a h(x) \mid h(x) \in \mathcal{R}_n\} = (e(x)\mu_a)$ as $\mu_a$ is an automorphism of $\mathcal{R}_n$ by Theorem 4.3.12(iv). Hence $\mathcal{C}_{\mu_a}$ is cyclic and has generating idempotent $e(x)\mu_a$ by Theorem 4.3.12(v), proving (i). If we show that $e(x)\mu_{\bar{q}} = e(x)$, then by part (i), $\mathcal{C}_{\mu_{\bar{q}}} = \mathcal{C}$ and so $\mu_{\bar{q}} \in \text{PAut}(\mathcal{C})$. By Theorem 4.3.8(v), $e(x) = \sum_{i \in T} \bar{e}(x)$ for some set $T$. By Theorem 4.3.12(ii), $e(x)\mu_q = e(x)$ if $\bar{e}(x)\mu_q = \bar{e}(x)$ for all $i$. But $\bar{e}(x)\mu_q = \bar{e}(x^q) = \bar{e}(x)^q$ by Theorem 3.7.4, the latter certainly being a nonzero element of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q(x)$. But by Theorem 4.3.12(v), $\bar{e}(x)\mu_q$ is also an idempotent of $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_q(x)$. Hence $\bar{e}(x)\mu_q = \bar{e}(x)$ by Theorem 4.3.8(v).

# **Corollary 4.3.14** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a cyclic code of length $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the group of order $n$ generated by the cyclic shift $i \to i + 1 \pmod n$. Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the group of order $\text{ord}_n(q)$ generated by the multiplier $\mu_q$. Then the group $\mathcal{G}$ of order $n \cdot \text{ord}_n(q)$ generated by $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{B}$ is a subgroup of $\text{PAut}(\mathcal{C})$.

Proof: The corollary follows from the structure of the normalizer of $\mathcal{A}$ in the symmetric group $\text{Sym}_n$ and Theorem 4.3.13(ii). In fact, $\mathcal{G}$ is the semidirect product of $\mathcal{A}$ extended by $\mathcal{B}$.

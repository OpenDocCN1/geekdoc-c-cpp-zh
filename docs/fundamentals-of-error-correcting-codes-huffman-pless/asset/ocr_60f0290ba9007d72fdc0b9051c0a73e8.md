The following, where the notation is given in Lemma 5.4.2: (a) Show that if \(\mu > v\), \(S_i = \sum_{j=1}^\mu E_j X_j^i\) for \(1 \le i \le 2t\). (b) Show that \(M_\mu = A_\mu B_\mu A_\mu^T\). \(\blacklozenge\)

To execute the second step of our algorithm, we attempt to guess the number \(v\) of errors. Call our guess \(\mu\) and begin with \(\mu = t\), which is the largest that \(v\) could be. The coefficient matrix of the linear system (5.11) that we are attempting to solve is \(M_\mu = M_\mu\) in Lemma 5.4.2. If \(M_\mu\) is singular, we reduce our guess \(\mu\) to \(\mu = t - 1\) and decide whether or not \(M_\mu = M_{t-1}\) is singular. As long as we obtain a singular matrix, we continue to reduce our guess \(\mu\) of the number of errors by one until some \(M_\mu\) is nonsingular. With \(v = \mu\), solve (5.11) and thereby determine \(\sigma(x)\).

The third step is then to find the roots of \(\sigma(x)\) and invert them to determine the error location numbers. This is usually done by exhaustive search checking \(\sigma(\alpha^i)\) for \(0 \le i < n\). The fourth step is to plug these numbers into (5.8) and solve this linear system for the error magnitudes \(E_j\). In fact we only need to consider the first \(v\) equations in (5.8) for the following reason. The coefficient matrix of the first \(v\) equations has determinant

\(\det \begin{bmatrix} X_1 & X_2 & \cdots & X_v \\ X_1^2 & X_2^2 & \cdots & X_v^2 \\ \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ X_1^v & X_2^v & \cdots & X_v^v \end{bmatrix} = X_1 X_2 \cdots X_v \det \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1 \\ X_1 & X_2 & \cdots & X_v \\ \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ X_1^{v-1} & X_2^{v-1} & \cdots & X_v^{v-1} \end{bmatrix} .\)

The right-hand side determinant is the determinant of a Vandermonde matrix; the latter is nonzero as the \(X/s\) are distinct.

The Peterson-Gorenstein-Zierler Decoding Algorithm for BCH codes is therefore the following:

+   I. Compute the syndromes \(S_i = y(\alpha^i)\) for \(1 \le i \le 2t\).
+   II. In the order \(\mu = t, \mu = t - 1, \dots\) decide if \(M_\mu\) is singular, stopping at the first value of \(\mu\) where \(M_\mu\) is nonsingular. Set \(v = \mu\) and solve (5.11) to determine \(\sigma(x)\).
+   III. Find the roots of \(\sigma(x)\) by computing \(\sigma(\alpha^i)\) for \(0 \le i < n\). Invert the roots to get the error location numbers \(X_j\).
+   IV. Solve the first \(v\) equations of (5.8) to obtain the error magnitudes \(E_j\).


The disadvantage of Construction A is that the largest minimum norm of a resulting lattice is 2. There are other constructions of lattices from codes that produce lattices of great interest. To conclude this section, we present one construction, due to J. H. Conway [60, Chapter 10], of the 24-dimensional Leech lattice \(\Lambda_{24}\) using the [24, 12, 8] extended binary Golay code. This lattice was discovered in 1965 by J. Leech [188]. The lattice \(\Lambda_{24}\) is known to have the highest kissing number (196 560) of any 24-dimensional lattice. Furthermore, \(\Lambda_{24}\) is the unique Type II lattice of dimension 24 with minimum norm 4, the highest norm possible for Type II lattices in \(\mathbb{R}^{24}\).

We need some notation. Label the coordinates \(\Omega = \{1, 2, \dots, 24\}\) and let \(\{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_{24}\}\) be the standard orthonormal basis of coordinate vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^{24}\). If \(S \subseteq \Omega\), let \(e_S = \sum_{i \in S} e_i\). Fix some representation of the [24, 12, 8] extended binary Golay code \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\). A subset \(S\) of \(\Omega\) is called a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set if it is the support of a codeword in \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\). Recall from Example 8.3.2 that the subsets of \(\Omega\) of size 4 are called tetrads, and that each tetrad \(T\) uniquely determines five other tetrads, all six being pairwise disjoint, so that the union of any two tetrads is a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set of size 8; these six tetrads are called the sextet determined by \(T\). We let \(\mathcal{T}\) denote the set of all tetrads and \(\mathcal{O}\) denote the set of 759 octads, that is, the set of all \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-sets of size 8. Recall from either Example 8.3.2 or the proof of the uniqueness of \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\) in Section 10.1 that the codewords of weight 8 in \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\) span \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\).

We first construct an intermediate lattice \(\Gamma_0\), then add one more spanning vector to form a lattice \(\Gamma_1\), and finally rescale to obtain \(\Lambda_{24}\). Define \(\Gamma_0\) to be the 24-dimensional lattice spanned by \(\{2e_S \mid S \in \mathcal{O}\}\).

### Lemma 10.6.6 The following hold in the lattice \(\Gamma_0\):

- (i) Every lattice point has even integer components.
- (ii) \(\Gamma_0\) contains all vectors \(4e_T\), where \(T \in \mathcal{T}\).
- (iii) \(\Gamma_0\) contains \(4e_i - 4e_j\) for all \(i\) and \(j\) in \(\Omega\).
- (iv) A vector in \(\mathbb{R}^{24}\) with all even integer components is in \(\Gamma_0\) if and only if the sum of its components is a multiple of 16 and the set of coordinates in the vector where the components are not divisible by 4 forms a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set.

Proof: Part (i) is immediate as \(2e_S\) with \(S \in \mathcal{O}\) has all even integer components. Let \(T = T_0, T_1, \dots, T_5\) be the sextet determined by the tetrad \(T\). Since \(4e_T = 2e_{T \cup T_1} + 2e_{T \cup T_2} - 2e_{T \cup T_3}\), we have (ii). Now let \(T = \{t, x, y, z\} \in \mathcal{T}\) and \(T' = \{j, x, y, z\} \in \mathcal{T}\). Then \(4e_T - 4e_{T'} = 4e_t - 4e_j\), showing (iii).

Let \(\mathbf{v} \in \Gamma_0\). Since a spanning vector \(2e_S\) for \(S \in \mathcal{O}\) has its sum of components equaling 16, the sum of the components of \(\mathbf{v}\) is a multiple of 16. If we take \(\mathbf{v}\), divide its components by 2 and reduce modulo 2, we obtain a binary combination of some of the \(e_S\) where \(S \in \mathcal{O}\), which can be viewed as codewords in \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\) and hence must sum to \(e_C\) for some \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set \(C\); but \(C\) is precisely the set of coordinates where the components of \(\mathbf{v}\) are not divisible by 4. Conversely, let \(\mathbf{v}\) be a vector in \(\mathbb{R}^{24}\) with all even integer components such that the sum of its components is a multiple of 16 and the set of coordinates in \(\mathbf{v}\) where the components are not divisible by 4 forms a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set \(C\). Let \(\mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{G}_{24}\) have support \(C\); then \(\mathbf{c} = e_{c_1} + \dots + e_{c_r}\) where each \(c_i\) is a weight 8 codeword of \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\), as the weight 8 codewords span \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\). Let \(S_i = \text{supp}(\mathbf{c}_i)\). Define \(\mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=1}^r 2e_{S_i} \in \Gamma_0\). Clearly, the coordinates of \(\mathbf{x}\) that are 2 modulo 4 are precisely the coordinates of \(C\) and the rest are 0 modulo 4. Therefore the vector \(v - x\) has sum of components a multiple of 16 and all components are divisible by 4. By Exercise 614 and parts (ii) and (iii), \(v - x \in \Gamma_0\) and hence \(v \in \Gamma_0\).

### Exercise 614

Show that the set of vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^{24}\) whose components are multiples of 4 and whose component sum is a multiple of 16 are precisely the vectors in the lattice spanned by \(4e_T\) for \(T \in \mathcal{T}\) and \(4e_i - 4e_j\) for \(i\) and \(j\) in \(\Omega\).

The lattice \(\Gamma_1\) is defined to be the lattice spanned by \(\Gamma_0\) and the vector \(s = (-3, 1, 1, \dots, 1)\). Notice that we obtain a vector with -3 in coordinate \(i\) and 1s elsewhere by adding \(4e_i - 4e_j\) to \(s\).

### Lemma 10.6.7

The vector \(v = v_1 v_2 \cdots v_{24}\) is in \(\Gamma_1\) if and only if the following three conditions all hold:

- (i) The components \(v_i\) are all congruent to the same value \(m\) modulo 2.
- (ii) The set of \(i\) for which the components \(v_i\) are congruent to the same value modulo 4 form a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set.
- (iii) \(\sum_{i=1}^{24} v_i \equiv 4m \pmod 8\), where \(m\) is defined in (i).

Furthermore, if \(x\) and \(y\) are in \(\Gamma_1\), then \(x - y\) is a multiple of 8 and \(x \cdot x\) is a multiple of 16.

Proof: Statements (i), (ii), and (iii) hold for the vectors spanning \(\Gamma_1\) and hence for any vector in \(\Gamma_1\). The final statement also holds for vectors in \(\Gamma_1\) as it too holds for the vectors spanning \(\Gamma_1\), noting that if \(x = \sum x_i e_i\), then \(x \cdot x = \sum x_i^2 + 2 \sum_{i<j} x_i x_j\).

Suppose that \(v\) satisfies (i), (ii), and (iii). Then we can subtract \(\alpha s\) from \(v\) where \(\alpha\) is one of 0, 1, 2, or 3, so that \(v - \alpha s\) has only even coordinates and its component sum is a multiple of 16. By (ii), the set of coordinates in \(v - \alpha s\) where the components are not divisible by 4 forms a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set. Hence \(v - \alpha s\) is in \(\Gamma_0\), by Lemma 10.6.6 and the proof is complete.

The lattice \(\Gamma_1\) is not self-dual. We obtain \(\Lambda_{24}\) by multiplying all vectors in \(\Gamma_1\) by \(1/\sqrt{8}\). Thus

\[
\Lambda_{24} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{8}} \Gamma_1.
\]

By Lemma 10.6.7, the inner product of any two vectors in \(\Lambda_{24}\) is an integer, making \(\Lambda_{24}\) integral. By the same lemma, the norm of a vector in \(\Lambda_{24}\) is an even integer. It can be shown that \(\det \Lambda_{24} = \pm 1\) implying that \(\Lambda_{24}\) is a Type II lattice. By Exercise 615, its minimum norm is 4 and its kissing number is 196 560.

### Exercise 615

Upon scaling a vector in \(\Gamma_1\) by multiplying by \(1/\sqrt{8}\), the norm of the vector is reduced by a factor of eight. In this exercise you will show that no vector in \(\Lambda_{24}\) has norm 2 and also find the vectors in \(\Lambda_{24}\) of norm 4. This is equivalent to showing that no vector in \(\Gamma_1\) has norm 16 and finding those vectors in \(\Gamma_1\) of norm 32.

- (a) Using the characterization of \(\Gamma_1\) in Lemma 10.6.7, show that \(\Gamma_1\) has no vectors of norm 16.
- (b) Show that a vector of shape \(((\pm 2)^8, 0^{16})\) with an even number of minus signs and the \(\pm 2\)s in coordinates forming an octad is in \(\Gamma_1\).
- (c) Show that there are \(2^7 \cdot 759\) vectors described in part (b).
- (d) Show that a vector of shape \(((\mp 3), (\pm 1)^{23})\) with the lower signs in a \(\mathcal{G}_{24}\)-set is in \(\Gamma_1\).
- (e) Show that there are \(2^{12} \cdot 24\) vectors described in part (d).
- (f) Show that a vector of shape \(((\pm 4)^2, 0^{22})\) with the plus and minus signs assigned arbitrarily is in \(\Gamma_1\).
- (g) Show that there are \(2 \cdot 24 \cdot 23\) vectors described in part (f).
- (h) Show that a vector in \(\Gamma_1\) of norm 32 is one of those found in part (b), (d), or (f).
- (i) Show that the minimum norm of \(\Lambda_{24}\) is 4 and that the kissing number is 196 560. \(\blacklozenge\)

There are a number of other constructions for \(\Lambda_{24}\) including one from the [24, 12, 9] symmetry code over \(\mathbb{F}_3\); see Section 5.7 of Chapter 5 in [60]. Lattices over the complex numbers can also be defined; the [12, 6, 6] extended ternary Golay code can be used to construct the 12-dimensional complex version of the Leech lattice; see Section 8 of Chapter 7 in [60].

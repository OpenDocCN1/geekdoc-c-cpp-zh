

### Example 7.5.4
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $[16, 8, 4]$ self-dual doubly-even binary code. From Example 7.3.3, its weight distribution is given by $A_0(\mathcal{C}) = A_{16}(\mathcal{C}) = 1$, $A_4(\mathcal{C}) = A_{12}(\mathcal{C}) = 28$, and $A_8(\mathcal{C}) = 198$. In the notation of Theorem 7.5.2, $S = \{4, 8, 12, 16\}$ and $s = 4$. Hence the covering radius of $\mathcal{C}$ is at most 4. Suppose that $\mathbf{v}$ is a coset leader of a coset of $\mathcal{C}$ of weight 4. (Note that we do not know if such a coset leader exists.) Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the $[16, 9, 4]$ linear code generated by $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathbf{v}$; thus $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{C} \cup (\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C})$. $\mathcal{D}$ is an even code, and $\mathcal{D}^\perp \subset \mathcal{C}$. Let $A_i = A_i(\mathcal{D}^\perp)$ and $A_i^\perp = A_i(\mathcal{D})$. Since $\mathcal{D}$ is even, $\mathcal{D}^\perp$ contains the all-one vector. Thus we know $A_0 = A_{16} = 1$ and $A_i = 0$ for all other $i$ except possibly $i = 4, 8,$ or $12$ as $\mathcal{D}^\perp \subset \mathcal{C}$. Thus using the first three equations from (7.8), we obtain

```
1 + A_4 + A_8 + A_{12} + 1 = 2^7.
4A_4 + 8A_8 + 12A_{12} + 16 = 2^6 \cdot 16.
16A_4 + 64A_8 + 144A_{12} + 256 = 2^5 \cdot 16 \cdot 17.
```

Solving produces $A_4 = A_{12} = 12$ and $A_8 = 102$. As $\mathcal{D}$ contains the all-one vector, $A_i^\perp = A_{16-i}^\perp$. As $\mathcal{D}$ is even with minimum weight 4, $A_1^\perp = 0$ for $i$ odd, and hence we only need $A_4^\perp$, $A_8^\perp$, and $A_{12}^\perp$ to determine the weight distribution of $\mathcal{D}$ completely. Using $(M_3)$, $(K)$, or $(P_1)$ we discover that $A_4^\perp = 44$, $A_8^\perp = 64$, and $A_{12}^\perp = 294$. The weight distribution of the coset $\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}$ is $A_i(\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}) = A_i(\mathcal{D}) - A_i(\mathcal{C})$. So the weight distribution of a coset of weight 4 is uniquely determined to be:

| Weight | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of vectors | 16 | 64 | 64 | 96 | 16 |

Notice that we still do not know if such a coset exists.

### Exercise 397
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $[16, 8, 4]$ self-dual doubly-even binary code. After having done Exercises 395 and 396, do the following: (a) Show that all odd weight vectors of $\mathbb{F}_2^{16}$ are in a coset of $\mathcal{C}$ of either weight 1 or weight 3. (b) Show that there are exactly 16 cosets of $\mathcal{C}$ of weight 1 and exactly 112 cosets of $\mathcal{C}$ of weight 3.

Notice that this exercise shows that the cosets of weight 3 in fact exist.

Before proceeding with the computation of the weight distributions of the cosets of weight 2 in the $[16, 8, 4]$ codes, we present the following useful result.

### Theorem 7.5.5
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a code of length $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, with $A \in \Gamma \operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C})$. Let $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n$. Then the weight distribution of the coset $\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}$ is the same as the weight distribution of the coset $\mathbf{v}A + \mathcal{C}$.

### Proof
If $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n$, then applying $A$ to $\mathbf{x}$ rescales the components with nonzero scalars, permutes the components, and then maps them to other field elements under a field automorphism. Thus $w(\mathbf{x}) = w(\mathbf{x}A)$. As $A \in \Gamma \operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C})$, $\mathbf{v}A + \mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{v}A + \mathbf{c} \mid \mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}\} = \{(\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{c})A \mid \mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}\}$ implying that $\mathbf{v}A + \mathcal{C}$ and $\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}$ have the same weight distribution.

### Example 7.5.6
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a $[16, 8, 4]$ self-dual doubly-even binary code. By [262], up to equivalence there are two such codes. Each code has different weight distributions for its cosets of weight 2. These two codes, denoted $\mathcal{C}_1$ and $\mathcal{C}_2$, have generator matrices $G_1$ and $G_2$, respectively, where

![](img/0af7e79235839ec0eb8bcf8d229f37d4_286_0.png)

and

![](img/0af7e79235839ec0eb8bcf8d229f37d4_286_1.png)

In this example, we compute the weight distribution of the cosets of $\mathcal{C}_1$ of weight 2. Notice that $\mathcal{C}_1$ is the direct sum of an $[8, 4, 4]$ code with itself, the only such code is $\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_3$ by Exercise 56. Let $\mathbf{v}$ be a weight 2 vector that is a coset leader of a coset of $\mathcal{C}_1$. As we want to find the weight distribution of $\mathbf{v} + \mathcal{C}_1$, by Theorem 7.5.5, we may replace $\mathbf{v}$ by $\mathbf{v}A$, where $A$ is a product of the automorphisms of $\mathcal{C}_1$ given in Exercise 398. We use the notation of that exercise. Let $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{v}) = \{a, b\}$; when we replace $\mathbf{v}$ by $\mathbf{v}A$, we will still denote the support of this new vector by $\{a, b\}$, where $a$ and $b$ will have different values. If both $a$ and $b$ are at least 9, apply P3 to $\mathbf{v}$. Thus we may assume that $1 \leq a < b \leq 8$ or $1 \leq a \leq 8 < b$. In the first case, if $a \neq 1$, apply powers of P3 followed by P1 so that we may assume that $a = 1$; now apply powers of P3 again so that we may assume that $b = 2$. In the second case, if $a \neq 1$, apply powers of P3 followed by P1 so that we may assume that $a = 1$; if $b \neq 9$, apply powers of P1 followed by P2 so that we may assume that $b = 9$. Thus there are only two possible coset leaders that we need to consider:

- (a) $\mathbf{v}_1 = 11000000000000000$, and
- (b) $\mathbf{v}_2 = 10000000100000000$.

We first consider $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_1$. Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the $[16, 9, 2]$ code $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{C}_1 \cup (\mathbf{v}_1 + \mathcal{C}_1)$. Let $A_i = A_i(\mathcal{D}^\perp)$ and $A_i^\perp = A_i(\mathcal{D})$. $\mathcal{D}$ is an even code and so $\mathcal{D}^\perp$ contains the all-one vector. Thus we know $A_0 = A_{16} = 1$ and $A_i = 0$ for all other $i$ except possibly $i = 4, 8,$ or $12$ by $\mathcal{D}^\perp \subset \mathcal{C}_1$. We also know $A_1^\perp = 0$ and need to determine $A_4^\perp$. The only weight 2 vectors in $\mathbf{v}_1 + \mathcal{C}_1$, other than $\mathbf{v}_1$, are $\mathbf{v}_1 + \mathbf{c}$, where $\mathbf{c}$ is a vector of weight 4 whose support contains $\{1, 2\}$. By inspecting $G_1$, it is clear that the only choices for such a $\mathbf{c}$ are the first three rows of $G_1$. Therefore $A_2^\perp = 4$. Thus using the first three equations from (7.8), we obtain

```
1 + A_4 + A_8 + A_{12} + 1 = 2^7,
4A_4 + 8A_8 + 12A_{12} + 16 = 2^6 \cdot 16,
16A_4 + 64A_8 + 144A_{12} + 256 = 2^5(16 \cdot 17 + 8).
```

Solving yields $A_4 = A_{12} = 20$ and $A_8 = 86$. As $\mathcal{D}$ contains the all-one vector, $A_i^\perp = A_{16-i}^\perp$. Using $(M_3)$, $(K)$, or $(P_1)$, we have $A_4^\perp = 36$, $A_6^\perp = 60$, and $A_8^\perp = 310$. As $A_i(\mathbf{v}_1 + \mathcal{C}_1) = A_i(\mathcal{D}) - A_i(\mathcal{C}_1)$, the weight distribution of the coset $\mathbf{v}_1 + \mathcal{C}_1$ is:

| Weight | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of vectors | 4 | 8 | 60 | 112 | 60 | 8 | 4 |

Now consider $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_2$. Let $\mathcal{D}$ be the $[16, 9, 2]$ code $\mathcal{D} = \mathcal{C}_1 \cup (\mathbf{v}_2 + \mathcal{C}_1)$, which is still even. As in the case $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_1$, $A_0 = A_{16} = 1$ and only $A_4, A_8,$ and $A_{12}$ need to be determined as the other $A_i$ are 0. Also $A_1^\perp = 0$, but $A_2^\perp = 1$ because there are clearly no weight 4 vectors in $\mathcal{C}_1$ whose support contains $\{1, 9\}$. Thus using the first three equations from (7.8), we obtain

```
1 + A_4 + A_8 + A_{12} + 1 = 2^7,
4A_4 + 8A_8 + 12A_{12} + 16 = 2^6 \cdot 16,
16A_4 + 64A_8 + 144A_{12} + 256 = 2^5(16 \cdot 17 + 2).
```

In the same manner as for $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}_1$, we obtain $A_4 = A_{12} = 14$, $A_8 = 98$, $A_4^\perp = 42$, $A_6^\perp = 63$, and $A_8^\perp = 298$. The weight distribution of the coset $\mathbf{v}_2 + \mathcal{C}_1$ is:

| Weight | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of vectors | 1 | 14 | 63 | 100 | 63 | 14 | 1 |

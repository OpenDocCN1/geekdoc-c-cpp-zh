+   (i) Duadic codes of length \( n \) over \( \mathbb{F}_2 \) exist if and only if \( p_i \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 8 \) for \( 1 \le i \le r \).

+   (ii) Duadic codes of length \( n \) over \( \mathbb{F}_4 \) exist if and only if \( p_i \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{12} \) for \( 1 \le i \le r \).

+   (iii) Duadic codes of length \( n \) over \( \mathbb{F}_4 \) exist for all (odd) \( n \).

**Proof:** Duadic codes of length \( n \) over \( \mathbb{F}_q \) exist if and only if \( q \) is a square modulo \( n \) by Theorem 6.3.2. By Lemma 6.3.1, \( q \) is a square modulo \( n \) if and only if \( q \) is a square modulo \( p_i^{r_i} \) for \( 1 \le i \le r \). By Lemma 6.2.3, \( q \) is a square modulo \( p_i^{r_i} \) if and only if \( q \) is a square modulo \( p_i \). Part (i) now follows from Lemma 6.2.5 and (ii) from Lemma 6.2.9. Finally, part (iii) follows from the simple fact that \( 4 = 2^2 \) is always a square modulo \( n \).

# **Exercise 346** Find the integers \( n \), with \( 3 < n < 200 \), for which binary and ternary duadic codes exist.

In the binary case, there are results that count the number of duadic codes of prime length \( n=p \). By Theorem 6.3.3, \( p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 8 \). Let \( e = (p-1)/\text{Ord}_p(2) \). Then it can be shown [67] that the number of duadic codes of length \( p \) depends only on \( e \). Further, the number of inequivalent duadic codes also depends only on \( e \). If \( e \) is odd, the number of pairs of odd-like (or even-like) binary duadic codes is \( 2^{e-1} \). When \( e \) is even, there is a more complicated bound for this number. For example, if \( p=31 \), then \( \text{ord}_p(2)=5 \) and so \( e=3 \). There are four pairs of odd-like (or even-like) duadic codes. One pair is a pair of quadratic residue codes; the other three pairs consist of six equivalent codes (see Example 6.4.8). These facts hold for any length \( p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod 8 \) for which \( e=3 \), such as \( p=223, 433, 439, 457, \text{and } 727 \).

# ## 6.4 Orthogonality of duadic codes

The multiplier \( \mu_{-1} \) plays a special role in determining the duals of duadic codes just as it does for duals of general cyclic codes; see Theorem 4.4.9. We first consider self-orthogonal codes.

# **Theorem 6.4.1** Let \( \mathcal{C} \) be any \( [n, (n-1)/2] \) cyclic code over \( \mathbb{F}_q \). Then \( \mathcal{C} \) is self-orthogonal if and only if \( \mathcal{C} \) is an even-like duadic code whose splitting is given by \( \mu_{-1} \).

**Proof:** Suppose \( \mathcal{C}_1 = \mathcal{C} \) is self-orthogonal with idempotent generator \( e_1(x) \). Let \( \mathcal{C}_2 = (e_2(x)) \), where \( e_2(x) = e_1(x)\mu_{-1} \). Since \( \mathcal{C}_1 \) is self-orthogonal and \( \overline{f(x)} \) is not orthogonal to itself, \( \overline{J(x)} \notin C_1 \). Thus by Lemma 6.1.2(iii), \( \mathcal{C} \) is even-like, which implies that \( \overline{J(x)} \in C_1^\perp \). By Theorem 4.4.9, \( \overline{J(x)} \in C_1^\perp = (1 - e_1(x)\mu_{-1}) \). As \( C_1^\perp \) has dimension \( (n+1)/2 \) and \( C_1 \subset C_1^\perp \), we have \( C_1^\perp = C_1 + \overline{J(x)} \). Since \( e_1(\overline{J(x)}) = 0 \) by Lemma 6.1.2(d), it follows from Theorem 4.3.7 that \( C_1^\perp \) has generating idempotent \( e_1(x) + \overline{J(x)} = 1 - e_1(x)\mu_{-1} = 1 - e_2(x) \), giving (6.1). Since \( e_2(x) = e_1(x)\mu_{-1} \), \( e_1(x) = e_2(x)\mu_{-1}^{-1} \) yielding (6.3). Thus \( C_1 \) and \( C_2 \) are even-like duadic codes with splitting given by \( \mu_{-1} \). Conversely, let \( \mathcal{C} = C_1 = \langle e_1(x) \rangle \) be an even-like duadic code with splitting given by \( \mu_{-1} \). Then \( C_2 = \langle e_1(x)\mu_{-1} \rangle \) and \( C_1 \subset \mathcal{D}_1 = (1 - e_1(x)\mu_{-1}) = C_1^\perp \) by Theorems 6.1.3(vi) and 4.4.9 and (6.4).

+   (ii) If the splitting defining the duadic codes is given by \( \mu_{-1} \), then \( d_2^2 - d_o + 1 \ge n \).

+   (iii) Suppose \( d_o^2 - d_o + 1 = n \), where \( d_o > 2 \), and assume the splitting is given by \( \mu_{-1} \). Then for \( i = 1 \) and 2:

+   (a) \( d_o \) is the minimum weight of \( \mathcal{D}_i \),

+   (b) the supports of the minimum weight codewords of \( \mathcal{D}_i \) form a cyclic projective plane of order \( d_o - 1 \),

+   (c) the minimum weight codewords of \( \mathcal{D}_i \) are multiples of binary vectors, and

+   (d) there are exactly \( n(q-1) \) minimum weight codewords in \( \mathcal{D}_i \).

Proof: Suppose the splitting defining the duadic codes is given by \( \mu_a \). Let \( c(x) \in \mathcal{D}_1 \) be an odd-like vector of weight \( d_o \). Then \( c'(x) = c(x)\mu_a \in \mathcal{D}_2 \) is also odd-like and \( c(x)(x) \in \mathcal{D}_1 \cap \mathcal{D}_2 \) as \( \mathcal{D}_1 \) and \( \mathcal{D}_2 \) are ideals in \( R_n \). But \( \mathcal{D}_1 \cap \mathcal{D}_2 = \langle \bar{j}(x) \rangle \) by Theorem 6.1.3(xviii). By Lemma 6.1.2(ii), \( c(x)(x) \) is odd-like, and in particular nonzero. Therefore \( c(x)(x') \) is a nonzero multiple of \( \bar{j}(x) \), and so \( \text{wt}(c(x)(x')) = n \). The number of terms in the product \( c(x)(x')(x) \) is at most \( d_o^2 \), implying (i). If \( \mu_a = \mu_{-1} \), then the number of terms in \( c(x)(x')(x) \) is at most \( d_o^2 - d_o + 1 \) because, if \( c(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} c_j x^j \), the coefficient of \( x^0 \) in \( c(x)(x')(x) \) is \( \sum c_j^2 \), where the sum is over all subscripts \( j \) with \( c_j \neq 0 \). This produces (ii).

We prove (iii) for \( i = 1 \). Suppose the splitting is given by \( \mu_{-1} \) and \( n = d_o^2 - d_o + 1 \). Let \( c(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + \cdots + c_{n-1} x^{n-1} \in \mathcal{D}_1 \) be an odd-like vector of weight \( d_o \), and let \( c'(x) = c(x)\mu_{-1} \). As in the proof of part (i), \( c(x)(x') \in \mathcal{D}_1 \cap \mathcal{D}_2 = \langle \bar{j}(x) \rangle \), implying \( c(x)(x') = \delta \bar{j}(x) \) for some \( \delta \neq 0 \). Let \( c_{j_1}, \dots, c_{j_{d_o}} \) be the nonzero coefficients of \( c(x) \). Let \( \mathcal{M} \) be the \( d_o \times n \) matrix whose \( j \)th row corresponds to \( c_{j_i} x^{-j_i} c(x) \) obtained by multiplying \( c(x) \) by the \( j \)th nonzero term of \( c'(x) = c(x^{-1}) \). Adding the rows of \( \mathcal{M} \) gives a vector corresponding to \( c(x)(x') = \delta \bar{j}(x) \). Then \( \mathcal{M} \) has \( d_o^2 \) nonzero entries. We label the columns of \( \mathcal{M} \) by \( x^0, x^1, x^2, \dots, x^{n-1} \). Column \( x^0 \) of \( \mathcal{M} \) has \( d_o \) nonzero entries. This leaves \( d_o^2 - d_o = n - 1 \) nonzero entries for the remaining \( n - 1 \) columns. Since the sum of the entries in the column labeled \( x^i \) is the coefficient of \( x^i \) in \( \delta \bar{j}(x) \), which is nonzero, each of the last \( n - 1 \) columns contains exactly one nonzero entry. Thus the supports of any two rows of \( \mathcal{M} \) overlap in only the \( x^0 \) coordinate.

Suppose \( m(x) = m_0 + m_1 x + \cdots + m_{n-1} x^{n-1} \in \mathcal{D}_1 \) is a nonzero even-like vector of weight \( w \). We show that \( w \ge d_o + 1 \). By shifting \( m(x) \), we may assume that \( m_0 \neq 0 \). The supports of \( m(x) \) and each row of \( \mathcal{M} \) overlap in coordinate \( x^0 \). If \( C_1 \) and \( C_2 \) are the even-like duadic codes corresponding to \( \mathcal{D}_1 \) and \( \mathcal{D}_2 \), then \( c_1 \) is self-orthogonal and \( C_1^\perp = \mathcal{D}_1 \) by Theorem 6.4.2. As \( m(x) \in C_1 \) by Theorem 6.1.3(vi) and \( C_1^\perp = \mathcal{D}_1 \), \( m(x) \) is orthogonal to every row of \( \mathcal{M} \). Therefore the support of \( m(x) \) and the support of each row of \( \mathcal{M} \) overlap in at least one more coordinate. As columns \( x^1, \dots, x^{n-1} \) of \( \mathcal{M} \) each have exactly one nonzero entry, \( \text{wt}(m(x)) = w \ge d_o + 1 \). This proves (iii)(a) and shows that the codewords in \( \mathcal{D}_1 \) of weight \( d_o \) must be odd-like.

# # 12 Codes over Z4

The study of codes over the ring Z4 attracted great interest through the work of Calderbank, Hammons, Kumar, Sloane, and Solé in the early 1990s which resulted in the publication of a paper [116] showing how several well-known families of nonlinear binary codes were intimately related to linear codes over Z4. In particular, for m ≥ 4 an even integer, there is a family K(m) of (2^m, 2^{2m-1} - 2^{m-2}/2) nonlinear binary codes, originally discovered by Kerdock [166], and a second family P(m) of (2^m, 2^{2m-2m}, 6) nonlinear binary codes, due to Preparata [288], which possess a number of remarkable properties. First, the codes K(4) and P(4) are identical to one another and to the Nordstrom-Robinson code described in Section 2.3.4. Second, the Preparata codes have the same length as the extended double error-correcting BCH codes but have twice as many codewords. Third, and perhaps most intriguing, K(m) and P(m) behave as if they are duals of each other in the sense that the weight enumerator of P(m) is the MacWilliams transform of the weight enumerator of K(m); in other words, if C = K(m), then the weight enumerator of P(m) is 1/|C| W_C(y-x, y+x) (see (M3) of Section 7.2). A number of researchers had attempted to explain this curious relationship between weight enumerators without success until the connection with codes over Z4 was made in [116].

In this chapter we will present the basic theory over Z4 including the connection between these codes and binary codes via the Gray map. We will also study cyclic, self-dual, and quadratic residue codes over Z4. The codes of Kerdock and Preparata will be described as the Gray image of certain extended cyclic codes over Z4. In order to present these codes we will need to examine the Galois ring GR(4^m), which plays the same role in the study of cyclic codes over Z4 as F_{2^m} (also denoted GF(2^m)) does in the study of cyclic codes over F_2.

# ## 12.1 Basic theory of Z4-linear codes

A Z4-linear code C of length n is an additive subgroup of Z4^n. Such a subgroup is a Z4-module, which may or may not be free.

# Theorem 12.3.20 If \( C = \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle \oplus \langle 2f(x)h(x) \rangle \) is a cyclic code of odd length \( n \) over \( \mathbb{Z}_4 \) with \( f(x)g(x)h(x) = x^n - 1 \), then \( C^\perp = \langle h^*(x)x^*(x) \rangle \oplus \langle 2h^*(x)f^*(x) \rangle \). Furthermore, if \( g(x) = 1 \), then \( C = \langle f(x) \rangle \) and \( C^\perp = \langle h^*(x) \rangle \); if \( h(x) = 1 \), then \( C = \langle 2f(x) \rangle \) and \( C^\perp = \langle g^*(x) \rangle \oplus \langle 2f^*(x) \rangle \).

Proof: Let \( s(x) = h^*(x)g^*(x) \); then \( s^*(x) = \pm h(x)g(x) \) and \( f(x)g(x)s^*(x) = \pm (x^n - 1) \times g(x) \) in \( \mathbb{Z}_4[x] \), implying \( f(x)g(x)s^*(x) = 0 \) in \( \mathfrak{R}_n \). In the same way \( 2f(x)h(x)s^*(x) = 0 \). Thus \( h^*(x)g^*(x) \in C^\perp \) by Lemma 12.3.18. Likewise \( 2h^*(x)f^*(x) \) is orthogonal to \( f(x)g(x) \) and its cyclic shifts. As \( 2a(x)2b^*(x) = 0 \) in \( \mathfrak{R}_n \), for all \( a(x) \) and \( b(x) \) in \( \mathbb{Z}_4[x] \), \( 2h^*(x)f^*(x) \) is orthogonal to \( 2f(x)h(x) \) and its cyclic shifts. Thus \( 2h^*(x)f^*(x) \in C^\perp \). As \( x^n - 1 = f^*(x)g^*(x)h^*(x) \), by Theorem 12.3.13, \( \langle h^*(x)g^*(x) \rangle + \langle 2h^*(x)f^*(x) \rangle \) is a direct sum. Thus
\( \langle h^*(x)g^*(x) \rangle \oplus \langle 2h^*(x)f^*(x) \rangle \subseteq C^\perp \). (12.11)
Since \( C \) has type \( 4^{\deg h} 2^{\deg g} 2^{\deg f} \), \( C^\perp \) has type \( 4^{\deg h - \deg k - \deg g} 2^{\deg g} 2^{\deg f} \) by (12.3). But \( \langle h^*(x)f^*(x) \rangle \) has type \( 4^{\deg g} 2^{\deg h} 2^{\deg f} \). As these types agree, we have equality in (12.11) proving the first statement. The second statement follows from the first by direct computation as \( g(x) = 1 \) implies \( g^*(x) = 1 \) and \( h^*(x)f^*(x) = x^n - 1 \), while \( h(x) = 1 \) implies \( h^*(x) = 1 \).

# 12.3.4 Generating idempotents of cyclic codes over \( \mathbb{Z}_4 \)

Let \( C \) be a nonzero cyclic code over \( \mathbb{Z}_4 \) of odd length \( n \). By Theorem 12.3.13 there exist unique monic polynomials \( f(x) \), \( g(x) \), and \( h(x) \) such that \( x^n - 1 = f(x)g(x)h(x) \) and \( C = \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle \oplus \langle 2f(x)h(x) \rangle \). By Theorem 12.3.12(i), \( \{f(x)g(x)\} \) and \( \{2f(x)h(x)\} \) can be expressed as a direct sum of ideals of the forms \( \langle \bar{g}_i(x) \rangle \) and \( \langle 2\bar{g}_i(x) \rangle \), respectively, where \( x^n - 1 = \bar{g}_1(x)\bar{g}_2(x) \cdots \bar{g}_t(x) \) is a factorization of \( x^n - 1 \) into irreducible polynomials in \( \mathbb{Z}_4[x] \) and \( \bar{g}_i(x) = \prod_{j \in J_i} g_j(x) \). Thus \( f(x)g(x) = \sum_{i \in J} \langle \bar{g}_i(x) \rangle \) and \( \langle 2g(x)h(x) \rangle = \sum_{j \in J} \langle 2\bar{g}_j(x) \rangle \) for some subsets \( J \) and \( \mathcal{J} \) of \( \{1, 2, \dots, t\} \). Theorem 12.3.12(iv) shows that \( \langle \bar{g}_i(x) \rangle = \langle \bar{e}_i(x) \rangle \), where \( \bar{e}_i(x) \) is an idempotent of \( \mathfrak{R}_n \). By Exercise 723(a) and (b), \( \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle = \langle e(x) \rangle \) and \( \langle g(x)h(x) \rangle = \langle E(x) \rangle \), where \( e(x) \) and \( E(x) \) are idempotents in \( \mathfrak{R}_n \). This proves the following theorem.

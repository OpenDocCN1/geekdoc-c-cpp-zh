+   **Lemma 4.4.7** Let \( f(x) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x] \).
+   
+   (i) If \( \beta_1, \dots, \beta_l \) are the nonzero roots of \( f \) in some extension field of \( \mathbb{F}_q \), then \( \beta_1^{-1}, \dots, \beta_l^{-1} \) are the nonzero roots of \( f^* \) in that extension field.
+   
+   (ii) If \( f(x) \) is irreducible over \( \mathbb{F}_q \), so is \( f^*(x) \).
+   
+   (iii) If \( f(x) \) is a primitive polynomial, so is \( f^*(x) \).

The connection between dual codes and reciprocal polynomials is clear from the following lemma.

**Lemma 4.4.8** Let \( \mathbf{a} = a_0 a_1 \cdots a_{n-1} \) and \( \mathbf{b} = b_0 b_1 \cdots b_{n-1} \) be vectors in \( \mathbb{F}_q^n \) with associated polynomials \( a(x) \) and \( b(x) \). Then \( \mathbf{a} \) is orthogonal to \( \mathbf{b} \) and all its shifts if and only if \( a(x)b^*(x) = 0 \) in \( \mathcal{R}_n \).

**Proof:** Let \( \mathbf{b}^{(h)} = b_h b_{h+1} \cdots b_{n+h-1} \) be the \( h \)th cyclic shift of \( \mathbf{b} \), where the subscripts are read modulo \( n \). Then

```
\( \mathbf{a} \cdot (\mathbf{b}^h)^i = 0 \) if and only if \( \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a_j b_{j+h} = 0 \). (4.7)
```

But \( a(x)b^*(x) = 0 \) in \( \mathcal{R}_n \) if and only if \( a(x)(x^{n-1-\deg b(x)}b^*(x)) = 0 \) in \( \mathcal{R}_n \). But \( a(x)(x^{n-1-\deg b(x)}b^*(x)) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} a_j b_{j+h}) x^{n-1-i} \). Thus \( a(x)b^*(x) = 0 \) in \( \mathcal{R}_n \) if and only if (4.7) holds for \( 0 \le i \le n-1 \).

We now give the generator polynomial and generating idempotent of the dual of a cyclic code. The proof is left as an exercise.

+   **Theorem 4.4.9** Let \( C \) be an \( [n, k] \) cyclic code over \( \mathbb{F}_q \) with generator polynomial \( g(x) \), generating idempotent \( e(x) \), and defining set \( T \). Let \( h(x) = (x^n - 1)/g(x) \). The following hold.
+   
+   (i) \( C^\perp \) is a cyclic code and \( C^\perp = C^p_{\mu-1} \).
+   
+   (ii) \( C^\perp \) has generating idempotent \( 1 - e(x)_{\mu-1} \) and generator polynomial \( \frac{x^k}{h(0)} h(x^{-1}) \).
+   
+   (iii) If \( \beta_1, \dots, \beta_l \) are the zeros of \( C \), then \( \beta_1^{-1}, \dots, \beta_l^{-1} \) are the nonzeros of \( C^\perp \).
+   
+   (iv) If \( \mathcal{N} = \{0, 1, \dots, n-1\} \), then \( \mathcal{N} \setminus (-1)T \text{ mod } n \) is the defining set of \( C^\perp \).
+   
+   (v) Precisely one of \( c \) and \( C^\perp \) is odd-like and the other is even-like.

The polynomial \( h(x) = (x^n - 1)/g(x) \) in this theorem is called the check polynomial of \( C \). The generator polynomial of \( C^\perp \) in part (ii) of the theorem is the reciprocal polynomial of \( h(x) \) rescaled to be monic.

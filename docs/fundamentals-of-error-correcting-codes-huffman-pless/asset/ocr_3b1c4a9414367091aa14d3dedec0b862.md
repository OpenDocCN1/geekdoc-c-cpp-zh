Because wt(v' + w') = 2\rho + 2, the left-hand side of (11.23) is a combination of 2\rho + 2 columns of H_0 from among the first \rho_0 columns of H_0, while the right-hand side of (11.23) is a combination of at most 2\rho columns of H_0 as wt((v' + w', 0) - (x_v + x_w)) \le 2\rho. As above, in the right-hand side of (11.21) we may replace 2\rho + 2 of the columns h_0^i by at most 2\rho columns of H_0 indicating that h_0^i is a linear combination of at most \rho_0 - 2 columns of H_0, which is a contradiction.

Therefore f is a coloring of K(\rho_0, \rho + 1) with at most 2^l - 1 colors. Since \rho_0 \ge 2\rho + 2, (11.19) implies that
\rho_0 - 2\rho = \chi(K(\rho_0, \rho + 1)) \le 2^l - 1.

Notice that the Calderbank Bound with i = 1 is the bound of Theorem 11.6.3.

There is another upper bound on \rho(C) due to Hou [139] that in some cases improves the bound of Theorem 11.6.4 and the Calderbank Bound. Its proof relies on knowledge of the graph G(n, s). The graph G(n, s) has vertex set \mathbb{F}_2^n; two vertices v and w form an edge whenever d(v, w) > s.

The following theorem of Kleitman [172] gives a lower bound on the chromatic number of G(n, 2r).

**Theorem 11.6.6** Let n and r be positive integers with n \ge 2r + 1. Then

\chi(G(n, 2r)) \ge \frac{2^n}{\binom{n}{0} + \binom{n}{1} + \cdots + \binom{n}{r}}.

We now state the bound on \rho(C) due to Hou; we leave the details of the proof as an exercise.

**Theorem 11.6.7 (Hou Bound)** Let C_0 be a subcode of codimension i \ge 1 of a binary linear code c. Let \rho = \rho(C) and \rho_0 = \rho(C_0). Then

\frac{2\rho}{\binom{\rho_0}{0} + \binom{\rho_0}{1} + \cdots + \binom{\rho_0}{\rho}} \le 2^i. (11.24)

**Proof:** If \rho_0 \le 2\rho, then (11.24) is true by Exercise 661. Assume that \rho_0 \ge 2\rho + 1. Let H_0 be the parity check matrix of C_0 as in the proof of Theorem 11.6.5. Analogously, define the same map f from the vertices of G(\rho_0, 2\rho) (instead of K(\rho_0, \rho + 1)) to \mathbb{F}_2^l. As before it follows that f is a coloring of G(\rho_0, 2\rho) with 2^l colors, and the result is a consequence of Theorem 11.6.6.

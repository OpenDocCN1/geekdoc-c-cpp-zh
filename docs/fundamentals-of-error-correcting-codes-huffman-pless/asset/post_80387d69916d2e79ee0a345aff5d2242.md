
### Theorem 11.6.5 (Calderbank Bound)
Let $C_0$ be a subcode of codimension $i \ge 1$ in a binary linear code $C$ of length $n$. Then

\(\rho(C_0) \le 2\rho(C) + 2^i - 1\).

Proof: Let H be a parity check matrix for C. We can add i rows to H to form a parity check matrix

\(H_0 = \begin{bmatrix} H \ H_1 \end{bmatrix}\)

for $C_0$. Let the $j$th column of $H_0$ be $h_0^j$ for $1 \le j \le n$.

Let \(\rho = \rho(C)\) and \(\rho_0 = \rho(C_0)\). If \(\rho_0 \le 2\rho + 1\), then (11.20) clearly holds. So assume that \(\rho_0 \ge 2\rho + 2\). There must be some syndrome $s_0^T$ of $C_0$ which is a sum of $\rho_0$ columns of $H_0$ but no fewer. By rearranging the columns of $H_0$, we may assume that

\(s_0^T = \sum_{j=1}^{\rho_0} h_0^j\).

Form the Kneser graph, $K(\rho_0, \rho+1)$. This graph has as vertex set $V$ the set of vectors in $\mathbb{F}_2^n$ of weight $\rho+1$. We write each vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{F}_2^n$ as $(\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{v}'')$, where $\mathbf{v}' \in \mathbb{F}_2^{\rho_0}$. We wish to establish a coloring $f$ of the vertices of $K(\rho_0, \rho+1)$. For every $\mathbf{v}' \in V$, there is an $\mathbf{x}_v \in C$ of distance at most $\rho$ from $(\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{0})$ as $\rho(C) = \rho$. For each $\mathbf{v}' \in V$ choose one such $\mathbf{x}_v \in C$ and define

\(f(\mathbf{v}') = H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}'}^T\).

Notice that $f(\mathbf{v}') \in \mathbb{F}_2^i$. We first establish that $H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}'}^T \neq 0$. Suppose $H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}}^T = 0$. Then $H_0 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}}^T = \mathbf{0}$ as $H \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}}^T = \mathbf{0}$ because $\mathbf{x}_v \in C$. Therefore,

\(H_0(\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{0})^T = H_0((\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{0}) - \mathbf{x}_v)^T\).

Because $wt(\mathbf{v}) = \rho+1$, the left-hand side of (11.22) is a combination of $\rho+1$ columns of $H_0$ from among the first $\rho_0$ columns of $H_0$, while the right-hand side of (11.22) is a combination of at most $\rho$ columns of $H_0$ as $wt((\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{0}) - \mathbf{x}_v) \le \rho$. Thus on the right-hand side of (11.21) we may replace $\rho+1$ of the columns of $H_0$ by at most $\rho$ columns of $H_0$ indicating that $\mathbf{s}_0^T$ is a linear combination of at most $\rho_0 - 1$ columns of $H_0$, which is a contradiction. This shows that $f(\mathbf{v}')$ is one of the $2^i - 1$ nonzero elements of $\mathbb{F}_2^i$.

We now establish that $f$ is indeed a coloring of $K(\rho_0, \rho+1)$. Let $\{\mathbf{v}', \mathbf{w}'\}$ be an edge of $K(\rho_0, \rho+1)$. We must show that $H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}'}^T \neq H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}'}^T$. Suppose that $H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{v}'}^T = H_1 \mathbf{x}_{\mathbf{w}'}^T$. Then as $\mathbf{v}'$ and $\mathbf{w}'$ are in $V$, where $supp(\mathbf{v}')$ and $supp(\mathbf{w}')$ are disjoint, $wt(\mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{w}') = 2\rho + 2$. Additionally, the distance between $(\mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{w}', \mathbf{0})$ and $\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w$ is at most $2\rho$. Since $H_1(\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w)^T = \mathbf{0}$ and $H(\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w)^T = \mathbf{0}$ because $\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w \in C$, we have $H_0(\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w)^T = \mathbf{0}$. As in (11.22),

\(H_0(\mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{w}', \mathbf{0})^T = H_0((\mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{w}', \mathbf{0}) - (\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w))^T\).

Because \(wt(\mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{w}') = 2\rho + 2\), the left-hand side of (11.23) is a combination of \(2\rho + 2\) columns of \(H_0\) from among the first \(\rho_0\) columns of \(H_0\), while the right-hand side of (11.23) is a combination of at most \(2\rho\) columns of \(H_0\) as \(wt((\mathbf{v}' + \mathbf{w}', \mathbf{0}) - (\mathbf{x}_v + \mathbf{x}_w)) \le 2\rho\). As above, in the right-hand side of (11.21) we may replace \(2\rho + 2\) of the columns of \(H_0\) by at most \(2\rho\) columns of \(H_0\) indicating that \(\mathbf{s}_0^T\) is a linear combination of at most \(\rho_0 - 2\) columns of \(H_0\), which is a contradiction.

Therefore \(f\) is a coloring of \(K(\rho_0, \rho + 1)\) with at most \(2^i - 1\) colors. Since \(\rho_0 \ge 2\rho + 2\), (11.19) implies that
\(\rho_0 - 2\rho = \chi(K(\rho_0, \rho + 1)) \le 2^i - 1\).

Notice that the Calderbank Bound with \(i = 1\) is the bound of Theorem 11.6.3.

There is another upper bound on \(\rho(C)\) due to Hou [139] that in some cases improves the bound of Theorem 11.6.4 and the Calderbank Bound. Its proof relies on knowledge of the graph \(G(n, s)\). The graph \(G(n, s)\) has vertex set \(\mathbb{F}_2^n\); two vertices \(v\) and \(w\) form an edge whenever \(d(v, w) > s\).

The following theorem of Kleitman [172] gives a lower bound on the chromatic number of \(G(n, 2r)\).

### Theorem 11.6.6
Let \(n\) and \(r\) be positive integers with \(n \ge 2r + 1\). Then

\(\chi(G(n, 2r)) \ge \frac{2^n}{\binom{n}{0} + \binom{n}{1} + \cdots + \binom{n}{r}}\).

We now state the bound on \(\rho(C)\) due to Hou; we leave the details of the proof as an exercise.

### Theorem 11.6.7 (Hou Bound)
Let \(C_0\) be a subcode of codimension \(i \ge 1\) of a binary linear code \(C\). Let \(\rho = \rho(C)\) and \(\rho_0 = \rho(C_0)\). Then

\(\frac{2\rho}{\binom{\rho_0}{0} + \binom{\rho_0}{1} + \cdots + \binom{\rho_0}{\rho}} \le 2^i.\) (11.24)

**Proof:** If \(\rho_0 \le 2\rho\), then (11.24) is true by Exercise 661. Assume that \(\rho_0 \ge 2\rho + 1\). Let \(H_0\) be the parity check matrix of \(C_0\) as in the proof of Theorem 11.6.5. Analogously, define the same map \(f\) from the vertices of \(G(\rho_0, 2\rho)\) (instead of \(K(\rho_0, \rho + 1)\)) to \(\mathbb{F}_2^i\). As before it follows that \(f\) is a coloring of \(G(\rho_0, 2\rho)\) with \(2^i\) colors, and the result is a consequence of Theorem 11.6.6.

## 11.7 Ancestors, descendants, and orphans

In this concluding section of the chapter we examine relationships among coset leaders of a code \(\mathcal{C}\). There is a natural relationship among cosets, and we will discover that all cosets of \(\mathcal{C}\) of maximum weight (that is, those of weight \(\rho(\mathcal{C})\)) have a special place in this relationship. We will also introduce the concept of the Newton radius of a code and investigate it in light of this relationship among cosets.

There is a natural partial ordering \(\le\) on the vectors in \(\mathbb{F}_2^n\) as follows. For \(\mathbf{x}\) and \(\mathbf{y}\) in \(\mathbb{F}_2^n\), define \(\mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{y}\) provided that \(\text{supp}(\mathbf{x}) \subseteq \text{supp}(\mathbf{y})\). If \(\mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{y}\), we will also say that \(\mathbf{y}\) *covers* \(\mathbf{x}\). We now use this partial order on \(\mathbb{F}_2^n\) to define a partial order, also denoted \(\le\), on the set of cosets of a binary linear code \(\mathcal{C}\) of length \(n\). If \(\mathcal{C}_1\) and \(\mathcal{C}_2\) are two cosets of \(\mathcal{C}\), then \(\mathcal{C}_1 \le \mathcal{C}_2\) provided there are coset leaders \(\mathbf{x}_1\) of \(\mathcal{C}_1\) and \(\mathbf{x}_2\) of \(\mathcal{C}_2\) such that \(\mathbf{x}_1 \le \mathbf{x}_2\). As usual, \(\mathcal{C}_1 < \mathcal{C}_2\) means that \(\mathcal{C}_1 \le \mathcal{C}_2\) but \(\mathcal{C}_1 \neq \mathcal{C}_2\). Under this partial ordering the set of cosets of \(\mathcal{C}\) has a unique minimal element, the code \(\mathcal{C}\) itself.

# Exercise 466 Use Theorem 8.7.6 to obtain $(v, k, \lambda)$ cyclic difference sets in $\mathbb{Z}_7, \mathbb{Z}_{11}, \mathbb{Z}_{19}, \mathbb{Z}_{23}, \text{and } \mathbb{Z}_{31}$. What are their parameters $v, k, \text{and } \lambda$?

When $\lambda = 1$ and $\mathbb{G} = \mathbb{Z}_v$, the symmetric designs are cyclic projective planes. Since it is so difficult to prove the nonexistence of projective planes with parameters not covered by the Bruck-Ryser-Chowla Theorem, it is reasonable to consider planes with additional conditions. One natural possibility is to examine criteria under which cyclic projective planes do not exist. Our next two theorems show that if a cyclic projective plane has order $\mu$ where either $\mu \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ or $\mu \equiv \pm 3 \pmod 9$, then $\mu = 2$ or $\mu = 3$. These results were proved in the more general setting of $(\mu^2+\mu+1, \mu+1, 1)$ difference sets in an abelian group of order $\mu^2+\mu+1$ in [353]. Using duadic codes, we can prove these two theorems rather easily. The proof of the first is found in [267].

# Theorem 8.7.7 Let $\mathcal{D}$ be a cyclic projective plane of order $\mu \equiv 2 \pmod 4$. Then $\mu = 2$.

Proof: Let $A$ be the incidence matrix of $\mathcal{D}$. By Theorem 8.7.1(ii), $\mathcal{D}_2(A)$ is a $[v, (v-1)/2]$ self-orthogonal even-like duadic code with splitting $\mu_{-1}$. By Theorems 8.5.3 and 6.4.2, $C_2(A)$ is an odd-like duadic code. By Theorem 8.6.3, $C_2(A)$ has minimum weight $\mu+1$, with minimum weight codewords being those whose supports are the lines. By Theorem 4.3.13, $\mu_2 \in \text{PAut}(C_2(A))$ and thus $\mu_2$ permutes the minimum weight codewords. Hence $\mu_2 \in \text{Aut}(\mathcal{D})$. Because $\mu_2$ fixes the coordinate $0$, by Theorem 8.7.2, $\mu_2$ fixes a line $\ell$ and thus $\mu_2$ fixes a minimum weight codeword $\mathbf{c}$ of $C_2(A)$. But any nonzero binary codeword fixed by $\mu_2$ must be an idempotent. By Theorem 8.7.5, the lines of $\mathcal{D}$ can be obtained by cyclic shifts of $\ell$; as the codewords whose supports are the lines $C_2(A)$, $\mathbf{c}$ must be the generating idempotent. But the generating idempotent of a binary duadic code of length $v$ has weight $(v-1)/2$ or $(v+1)/2$ by equations (6.1) and (6.3). Thus $\mu+1 = (v \pm 1)/2 = (\mu^2+\mu+1 \pm 1)/2$, which implies $\mu = 2$.

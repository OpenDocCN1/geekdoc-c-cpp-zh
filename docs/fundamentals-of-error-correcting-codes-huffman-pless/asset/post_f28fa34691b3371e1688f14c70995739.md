
But if you puncture $C_i(\alpha)$ on coordinate $i$, you obtain an $(n-1, m_{i,\alpha}, d)$ code with codewords of weight $w-1$. Thus $m_{i,\alpha} \le A_q(n-1, d, w-1)$, yielding

$$
wM = \sum_{\alpha \in \mathbb{F}_q^*} \sum_{i=1}^n m_{i,\alpha} \le q^* n A_q(n-1, d, w-1).
$$

Therefore,

$$
A_q(n, d, w) \le \left\lfloor \frac{nq^*}{w} A_q(n-1, d, w-1) \right\rfloor. \tag{2.11}
$$

By induction, repeatedly using, (2.11),

$$
A_q(n, d, w) \le \left\lfloor \frac{nq^*}{w} \left\lfloor \frac{(n-1)q^*}{w-1} \left\lfloor \dots \left\lfloor \frac{(n-i+1)q^*}{w-i+1} A_q(n-i, d, w-i) \right\rfloor \dots \right\rfloor \right\rfloor \right\rfloor
$$

for any $i$. If $d = 2e - 1$, let $i = w - e + 1$; then $A_q(n-i, d, w-i) = A_q(n-w+e-1, 2e-1, e-1) = 1$ by Theorem 2.3.3(i) and part (ii) holds in this case. Let $i = w - e$, then $A_q(n-i, d, w-i) = A_q(n-w+e, 2e, e) \le \frac{(n-w+e)q^*}{e}$ by Theorem 2.3.3(ii) and part (ii) again holds in this case.

Parts (iii) and (iv) follow from (i) and (ii) with $d = 2e - 1$ using Theorem 2.3.3(iv).

Example 2.3.7 In Example 2.3.2 we showed that $A_2(24, 8, 8) \ge 759$. The Restricted Johnson Bound cannot be used to obtain an upper bound on $A_2(24, 8, 8)$, but the Unrestricted Johnson Bound can. By this bound,

A_2(24, 8, 8) \le \left\lfloor \frac{24}{8} \left\lfloor \frac{23}{7} \left\lfloor \frac{22}{6} \left\lfloor \frac{21}{5} \left\lfloor \frac{20}{4} \right\rfloor \right\rfloor \right\rfloor \right\rfloor \right\rfloor = 759.
$$

Thus $A_2(24, 8, 8) = 759$.

## 2.3.3 The Johnson Bound for \( A_q(n, d) \)

The bounds on \( A_q(n, d, w) \) can be used to give upper bounds on \( A_q(n, d) \) also due to Johnson [159]. As can be seen from the proof, these bounds strengthen the Sphere Packing Bound. The idea of the proof is to count not only the vectors in \( \mathbb{F}_q^n \) that are within distance \( t = \lfloor (d-1)/2 \rfloor \) of all codewords (that is, the disjoint spheres of radius \( t \) centered at codewords) but also the vectors at distance \( t+1 \) from codewords that are not within these spheres. To accomplish this we need the following notation. If \( \mathcal{C} \) is a code of length \( n \) over \( \mathbb{F}_q \) and \( \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n \), let \( d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) \) denote the distance from \( \mathbf{x} \) to \( \mathcal{C} \). So \( d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = \min\{d(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) \mid \mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}\} \).

## Theorem 2.3.8 (Johnson Bound for \( A_q(n, d) \)) Let \( t = \lfloor (d-1)/2 \rfloor \).

- (i) If \( d \) is odd, then
  $$
  A_q(n, d) \le \frac{q^n}{\sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i}(q-1)^i + \frac{\binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1}}{\binom{t+1}{t} - \left(\frac{d}{t}\right)} A_q(n, d, d)}.
  $$

- (ii) If \( d \) is even, then
  $$
  A_q(n, d) \le \frac{q^n}{\sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i}(q-1)^i + \frac{\binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1}}{A_q(n, d, t+1)}}.
  $$

- (iii) If \( d \) is odd, then
  $$
  A_2(n, d) \le \frac{2^n}{\sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i} + \frac{\binom{n}{t+1} - \binom{d}{t} A_2(n, d, d)}{\lfloor \frac{n}{t+1} \rfloor}}.
  $$

- (iv) If \( d \) is even, then
  $$
  A_2(n, d) \le \frac{2^n}{\sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i} + \left\lfloor \frac{n}{t+1} \right\rfloor}.
  $$ (2.12)

- (v) If \( d \) is odd, then
  $$
  A_2(n, d) \le \frac{2^n}{\sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i} + \frac{\binom{n}{t} \left( \frac{n-t}{t+1} - \frac{n-t}{t+1} \right)}{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{t+1} \right\rfloor}}.
  $$ (2.13)

**Proof:** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $(n, M, d)$ code over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Notice that $t$ is the packing radius of $\mathcal{C}$; $d = 2t + 1$ if $d$ is odd and $d = 2t + 2$ if $d$ is even. So the spheres of radius $t$ centered at codewords are disjoint. The vectors in these spheres are precisely the vectors in $\mathbb{F}_q^n$ that are distance $t$ or less from $\mathcal{C}$. We will count these vectors together with those vectors at distance $t + 1$ from $\mathcal{C}$ and use this count to obtain our bounds. To that end let $\mathcal{N}$ be the vectors at distance $t + 1$ from $\mathcal{C}$; so $\mathcal{N} = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n \mid d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \}$. Let $|\mathcal{N}|$ denote the size of $\mathcal{N}$. Therefore,

$$
M \sum_{i=0}^t \binom{n}{i} (q-1)^i + |\mathcal{N}| \le q^n. \tag{2.14}
$$

as the summation on the left-hand side counts the vectors in the spheres of radius $t$ centered at codewords; see (1.11). Our bounds will emerge after we obtain a lower bound on $|\mathcal{N}|$.

Let $\mathcal{X} = \{ (\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) \in \mathcal{C} \times \mathcal{N} \mid d(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \}$. To get the lower bound on $|\mathcal{N}|$ we obtain lower and upper estimates for $|\mathcal{X}|$.

We first obtain a lower estimate on $|\mathcal{X}|$. Let $\mathcal{X}_c = \{ \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{N} \mid (\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) \in \mathcal{X} \}$. Then

$$
|\mathcal{X}| = \sum_{\mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}} |\mathcal{X}_c|. \tag{2.15}
$$

Fix $\mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C}$. Let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{F}_q^n$ be a vector at distance $t + 1$ from $\mathbf{c}$ so that $wt(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) = t + 1$. There are exactly

$$
\binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1}
$$

such vectors $\mathbf{x}$ because they are obtained by freely changing any $t + 1$ coordinates of $\mathbf{c}$. Some of these lie in $\mathcal{X}_c$ and some do not. Because $wt(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) = t + 1$, $d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) \le t + 1$. Let $\mathbf{c}' \in \mathcal{C}$ with $\mathbf{c}' \neq \mathbf{c}$. Then by the triangle inequality of Theorem 1.4.1, $d(\mathbf{c}', \mathbf{c}) = wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}) \le wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) + wt(\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) = wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) + t + 1$, implying

$$
d - t - 1 \le wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}). \tag{2.16}
$$

If $d = 2t + 2$, $wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) \ge t + 1$ yielding $d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1$ since $\mathbf{c}' \in \mathcal{C}$ was arbitrary and we saw previously that $d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) \le t + 1$. Therefore all such $\mathbf{x}$ lie in $\mathcal{X}_c$ giving

$$
|\mathcal{X}_c| = \binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1},
$$

hence

$$
|\mathcal{X}| = M \binom{n}{t+1}(q-1)^{t+1} \quad \text{if } d = 2t + 2. \tag{2.17}
$$

## 2.3 The Johnson Upper Bounds

If \( d = 2t + 1 \), \( wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) \ge t \) by (2.16) yielding \( t \le d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) \le t + 1 \). As we only want to count the \(\mathbf{x}\) where \( d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \), we will throw away those with \( d(\mathcal{C}, \mathbf{x}) = t \). Such \(\mathbf{x}\) must simultaneously be at distance \( t \) from some codeword \(\mathbf{c}'\) in \(\mathcal{C}\) and at distance \( t + 1 \) from \(\mathbf{c}\). Hence the distance from \(\mathbf{c}'\) to \(\mathbf{c}\) is at most \( 2t + 1 = d \), by the triangle inequality; this distance must also be at least \( d \) as that is the minimum distance of \(\mathcal{C}\). Therefore we have \( wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c}) = 2t + 1 \). How many \(\mathbf{c}'\) are possible? As the set \( \{ \mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c} \mid \mathbf{c}' \in \mathcal{C} \} \) forms a constant weight code of length \( n \) and minimum distance \( d \), whose codewords have weight \( d \), there are at most \( A_q(n, d, d) \) such \(\mathbf{c}'\). For each \(\mathbf{c}'\), how many \(\mathbf{x}\) are there with \( wt(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}) = t + 1 \) and \( t = wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x}) = wt((\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c}) - (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c})) \)? Since \( wt(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c}) = 2t + 1 \), \(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{c}\) is obtained from \(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c}\) by arbitrarily choosing \( t \) of its \( 2t + 1 \) nonzero components and making them zero. This can be done in \(\binom{n}{t}\) ways. Therefore

$$
\binom{n}{t}(q-1)^{t+1} - \binom{d}{t} A_q(n, d, d) \le |\mathcal{X}_c|
$$

showing by (2.15) that

$$
M \left[ \binom{n}{t+1} (q-1)^{t+1} - \binom{d}{t} A_q(n, d, d) \right] \le |\mathcal{X}| \quad \text{if } d = 2t + 1. \tag{2.18}
$$

We are now ready to obtain our upper estimate on \( |\mathcal{X}| \). Fix \(\mathbf{x}\) in \(\mathcal{N}\). How many \(\mathbf{c}\) in \(\mathcal{C}\) are there with \( d(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \)? The set \( \{ \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x} \mid \mathbf{c} \in \mathcal{C} \text{ with } d(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \} \) is a constant weight code of length \( n \) with words of weight \( t + 1 \) and minimum distance \( d \) because for \(\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{x} = (\mathbf{c} - \mathbf{x}) - (\mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c}) = \mathbf{c}' - \mathbf{c}\). Thus for each \(\mathbf{x}\) in \(\mathcal{N}\) there are at most \( A_q(n, d, t + 1) \) choices for \(\mathbf{c}\) with \( d(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{x}) = t + 1 \). Hence \( |\mathcal{X}| \le |\mathcal{N}| A_q(n, d, t + 1) \) or

$$
\frac{|\mathcal{X}|}{A_q(n, d, t + 1)} \le |\mathcal{N}|. \tag{2.19}
$$

We obtain bound (i) by combining (2.14), (2.18), and (2.19) and bound (ii) by combining (2.14), (2.17), and (2.19). Bounds (iii) and (iv) follow from (i) and (ii) by observing that \( A_q(n, 2t + 1, t + 1) = A_q(n, 2t + 2, t + 1) = (n / (t + 1)) \) by Theorem 2.3.3. Finally, bound (v) follows from (iii) and the observation (with details left as Exercise 102) that

$$
\binom{d}{t} A_q(n, d, d) = \binom{d}{t} A_2(n, 2t + 1, 2t + 1) = \binom{d}{t} A_2(n, 2t + 2, 2t + 1) \le \binom{n}{t} \left\lfloor \frac{n-t}{t+1} \right\rfloor
$$

by Theorem 2.3.3(iv) and the Unrestricted Johnson Bound.

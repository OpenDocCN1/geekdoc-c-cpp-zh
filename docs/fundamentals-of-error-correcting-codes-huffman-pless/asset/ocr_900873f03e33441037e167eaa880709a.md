There is a relationship between the weight hierarchy of $\mathcal{C}$ and the weight hierarchy of $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ due to Wei [348]. First we prove a lemma.

Lemma 7.10.3 Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $[n, k]$ code over $\mathbb{F}_q$. For a positive integer $s < n$, let $r$ be the largest integer such that $\mathcal{C}$ has a generator matrix $G$ of the form

$G = \begin{bmatrix} G_1 & O \\ G_2 & G_3 \end{bmatrix}$.

where $G_1$ is an $r \times s$ matrix of rank $r$ and $G_3$ is a $(k-r) \times (n-s)$ matrix. Then $\mathcal{C}$ has a parity check matrix of the form

$H = \begin{bmatrix} H_1 & H_2 \\ O & H_3 \end{bmatrix}$.

where $H_1$ is a $(s-r) \times s$ matrix of rank $s-r$ and $H_3$ is an $(n-k-s+r) \times (n-s)$ matrix of rank $n-k-s+r$. Furthermore, $n-k-s+r$ is the largest dimension of a subspace of $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ with support contained in the last $n-s$ coordinates.

Proof: Let $H = [A \ B]$, where $A$ is an $(n-k) \times s$ matrix and $B$ is an $(n-k) \times (n-s)$ matrix. Since the rows of $H$ are orthogonal to the rows of $G$, the rows of $A$ are orthogonal to the rows of $G_1$. Hence $A$ has rank at most $s-r$. Suppose that the rank of $A$ is less than $s-r$. Then there is a vector of length $s$ not in the code generated by $G_1$ that is orthogonal to the rows of $A$. Appending $n-s$ zeros to the end of this vector, we obtain a vector orthogonal to the rows of $H$, which therefore is in $\mathcal{C}$ but is not in the code generated by $[G_1 \ O]$, contradicting the maximality of $r$. Thus the rank of $A$ equals $s-r$ and hence $\mathcal{C}$ has a parity check matrix $H$ satisfying the conclusions of the lemma.

Theorem 7.10.4 Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $[n, k]$ code over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Then

$\{d_r(\mathcal{C}) \mid 1 \le r \le k\} = \{1, 2, ..., n\} \setminus \{n+1-d_{\mathcal{C}^\perp}(l) \mid 1 \le l \le n-k\}.$

Proof: Let $s = d_{\mathcal{C}^\perp}(\mathcal{C}^\perp)$ for some $r$ with $1 \le r \le n-k$. It suffices to show that there does not exist an $r$ with $1 \le l \le k$ such that $d_l(\mathcal{C}) = n+1-s$. As $s = d_{\mathcal{C}^\perp}(\mathcal{C}^\perp)$, there is a set of $s$ coordinates that supports an $r$-dimensional subcode of $\mathcal{C}^\perp$. Reorder columns so that these are the first $s$ coordinates. Thus $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ has an $(n-k) \times n$ generator matrix of the form

$H = \begin{bmatrix} H_1 & O \\ H_2 & H_3 \end{bmatrix}$.

where $H_1$ is an $r \times s$ matrix of rank $r$. As $d_r(\mathcal{C}^\perp) < d_{r+1}(\mathcal{C}^\perp)$ by Theorem 7.10.1, no larger subcode has support in these $s$ coordinates. Applying Lemma 7.10.3 with $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ in place of $\mathcal{C}$ and $n-k$ in place of $k$, there is a $(k-s+r)$-dimensional subcode of $\mathcal{C}$ which is zero on the first $s$ coordinate positions. Hence

$d_{k-s+r}(\mathcal{C}) \le n-s$. (7.17)

## Example 13.3.2 Let \(q = r^2\) where \(r\) is a prime power. The Hermitian curve \(\mathcal{H}_r(\mathbb{F}_q)\) is the projective plane curve over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) defined by \(f(x, y, z) = x^{r+1} - y^r z - y z^r = 0\).

In Exercise 774, you will show that \(\mathcal{H}_r(\mathbb{F}_q)\) has only one point at infinity, namely \((0 : 1 : 0)\). We show that there are \(r^3\) affine points \((x : y : 1)\) of \(\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_q)\) on \(\mathcal{H}_r(\mathbb{F}_q)\). As \(z = 1\), \(x^{r+1} = y^r + y\). But \(y^r + y = \text{Tr}_2(y)\), where \(\text{Tr}_2 : \mathbb{F}_{r^2} \to \mathbb{F}_r\) is the trace map from \(\mathbb{F}_{r^2}\) to \(\mathbb{F}_r\). As its image is a nonzero subspace of the 1-dimensional space \(\mathbb{F}_r\), \(\text{Tr}_2\) is surjective. So its kernel is a 1-dimensional subspace of \(\mathbb{F}_{r^2}\), implying there are \(r\) elements \(y \in \mathbb{F}_{r^2}\) with \(\text{Tr}_2(y) = 0\). When \(\text{Tr}_2(y) = 0\), then \(x^{r+1} = 0\) has one solution \(x = 0\); this leads to \(r\) affine points on \(\mathcal{H}_r(\mathbb{F}_q)\). If \(x \in \mathbb{F}_{r^2}\), \(x^{r+1} = x^r x = (r + 1)(x - 1) = 0\) and the nonzero elements of \(\mathbb{F}_{r^2}\) in \(x_{r+1}\) are precisely those satisfying \(\beta^{r-1} = 1\). When \(y\) is one of the \(r^2 - r\) elements of \(\mathbb{F}_{r^2}\) with \(\text{Tr}_2(y) \neq 0\), there are \(r + 1\) solutions \(x \in \mathbb{F}_{r^2}\) with \(x^{r+1} = \text{Tr}_2(y)\). This leads to \((r^2 - r)(r + 1) = r^3 - r\) more affine points. Hence, \(\mathcal{H}_r(\mathbb{F}_q)\) has \(r^3 - r + r = r^3\) affine points and a total of \(r^3 + 1\) projective points.

When examining the points on a curve, we will need to know their degrees. The degree \(m\) of a point depends on the field under consideration. Let \(q = p^r\) where \(p\) is a prime, and let \(m \ge 1\) be an integer. The map \(\sigma_q : \mathbb{F}_{q^m} \to \mathbb{F}_{q^m}\) given by \(\sigma_q(\alpha) = \alpha^q\) is an automorphism of \(\mathbb{F}_{q^m}\) that fixes \(\mathbb{F}_q\); \(\alpha_q\) where \(\sigma_q\) is the Frobenius map defined in Section 3.6. If \(P = (x, y)\) or \(P = (x : y : z)\) have coordinates in \(\mathbb{F}_{q^m}\), let \(\sigma_q(P)\) denote \((\sigma_q(x), \sigma_q(y))\) or \((\sigma_q(x) : \sigma_q(y) : \sigma_q(z))\), respectively. By Exercise 776, in the projective case, it does not matter which representative is chosen for a projective point when applying \(\sigma_q\) to the point. Suppose \(f\) is in either \(\mathbb{F}_q[x, y]\) or \(\mathbb{F}_q[x, y, z]\), with \(f\) homogeneous in the latter case. So \(f\) defines either an affine or projective curve over \(\mathbb{F}_q\) or over \(\mathbb{F}_{q^m}\). Exercise 776 shows that if \(P\) is a point on \(\mathcal{X}/(\mathbb{F}_{q^m})\), so is \(\sigma_q(P)\). Therefore \(\{\sigma_q^i(P) \mid i \ge 0\}\) is a set of points on \(\mathcal{X}/(\mathbb{F}_{q^m})\); however, there are at most \(m\) distinct points in this set as \(\sigma_q^m\) is the identity automorphism of \(\mathbb{F}_{q^m}\). This allows us to extend our notion of a point. A _point_ \(P\) _on_ \(\mathcal{X}/(\mathbb{F}_q)\) _of degree_ \(m\) _over_ \(\mathbb{F}_q\) is a set of \(m\) distinct points \(P = \{P_0, \dots, P_{m-1}\}\) with \(P_i\) on \(\mathcal{X}/(\mathbb{F}_{q^m})\), where \(P_i = \sigma_q^i(P_0)\); the _degree of_ \(P\) _over_ \(\mathbb{F}_q\) is denoted deg(\(P\)). The points of degree one on \(\mathcal{X}/(\mathbb{F}_q)\) are called _rational_ or \(\mathbb{F}_q\)_-rational points_.\(^2\) This definition is motivated by the well-known situation with polynomials over the real numbers. Conjugation is the automorphism of the complex numbers that fixes the real numbers. The polynomial \(p(x) = x^2 - 4x + 5\) with real coefficients has no real roots but has two complex conjugate roots, \(2 + i\) and \(2 - i\). In our new terminology, the roots of \(p(x)\) are lumped together as the pair \(\{2 + i, 2 - i\}\) and is a point on \(p(x) = 0\) of degree 2 over the real numbers (even though each individual root is not real). In the definition of a point \(P = \{P_0, \dots, P_{m-1}\}\) of degree \(m\) over \(\mathbb{F}_q\), the \(P_i\)s are required to be distinct. It is possible, beginning with a point \(P_0\) in \(\mathbb{A}^2(\mathbb{F}_{q^m})\) or \(\mathbb{P}^2(\mathbb{F}_{q^m})\), that \(P_i = \sigma_q^i(P_0)\) for \(0 \le i \le m - 1\) are not distinct. In this case the distinct \(P_i\)s form a point of smaller degree; see Exercise 777(c).

## Example 13.3.3 Let \(f(x, y, z) = x^3 + xz^2 + z^3 + y^2z + yz^2 \in \mathbb{F}_2[x, y, z]\). The curve determined by this equation is an example of an _elliptic curve_.

$\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}^*]$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}]$ but not a subalgebra. So elements of $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}^*]$ are of the form

$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} a_{q^i} X^{q^i}.$

while elements of $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}]$ are of the form

$a_0 X^0 + \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} d_{q^i} X^{q^i}.$

The vector space $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}^*]$ will be the new setting for primitive cyclic codes, and the algebra $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}]$ will be the setting for the extended cyclic codes. So in fact both codes are contained in $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}]$, which makes the discussion of affine-invariant codes more tractable.

Suppose that $\mathcal{C}$ is a cyclic code over $\mathbb{F}_q$ of length $n = q' - 1$. The coordinates of $\mathcal{C}$ have been denoted $\{0, 1, \dots, n-1\}$. In $\mathcal{R}_n$, the $i$th component $c_i$ of a codeword $\mathbf{c} = c_0 c_1 \cdots c_{n-1}$, with associated polynomial $c(x)$, is the coefficient of the term $c_i x^i$ in $c(x)$; the component $c_i$ is kept in position $x^i$. Now we associate $\mathbf{c}$ with an element $C(X) \in \mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}^*]$ as follows:

$\mathbf{c} \leftrightarrow C(X) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} C_{q^i} X^{q^i} = \sum_{g \in \mathbb{Z}^*} C_g X^g, \quad (4.9)$

where $C_{a^i} = c_i$. Thus the $i$th component of $\mathbf{c}$ is the coefficient of the term $C_{a^i} X^{a^i}$ in $C(X)$; the component $c_i$ is kept in position $X^{a^i}$.

**Example 4.7.1** Consider the element $c(x) = 1 + x + x^3$ in $\mathcal{R}_7$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$. So $n = 7 = 2^3 - 1$. Let $\alpha$ be a primitive element of $\mathbb{F}_8$. Then $c_0 = C_{a^0} = 1, c_1 = C_{a^1} = 1$, and $c_3 = C_{a^3} = 1$, with the other $c_i = C_{a^i} = 0$. So

$c(x) = 1 + x + x^3 \leftrightarrow C(X) = X + X^a + X^{a^3}.$

We now need to examine the cyclic shift $xc(x)$ under the correspondence (4.9). We have

$xc(x) = c_{n-1} + \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} c_{i-1} x^i \leftrightarrow \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} C_{a^{i-1}} X^{a^i} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} C_{q^i} X^{a \cdot q^i}.$

**Example 4.7.2** We continue with Example 4.7.1. Namely,

$xc(x) = x + x^2 + x^4 \leftrightarrow X^a + X^{a^2} + X^{a^4} = X^{aa'} + X^{aa} + X^{aa'}.$

In our new notation a primitive cyclic code over $\mathbb{F}_q$ of length $n = q' - 1$ is any subset $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathbb{F}_q[\mathcal{I}^*]$ such that

$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} C_{q^i} X^{a q^i} = \sum_{g \in \mathbb{Z}^*} C_g X^{ag} \in \mathcal{C} \quad \text{if and only if}$

$\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} C_{q^i} X^{a q^i} = \sum_{g \in \mathbb{Z}^*} C_g X^{ag} \in \mathcal{C}. \quad (4.10)$

For (ii), we note that the matrix obtained from $[1 \ \xi \ \xi^2 \ \cdots \ \xi^{r-1}]$ by replacing each $\xi^i$ with the coefficients from (12.21) is the $r 	imes r$ identity matrix. Hence the $r+1$ rows of $\widehat{G}_{r+1}$ generate a code of type $4^{r+1}$; the same will be true of the $r+1$ rows of $G_{r+1}$ once we show that the parity check of $\mathbf{r}_1 = [1 \ 1 \ 1 \ \cdots \ 1]$ is 1 and the parity check of $\mathbf{r}_2 = [1 \ \xi \ \xi^2 \ \cdots \ \xi^{r-1}]$ is 0. Since $\mathbf{r}_1$ has $2^r - 1$ 1s and $2^r - 1 \equiv 3 \pmod 4$ as $r \ge 2$, the parity check for $\mathbf{r}_1$ is 1. The parity check for $\mathbf{r}_2$ is $-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \xi^i = -(\xi^q - 1)/(\xi - 1) \equiv 0$ as $\xi^q = 1$.

To complete the proof of (ii), it suffices to show that $c(x) \in K(r+1)$ where $c(x)$ is the polynomial associated to $\mathbf{r}_1$ and $\mathbf{r}_2$. For $\mathbf{r}_1$, let $c(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} x^i$. As $(x-1)^q c(x) = (x-1) \sum_{i=0}^{q-1} x^i = x^q - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_4[x]$, $(x-1)H(x)c^*(x) = 0$ in $\mathfrak{R}_n$, showing $c(x) \in K(r+1)$ by (i). For $\mathbf{r}_2$, we are actually working with $r$ possible codewords. We do not wish to deal with them individually and so we let $c(x) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \xi^i x^i$, thus allowing the coefficients of $c(x)$ to lie in the ring $GR(4^r)$ rather than $\mathbb{Z}_4$. Clearly, by (ii), we only need to show that $H(x)c^*(x) = 0$ even when we allow the coefficients of $c(x)$ to be elements of $GR(4^r)$. So $c^*(x) = \xi \sum_{i=0}^{q-1} \xi^{q-1-i} x^i$ (where $\xi$ in front of the summation makes $c^*(x)$ monic). Let $H(x) = \sum_{i=0}^n H_i x^i$. By Exercise 754, the coefficient of $x^k$ for $0 \le k \le n-1$ in the product $H(x)c^*(x)$ is

$$\xi \sum_{i+j \equiv k (\text{mod } n)} H_i \xi^{n-1-j} = \xi \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} H_i \xi^{n-1-k+i} = \xi^{n-k} \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} H_i \xi^i, \quad (12.22)$$

since $\xi^q = 1$. The right-hand side of (12.22) is $\xi^{n-k} H(\xi) = 0$ as $\xi$ is a root of $H(x)$. Therefore $(x-1)H(x)c^*(x) = 0$.

We remark that $(x-1)H(x)$ is called the check polynomial of $K(r+1)$, analogous to the terminology used in Section 4.4 for cyclic codes over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Notice that the matrix $G_{r+1}$ of Lemma 12.7.2 is reminiscent of the parity check matrix for the subcode of even weight vectors in the binary Hamming code, where we write this parity check matrix over an extension field of $\mathbb{F}_2$, rather than over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

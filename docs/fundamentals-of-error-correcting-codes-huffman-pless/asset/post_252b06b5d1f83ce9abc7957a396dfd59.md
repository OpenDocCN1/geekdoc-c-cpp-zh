## 12.3.3 Generating polynomials of cyclic codes over $\mathbb{Z}_4$

When $n$ is odd we can give a pair of polynomials that “generate” any cyclic code of length $n$ over $\mathbb{Z}_4$. The following result was proved in [279, 289].

**Theorem 12.3.13** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a cyclic code over $\mathbb{Z}_4$ of odd length $n$. Then there exist unique monic polynomials $f(x)$, $g(x)$, and $h(x)$ such that $x^n - 1 = f(x)g(x)h(x)$ and $\mathcal{C} = \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle \oplus 2\langle f(x)h(x) \rangle$. Furthermore, $\mathcal{C}$ has type $4^{\deg h(x)} 2^{\deg g}$. 

**Proof:** Using the notation of Theorem 12.3.12, $\mathcal{C}$ is the direct sum of some $\langle \widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle$'s and some $\langle 2\widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle$'s where $x^n - 1 = g_1(x)g_2(x) \cdots g_r(x)$. Rearrange the $g_i(x)$ so that $\mathcal{C} = \sum_{i=1}^k \langle \widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle \oplus \sum_{i=k+1}^r \langle 2\widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle$. Let $f(x) = \prod_{i=k+1}^r g_i(x)$, $g(x) = \prod_{i=k+1}^r g_i(x)$, and $h(x) = \prod_{i=1}^k g_i(x)$. Then $x^n - 1 = f(x)g(x)h(x)$. Since $\widehat{g}_i(x)$ is a multiple of $f(x)g(x)$ for $1 \le i \le k$, we have $\sum_{i=1}^k \langle \widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle \subseteq \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle$. By Lemma 12.3.11, $\langle f(x)g(x) \rangle$ has size $4^{\deg g} = 4^{\deg h}$, which is also the size of $\sum_{i=1}^k \langle \widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle$ by Theorem 12.3.12(iii) and (v). Hence $\sum_{i=1}^k \langle \widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle = \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle$. Using the same argument and Exercise 719, $\sum_{i=k+1}^r \langle 2\widehat{g}_i(x) \rangle = \langle 2f(x)h(x) \rangle$. The uniqueness follows since any monic polynomial factor of $x^n - 1$ must factor into a product of basic irreducibles that must then be a product of some of the $g_i(x)$'s that are unique by Theorem 12.3.7. The type of $\mathcal{C}$ follows from the fact that $\langle f(x)g(x) \rangle$ has size $4^{\deg h}$ and $\langle 2f(x)h(x) \rangle$ has size $2^{\deg g}$. $\square$

**Corollary 12.3.15** Let \( n \) be odd. Assume that \( x^n - 1 \) is a product of \( k \) irreducible polynomials in \( \mathbb{Z}_4[x] \). Then there are \( 3^k \) cyclic codes over \( \mathbb{Z}_4 \) of length \( n \).

**Proof:** Let \( x^n - 1 = g_1(x)g_2(x) \cdots g_k(x) \) be the factorization of \( x^n - 1 \) into monic irreducible polynomials. If \( \mathcal{C} \) is a cyclic code, by Theorem 12.3.13, \( \mathcal{C} = \langle f(x)g(x) \rangle \oplus \langle 2f(x)h(x) \rangle \) where \( x^n - 1 = f(x)g(x)h(x) \). Each \( g_i(x) \) is a factor of exactly one of \( f(x) \), \( g(x) \), or \( h(x) \). The result follows.

**Lemma 12.3.18** Let $a = a_0 a_1 \cdots a_{n-1}$ and $b = b_0 b_1 \cdots b_{n-1}$ be vectors in $\mathbb{Z}_4^n$ with associated polynomials $a(x)$ and $b(x)$. Then $a$ is orthogonal to $b$ and all its shifts if and only if $a(x)b^*(x) = 0$ in $\mathfrak{R}_n$.

We observe that if $n$ is odd and $g(x)$ is a monic irreducible factor of $x^n - 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}_4[x]$, then $g^*(x)$ is also a monic irreducible factor of $x^n - 1$ as the constant term of $g(x)$ is $\pm 1$. The reciprocal polynomial $\mu(g_1^*(x))$ of $(g(x))$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$ is an irreducible factor of $x^n + 1$ in $\mathbb{F}_2[x]$. Thus $g^*(x)$ is a basic irreducible factor of $x^n - 1$. By the unique factorization of $x^n - 1$ from Theorem 12.3.7, $g^*(x) = g_j(x)$ for some $j$ since $g^*(x)$ is monic.

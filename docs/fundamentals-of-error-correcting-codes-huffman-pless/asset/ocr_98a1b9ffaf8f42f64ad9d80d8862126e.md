Recall that an $[n, k]$ generalized Reed-Solomon code over $\mathbb{F}_q$ is defined by

$$GRS_k(\gamma, v) = \{(v_0 f(\gamma_0), v_1 f(\gamma_1), \dots, v_{n-1} f(\gamma_{n-1})) \mid f \in \mathcal{P}_k\},$$

where $\gamma = (\gamma_0, \gamma_1, \dots, \gamma_{n-1})$ is an $n$-tuple of distinct elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$, $v = (v_0, v_1, \dots, v_{n-1})$ is an $n$-tuple of nonzero elements of $\mathbb{F}_q$, and $\mathcal{P}_k$ is the set of polynomials in $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$ of degree $k-1$ or less including the zero polynomial. Suppose that $c = c_0 c_1 \dots c_{n-1} \in GRS_k(\gamma, v)$ is sent and $y' = y'_0 y'_1 \dots y'_{n-1} = c + e$ is received. Then there is a unique $f \in \mathcal{P}_k$ such that $c_i = v_i f(\gamma_i)$ for $0 \le i \le n-1$. We can find $e$ if we can determine the polynomial $f$. Let $\mathcal{A} = \{(\gamma_0, y_0), (\gamma_1, y_1), \dots, (\gamma_{n-1}, y_{n-1})\}$ where $y_i = y'_i / v_i$. Suppose for a moment that no errors occurred in the transmission of $c$. Then $y_i = c_i / v_i = f(\gamma_i)$ for $0 \le i \le n-1$. In particular, all points of $\mathcal{A}$ lie on the polynomial $p(x, y) = y - f(x)$. Now suppose that errors occur. Varying slightly our terminology from earlier, define an _error locator polynomial_ $\Lambda(x, y)$ to be any polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ such that $\Lambda(\gamma_i, y_i) = 0$ for all $i$ such that $y_i \neq c_i / v_i$. Since $y_i - f(\gamma_i) = 0$ if $y_i = c_i / v_i$, all points of $\mathcal{A}$ lie on the polynomial $p(x, y) = \Lambda(x, y)(y - f(x))$. The basic idea of the Sudan-Guruswami Algorithm is to find a polynomial $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ where each element of $\mathcal{A}$ is a root with a certain multiplicity and then find the factors of that polynomial of the form $y - f(x)$. Further restrictions on $p(x, y)$ are imposed to guarantee the error-correcting capability of the algorithm.

The Sudan-Guruswami Decoding Algorithm for the $[n, k, n-k+1]$ code $GRS_k(\gamma, v)$ is:

+   I. Fix a positive integer $m$. Pick $\delta$ to be the smallest positive integer to satisfy
+   
+   $$rac{nm(m+1)}{2} < N_{k-1}(\delta). \tag{5.21}$$

Recall that $N_{k-1}(\delta)$ is the number of monomials $x^i y^j$ whose $(1, k-1)$-weighted degree is $\delta$ or less. Set

$$t = \left\lfloor \frac{\delta}{m} \right\rfloor + 1.$$

+   II. Construct a nonzero polynomial $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ such that each element of $\mathcal{A}$ is a root of $p(x, y)$ of multiplicity at least $m$ and $p(x, y)$ has $(1, k-1)$-weighted degree at most $\delta$.

+   III. Find all factors of $p(x, y)$ of the form $y - f(x)$ where $f(x) \in \mathcal{P}_k$ and $f(\gamma_i) = v_i$ for at least $t \gamma_i$s. For each such $f$ produce the corresponding codeword in $GRS_k(\gamma, v)$. 

We must verify that this algorithm works and give a bound on the number of errors that it can correct. The following three lemmas are needed.

**Lemma 5.4.15** Let $(\alpha, \beta) \in \mathbb{F}_q^2$ be a root of $p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y]$ of multiplicity $m$ or more. If $f(x)$ is a polynomial in $\mathbb{F}_q[x]$ such that $f(\alpha) = \beta$, then $g(x) = p(x, f(x)) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x]$ is divisible by $(x - \alpha)^m$.

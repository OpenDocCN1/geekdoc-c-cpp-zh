### Theorem 8.3.6

There is no known general result that characterizes all codes whose codewords of a given weight, such as minimum weight, hold a $t$-design. There are, however, results that show that codes satisfying certain conditions have weights where codewords of that weight hold a design. The best known of these is the Assmus–Mattson Theorem presented in the next section. It is this result that actually guarantees (without directly showing that supports of codewords satisfy the conditions required in the definition of a $t$-design) that the weight 4 codewords of the $[8, 4, 4]$ extended binary Hamming code hold a $3-(8, 4, 1)$ design and the weight 8 codewords of the $[24, 12, 8]$ extended binary Golay code hold a $5-(24, 8, 1)$ design.

## 8.4 The Assmus–Mattson Theorem

If the weight distribution of a code and its dual are of a particular form, a powerful result due to Assmus and Mattson [6] guarantees that $t$-designs are held by codewords in both the code and its dual. In fact, the Assmus–Mattson Theorem has been the main tool in discovering designs in codes.

For convenience, we first state the Assmus–Mattson Theorem for binary codes. We prove the general result, as the proof for binary codes is not significantly simpler than the proof for codes over an arbitrary field.

### Theorem 8.4.1 (Assmus–Mattson)
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a binary $[n, k, d]$ code. Suppose $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ has minimum weight $d^\perp$. Suppose that $A_i = A_i(\mathcal{C})$ and $A_i^+ = A_i(\mathcal{C}^\perp)$, for $0 \le i \le n$, are the weight distributions of $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{C}^\perp$, respectively. Fix a positive integer $t$ with $t < d$, and let $s$ be the number of $i$ with $A_i^+ \neq 0$ for $0 < i \le n - t$. Then: (i) the vectors of weight $i$ in $\mathcal{C}$ hold a $t$-design provided $A_i \neq 0$ and $d \le i \le n$, and (ii) the vectors of weight $i$ in $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ hold a $t$-design provided $A_i^+ \neq 0$ and $d^\perp \le i \le n - t$.

### Theorem 8.4.2 (Assmus–Mattson)
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be an $[n, k, d]$ code over $\mathbb{F}_q$. Suppose $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ has minimum weight $d^\perp$. Let $w$ be the largest integer with $w \le n$ satisfying $w = \left\lfloor \frac{w+q-2}{q-1} \right\rfloor < d$.

(So $w = n$ when $q = 2$.) Define $w^\perp$ analogously using $d^\perp$. Suppose that $A_i = A_i(\mathcal{C})$ and $A_i^+ = A_i(\mathcal{C}^\perp)$, for $0 \le i \le n$, are the weight distributions of $\mathcal{C}$ and $\mathcal{C}^\perp$, respectively. Fix a positive integer $t$ with $t < d$, and let $s$ be the number of $i$ with $A_i^+ \neq 0$ for $0 < i \le n - t$. Suppose $s \le d - t$. Then: (i) the vectors of weight $i$ in $\mathcal{C}$ hold a $t$-design provided $A_i \neq 0$ and $d \le i \le w$, and (ii) the vectors of weight $i$ in $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ hold a $t$-design provided $A_i^+ \neq 0$ and $d^\perp \le i \le \min(n - t, w^\perp)$.

**Proof:** Let $T$ be any set of $t$ coordinate positions, and let $\mathcal{C}^T$ be the code of length $n - t$ obtained from $\mathcal{C}$ by puncturing on $T$. Let $\mathcal{C}^\perp(T)$ be the subcode of $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ that is zero on $T$, and let $(\mathcal{C}^\perp)^T$ be the code $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ shortened on $T$. Since $t < d$, it follows from Theorem 1.5.7 that $\mathcal{C}^\perp$ is an $[n - t, k, d']$ code with $d' \ge d - t$ and $(\mathcal{C}^\perp)^T = (\mathcal{C}^\perp)^T$. Let $A_i^+ = A_i(\mathcal{C}^T)$ and $A_i^{\perp+} = A_i((\mathcal{C}^\perp)^T)$, for $0 \le i \le n - t$, be the weight distributions of $\mathcal{C}^T$ and $(\mathcal{C}^\perp)^T$, respectively. As $s \le d - t \le d'$, $A_i^+ = 0$ for $1 \le i \le s - 1$. If $S = \{i \mid A_i^+ \neq 0, 0 < i \le n - t\}$, then, as $A_i^{\perp} \le A_i^+$ and $|S| = s$, the $A_i^{\perp}$ are unknown only for $i \in S$. These facts about $A_i^+$ and $A_i^{\perp+}$ are independent of the choice of $T$. By Theorem 7.3.1, there is a unique solution for all $A_i^+$ and $A_i^{\perp+}$, which must therefore be the same for each set $T$ of size $t$. The weight distribution of $\mathcal{C}^T$ is the same as the weight distribution of $(\mathcal{C}^\perp)^T$; hence the weight distribution of $\mathcal{C}^\perp(T)$ is the same for all $T$ of size $t$.

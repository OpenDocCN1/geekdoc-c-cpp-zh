which implies that \( g_1(x - \alpha) \) is divisible by \( (x - \alpha)^m \). However, \( g_1(x - \alpha) = p(x, f_1(x - \alpha) + \beta) = p(x, f(x)) = g(x) \), showing that \( g(x) \) is divisible by \( (x - \alpha)^m \).

**Lemma 5.4.16** Fix positive integers \( m, t, \text{and } \delta \) such that \( mt > \delta \). Let \( p(x, y) \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y] \) be a polynomial such that \( \gamma(y, i) \) is a root of \( p(x, y) \) of multiplicity at least \( m \) for \( i \le n - 1 \). Furthermore, assume that \( p(x, y) \) has \( (1, k - 1) \)-weighted degree at most \( \delta \). Let \( f(x) \in \mathcal{P}_k \) where \( y_i = f(\gamma_i) \) for at least \( t \) values of \( i \) with \( 0 \le i \le n - 1 \). Then \( y - f(x) \) divides \( p(x, y) \).

**Proof:** Let \( g(x) = p(x, f(x)) \). As \( p(x, y) \) has \( (1, k - 1) \)-weighted degree at most \( \delta \), \( g(x) \) is either the zero polynomial or a nonzero polynomial of degree at most \( \delta \). Assume \( g(x) \) is nonzero. Let \( S = \{i \mid 0 \le i \le n - 1, f(\gamma_i) = y_i\} \). By Lemma 5.4.15, \( (x - y_i)^m \) divides \( g(x) \) for \( i \in S \). As the \( y_i \)s are distinct, \( h(x) = \prod_{i \in S} (x - y_i)^m \) divides \( g(x) \). Since \( h(x) \) has degree at least \( mt > \delta \) and \( \delta \) is the maximum degree of \( g(x) \), we have a contradiction if \( g(x) \) is nonzero. Thus \( g(x) \) is the zero polynomial, which implies that \( y = f(x) \) is a root of \( p(x, y) \) viewed as a polynomial in \( y \) over the field of rational functions in \( x \). By the Division Algorithm, \( y - f(x) \) is a factor of this polynomial.

**Lemma 5.4.17** Let \( p(x, y) = \sum_j \sum_t p_{j,t} x^j y^t \in \mathbb{F}_q[x, y] \). Suppose that \( (\alpha, \beta) \in \mathbb{F}_q^2 \) and that \( p'(x, y) = \sum_a \sum_b p'_{a,b} x^a y^b = p(x + \alpha, y + \beta) \). Then

\( p'_{a,b} = \sum_{j \ge a} \sum_{t \ge b} \binom{j}{a} \binom{t}{b} \alpha^{j-a} \beta^{t-b} p_{j,t} \).

**Proof:** We have

\( p'(x, y) = \sum_j \sum_t p_{j,t} (x + \alpha)^j (y + \beta)^t \)

\( = \sum_j \sum_t p_{j,t} \sum_{a=0}^j \binom{j}{a} x^a \alpha^{j-a} \sum_{b=0}^t \binom{t}{b} y^b \beta^{t-b} \).

Clearly, the coefficient \( p'_{a,b} \) of \( x^a y^b \) is as claimed.

We are now in a position to verify the Sudan-Guruswami Algorithm and give the error bound for which the algorithm is valid.

**Theorem 5.4.18** The Sudan-Guruswami Decoding Algorithm applied to the \( [n, k, n - k + 1] \) code \( GRS_k(\gamma, v) \) will produce all codewords within Hamming distance \( e \) or less of a received vector where \( e = n - \lfloor \delta m \rfloor - 1 \).

**Proof:** We first must verify that the polynomial \( p(x, y) \) from Step II actually exists. For \( p(x, y) \) to exist, \( p(x + \gamma_i, y + y_i) \) must have no terms of degree less than \( m \) for \( 0 \le i \le n - 1 \). \( \sum_{j \ge a} \sum_{t \ge b} \binom{j}{a} \binom{t}{b} \gamma_i^{j-a} y_i^{t-b} p_{j,t} = 0 \quad \text{for all } a \ge 0, b \ge 0 \text{ with } a + b < m. \quad (5.22) \)

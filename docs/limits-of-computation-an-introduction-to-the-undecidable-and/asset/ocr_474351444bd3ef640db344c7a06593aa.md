There are other standard NP-complete problems that look different superficially and (may or not actually) be similar to some that have been discussed. Multiprocessor Scheduling is one; it is actually very similar to Subset Sum.

There are many known NP-complete problems. Garey and Johnson (1979) list hundreds of problems known to be NP-complete at that time. It would be possible to take any—or even all—of the problems listed in that text and assign them as exercises. Some are easy and some are very difficult. The-educated computer scientist does not need to be able to identify NP-complete problems and prove their NP-completeness. He or she needs to have some experience with these problems, be able to guess if a problem may be NP-complete, and have some idea what might predict for solutions.

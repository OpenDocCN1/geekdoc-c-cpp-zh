Games such as chess, checkers, and Go can be generalized to n x n board, easily for checkers and Go with their pieces that are all the same, and less easily for chess with its pawns, rooks, knights, and so forth. Under such generalizations, it is known that the games of Geography and Hex are PSPSPACE-complete, while the games of checkers and Go are PSPSPACE-hard.

# 10.11 THE CLASS EXP

Another interesting class of problems is the set of decision problems that are solvable in exponential time; that is, have time bounded by cpⁿn) for some constant c, and polynomial p, with n the size of the input. This class is naturally called EXPTIME, or simply EXP.

# Facts

NP ⊆ EXP

This was proved above; use transformation from NDTM to TM.

PSPACE ⊆ EXP

This follows directly as corollary to Theorem 10.8.

To reiterate the important point, time and space are related.

P (poly time) ⊆ PSPACE (poly space) ⊆ EXP (exp time)

There is no easy way to find a problem in EXP but proved not in NP or PSPACE. Most problems are produced by diagonalization arguments, so the problem produced is not natural and is easy to describe. The proof that a particular problem is not in either of these classes is really difficult: one that no algorithm can ever be found that will run in polynomial space.

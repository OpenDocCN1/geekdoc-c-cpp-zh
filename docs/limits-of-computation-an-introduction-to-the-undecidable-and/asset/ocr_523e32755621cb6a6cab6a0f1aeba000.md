6.10 P_Rev revised with simple revisions.

Suppose P_Rev halts on P_Rev. That means that when we send P_Rev into P, P analyzed what P_Rev would do on P_Rev and said “stops,” but in the action of P_Rev this has become “while true x=1” infinite loop and P_Rev loops on P_Rev.

Suppose P_Rev never halts on P_Rev. That means that P would have known this by the revision of P_Rev is written “yes, halts” and stops, that is, P_Rev halts on input P_Rev.

In words, P_Rev halts on itself when it does not halt on itself. That cannot happen—so P_Rev, and thus P, cannot exist. Again, this is summarized in Figure 6.10.

This is a version of the halting problem—answering the question whether a Turing machine, or a given program running on some computer, will ever halt. And, as the above discussion shows, this is undecidable.

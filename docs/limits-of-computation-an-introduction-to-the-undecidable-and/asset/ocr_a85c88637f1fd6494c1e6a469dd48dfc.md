# Proof

Left to the reader (see the Exercises). Hint: There are standard transformations from graphs to directed graphs and vice versa. The obvious transformation (one edge becomes two arrows) often works from a graph to a directed graph. For directed to undirected, one can try changing arrows to edges, but this generally loses too much information. A better choice is to replace each vertex by a trio of vertices, so v becomes three vertices $v_{in} - v_{mid} - v_{out}$ connected as shown, and the arrow (w, v) becomes the edge $v_{out}$ to $w_{in}$.

# Corollary 9.16 (K-TSP or Decision Problem TSP)

Decision problem TSP is NP-complete. (Given a weighted graph G with vertices $v_1, v_2 ... v_n$ and edges $e_{ij}$ with positive integer weight $w_{ij}$ on each edge $e_{ij}$, and a bound $K$, does G have a path that starts at $v_1$, visits every vertex of G, and returns to $v_1$ such that the weighted length of the path is less than K?)

It is important to carefully read and fully understand the definitions. At a superficial glance, they may look the same. However, the definition of *accepting* does not require the Turing machine to enter $q_{reject}$ for strings not in L. It only forbids it enter $q_{accept}$. It can do this by entering $q_{reject}$ by moving right forever, alternating between two adjacent tape squares, or by some other set of moves.

And of course, if a Turing machine decides a language L, then it accepts L. If it accepts L, it might also decide L, but would not necessarily do. "Deciding" is a stricter condition than "accepting."

A word on notation: Various authors will use different words for these classes. The following groups cover most of the variations you might meet:

decidable == recursive == Turing decidable

acceptable == recognizable == semidecidable == Turing acceptable == recursively enumerable == RE

The terms recursive and recursively enumerable come from mathematical function. However, since we are not dealing with recursion as most computer science students know the term, we will use the words decidable and acceptable.

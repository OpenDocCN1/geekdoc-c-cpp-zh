# EXERCISES

+   1. Prove that the following sets have the same cardinalities.
   a. The natural numbers (1, 2, 3 ...) and the odd natural numbers (1, 3, 5 ...)
   b. The natural numbers and the rational numbers (n/m, where n and m are natural numbers, m ≠ 0)

+   2. Definition: An algebraic number is any number that satisfies an equation of the form
$$a_nx^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1x + a_0 = 0$$
   where $n > 0$, every $a_i$ is an integer, and $a_n$ is nonzero.
   a. Show that the number $\sqrt{2}$ and the complex number i are both algebraic.
   b. Show that the cardinality of the algebraic numbers is countable.
   c. What does this say about the existence of nonalgeic numbers? Can you produce a nonalgeic number?

+   3. Let $F = \{f: f \text{ is a function from the reals $(\mathbf{R})$ to the reals\}$. Show that the cardinality of F is is greater than $\aleph_1$.

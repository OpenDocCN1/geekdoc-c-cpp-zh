Note: The first subscript i, j is only necessary to differentiate the vertices v from edge $\{i, j\}$ from the six very similar vertices that come from edge $\{i, k\}$. We will drop that pair of subscripts in most of our diagrams. See the simpler figure on the right side of Figure 9.10.

We will call these complicated subgraphs "flags" since they have some resemblance to maritime signal flags.

The flags will be connected to other parts of the graph only at the four corners. This means that any path visiting the nodes in the flag enters at one corner, exits at another corner, and there are only two ways to visit all the nodes in the flag visit half of the vertices on this pass—come at one corner, go straight up or down—and to visit the other side on a different pass (left side of Figure 9.11). Another path might be to come in a corner (for example, upper left), go down, over and up, back across, and out the corner on the same side as the corner one came in on. Of course, this has a similar path coming in upper right. These options are the second and third shown in Figure 9.11.

## Communication styles

In NLP these are referred to as representational systems – for simplicity I'm going to refer to them as our communications styles. It all started when Bandler and Grinder noticed people spoke using three different styles of language – visual, auditory or kinaesthetic. A fourth category 'audio digital' was added. They knew, of course, that people structure their experience of the world through the five senses – seeing, hearing, feeling, tasting and smelling. They began to realise that there was a link to a matching set of 'internal senses' which they later called 'representational systems'. In other words, what we actually perceive are representations or models of what each of our sensory organs transmits to us. Another way of describing this would be preferences in our thinking patterns. If I say to you the word 'sea' what comes to mind? Did you see a picture of the sea, or hear the sound of the waves or remember the feeling of hot sand under your toes? Or recall some statistics about the sea? Your answer will give you a clue to your preferred style. Although we use all of these four styles of communicating, we have a preference towards one.

## Characteristics of the different styles

### Kinaesthetic

Kinaesthetic people take their time to process information because they process through their feelings. They breathe from the bottom of their lungs and tend to speak and move in deliberate way. They may be physically larger than the other styles as they are more alert to bodily needs including hunger and eating. They have a lower pitched voice.

### Auditory

Auditory people tend to be slim and often seen with arms folded and their head tilted to one side as they concentrate on listening to what is said. They breathe from the middle of their chest. They often talk to themselves and are very distracted by noise. For example, one of my NLP trainers, one of my students gave me feedback on the last day that the course had changed her life. They remember things verbally and can repeat back exactly what you said to them. They remember things that were said months or years before. As you might expect, they enjoy talking on the phone and listening to music. They are particularly sensitive to tone of voice and they memorise things by steps, procedures and sequences.

### Visual

Visual people process information quicker than the other styles because they process through pictures in their heads and they can change these pictures very fast. This happens unconsciously. They can get frustrated with the slower pace taken by others. They tend to be thinner than the other styles, maybe because they tend to move around faster. They like to stand tall and often stand back from the crowd so they can see what is going on. They like to keep more personal space than other styles and will move backwards if you get too close. They will want the seat in the restaurant that gives them the best vantage point to watch what is going on. They are often well dressed and like to ‘stand out’ in the crowd and enjoy the people/places/things around them to look good too. They are not good at remembering verbal instructions, preferring to memorise pictures. They tend to breathe from the tops of their lungs and speak faster than the other styles because their voices and breathing have to keep up with their thoughts.

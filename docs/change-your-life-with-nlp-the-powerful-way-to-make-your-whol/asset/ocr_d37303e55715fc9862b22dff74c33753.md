If you focus on and place your attention on what you don't want (see Chapter 4), you are likely to attract exactly that thing to you. How do you avoid this? One way is to consider the worst thing that might happen if you don't get your goal – but only for as long as it takes for you to be OK with that and let it go. For example, I considered what would be the worst thing that could happen if I didn't get my goal of running a successful company. For me that would mean that I would need to go back into paid employment and felt fine. I then put the concept out of my mind and focused all my energy on the success of my company.

# Keep going

John Grinder, the co-creator of NLP, said that in his experience the single key factor which stops people achieving their goal is that they don't have a goal beyond a goal. If there's nothing to aim for, they can become demotivated and even sabotage themselves. For example, let's say my goal is to climb a mountain and I train hard for it over a long period of time. If, when I reach the peak of the mountain, I am not already looking out at the next highest peak I may become demotivated. I may even begin to slide back into the Grey Zone because I have lost focus and direction in my life. So it's very important to enjoy the journey and always be aware of what comes next.

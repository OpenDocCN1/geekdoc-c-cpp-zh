Often we are unconsciously aware of these signals. What's most important is to start to notice minute changes in others from moment to moment and to work out what that means for our communication them. Our internal thoughts are expressed through our external behaviour. They literally 'leak' out of our physiology. Remember we learnt earlier that 55 per cent of the communication is non verbal. However, you also need to learn how to calibrate these changes. For example, if someone is chatting suddenly turns red in the face you might interpret as anger. However, that is your mind-read based on your own experiences and perceptions. You will need to check out your interpretation with them. If you were to say to them something like 'are you ok?' They might answer saying that they suddenly felt hot whilst your mind-read it as 'anger'. Your reread anger would have been incorrect. That's why a sensory description and then calibration is so important. The alternative is your mind-read and imagination which can be dangerous.

# Recognising the non-verbals

What are the kinds of non-verbal reactions to watch out for others? Some of the changes are easier to spot than others. If you are in peripheral vision, you'll find it easier to spot these changes. This is the vision you naturally use when you are driving. It allows you to take in much more information by second by second. When you are driving you need to be aware of what is occurring all around you so you can react quickly if you need to. Sensory acuity is no different. (The opposite of peripheral vision is foveal vision. This is when you focus intently on one thing at a time.)

Things to notice are as follows. Skin colour can change from light to dark or vice versa. The actual tone of the skin might change too. It can go from shiny to not shiny or visa versa.

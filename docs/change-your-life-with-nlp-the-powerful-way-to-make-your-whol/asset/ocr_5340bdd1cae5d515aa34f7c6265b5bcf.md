# Exercise 31

Keeping a diary of your proud moments

Keep a diary for a week of all your achievements during each day. Set yourself the challenge to ensure the list gets longer every day. As human beings, we naturally focus on the things that don’t go plan. If you notice yourself doing that then, each day in your diary, set out two columns — one for the things you were proud of and the other for the things that you do better next time. The only rule is that the list of things you are proud of must be longer.

As you record the things that you are proud of, notice how you begin to build new reference points for your belief system. Remember the analogy of building the house. Create new reference points and the old blocks fall away leaving the building unstable. Perhaps you’ll spot how much more successful than you thought or how good you really are at so many things. I know you’ll notice things for the very first time that previously you were closed to.

# Enlist support when you need it

On your journey towards your goals there will be times when the road seems rough and long. You may need some additional help at moment to keep your momentum going. It saves you valuable time in the longer term.

If you need someone who is an ‘expert’ in the relevant field to work with you this person is called a mentor. A mentor can provide you with technical support to keep you on track. A mentor will assist you in gathering the tools you need to move closer to your goals. In organisations, high-potential staff are often provided with a mentor to guide them. I have worked with a mentor in the writing of this book. She is an expert in the field as she specialises in helping authors to write and publish books. She also has written books herself.

# 4 Reconstruction of an image from its projections

Projection tomography is the process of forming images of the spatial distribution of some parameter of an object by measuring its projections. The physics of the imaging process and the form of the projections depend on the nature of the parameter and the particular measuring equipment.

The term projection is defined as an integral of the parameter values along the path of the probing signal. In general, the path is a line or a curve, but for X-ray, radio, and optical tomography, as well as in many other applications where the probing signal is not deflected or refracted, the path is a straight line. The most familiar example of straight ray projection is an X-ray image used in medical diagnosis. A narrow beam (pencil beam) of X-rays passing through an object undergoes attenuation, which depends on the density and the atomic number of the matter along the beam path. The value measured by a detector behind the object is the result of the total attenuation along the beam path and can be related to the integral of the attenuation coefficient of the object. The result of such measurement is a single projection value. Moving the beam and the detector along a line and repeating the measurement in many positions gives a one-dimensional projection of the attenuation coefficient distribution in a slice

of the object. A series of such projections measured at many directions can be used for the reconstruction of a two-dimensional distribution of the attenuation coefficient. In this book we are interested in such slice images, their reconstruction, and their processing, as described in the following sections.

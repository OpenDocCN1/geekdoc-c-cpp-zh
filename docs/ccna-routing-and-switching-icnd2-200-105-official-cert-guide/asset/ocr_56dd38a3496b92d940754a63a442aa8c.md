A router determines whether a route is a feasible successor based on the feasibility condition:

If a non successor’s RD is less than the FD, the route is a feasible successor route.

Although it is technically correct, this definition is much more understandable with an example. Figure 9-13 begins an example in which router E chooses its best route to subnet 1. Router E learns three routes to subnet 1, with next-hop routers B, C, and D. The figure shows the metrics as calculated on router E, as listed in router E’s EIGRP topology table. Router E finds that the route through router D has the lowest metric, making that router E’s successor route for subnet 1. Router E adds that route to its routing table, as shown. The FD is the metric calculated for this route, a value of 14,000 in this case.

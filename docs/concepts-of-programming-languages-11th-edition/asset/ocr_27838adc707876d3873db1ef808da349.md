When a grammar rule has its LHS also appearing at the beginning of its RHS, the rule is said to be left recursive. For example, the left recursion of the rules of the grammar of Example 3.4 causes it to make both addition and multiplication left associative. Unfortunately, left recursion disallows the use of some important syntax analysis algorithms. When one of these algorithms is to be used, the grammar must be modified to remove the left recursion. This, in turn, disallows the grammar from precisely specifying that certain operators are left associative. Fortunately, left associativity can be enforced by the compiler, even though the grammar does not dictate it.

In most languages that provide it, the exponentiation operator is right associative. To indicate right associativity, right recursion can be used. A grammar rule is right recursive if the LHS appears at the right end of the RHS. Rules such as

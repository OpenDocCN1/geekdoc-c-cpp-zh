Today, there is no significant difference in the way libraries create metadata* and the way they did 100 years ago. Library metadata is still created manually by highly trained professionals. This is expensive and does not scale. We need to think differently. Machine learning offers a potential way forward.

+   - Publishers produce metadata for their own publications
+   - Libraries are still in a manual data entry business
+   - Library standards are complex and expensive to implement

> > ...to organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful.

Search engines and libraries have not reduced the cost of producing metadata. They have simply transferred it from one party to another. The arrival of Google Books did not reduce this cost.

The cost of producing metadata has not changed in 100 years — and neither has the process. The graph above illustrates the trend.

One of the key tasks ahead is to understand what metadata is really needed, and to make its creation more efficient. Artificial intelligence and machine learning can help us automate the more routine parts of the process.

Several initiatives are already emerging in this direction — some from within the library community and some from outside. We will see many more in the coming years.

Figures 10.13(a) and (b) present the cases where SI’s are randomly generated. The PAR parameter for these cases is also selected randomly. Therefore, the performance of F-ECT and B-ECT tend to remain closed to each other. However, it is observed that for low values of NAC, the JIT scheduling algorithm yields a better performance while for high values of NAC, AUS-F outperforms the other algorithms. This is intuitive as low values of NAC correspond to a system with sufficient capacity. In that case, the JIT schedule yields a better QOP. On the other hand, high values of NAC correspond to a capacity deficient system in which controlled filtering of AUSs reduces the overall impact of the QOP. The neuro-fuzzy scheduler learns this distinction effectively and outperforms the rest of the algorithms. Furthermore, the neuro-fuzzy scheduler consistently yields an overall good performance for various values of NAC. We also note that even for the cases in which the neuro-fuzzy scheduler fails to select the winner algorithm, it mostly selects the second best algorithm.

10 The fact a pseudorandom number generator eventually begins to repeat itself is an obvious deviation from randomness; whereas knowing no single value will be repeated during a run through of the sequence may appear to be a more counterintuitive deviation; however, for a truly random sequence, the probability of every value being unique is considerably lower than the probability of at least one value appearing more than once. This is most clearly illustrated by considering the number of possible output sequences and comparing to the number of sequences where every value is unique: even for a random sequence of only eight three-bit numbers, there are over 16 million possible sequences, but only about 40,000 of those contain no repeated values; the chances of such a sequence occurring by chance is slightly less than 0.25%. For longer sequences, the number of permutations quickly become unfathomably large and the probability of a sequence with no repeats vanishingly small.

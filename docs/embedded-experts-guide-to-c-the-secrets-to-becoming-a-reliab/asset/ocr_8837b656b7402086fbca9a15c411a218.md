In practice, multiple phases may be combined into one, but a conforming C implementation is required to behave as though they occur individually and in sequence. We are also not generally concerned with some of the stages – line splicing and string concatenation, for instance, are of little interest to the programmer and serves only to convert source code which was (presumably) intended to be human-readable to a more concise format for the compiler. To simplify matters, we may group the phases into larger stages. There are a minimum of three stages worth of understanding, are these are usually completed by distinct pieces of software, although modern IDEs will generally bundle them together and allow them to be executed as one task as far the programmer is concerned:

+   Preprocessing.

+   Compilation.

As a rule, an ISR should only perform what is absolutely necessary before returning control to the process it interrupted. A UART interrupt may only need to copy data from the UART buffer for instance; actual processing of the data can be deferred until it is required. Where possible, avoid loops and function calls within ISRs as these add additional overhead and loops in particular can introduce a degree of unpredictability into the interrupt timing. Minimising the time spent servicing interrupts prevents the processor from spending too much time in any single interrupt and helps to mitigate the disruption to normal processing caused by servicing interrupts; it also helps to reduce the risk of jitter – where events which are supposed to be periodic are not triggered at the correct time, often because some other process is blocking them.

In extreme cases, usually because of misconfigured software or hardware, an interrupt storm may occur. In these cases, most processor time is allocated to servicing interrupts and other processing progresses very slowly or not at all as a result.

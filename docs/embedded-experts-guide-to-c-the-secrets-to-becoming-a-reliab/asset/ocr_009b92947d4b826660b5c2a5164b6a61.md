Phase 2 of the translation process removes superfluous newlines from the source code. Any instance of a backslash followed by a newline is deleted. This is nothing more than a piece of housekeeping on the part of the preprocessor and of little concern to the programmer.

Preprocessor tokenisation is the next step of translation. Tokenisation is the process of analysing and decomposing source code into its constituent parts – in the same way as a sentence may be analysed to identify nouns, verbs and other types of word, code may be analysed to extract identifiers, operators and other classes of token. The tokenisation process in C is context-sensitive – the token class may not be unambiguously resolved based solely on a sequence of characters. Occasionally this can lead to some cryptic errors, such as a typedef alias being treated as a variable name because an appropriate typedef is not found in the correct place. The fact the tokenisation process is context-sensitive will not stop the code from being interpreted in a different way to what seems obvious to the programmer: just try compiling a program which includes the line a=b/*c; or something similar.

Digraphs are also replaced during the preprocessor tokenisation phase. Digraphs are a slight improvement over the trigraphs replaced in Phase 1, in part because each digraph must form a full token on its own or it will not be substituted. Nonetheless, their use is still discouraged since they do not introduce any new functionality and will tend to confuse programmers unfamiliar with them.

Frequencies with greater deviations from the carrier will result in aliasing; they will be shifted into the range of sampled frequencies and cause corruption of the signal. Conversely, when generating a signal from output samples, the output should be updated at a rate at least twice the desired signal bandwidth. This analysis is most commonly associated with sampling analogue signals, but it is equally true of digital signals as well, although it is the bit rate, which is sometimes confusingly referred to as bandwidth, of a digital signal which is important rather than the bandwidth of the underlying electrical signal. Analogue inputs almost always need to be processed using an analogue low-pass or band-pass filter prior to sampling – these are known as anti-aliasing filters and cannot be replaced by digital filters.

Synchronous buses are those where devices also share a clock signal to control data transfer timings, whereas asynchronous buses control timing by some other means. Many common buses, such as Ethernet and USB are asynchronous, serial buses; although today they are less familiar to the average end-user, RS232 and RS485 are also asynchronous, serial buses and are still used in many embedded systems. Synchronous, serial buses such as SPI and I2C are often found internally within devices. Parallel buses are now much less common than they once were, at least for implementing general purpose communications.

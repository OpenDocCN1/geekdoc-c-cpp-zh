# Chapter 6:
Data Security and Integrity

A topic that is sadly neglected in many software development resources is that of security and integrity. This area is, however, more important than ever – the enormous growth in connectivity over the past twenty years or so now means that many seemingly trivial products have the potential to expose their users to problems ranging from nuisance and inconvenience to severe financial losses or physical harm. You may question the need for a plethora of everyday, Internet connected widgets (quite rightly, if you ask me!), but the fact remains that they are being built and sold. Of course, there are many other reasons why this topic is important – it is just no longer to be considered a specialist topic; it is something that every embedded engineer needs to know about.

First, a couple of definitions:

Integrity is about ensuring that data is “correct” – it has not been corrupted between being transmitted or stored and being received or retrieved. Data that is erroneous should be detected and either corrected where possible or discarded.

Security is about ensuring that data cannot be accessed, or commands issued by, those with no right to do so, either intentionally or by mistake. This includes the subject of encryption – the process of making data completely inaccessible without access to additional information.

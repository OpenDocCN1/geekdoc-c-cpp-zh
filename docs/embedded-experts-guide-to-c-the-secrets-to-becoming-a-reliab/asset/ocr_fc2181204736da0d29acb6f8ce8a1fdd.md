Where available, Direct Memory Access (DMA) can be useful for ADC inputs which require a high sample rate; this allows the CPU to spend less time servicing interrupts and to treat samples in batches rather than individually. Similarly, when oversampling, hardware may be used to sum or average samples without requiring any action from the CPU if such hardware is available. Such features are not supported directly through the C Language, but libraries to provide them should be available where the required hardware is present.

Switches and other similar components such as relays can present an unexpected challenge to those who assume their behaviour is ideal. Although many processors include interrupt-on-change capabilities for digital inputs, these are usually not the best choice for these types of input and periodic polling of the inputs is often more appropriate. The problem is the contacts “bounce” – after changing state, there is a delay before they settle definitively into the new state, resulting in multiple, rapid transitions. This process can take several milliseconds to complete and potentially dozens of transitions can occur during that time. The spurious transitions may be filtered in hardware, but this requires a minimum of two additional components, and probably more, for each input. Alternatively, it is relatively straightforward to periodically sample the inputs and implement debouncing in software in an efficient manner – all switches can potentially be sampled in one go and collectively debounced without requiring any additional hardware.

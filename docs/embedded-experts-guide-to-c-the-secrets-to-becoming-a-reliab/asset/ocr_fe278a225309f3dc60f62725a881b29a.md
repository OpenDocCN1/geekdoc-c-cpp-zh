13 Such requirements are common, although timing is often not so critical for a small mismatch to cause problems (the rationale for using opposing clock edges for read and write operations is to ensure hardware set-up and hold times are satisfied, even allowing for mismatched signal propagation delays). At the data rates where bit-banging is useful you are unlikely to encounter problems, although it is another factor limiting the achievable bandwidth without dedicated hardware. Other requirements may prove much more difficult to ignore. I2C, for instance, relies upon open-drain output drivers being used to negotiate communication speed and bus arbitration (determining which of several devices should be transmitting at a given time); without such outputs implementing a true I2C bus through bit-banging is impossible and may even result in hardware damage; even with them you will still encounter significant challenges.

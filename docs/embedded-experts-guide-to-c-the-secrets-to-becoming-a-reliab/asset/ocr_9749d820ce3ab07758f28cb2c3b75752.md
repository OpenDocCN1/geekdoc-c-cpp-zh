As with inputs, outputs may be updated periodically or only when required and the appropriate choice is largely determined by their purpose. Most outputs will be digital; either single signals which may be either on or off, or multiple outputs, often referred to as an output which may carry binary However, this is not always the case and many platforms include analogue outputs as well.

Simple outputs can include LEDs, sounders, solenoids and These may all be controlled using single outputs which switch them on and off; although it is also possible to vary properties such as the intensity of an LED or speed of a motor by rapidly switching between on and off states, with the relative time in each state determining the output – a technique known as Pulse Width Modulation For outputs requiring limited bandwidth, this can be achieved without any specialist hardware, although PWM modules are a common feature in modern microcontrollers. More precise analogue outputs may be achieved, either by using PWM at a higher switching frequency or by using a dedicated digital-to-analogue converter (DAC).

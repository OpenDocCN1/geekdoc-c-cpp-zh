Although there is a degree of overlap between the two topics, it is important to distinguish between them. Integrity is important to any system where data errors may cause unwanted or potentially dangerous behaviour; the data need not be sensitive and there may be no need for such a system to be secure. Conversely, a secure system may not always need to pay close attention to data integrity even when that data may be regarded as sensitive. However, the two often go hand-in-hand and many of the methods used to ensure data integrity are also useful in ensuring its security.

# Data encoding

Before diving into a discussion of specialist methods used for ensuring the integrity of data, it is worth mentioning the need for data encoding. Many older communications protocols designed for connecting systems were not intended for transmitting arbitrary binary data; they expect to send and receive text, with certain character codes interpreted as control codes. Many assume 7-bit ASCII encoding (or default to it) and any arbitrary data must first be encoded to match. A considerable number of so-called binary-to-text encodings exist for this purpose. Although many newer protocols do not require such encodings, it is still worth having a basic understanding of them when setting up communications between systems; not only are the encodings still widely used to ensure compatibility but encoding data can also be useful when it needs to be mixed with control codes (to indicate the beginning and end of a stream, for instance).

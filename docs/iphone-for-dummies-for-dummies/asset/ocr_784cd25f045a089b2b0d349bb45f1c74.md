If the list is longer than one screen (which it probably is), flick upward to scroll down or flick downward to scroll up. An easier way to find an artist is to tap one of the letters on the right side of the screen to jump directly to artists whose names start with that letter. In Figure 8-2, that letter is M. The letters on the right are extremely small, so unless you have tiny fingers, you may have to settle for a letter close to the one you want or tap several times until you select the correct one.

Our technical editor for previous editions, the late Dennis Cohen, suggested that using a stylus instead of your finger might help. He was right.

But there's an even easier way to find an artist — a song, an album, or a music compilation, for that. Tell Siri what you want to hear. All you have to do is see (“Using your voice to control your music,” Later in this chapter, for details).

If Siri isn't a viable option for you, you can achieve the same effect by typing your query. Tap the lower-right corner and tap in the search field at the top of the screen to activate the virtual keyboard. Now type the name of the artist (or song or album) you're looking for, tap the Search button, and enjoy.

When you tap an artist's name in the main list or in a list of search results, one of two things occurs:

> **If you have songs from more than one album by an artist in your music library:** A list of albums and the songs they contain. Tap a song to play it or tap the first item in the list of songs on a particular album — Shuffle — to hear all songs on that album at random.

> **If all songs in your music library by that artist are on the same album or aren't associated with a specific album:** A list of all songs by that artist appears.

Every way, tap a song and the music starts.

# On the bottom

On the bottom of your iPhone, you find a microphone, the Lightning connector, the speaker (or speakers), and the headset jack (all but iPhone 7 models), as shown in Figure 1-2:

+   - Headset jack (iPhone SE and 6): The headset jack lets you plug in the included iPhone headset, which looks a like EarPods with an elliptical shape.

+   - Microphone: The microphone lets hear your voice when you're not using a headset.

The iPhone sports three microphones (top front, top back, and bottom). The top ones are for FaceTime calls and also work with the main mic (located on the bottom) to suppress unwanted and distracting sounds on phone calls using dual-mic noise suppression or beam-forming technology.

+   - Lightning connector: The Lightning connector has three purposes for most iPhones and for iPhone 7 models. One, you can use it to recharge your battery. Simply connect one end of the Lightning connector-to-USB cable to the iPhone and the other to the USB power adapter. Then, you can use the port to synchronize. Connect one end of the port on your iPhone and the other to a USB port on your Mac or PC. Then, you can use the Lightning or dock connector to connect your iPhone to other devices, such as a camera or television using an adapter such as the Camera Kit or one of Apple's AV adapters. Four (iPhone 7 models only), you can use to connect EarPods or the included 3.5mm headphone jack adapter so you can listen to audio or talk on the phone without holding a slab of glass and metal in front of your face.

Little-known fact: The EarPods with Lightning connector that come with iPhone 7 and Plus will will work with any iOS device with a Lightning connector.

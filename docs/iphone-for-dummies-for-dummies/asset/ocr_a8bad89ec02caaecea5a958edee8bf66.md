# Using your voice to control your music

Here's something cool: You can boss around your music by using nothing but your voice. Just press and hold down the Home button (or the equivalent button on your headset) and, after you hear the tune, you can:

+   - Play an album, an artist, or a playlist: Say "Play" and then say "album," "artist," or "playlist," and the name of the album, artist, or playlist, respectively. You can issue these voice commands at any time when you're on a phone call or having a FaceTime video chat. In other words, you don't have to have music playing for these voice commands to work.
- Shuffle the current playlist: Say "Shuffle." This voice command works only if you're listening to a playlist.
- Find out about the song that's playing: Ask "What's playing?" "What is this song?" "Who is this song by?" Again, these commands work only if you're already listening to music.
- Get creative: In iOS 10, Siri has a much better grasp on music than before, and remember to use her things like these:

+   - Play songs popular in 1973.
- Play music by The Beatles.
- Play the playlist "Music I Love."
- Play James Taylor's first album.
- Play some classic rock.

Siri can interpret commands such as these, and in seconds you'll be listening to exactly what you want to hear. Plus, if you're an Apple Music subscriber, you can ask Siri to play virtually any song in the iTunes Store (30 million plus at last). Finally, because your listening to music on your iPhone, you won't look stupid when you're talking to it

Although we found that controlling your music by speaking words works most of the time, in noisy environments the iPhone may mislead your request and start playing the wrong song or artist or try to call someone on the phone. Using the wired headset helps. And syntax matters, you can use the exact wording in the list.

To divorce a Bluetooth device from the iPhone altogether, tap the i-in-a-circle to the right of the Bluetooth device you're unceremoniously dumping. On the next screen, Forget This Device. At least you won't have to pay alimony.

iPhones support Bluetooth headphones, car kits, and other accessories, so you can stream stereo audio from the iPhone to these devices.

The iPhone can tap into Bluetooth in other ways. One is through peer-to-peer connectivity, which you can engage in multiplayer games with other iPhone users. You can also do things such as exchange business cards, share pictures, and send short notes. And, you don't even have to pair the devices, as you do with a headset or car kit. What's more, you can use an Apple Wireless Keyboard or other Bluetooth to manage more easily on the iPhone.

## Cellular

Several major controls appear under the Cellular setting, as follows:

- **Cellular Data:** Turn off this option if you don't want to exhaust your cellular minutes. Of course, turning off this setting means you can access the Internet only through Wi-Fi.
- **Enable LTE:** You'll find LTE in Settings under Cellular Data Options. You'll appreciate the zippy speeds that LTE (Long Term Evolution) is capable of delivering on your iPhone. If you're concerned about consuming too much data or overtaxing the battery, you may want to turn off LTE times. Here's where you flip the switch for that purpose.
- **Data Roaming:** This is another option found when you tap Cellular Data Options. You can unintentionally rack up lofty roaming fees when using Safari, exchanging emails, and engaging in data-heavy activities while traveling in a foreign country. Turn off data roaming to avoid any excess charges.
- **Personal Hotspot:** If your provider offers tethering, when it's turned on you can share your iPhone's Internet connection with a PC and iOS devices via Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or USB. A fee may be involved; check with your carrier for rates. Depending on your phone model, you may see the Personal hotspot listing under the Cellular setting, as a standalone setting, or both. Benefit: Other devices that you have signed into your iCloud account will be able to tap into your iPhone.
- **Call Time:** As the name suggests, Call Time tells you how much time you've spent on calls.
- **Cellular Data Usage:** This shows how much cellular data you've used.
- **Personal Hotspot:** Other devices that you have signed into your iCloud account will be able to tap into your hotspot.

## Report, Sound, and Appearance

Under Settings, you control what the iPhone reports, sounds like, and looks like.

## Notifications

By swiping down from the top of the screen, you can access notifications. Under Notifications, you turn on or off specific apps. You can also choose the notification style (a banner, which appears at the top, or an alert, which stays there). You may also decide whether to show notifications when the phone is locked.

## Control Center

Even Control Center, which in many cases bypass Settings, has its own place in Settings. Here, you get to choose to allow access to Control Center on the Lock screen and whether to allow access from apps.

## Do Not Disturb

When the Do Not Disturb feature is turned on (from here in Settings or more conveniently from Control Center), the iPhone respects wishes not to be bothered by needy calls or alerts. The moon icon in the status bar reminds you that the feature is turned on. You can schedule the time that the Do Not Disturb is in effect and customize it to allow calls from favorites, designated contacts, or designated groups. You can also turn on a Repeated Calls option that bypasses Do Not Disturb if the same caller rings twice within three minutes. Another decision involves choosing to silence incoming calls and notifications when Do Not Disturb is enabled only when the phone is locked.

## Privacy

Location, location, location. The iPhone makes good use of knowing where you are in the Maps app and several other apps by geotagging photos taken with its camera. It exploits built-in GPS but also by triangulating signals from Wi-Fi and cellular towers and crowdsourcing locations. Bluetooth may be used to determine your approximate position.

If your iPhone knowing your location creeps out a little, don't fret. To protect your right to privacy, individual apps pop up quick messages (similar to the one shown in Figure 14–5), asking whether you want to use your current location.

You can turn off notifications for individual apps. Simply tap an app in the list and turn off sounds.

- 10. If you want high-definition videos you sync to be automatically converted to smaller standard-definition video files when you transfer them to your iPhone, select the Prefer Standard Definition video check box.

Standard-definition video files are significantly smaller than high-definition video files. You'll hardly notice the difference when you watch the video on your iPhone, but you can have more videos on your iPhone because they'll take less space.

That, if you choose to sync video from your iPhone on an HDTV with an A/V adapter cable or wirelessly via AirPlay (as discussed in Chapter 8), you'd notice a big difference.

Finally, if you have a wireless network at home, you don't need to sync video that you intend to watch on your iPhone. Instead, you can stream it from your computer to your iPhone or to HDTV (with an Apple TV), as described in Chapter 8.

> The conversion from HD to standard definition is time-consuming, so be prepared for long times when you sync new HD video and have this option enabled.

- 11. If you want iTunes to automatically create smaller audio files (so you can fit more music on your iPhone), select the Convert High Bit Rate Songs to AAC check box and choose a bit rate from the pop-up menu.

A higher bit rate means that the song has better quality but use more storage space. Songs that you buy in the iTunes Store or on Amazon, have bit rates around 256 Kbps. A 4-minute song with 256 Kbps bit rate is around 8MB; convert the song to 128 Kbps AAC and it becomes roughly half size (that is, around 4MB) while sounding as good.

Most people don't notice (if any) difference in audio quality when listening to music on typical consumer audio gear. Unless you expect to hook your iPhone up to a great pre-amp, amplifier, and superb speakers or headphones, probably won't hear much difference with this option

## Syncing Your Data

Now that your data is backed up and you've completed the prep work, you're ready to sync. The next order of business is to tell iTunes what data you want to synchronize between your iPhone and your computer. You start this process by clicking Info in the sidebar on the left side of the window.

If you're using iTunes 11 or 12.0, do yourself a favor and upgrade to iTunes 12.5 or higher. To see which version of iTunes you have, choose iTunes  About iTunes.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_73_0.png)

Moving right along, the Info pane has three sections: Sync Contacts, Sync Calendars, and Advanced, covered one by one in the following sections.

If you've enabled iCloud to sync contacts or calendars but want to sync these items using iTunes, you must turn off iCloud syncing on your iPhone (choose Settings  iCloud).

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_73_1.png)

If you want to sync reminders, mail settings, notes, Safari bookmarks, or wallet items, you must do so with iCloud, not with iTunes. Tap Settings  iCloud and enable the on/off switch for each item.

## Syncing contacts

The Sync Contacts section of the Info pane determines how synchronization is handled for your contacts. One method is to synchronize all your contacts, as shown in Figure 3–2. Or you can synchronize any or all groups of contacts you've created in your computer's address book program.

The iPhone syncs with the following address programs:

- **Address Book** and other address books that sync with Address Book, such as Microsoft Outlook.
- **Windows**: Vista, 7, 8, 10.
- **Mac**: Entourage, Google.
- **Yahoo!**

Now, here's what each option does:

- **All Contacts**: Sync all your contacts.
- **Selected Contacts**: Sync any or all groups of contacts you've created in your computer's address book program.
- **New Contacts Created on iPhone**: Sync only contacts created on your iPhone.

# Do the Slide for Accuracy and Punctuation

Our first tip can help you type faster in two ways: by helping you type more accurately and enabling you to type punctuation and numerals faster than before. While reading this book, you find out how to tap, double-tap, double-tap with two fingers, and press with 3D Touch. Now we want to introduce you to a new gesture we call the *slide*.

To do the slide, you start by performing the first half of a tap. That is, you touch your finger to the keyboard screen but don't lift your finger. Now, without lifting your finger, slide it onto the key you want to type. You'll know you're on the right key because it pops up and enlarges.

First, try the slide during normal typing. Tap a key. If you miss, rather than lifting your finger, backspacing, and trying again, do the slide onto the proper key. After you get the hang of the slide, you'll see that it saves a lot of time and improves your accuracy as well. Get in the habit of not lifting your finger until you're certain it's on the correct key.

Now here's the best part: you can use the slide to save time with punctuation and numerals. The next time you need to type a punctuation mark or number, try this technique:

1. Start an action with your finger on the 123 key. The 123 key is to the left of the space key when the alphabetical keyboard is active. This is a slide, not a tap, so don't lift your finger just yet.
2. When the punctuation and numeric keyboard appears off-screen, slide your finger onto the punctuation mark or number you want to.
3. Lift your finger.

The cool thing is that the punctuation and numeric keyboard disappears and the alphabetical keyboard reappears — without tapping the 123 key to display the punctuation and numeric keyboard without tapping the ABC key (the key to the left of the space key when the punctuation and numeric keyboard is active).

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_391_0.png)

A set of alternate characters will appear, as shown in Figure 19-1, preventing you from continuing the slide. The keys that react this way are all vowels and some consonants, including N, Z, and L.

In some instances, you'll see a tiny Bluetooth battery status icon at the upper-right corner of the screen, a convenient indicator that lets you know when it's time to recharge your Bluetooth device.

To unpair a device, tap the i-in a circle to the right of the name from the device list in Figure 14-3 and then Disconnect on the next screen. When you return to the prior device list, the word Connected becomes Not. Tap the device to regain access.

To divorce a Bluetooth device from the iPhone altogether, tap the i-in-a-circle to the right of the Bluetooth device you're unceremoniously dumping. On the next screen, Forget This Device. At least you won't have to pay alimony.

iPhones support Bluetooth headphones, car kits, and other accessories, so you can stream stereo audio from the iPhone to these devices.

The iPhone can tap into Bluetooth in other ways. One is through peer-to-peer connectivity, which you can engage in multiplayer games with other iPhone users. You can also do things as exchange business cards, share pictures, and send short notes. And, you don't even have to pair the devices, as you do with a headset or car kit. What's more, you can use an Apple Wireless Keyboard or other Bluetooth to manage more easily on the iPhone.

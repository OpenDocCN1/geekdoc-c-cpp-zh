## TV shows

The procedure for syncing TV shows is almost the same as for syncing movies. First, select the TV Shows pane. Next, select the Sync Shows check box to enable TV syncing. Then choose how many episodes to include from the first pop-up menu and whether you want all shows or only selected from the second pop-up menu. If you want to also include individual episodes or episodes on playlists, select the appropriate check boxes in the Shows, Episodes, and Include Episodes from Playlists sections of the TV Shows.

## Podcasts, iTunes U, and books

You can also sync podcasts, educational content from iTunes U, two types of books — e-books for reading and audiobooks for listening — and phone apps.

If you need to read e-books or listen to podcasts in iTunes U, visit the App Store (Chapter 15) and grab copies of Apple’s free podcasts, eBooks, and iTunes U audiobooks, on the other hand, don’t require a special app; you can listen to them with the Music app (Chapter 8).

Content for the Podcasts, eBooks, and iTunes U is synced in a similar manner to movies and TV shows. The following sections detail the minor differences.

## Podcasts

To transfer podcasts to your iPhone, select the Podcasts pane and then select the Sync Podcasts check box. Next, you can include however many podcasts you want by making selections from the Automatically Include and or Selected Podcasts pop-up menus.

## iTunes U

To sync educational content from iTunes U, first select the iTunes U check box to enable iTunes syncing. Next, from the two pop-up menus, choose how many episodes to include and whether you want all collections or selected collections. If you want to also include individual items from a collection, click the individual items from the collection.

## Books

By now we're sure you know the drill: you can sync e-books and audiobooks by selecting the appropriate check boxes.

If you need to read e-books or listen to podcasts in iTunes U, visit the App Store (Chapter 15) and grab copies of Apple’s free podcasts, eBooks, and iTunes U audiobooks, on the other hand, don’t require a special app; you can listen to them with the Music app (Chapter 8).

## Photos

Syncing photos is a little different from syncing media because your iPhone has a built-in camera.

To sync photos, select the Photos pane and then select the Sync Photos check box. Next, from the two pop-up menus, choose how many episodes to include and whether you want all collections or selected collections. If you've taken any photos with your iPhone or saved images from a web page, an email, an MMS message, or an iMessage since the last time synced, the appropriate program launches (the appropriate folder is selected) when you connect your iPhone, and you have the option of uploading the pictures on your iPhone to computer.

If you choose a folder of images, you can create subfolders inside it that will appear as albums on your iPhone. But if you choose an application that doesn't support albums, or a single folder of images with no subfolders, you have to transfer all or nothing.

Because we selected Photos in the Sync menu, we have the option of syncing any combination.

## Manual Syncing

This chapter has focused on automatic syncing thus far. Automatic syncing is great; it selects items to sync based on criteria you've specified, such as genre, artist, playlist, and album. But it's not efficient for transferring a few items songs, movies, podcasts, or other files — to your iPhone.

The solution? Manual syncing. With automatic syncing, iTunes updates your iPhone to match your criteria. Changes to your library since your last sync are synced automatically to your iPhone. With manual syncing, you merely drag individual items to your iPhone.

Automatic and manual sync aren't mutually exclusive. If you set up automatic syncing, you can still sync individual items manually.

You can manually sync music, movies, TV shows, podcasts, and iTunes U less not photos and info as contacts, calendars, and bookmarks.

Use the On My Device section in the sidebar (if the sidebar isn't displayed, View Show Sidebar) to see which songs, movies, TV shows, and other media are already on your device.


# Adding a title to a recording

When a memo is added to your list of recordings, it shows up with the date and time of the recording and the uninspiring title *New Recording*. You could have named something else at that point, but if you didn't, you might have a tough time remembering which recording was made for which purpose. Fortunately, you can easily create a custom title after the fact:

1. From the list of recordings, tap a recording.
2. Tap the name Apple assigned (New Recording 1, New Recording 2, and so) or the date.
3. Type your name for the voice memo.

Professor Snooks on *Biology*, for example. That's it. Your recording is duly identified.

You may want to share with others the good professor's wacky theories. Tap the share icon for the given voice memo. You then have the option to email the memo or send it in a message. Or you can share it via AirDrop, which we get to later this chapter, or add it to the Notes app or use the Music app. You might also import the recording to a third-party app such as WhatsApp.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_150_0.png)

You can also sync voice memos to your Mac or PC by using iTunes, as described in Chapter 3. Recordings synced from your phone to the computer appear in the Music list and in the Voice Memos playlist in iTunes. And memos synced from your computer appear in the Voice Memos app on the iPhone.

Here's another cool trick you can do with a recording. Email the recording to yourself, download it to a Mac or PC, change the extension to .m4r, and put the recording in iTunes. Presto, you've added a ringtone.

When you have no further use for a recording, you can remove it from the Voice Memos app by tapping it in the list and then tapping the trashcan icon. Alternatively, slide your finger from right to left and tap Delete.

# Reminding me

The Reminders app isn't just any ordinary to-do list: Reminders on the iPhone can be tagged by your location, and it also works nicely with Calendar and Reminders on a Mac running Mountain Lion, Mavericks, Yosemite, El Capitan, Sierra, or iCloud. You can keep reminders on the iPhone, or iCloud can keep reminders in sync across all your iOS devices and computers.

Consider the power of tying a reminder to your location. You'll be notified of items you must pick up at the supermarket the moment you drive into the parking lot. Or perhaps a reminder to call your spouse when you land following a business trip. If you take advantage of Family Sharing, each family member will also receive reminders, some based on location.

Reminders are organized in lists, as shown in Figure 7-13. Apple supplies a list of the current day's reminders. If you've set up Family Sharing, you'll also see a Family list. The number to the right of a list indicates the number of uncompleted items.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_151_0.png)

Figure 7-13: Listing your Reminders lists such as groceries.

You can create your own lists, of course, perhaps one for groceries and another for tasks you must do. Simply tap the + and name your list you want. If you want to color-code your list, tap a dot. When you go into a list, you'll see any due dates you've assigned. To add a reminder to a list, tap a blank line in the list and type the next item to add to the list.

If you want to remove any items, tap Edit and tap the red circle that appears. As an alternative, swipe to the left on an item and tap Delete. You can change the order in which an item appears by dragging the three horizontal lines to the item. When you tap Edit, you can also delete the entire list. As we just mentioned, when you swipe to the left on an item labeled More, a Delete button is summoned. Tap More to display a Details screen. From here you are presented with several choices: You can tap the name of the item in the list to rename it. You can assign a priority to the reminder (using one, two, or three exclamation marks for emphasis). You can add notes to the task (“When you get milk, make sure to buy”). You can also choose a date and time to be reminded, and tack on an alarm if you need.

But the feature that we think is specially cool is that you can also request to be reminded at a location. The location can be your current whereabouts, a previous address, a designated address you type in a search box, or even when you get out of a car paired to your phone via Bluetooth. (Remember to turn on Location Services in Settings, if it's not already.)

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_152_0.png)

Reminders are another great reason to call on Siri. Ask Siri to remind you of something and she (or it) will do your bidding. On the 6s or 6s Plus, SE, 7, and 7 Plus, a Touch Quick Actions shortcut menu lets you add reminders to specific to-do lists.

Reminders is another app that exploits the extra-large display on the iPhone 6 Plus, 6s Plus, and 7 Plus. Rotate each one to landscape mode, and you get panes of information, not one. On the left, you see a list of Reminder headers. On the right, you see the actual reminder within a selected heading.

You can view your reminders and see alerts in Notification Center and the today view. And we want to add our own little reminder that you can take immediate action on any interactive notification pertaining to reminders that you receive. From those notifications, you can mark a reminder as having been completed, or tap to be reminded again.

In Settings and Control Center, you can turn on Do Not Disturb (Chapters 4 and 14) if you don't want to be bothered by a reminder. While in Settings (under the Reminders listing), you can also choose a default reminders list, and determine how far back to sync reminders (from 2 weeks back to 6 months back, assuming that you don't want to sync all your reminders).

## It’s a Wallet

We reckon that most of you never leave the house without three things: your keys, your cellphone, and your wallet. That is a habit unlikely to change anytime soon. Nor do we expect most of you to ditch the plastic cards in your physical wallet for digital versions. That said, the tech and financial worlds are combining efforts to turn modern smartphones such as the iPhone into a version of a digital wallet.

Apple's relatively early push in this area was with the Passbook feature, which arrived with iOS 6 and was renamed Wallet with iOS 9. The introduction of Apple Pay, which launched in October 2014, placed Apple smack in the middle of the mobile payments space, with what potentially is a big deal, especially in the long term.

As of this writing, Apple Pay works with the iPhone 6, 6 Plus, 6s, 6s Plus, SE, and 7 Plus, along with some recent iPad models.

If you have a 3D Touch-capable model, you can press against the Wallet app and summon a Quick Actions menu, allowing you, for example, to view your last transaction. If you have the Apple Watch, check out the Watch app on the iPhone. It is the place to pair your iPhone with your Apple Watch and choose settings and apps for the watch. Activity tied to the Apple Watch also appears on the phone.

But with any of the credit or debit cards stored in the Wallet app on your phone, you can press the Touch ID button on the phone to make a payment at a participating merchant — more than a million retail locations have contactless payments terminals with the NFC (near field communication) capability required to complete transactions in physical stores and are otherwise compatible with Apple Pay. NFC technology is built into all iPhone 6 and 7 models. (Apple Pay works with online transactions.)

With the arrival of iOS 10, you can use Apple Pay on your iPhone to pay for stuff on the Safari website.


## Displaying multiple calendars

By tapping the Calendars button at the bottom of the yearly, monthly, or daily view, you can choose the calendar or calendars to display on your phone. Merely tap the calendar that you want to include such that a check mark appears next to it, as shown in Figure 7-7.

You can tap the Hide All Calendars button when you don't want any calendars to be visible, or conversely tap Show All calendars when you want your entire schedule to be an open book. You can also scroll down to turn events found in the app to display them in your calendar, as well as display birthdays, including those of the people you've friended on the Facebook social network. You can even tap a switch to display events you've declined.

From the Calendars view, tap the *i*-in-a-circle for even more tricks. You can assign a color to your calendar, share the calendar with a given individual (tap Add Person to do), make a calendar public (by flipping a switch), or delete the calendar.

## Responding to meeting invitations

The iPhone has one more important button in the Calendar app. It's the Inbox button, located at the bottom–right corner of the yearly, monthly, and daily views. If you partake in a Microsoft Exchange account, or have a calendar that adheres to the CalDAV standard, you can send and receive a meeting invitation, though you'll also need a compatible email application that lets you only receive but respond to the invitation.

If you have any pending invitations, you'll see them when you tap the inbox, which is separated into new invitations and invitations to which you've already replied. You can tap any of the items in the list to see more details about which you've been invited.

As noted, you can also accept or decline a meeting invitation that arrives via an interactive notification. In other words, you wouldn't have to open the Calendar app to see such an invitation. Suppose that a meeting invitation arrives from your boss. You can see who is attending the shindig, check scheduling conflicts, and more. Tap Accept to let the meeting organizer know you're attending, Decline if you have something better to do (and aren't worried about upsetting the person who signs your paycheck), or tap Maybe if you're writing for a better offer.

And as pointed out previously, you can also invite folks to attend an event that you're putting together.

Meantime, if you run into a conflict, why not ask Siri to change your schedule? For that matter, you can also call Siri to remind you when you have your next appointment. Visit Chapter 5 for this clear feature.

As mentioned, if you take advantage of iCloud, you can keep calendars synchronized between your iPhone, iPad, iPod, and Mac or PC. When you make a scheduling change on your iPhone, it's automatically updated on your computer and other devices, and vice versa. Choose iCloud from the Account Screen to get started, unless you didn't when you activated your phone.

## Subscribing to calendars

You can subscribe to calendars that adhere to the CalDAV and iCalendar (.ics) standards, which are supported by the popular Google and Yahoo! calendars and by the Mac's Calendar app. Although you can read entries on the iPhone from the calendars you subscribe to, you can't create entries from the phone or the entries that are already present.

To subscribe to one of these calendars, tap Settings > Accounts. Then tap Account > Other. Then you choose CalDAV Account or Addscribed Calendar. Next, enter the server where the iPhone can find the calendar you have in mind, and if need, be a username, a password, and an optional description.

## Sharing a family calendar

Through a Family Sharing feature, you can, well, share a calendar with up to five other members. This family calendar shows up in the Calendar app of each participating member of your clan.

The requirements: One in the household (a.k.a. the family organizer) invites everyone to partake. Everyone must be signed into the same iCloud account. Family Sharing works with iOS 8, iOS 9, or iOS 10 devices, Mac OS X 10.10 (or later), macOS Sierra, Find My Friends 3.0 (or later), Find My iPhone 3.1 (or later), and iCloud for Windows 4.0 (or later).

Because Family Sharing is designed to be used by only a single-family unit, you can be part of only one family group at a time, and you can switch to another family group only twice a year. (We suppose you might switch, say, if you married into a new family.)

Each participating family member can add or modify events in the family calendar and receive notifications, alerts, and location-based reminders.

Family Sharing extends well beyond the calendar. Family members can share photos, music, TV shows, books, apps, their respective locations, and even the location of their iPhones (or other devices). Through Parental Controls, the family organizer can limit the content that minors can access.

If you're using Family Sharing and iTunes on a PC or Mac, you can hide-purchased music, TV shows, and movies from other computers. To hide use, iBooks on a Mac or iTunes on a Windows PC. But you can't hide any of your purchased-content directly from your iPhone (or, for that, from an iPod touch or an iPad), unless you're a member of a Family Sharing group.

To get started, tap Settings > Set Up Family Sharing. The family organizer enters his or her.

# WHAT'S IN THE BOX?

Somehow we think you've already opened the elegant box that the iPhone came in. But if you didn't, here's what you can expect to find inside:

- EarPods: Use them for music, videos, and, yes, phone calls. iPhone 7 and 7 Plus have a Lightning connector; all other models have standard 3.5mm headplugs.
- Lightning-to-USB cable: Use this handy cable to sync or charge your iPhone. You can plug the USB connector into your PC or Macintosh to sync or the included USB power adapter. By the way, if you prefer to have your iPhone standing up on your desk while you sync or charge it, check out one of the charging/syncing docks from Apple and others.
- USB power adapter: Use this adapter to recharge your iPhone from a standard AC outlet.
- Some Apple logo decals: Of course.
- A quick start guide: Not to use it. You see the names of the buttons; how to turn on the phone; and where to learn more and get support. We save you time of looking; you can learn more at http://www.apple.com/iphone and get help at www.apple.com/support/iphone. You're welcome.
- iPhone info sheet: Here where you find safety warnings, legality, warranty information, and regulatory compliance de-abble, other things.
- SIM eject tool: Just kidding. Apple once included a SIM eject tool in the box but many years ago. Now — just use a straightened paper clip or safety pin if you need to remove your SIM card. (See Chapter 16 for more on the SIM card.)
- iPhone: You were starting to worry. Yes, the iPhone itself is in the box.

And what a display it is. We venture that you've never seen a more beautiful scene on a handheld device in your life. The accelerometer detects when you rotate the device from portrait to landscape mode and adjusts what's on the display accordingly.

The iPhone's built-in display still knocks your socks off. A proximity sensor detects when the iPhone gets near your face, so it can turn off the display to save power and prevent touches by your cheek. A light sensor adjusts the display's brightness in response to the current ambient lighting situation. The iPhone even has a gyroscope for advanced motion sensing and GPS sensors so your phone can determine where in the world you are. Our favorite example of motion sensing is that our iPhones rarely ask if we want to join a Wi-Fi network when we're in a moving car. That's smart smartphone.

## PART 1: Meet Your iPhone

In this section, we take a brief look at some of the iPhone's features, broken down by product category.

## The iPhone as a phone and a digital camera or a camcorder

On the phone side, the iPhone synchronizes with the contacts and events on your Mac or PC, as well as contacts and events on iCloud, Google, Yahoo!, and Facebook. It includes a full-featured QWERTY soft, or virtual, keyboard, makes typing text easier than ever before — for some folks. Granted, the virtual keyboard takes a bit of time to get used to. But we think that many of you will eventually be whizzing along at a much faster pace than you thought possible on a mobile keyboard of this type.

The 12-megapixel camera (iPhone 6s and newer) is paired with iOS 10's improved Camera and Photos apps, so taking and managing digital photos and videos on your iPhone is a pleasure rather than the nightmare it can be on others. Plus, you can automatically synchronize iPhone photos and videos with the digital photo library on your Mac or PC.

The iPhone 6s Plus has better cameras than the iPhone SE or iPhone 6s, and the iPhone 7 models have the best cameras yet. Of course, both iPhone 7 models include all the features of the previous generation (iPhone 6s and Plus camera), including autofocus with focus pixels to prevent out-of-focus pictures. The 6 Plus and both iPhone 7 models have one of the coolest features we've seen on any camera-phone: optical image stabilization, which uses data from the processor, gyroscope, and motion processor to determine camera motion when you're shooting. It then provides lens movement to compensate for your shaky hands or low light. The iPhone 7 models shoot better low-light photos than we've seen from any previous iPhone, and the iPhone 7 Plus, with two lenses and true optical zoom, shoots the best pictures and videos.

Finally, don't miss the Live Photos feature (all iPhone models), which captures a bit of video before and after the still image. This brings images to life when viewed and may be the coolest iPhone camera feature since, well, last year's optical image stabilization.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_20_0.png)

Before iOS 10, if you chose to edit a live photo with the Photos app, you destroyed its "liveness" and turned it into a still image. With iOS 10, you can edit and adjust photos without turning them into stills. Yeah!

Another of our favorite phone accouterments is visual voicemail. (Try saying that three times fast.) This feature lets you see a list of voicemail messages and choose which ones to listen to or delete without being forced to deal with every message in your voice mailbox in sequential order. Now, that's handy!

Finally, all iPhone models include Siri, an intelligent voice-controlled assistant that understands what you tell him or her (most of the time). We say "him or her" because you can talk to him or her (most of the time).

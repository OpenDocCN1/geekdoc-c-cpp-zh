There's little you can do on your Mac, PC, or Linux computer that you can't control remotely with iTeleport — though of course you're dealing with a smaller screen on the iPhone. If you use iTeleport to check mail accounts other than ones on your iPhones, to grab files from our hard drives and email them to ourselves (at our iPhone email addresses) or others, to make sure backups are running when scheduled.

Another cool use for the app is as a spy cam. This feature requires that you have a computer with a built-in camera, such as an Mac or any of the MacBook models. Open an app that uses the camera (for example, Photo Booth on the Mac or a webcam program under Windows), and you'll be able to watch what's happening in front of that computer on your iPhone no matter where in the world you happen to be.

Several free or less expensive VNC apps are available in the iTunes Store, but iTeleport is one of the only ones we've found that is robust and reliable enough to recommend.

# Baseball Statistics

Consider Baseball Statistics a dream app for a passionate baseball fan. One of the things that makes baseball such great game is the statistics that have defined the sport since, with the 19th century. (We won't add a steroid comment — oops, just did.) Baseball Statistics from Mark Knopper and Bulbous Ventures puts all those stats at your fingertips, so you can settle bettors or just remove favoritos of favorite players when you were a kid.

The app has easily accessible yearly stats for every Major League player and team since 1871 — nope, we weren't around — batting, fielding and pitching statistics to team wins, losses, and attendance. As of this writing, the stats go through the 2015 season — heck, none of the 2016 pennant winners have been declared yet.

If a player has a Wikipedia entry, you can tap a button to see it. A baseball card feature displays a player's career stats, as it when you rotate the iPhone to its side. We only wish we also could get a front baseball-card view with a picture of a clad player, but we're quibbling.

You can also view transactions and what player was involved in, and find links to Baseball-Reference.com, BaseballAlmanac.com, and Retrosheet.org.

# The iPhone as a phone and a digital camera or a camcorder

On the phone side, the iPhone synchronizes with the contacts and events on your Mac or PC, as well as contacts and events on iCloud, Google, Yahoo!, and Facebook. It includes a full-featured QWERTY soft, or virtual, keyboard, makes typing text easier than ever before — for some folks. Granted, the virtual keyboard takes a bit of time to get used to. But we think that many of you eventually be whizzing along at a much faster pace than you thought possible on a mobile keyboard of this type.

The 12-megapixel camera (iPhone 6s and newer) is paired with iOS 10's improved Camera and Photos apps, so taking and managing digital photos and videos on your iPhone is a pleasure rather the nightmare it can be on others. Plus, you can automatically synchronize iPhone photos and videos with the digital photo library on your Mac or PC.

The iPhone 6s Plus has better cameras than the iPhone SE or iPhone 6s, and the iPhone 7 models have the best cameras yet. Of course, both iPhone 7 models include all the features of the previous generation (iPhone 6s and Plus camera), including autofocus with focus pixels to prevent out-of-focus pictures. The 6 Plus and both iPhone 7 models have one of the coolest features we've seen on any camera-a-phone: optical image stabilization, which uses data from the processor, gyroscope, and motion processor to determine camera motion when you're shooting. It then provides lens movement to compensate for your shaky hands or low light. The iPhone 7 models shoot better low–light photos than we've seen from any previous iPhone, and the iPhone 7 Plus, with two lenses and true optical zoom, shoots the best pictures and videos.

Finally, don't miss the Live Photos feature (all iPhone models), which captures a bit of video before and after the still image. This brings images to life when viewed and may be the coolest iPhone camera feature since, well last year's optical image stabilization.

To get at these, tap the third icon at the bottom of the display, which appears just to the left of the Done button. From there, tap Light, Color or B&W to selecting the control you want to mess with. Then drag the slider at the bottom of the screen in either direction until you reach a level you're satisfied with — you see the effect on the image as you drag the slider. Tap Done when you're finished or Cancel to start over.

You can fine-tune these Light, Color, and B&W by summoning settings within. Do one of the following:

+   - If you tapped the Light, Color, or B&W as mentioned — again this is the icon found to the left of the Done button — tap the circled downward arrow anywhere in Light, Color, or B&W.

+   - If you already edited an editing control so one of the sliders is visible, tap the icon that replaces three lines precedes a dot.

If you aren't satisfied with any of the edits you've applied to your pictures, you can always tap Cancel and then Discard Changes or tap Revert and then Revert to Original.

You see a live photo when you tap Edit on a photo shot with the Live Photo feature enabled on the 6s, 6s Plus, SE, 7 and 7 Plus. As of iOS 10, you could apply various edits to live photos, both video and style.

Apple allows third-party apps to make their own filters and editing tools readily accessible from the Photos app. The last icon you'll see the word Done at the bottom of an editing controls shows three dots in a single circle. Tapping this lets you mark up a photo, which we touch on further in Chapter 12. It also gives you access to any third-party editors that you added to the phone.

We told a tiny fib by intimating that photographs are meant to be seen. We should amended that statement by saying that some pictures are meant to be seen. Others, you can't get rid of them fast enough. Fortunately, the iPhone makes it a cinch to bury the evidence:

+   1. Tap the objectionable photograph.
2. Tap to display the picture controls, if they're not already displayed.
3. Tap the trashcan icon.
4. Tap Delete Photo (or Cancel, if you change your mind).

The photo gets sucked into the trashcan and mercifully disappears. It's also deleted from your iCloud Photo Library across all your devices.

# The incredible, intelligent, and virtual iPhone keyboard

Before you consider how to use the keyboard, we'd like to share a bit of the philosophy behind its so-called intelligence. In fact, with iOS 10, the QuickType keyboard managed to raise its IQ. For one thing, it leverages Siri. Now, it relies on deep neural network technology, which sounds as smart as it is. Knowing what makes this keyboard smart will help you make it even smarter when you use it. The iPhone keyboard

+   - Uses a bundled English dictionary that even includes words from today's popular culture.
- Adds your contacts to its dictionary automatically.
- Uses complex analysis algorithms and the aforementioned neural technology to predict the word you're trying to.
- Suggests corrections as you type, and then offers the suggested word just below the word you typed.
- Reduces the number of mistakes you make as you type by intelligently and dynamically resizing the touch zones for certain keys. The iPhone increases the zones for keys it predicts might next and decreases the zones for keys that are unlikely to come next, though you can't see these changes.

# Anticipating what comes next

The keyboards on your iPhone have become even more useful through generations of iOS. These days, the keyboard takes an educated stab at the next word you mean to type and presents what it surm to be the best possible word choices front center. Say you're preparing a note in the Notes app. You start typing. *This is a*. Above the row of keys in the iPhone keyboard, you'll see buttons with three suggestions: *great, good,* and very (as shown in Figure 2–4). If one of those were the appropriate response, you could tap the button to insert its text into your reply.

Of course, if you wanted to respond with something different than the three options presented by Apple, just type your response with the regular QWERTY keys. As you add additional letters and words, the three suggested choices above the keyboard change in real time.

Such QuickType keyboard predictions vary by app and by whom you are communicating. So the predictive text choices that show up in Messages when you're involved in an exchange with a friend are likely to be more casual than the those in an email to your boss.

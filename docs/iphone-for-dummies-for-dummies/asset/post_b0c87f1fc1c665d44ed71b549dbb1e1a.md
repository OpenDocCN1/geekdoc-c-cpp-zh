# Recents

Tapping the Recents icon displays the iPhone call log. The Recents feature logs logs of all, well, recent calls made or received, as well as calls you missed. Tap to show all the recent calls or missed to show just what you missed. Under the All list, completed calls and missed calls that have been returned by tapping the red entry are shown in black, and missed calls that haven't been returned in this fashion are red, with a descriptor of the phone you were calling or received a call from (home, mobile, or location). You also get an indicator of whether the call was a phone call or FaceTime call.

By tapping the $i$-in-circle to an item in the list, you can find out the calls were made or missed, as well as any new info about the caller from your Contacts information. Also shown is the length of the time you spoke to the person, assuming a conversation took place.

To return a call, tap anywhere on the name.

If one of the calls you missed came from someone who isn't already in your Contacts, you can add that person. Tap the right-pointing arrow, and then tap Create New. If the person is among your contacts but has a new number, tap Add to Existing Contact and choose the contact from your list.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_89_1.png)

Tap Share to share a contact with a friend, family member, or colleague. You have the option to send that person an email or a message (MMS, text, or iMessage message). The contact info is already embedded in the message that you send.

You can share with people also using the wireless AirDrop feature, which works on the iPhone 5 and later models. For more on AirDrop, read Chapter 7.

You can clear an individual entry from the Recents list by swiping right to left on a name and tapping the red delete button. Or tap Edit, the red circle next to an entry, and then tap the red delete button.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_89_2.png)

Meantime, if you press down on a name or number in the Recents list on a 3D Touch–capable iPhone, you can add the person as a contact or modify an existing contact. You can also use alternative ways to connect to that person again, perhaps via an iMessage or FaceTime.

On the subject of 3D Touch, you can press against the iPhone docked at the bottom of the display to call upon a Quick Actions menu with initials representing names of some favorites you might want to call or search for a contact or create a contact. You can also view your most recent call.

# Recents

Tapping the Recents icon displays the iPhone call log. The Recents feature logs logs of all, well, recent calls made or received, as well as calls you missed. Tap to show all the recent calls or missed to show just what you missed. Under the All list, completed calls and missed calls that have been returned by tapping the red entry are shown in black, and missed calls that haven't been returned in this fashion are red, with a descriptor of the phone you were calling or received a call from (home, mobile, or location). You also get an indicator of whether the call was a phone call or FaceTime call.

By tapping the $i$-in-circle to an item in the list, you can find out the calls were made or missed, as well as any new info about the caller from your Contacts information. Also shown is the length of the time you spoke to the person, assuming a conversation took place.

To return a call, tap anywhere on the name.

If one of the calls you missed came from someone who isn't already in your Contacts, you can add that person. Tap the right-pointing arrow, and then tap Create New. If the person is among your contacts but has a new number, tap Add to Existing Contact and choose the contact from your list.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_89_1.png)

Tap Share to share a contact with a friend, family member, or colleague. You have the option to send that person an email or a message (MMS, text, or iMessage message). The contact info is already embedded in the message that you send.

You can share with people also using the wireless AirDrop feature, which works on the iPhone 5 and later models. For more on AirDrop, read Chapter 7.

You can clear an individual entry from the Recents list by swiping right to left on a name and tapping the red delete button. Or tap Edit, the red circle next to an entry, and then tap the red delete button.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_89_2.png)

Meantime, if you press down on a name or number in the Recents list on a 3D Touch–capable iPhone, you can add the person as a contact or modify an existing contact. You can also use alternative ways to connect to that person again, perhaps via an iMessage or FaceTime.

On the subject of 3D Touch, you can press against the iPhone docked at the bottom of the display to call upon a Quick Actions menu with initials representing names of some favorites you might want to call or search for a contact or create a contact. You can also view your most recent call.

Setting up Favorites is a breeze. When looking at one of your contacts, you may have noticed the Add to Favorites option. When you tap this, all the numbers you have for that pop up. Tap the number you want to make into favorite, and it turns up on the list.

You can get to your contacts also by tapping + in the upper-left corner of the screen.

If any of your chosen folks happen to fall out of favor, you can easily kick them off the Favorites roster. Here's how:

- 1. Tap the Edit button in the upper-right corner of the screen.
  Note that a red circle with a horizontal line appears to the left of each name in the list.
- 2. Tap the circle next to the name getting the leave. Swipe from right to left on the person's name.
  The swiping gesture is common throughout your iPhone experience. A red Delete button appears to the right of the name, as shown in Figure 4-2.
- 3. Tap Delete.
  The person (or one of his given phone numbers) is no longer afforded the privilege of being in your iPhone inner circle.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_88_0.png)

Booming off the Favorites list does not remove that person from the main Contacts.

You can rearrange the order in which your favorites are displayed. Tap Edit, and to the right of the person you want to move, press your finger against the symbol that looks like three stacked horizontal lines. Drag that symbol to the place on the list where you want your favorite contact to appear. Tap Done when the listings are as you like them.

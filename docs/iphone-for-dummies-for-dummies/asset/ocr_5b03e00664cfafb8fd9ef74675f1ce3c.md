Tap the years view (Figure 9–9, left), and slightly bigger thumbnails appear as part of the collections view (Figure 9–9, center). Tap again, and the thumbnails get just a little bigger in the moments view (Figure 9–9, right).

Through all these views, you'll see location information headings that get tad more specific as you move from years to collections, assuming your phone knows where the pictures were taken. (Location Services must be turned on under Privacy Settings.)

To quickly skim all the pictures in the years or collections views, press and drag your finger across the grid – as you do so, the thumbnails shrink in size, one by one. Be careful not gently if you're using an iPhone with Touch ID. Lift your finger (or press down hard) a chunk of the screen, ready for you to edit it, or share it.

You can also tap a thumbnail in moments views to see controls for sharing the picture, making it favorite (the heart icon), editing it, or discarding it (bottom), as in Figure 9–10. Tap again and those controls appear and the picture is bordered on the top and bottom by black bars.

Apple summarizes photos in the collections and years by showing you only a representative sample for those collections and years. To see every photo in your library that fits those, go to Settings > Camera and turn on Summarize Photos by tapping the switch to make it.

# Searching for pictures

iOS 9 and 10 have one more feature to help you find a given photo among the thousands or tens of thousands you've shot. You can search your entire photo library in the cloud. From the Photos app, tap the search icon, one that resembles a magnifying glass.

Apple has grouped some of your pictures into potentially helpful search categories: Nearby, Home, those taken from a specific period or location, and Favorites, which are designated each time you tap the aforementioned heart icon

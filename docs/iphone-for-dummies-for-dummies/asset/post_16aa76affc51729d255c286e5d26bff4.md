# Alarm clock

Ever try to set the alarm in a hotel room? It's remarkable how complicated an alarm can be, even on the most inexpensive clock radio. Like almost everything else, the procedure is dit-simple on the iPhone:

- 1. Tap Clock on the Home screen to display the Clock app.
- 2. At the bottom of the screen, tap the Alarm icon.
- 3. In the upper-right corner of the screen, tap the + icon.
- 4. Choose the time of the alarm by rotating the carousel controls in the upper half of the screen.

This step is similar to the action required to set the time that an event starts or ends on your calendar.

- 5. If you want the alarm to go off on other days, tap Repeat and tell the iPhone the days you want the alarm to be repeated, as in Every Monday, Every Tuesday, and so on.
- 6. Tap Sound to choose the ringtone (see Chapter 4) to use to wake you up.

You can use a custom ringtone you created or choose a song from Music library.

- 7. Tap Snooze to display a Snooze button along with the alarm.

Tap the Snooze button to shut down the alarm for nine minutes.

- 8. If you want to call the alarm something other than um, Alarm, tap the Label field and use the virtual keyboard to type another descriptor.

- 9. When the alarm settings are to you want really simple, like Save.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_146_0.png)

Simple stuff, really. If you want really simple ask Siri to set the alarm for you.

By now you surely appreciate 3D Touch on the 6 and 7 models affords you other shortcut options. So on either of these models, you can press against the Clock icon to dial a Quick Actions menu that lets you learn or start the stopwatch, the timer, topics that are coming up in the next two sections.

You know that an alarm has been set and activated by the tiny status icon (surprise, surprise — it looks like an alarm clock) that appears in the upper-right corner of the screen.

An alarm takes precedence over any tracks you're listening to your iPhone in the Music app. A song momentarily pauses when an alarm goes off and resumes when you turn off the alarm (or tap the Snooze button).

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_146_1.png)

When your ring/silent switch is set to silent, your iPhone doesn't ring, play alert effects, or make iPod sounds. But it will play alarms from the Clock app. That's good to know when you set your phone to silent at a movie or an opera. And, although it seems obvious, if you want to hear an alarm, you have to make sure that iPhone volume is turned up.

If you've set an alarm, it takes precedence over the Do Not Disturb feature we de-cuss in Chapters 4 and 14.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_146_2.png)

Not all phone carriers support the network time option in all locations, or an alarm may not sound at the correct time in a given area.

# Stopwatch

If you're helping a loved one train for a marathon, the iPhone Stopwatch function can provide an assist. Open it by tapping Stopwatch in the Clock app.

Just tap Start to begin the count, and then tap Stopwatch at the finish line. You can also tap a Lap button to monitor the times of individual laps. If you don't like your results, tap the Reset button after tapping Stopwatch and pretend that your less-than-gold-medal-winning performance never happened.

# Timer

Cooking a hard-boiled egg or Thanksgiving turkey? Again, the iPhone comes to the rescue. Tap Timer (in the Clock app) and rotate the hour and minute controls until the time you have in mind is selected. When Timer Ends to choose the ringtone that will signify time's up.

After you set the length of the timer, tap Start when you're ready to begin. You can watch the minutes and seconds down on the screen, if you have nothing better to do.

If you're doing anything else on the iPhone — admiring photos, say — you hear the ringtone and set a Timer Done notification on the screen at the appropriate time. Tap Stop to silence it.

You can also set a timer in the iPhone to shut down music or video after you've shut down. Set the amount of time that you think'll want the iPhone to be spitting out sound, and tap Timer Ends. Then stop Playing. The iPhone will be silenced by the time you've been exhausted.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_147_0.png)

Apple considers the Timer app to be so essential that it deserves a spot for its icon in Control Center. And when you open Timer, you can also access the other components of the Clock app: World Clock, Alarm, Stopwatch, and the newest addition, Bed.

# Bedtime

The aptly named Bedtime feature, inside the Clock app, is all about getting a healthy and consistent night's sleep. To get started, you're asked a few simple questions, such as what time you would like to wake up, which days of the week the alarm should go off (if it is every day), and how many hours of sleep you need each night. You choose when to receive a bedtime reminder and which tone to hear when you wake up.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_147_2.png)

# Voicing Memos

Consider all the times you'd find it useful to have a voice recorder in your pocket — perhaps when you're attending a lecture or interviewing an important source (that's a big for us journalist types). Or maybe you just want to leave yourself a quickie reminder about something (“Pick up milk after work”). Well, you're in luck. Apple has a built-in digital voice recorder.

A bunch of third-party apps add voice recording to the iPhone. Ed sometimes uses Recorder from Retronyms and Dictation from Nuance, and Bob uses iTalk Recorder from Griffin.

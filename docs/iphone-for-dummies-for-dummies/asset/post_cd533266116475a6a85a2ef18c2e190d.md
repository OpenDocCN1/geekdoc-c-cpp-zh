
When Family Sharing has been implemented, you can all share a calendar, photos, reminders, and your respective locations. Family Sharing may also help find a missing device through Find My iPhone.

Should you leave Family Sharing, your account is removed from the group and you can no longer share content with everybody. You won't be able to use DRM-protected music, movies, TV shows, or apps that another member purchased. And you won't be able to access the family calendar, reminders, or photos.

## Facebook

Most of us know Facebook as a great service to help you stay in touch with relatives, associates, and old pals, rekindle those relationships, and make new friends. But some people collect Facebook friends like baseball cards. If it seems like you're acquainted with all 1.7 billion or so counting members of the mammoth social network, know what you mean.

Fortunately, Apple organizes your Facebook relationships on the iPhone. If you turn on the Calendar and Contacts settings under Facebook settings on the iPhone, your Facebook friends automatically populate your Contacts list, complete with their profile pictures as well as their email addresses and phone numbers (if they made them on Facebook). Birthdays and calendar appointments appropriately turn you in the iPhone Calendar app.

Think of these Facebook entries as synced contact entries. If a person changes his phone number and email address on Facebook, that change will be reflected on your iPhone, provided you have Wi-Fi or cellular coverage or the next time you have coverage. And if your friends de-friend you — what did you do, anyway?— their contact info will disappear altogether.

If the iPhone can correctly match a Facebook friend entry with an existing contact entry, it will try to unify contact under a single view. Meanwhile, the Update All Contacts option under Facebook settings on the iPhone serves a slightly different-purpose. It tries to add Facebook profile information to your contacts who are on Facebook but not your Facebook friends.

## Sorting and displaying contacts

Do you think of us as Ed and Bob or Baig and Levit? The answer to that question will probably determine whether you choose to sort your Contacts list by last name or first name.

Tap Contacts; scroll down to the Contacts section; and peek at Sort Order. Then tap Last, First or First, Last. You can determine whether you want to display a first name or last name by tapping Display Order and then choosing First, Last or Last. You can also go with a Short Name setting that lets you fit more names on the screen. (Chinese, Japanese, and Korean names are not affected.) You can choose a first name and last initial, first initial and last name, first name only, or last name only. If you prefer nicknames, you can choose those.

You'll also find a Contact Found in Mail switch. If you turn it off, any unconfirmed contact suggestions are deleted. You won't see suggestions when you try to complete a mail address, on the incoming call screen, and in the Contacts app.

In My Info, make sure your own name is chosen so that Siri knows where you live, among other reasons.

You can also import contacts that may be on a new SIM card that you inserted into the phone.

## Nothing phone-y about these settings

In Chapter 4, we tip our hand and indicate that we save a few more phone tricks — those found in Phone settings — for this chapter. Tap Phone now to review of the choices we don't get to in that chapter. Be aware that certain options may vary by carrier and that you have to scroll down the screen to find Phone settings:

- **Wi-Fi Calling:** Tap the switch here to make and receive calls over Wi-Fi. Consult your carrier to make sure this option is available.
- **Allow calls on other devices:** Through what Apple refers to Continuity (of which the Handoff setting you met earlier in this chapter is a part of), you can share your iPhone's cellular connection to make and receive calls with other Apple devices on the same Wi-Fi network. Select your Macs, other phones, and iPads here.
- **Respond with text:** If you're tired and can't take a call but want the wondering whether you're avoiding him or her, you can respond with canned responses as "on my way" and "Can I call you later?”
- **Call forwarding:** If you expect to spend time in an area with poor or no coverage, you may want to temporarily forward calls to a landline or other portable handset. Simply do the following:
    1. On the Settings screen, tap *Call Forwarding*.
    2. Tap the switch to turn on *Call Forwarding*.
    3. Use the virtual keypad to enter the number where you want incoming calls to ring.
    4. Tap Phone to return to the main Phone settings screen.
  To change the forwarding number, tap the circle with the X in the Phone Number to get rid of the old number, and then enter a new one.
  Remember to turn off Call Forwarding to receive calls directly on your iPhone again. You must have cellular coverage while setting the Call Forwarding feature.
- **Call waiting:** Tap the Call Waiting button to turn on the feature or off. If Call Waiting is off and you're speaking, the call is goes to voicemail.
- **Show My Caller ID:** Don't want your name or number displayed on the phone when you're calling? Make sure to tap Caller ID and turn off.
- **Blocked:** By adding people from your contacts to Blocked list, you'll longer receive calls, messages or FaceTime.
- **TTY:** Folks who are hearing impaired sometimes rely on teletype, or TTY, to hold conversations.
- **SIM PIN:** The tiny SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) inside your iPhone holds your phone number and other data. Tap to turn on SIM PIN and enter a password with the Keypad. If you assign a PIN to your SIM, he can't use it on another phone without the password.
- **Change voicemail:** This setting is straightforward enough. Change Voicemail Password, type your 4-digit password, and tap Done. Then type the new password and tap Done.
- **Carrier services:** A major difference between the iPhone and other Apple products you buy is that you enter into a relationship with not only Apple but also the company when you have an iPhone. Under this setting, you can check your balance and pay your tab; access other services your carrier may provide.

## Find My iPhone

We hope you never have to use the Find My iPhone feature — though it's pretty darn. If you inadvertently lose your iPhone in a taxi or restaurant, Find My iPhone may help you retrieve it. It helped Ed retrieve a phone that slipped out of his pocket on a bus.

To find My iPhone, open Settings and tap iCloud. Make sure the Find My iPhone setting is turned on. Now suppose that you lose your phone — and we only assume that you're beside yourself. Log in to your iCloud account from your computer, and click Find iPhone.

Apple now supplies a free Find iPhone app for iOS. It hangs out in the Extras folder. If you don't have iPhone on your phone, you can grab it in the App Store, where it is still called the Find My app. So you could use an iPhone loaded with this app to locate another iPhone (or iPad, iPod touch, or Mac).

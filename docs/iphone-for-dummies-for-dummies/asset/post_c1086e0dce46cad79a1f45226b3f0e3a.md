## MMS: Like SMS with media

To send a picture or video in a message, follow the instructions for sending a text message, before you tap Send, tap the camera icon to the left of the text-entry field. You will then have the option of using an existing picture or video or taking a new one. You can add text to photos or videos (by typing in the text-entry field) before or after you select them. When you're finished, tap the Send button.

If you receive a picture or video (or voice message) in an iMessage, it appears in a bubble like this. Tap the element to see it full-screen.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_126_1.png)

If you enable the Raise to Listen option in Settings: Messages, you can quickly listen to and reply to a voice message by merely raising your iPhone to your ear. Neat!

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_126_2.png)

Tap the share icon in the lower-left corner (and shown in the margin) for options including Message, Email, Twitter, Facebook, Save Image, Assign to Contact, Copy, and Print. If you have an iPhone 5 or newer, you'll also see an option to share the photo or video via AirDrop (see chapter 7).

If you don't share the icon, tap the picture or video once and the icon will magically appear.

When you're viewing an image or video, tap the list icon in the lower-right corner (and shown in the margin) to see every image from this conversation. Tap a picture to view it; swipe left or right to see earlier or later videos.

## Massive multimedia effects overhaul

iOS 10 includes the biggest batch of new Messages features since, well, ever, including new tapbacks, bubble and screen effects, handwriting, annotation, Digital Touch, the fastest and easiest way we've ever seen to replace words with emojis. The Messages app in iOS 10 also introduces a new open architecture and built-in App Store for third-party add-ons, including stickers and additional emojis.

Here's the scoop:

- **To comment on a message with a tapback:** Press and hold on the message until the tapback bubble appears, as shown in Figure 6-6.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_127_0.png)

If you change your mind and want to dismiss the tapback bubble without adding a tapback icon to the message, just tap the screen anywhere outside the tapback bubble.

Tapback works only with other devices running iOS 10 or macOS Sierra. If the recipients are using any other operating system, they won't see any cute balloons, *Your-name-here Loved/Disliked/LaughedAtEmphasized/Questioned item-name-*.

- **To replace text with an emoji:** Type your message and tap the emoji keyboard icon. As soon as you do, words that have emoji turn orange; tap an word to replace it with an emoji. If the word is associated with more than one emoji, you'll see multiple emoji in a bubble; tap the one and it replaces the word.

## Group messaging has never better

One-on-one conversations in Messages are good, conversing with a group is even better.

To start a group, just add two or more people in To field. That, group conversations work the way as individual messages, But we'd be remise if we didn't mention a couple of innovations that make group messaging even.

Tap Details in the upper-right corner of any group conversation. In addition to the features described (initiate a phone call or FaceTime chat, send your current location, and so on), you see a Leave Conversation button (below the Do Disturb switch). Now imagine that you're part of a lively conversation with a handful of friends. Enable Do Disturb.

## Taking Note of Notes

The Notes app gets a major overhaul in iOS 9, and not much has changed. You can create notes that you can save or send through email (iPhone 5 and later), capture maps, photos, URLs, draw with your finger and easily locate photos, maps, and anything else you attach using Attachments.

Start by tapping the Notes icon on the Home screen. If your iPhone has 3D Touch, you can press the Notes icon and use the quick action to open directly to a new blank note, a photo, or a sketch.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_130_0.png)

## Categorizing your pics

Placing pictures into photo albums seems to us like it's been the way of the world forever. But albums per se are not the only organizing structure that makes sense. Apple has cooked up a simple but ingenious interface for presenting pictures that is essentially a timeline of pictures, grouped by years, collections, and moments.

Pictures categorized by years are indeed all the pictures taken in a given year. Can't be more straightforward than that.

The collections are a subset within a year, such as your holiday pictures in Las Vegas. Within that grouping is another subset called — the pictures, say, that you took by the dancing fountains at the Bellagio Hotel.

## Sharing your photos

Apple in its infinite world recognizes that you might want to share your best images with friends and family and have those pictures automatically appear on those's devices.

An impressive and aptly named solution called Shared Photo Streams arrived on the iPad, iPod, and iPhone back in iOS 6 (and a bit earlier on Macs running OS Lion). It was modified in iOS 7 and now referred to as iCloud Photo Sharing. The feature enables you to share pictures and videos with other folks and lets you receive photo streams that they make available to you. Here's how:

1. On the Home screen, tap Settings.
2. Tap Photos & Camera.
3. If the iCloud Photo Sharing isn't on, tap it to turn it on.
4. Open the Photos app, and then tap the Shared tab.
5. Tap the + at the upper-left corner of the screen, and then type a name for your stream in the iCloud dialog that appears.
6. Tap Next and choose who will receive your shared photos, as shown in Figure 9-11.
7. Above the To field, tap Create.

The name is yours, but we recommend something descriptive, like the lines of My Trip to Tahiti (and you should be so lucky).

You can type a phone number, a text address, or an email address, or choose one of your contacts by tapping the + in a circle in the To field.

8. Add photos to the shared album by selecting the album, making sure the Photos tab at the bottom of the display (as opposed to the adjacent People tab) is selected and tapping +. You can choose from years, collections, or moments.
9. (Optional) Enter a comment.
10. Tap Post.

The recipient will receive an email similar to the one in Figure 9-12 and can choose to subscribe to the album by tapping the button shown.

You can share photos and videos with pretty much anyone who has online access — people don't need to join iCloud. If you want to share your pictures with everyone, you can do so through a public gallery on iCloud.com. To do that, tap the Shared icon at the bottom of the Photos app and then tap the album in question. This time, tap the People tab instead of the Photos tab and flip the Public Website switch to on.

If the people whom you're sharing have their own iCloud accounts and are on an iOS 6 account or later using a Macintosh computer running OS X Mountain Lion, Mavericks, Yosemite, or El Capitan, they cannot not glom onto your photos but also leave comments about them. At your discretion, you can also receive notifications when subscribers weigh in with a comment or add their pictures.

If you're ultimately unhappy with the shared album or the people with you you're sharing it, you can kill the shared album or kick those people off the list. To kill the album, tap the Delete Shared Album button. To remove a subscriber, tap the shared album, tap the People tab, and tap the name of the person with whom you're sharing. Remove Subscriber. If you share a live photo with people who have another Apple device (including the Apple Watch with watchOS software), they can press against the screen to view the living photo. You can share it with them via iMessage, iCloud Photo Sharing, or AirDrop. If you share it any other way, the live photo is sent as a still photo.

# Dropping in AirDrop

At various points in this book, you encounter AirDrop, a fast, safe, and secure (through encryption) wireless method of sharing photos, videos, contacts, documents, and more with people you are close to physically. You just tap the share when it is available in an app.

AirDrop exploits both Wi-Fi and Bluetooth so sure both have been turned on for it to work properly. No other device setup is required.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_154_0.png)

To be part of an AirDrop exchange, you and the recipient must be iOS 7 or later, and an iPhone 5 or later, a fourth-generation iPad or later, an iPad mini, or a fifth-generation iPod touch or later. Taking advantage of this clever feature involves three simple steps:

- 1. In Control Center, turn on the AirDrop feature (if it's not already). You have the option to make your pass visible (within the vicinity) or just to your contacts. You can also turn on and off an option to receive files and photos.

- 2. Tap the share icon when it presents itself in an app and choose the file or files that you want to share.

- 3. Choose the recipients of those items by tapping the circle for the person, as shown in Figure 7-14.

Yes, you can choose more than one person. People in range who are eligible to receive the file are represented on your iPhone a circle (The circles may even contain their pictures.)

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_155_0.png)

The AirDrop process begins. The people on the receiving end will get a prompt asking them to accept the picture, video, or whatever you're offering. Assuming they take kindly to your offer and grant permission (by tapping Accept rather than Decline), the file lands on their devices in short order, where it is routed to its proper location. That is, a picture or video ends in the Photos app, a contact in the Contacts app, and the aforementioned Wallet password in the Wallet app.

For You: Many said this tab — For You — is the best thing about the Music app, and we have to agree. Here you'll find music iTunes thinks you'll enjoy: playlists, albums, artists, and songs that (mostly) aren't part of your iTunes library. It's scary how often iTunes suggests music we enjoy; these days we listen to the For You tab more than we listen to the music we already own. Remember, you see this tab only if Show Apple Music is enabled in Settings ♫ Music.

Browse: Here you'll find the latest releases, plus song and album charts for dozens of musical genres as curated playlists featuring new songs and artists. Again, you'll see this tab only if Show Apple Music is enabled in Settings ♫ Music.

Radio: If you've ever listened to the radio, you will know the Radio tab immediately.

For You, Browse, and Connect tabs operate in the same fashion; each is a long page filled with content. Scroll down to see more, and then any item you see shows additional information.

While you're browsing these tabs, you'll encounter small icons; here's what each of them does:

+   Tap the + to add this song, album, or playlist to library (available only to Apple Music subscribers).

+   Tap the broken heart icon to tell iTunes you dislike this artist, song, album, or playlist, which helps the For You tab suggest music you'll enjoy.

+   Tap the ellipsis icon to see additional options, which may include Play Next, Add to Up Next, Add to a Playlist, create Genius Playlist, Make Available Offline, and Show in iTunes Store.

+   Tap the play icon to listen to this song, album, or playlist.

By now we're ready to start listening to music. We begin with some radio because we know every of you has Radio (and some of you may have anything in your Library tab yet).

To get start, tap the Music icon on your Home screen and tap the Radio icon at the bottom of the screen.

The first thing you see at the top of the screen is Beats 1, Apple's live radio station. On the air worldwide 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, with world-class programming, interviews, and music. To listen, tap the Play Now button.

# From the front to the rear — and back

We figure that most of the time, you'll use the main rear camera while shooting pictures (or). But you may want to capture a selfie, or a shot of your pretty face, to post, or on a social network such as Facebook. Not problem. just tap the front/rear camera switch at the bottom-right corner of the screen (labeled in Figure 9-1) to toggle between the front and rear cameras.

Apple calls the front camera because you can use it for the FaceTime video-calling feature discussed in Chapter 4. The rear camera is called the iSight camera.

# Firing the flash

The iPhone has an LED (light-emitting diode) flash that controls pictures taking with the rear camera. Because no flash is associated with the front-facing camera on the 6 or 6 Plus, you won't see the flash button (labeled in Figure 9-1) when you're using that. As we get to a moment, you will see a flash icon when you're using the front camera on the 6s and 6 Plus, SE, and 7 and 7+. When the button is available, tap it to change the setting to On, Off, or Auto. We suggest using the Auto setting, which lets the iPhone decide when it's a good idea to fire up the flash.

The iPhone has not one but two rear flashes as a um, flashy flash Apple refers to as True Tone flash. The flashes — one white, one amber — work in tandem to match the flash to the ambient lighting in your shooting environment. The system determines the intensity and quality of the two flashes to fire off automatically, with more than 1,000 possible combinations, Apple says.

Apple claims the Quad-LED True Tone flash on the 7 and Plus is 50 percent brighter than the 6 models.

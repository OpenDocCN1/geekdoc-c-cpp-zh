# Updating an app from your iPhone

As mentioned earlier in this chapter, every so often (or, for some apps, far too often), the developer of an iPhone app releases an update. If (one or more) of these is waiting for you, a little circle appears on the App Store icon on your Home screen as well as the Updates icon at the bottom of the screen. Tap the Updates icon if any of your apps needs updating.

If you tap the Updates icon and don't see a *Pending Updates* section at the top of the screen, none of the apps on your iPhone requires an update at this. If an app needs updating, it will appear in the Pending Updates section with an Update button to its right. Tap the Update button to update the app. If more than one app needs updating, you can update them all by tapping the All button in the upper-right corner of the screen.

# Deleting and Organizing Your Apps

That's almost everything you need to know about installing apps on your iPhone. However, you might find it helpful to know how to delete and organize apps.

# Deleting an app

You can delete an app in two ways: in iTunes on your computer or directly from your iPhone.

To delete an app in iTunes, click the Apps icon and then do of the following:

- Click the app's icon to select it, and then choose Delete or Backspace.
- Right-click (or Control+click on a Mac) the app's icon and choose Delete.

Either way, you see a dialog asking you sure you remove the selected app. If you click the Remove button, the app is removed from your iTunes library, as well as from any device that syncs with your iTunes library. You can download any app you've purchased again, for free, from the App Store. So deleting it means it's gone only from your device or computer. You can always get it back by downloading it again, unless the app is no longer available in the App Store.

Finally, iOS lets delete most of the apps that come with your iPhone if you care. Here's how to delete almost any on iPhone:

1. Press and hold any icon until the icons begin to jiggle.
2. Tap the x in the upper-left corner of the app you want to delete, as shown in Figure 15–9. A dialog appears, informing you that deleting this app also deletes its data.
3. Tap the Delete button in the dialog.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_336_0.png)

Did you notice that Wallet, App Store, Photos, Clock, Health, Settings, Phone, and Messages are jiggling but they don't have x's? (Those with Find My iPhone in the second Home page) are the apps that can't removed.

Just deleting an app from your iPhone doesn't get of permanently. The app remains in your iTunes library until you delete it from iTunes, as described earlier in this chapter. If you want to get an app for free after you delete it on your iPhone, you must also delete it from your iTunes. Even if it's not gone before, you can download apps again for free, they'll be.

If you see that deleting the app also deletes data, you may want to save the data before deleting the app. Different apps have different schemes for importing and exporting data; the important thing is that if you create with an app (notes, images, and such), deleting the app will delete any files you've created with that app unless they're saved to iCloud or elsewhere.

# Organizing your apps

You can have up to 19 Home screens (or pages) of apps. And if you're like many iPhone users, you'll soon have a substantial collection of apps in your iTunes library and on your iPhone. So let's look at a few ways to organize those apps for easy access.

# On your computer (in iTunes)

First: On your computer, make sure you've clicked your iPhone's icon near the top-left corner of the iTunes window, and then click Apps on the list on the left (labeled in Figure 15–10).

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_337_0.png)

FIGURE 15–10: Organizing apps in iTunes with Home page 2 selected.

You can organize your apps in the following ways:

- In the Home screens section, double-click any Home screen to expand it so you can click and drag an app icon to a new location on the same screen.
- You can drag an app from the Apps list to the left of any of the 19 available screens in the scrolling list of Home screens on the right, as long as the screen has no empty screens preceding it. In other words, you can drag an app to screen 7 as long as at least one app is on the screens numbered 1 through 6.
- You can add new Home screens by clicking the + icon.
- You can change the order of screens in the Home screens grid by dragging it to a different position on the grid. After you have a few pages' worth of apps, give it a try — it's easy and kind of fun.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_338_0.png)

You can change the order of Home screens only in iTunes. While you can move individual app icons around your heart's content on your iPhone, there's no method for reordering Home screens.

You can also create folders to organize your apps by dragging one app onto another app. After, folders work a lot like Home screens:

- You can click and drag an app icon to a new location in a folder or on any page.
- You can drag an app from the Apps list to the left to any folder.
- You can change the order of pages in folders the same way you change the Home screens. First, scroll down past your last Home screen, where you'll see all folders on your iPhone (if you have any folders), and then click the page you want to move, and drag it to your new location.
- You can add new pages to folders by clicking their icons.

# On your iPhone

To rearrange apps and folders on your iPhone, first press and hold down on any app or folder until all the apps begin to jiggle and the little “delete” x’s appear.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_338_1.png)

A folder icon is the same as an app icon when it comes to rearranging icons-screen. Also, the app or folder you press and hold down to start the jiggle doesn't have to be the one you want to move — any app or folder will do.

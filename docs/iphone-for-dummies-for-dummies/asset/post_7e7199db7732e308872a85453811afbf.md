
# Altering account settings

The next group of settings we explore in this chapter deals with your email accounts. You most likely will never need most of these settings, but we'd be remiss if we didn't least mention them briefly. So they are here, whether you need 'em' or not:

- To stop using an email account: Tap the Settings icon on the Home screen, tapping Mail Accounts, and then tap the account name. Tap the Mail to switch off the account. Depending on the account, you may also turn off other settings. For example, in Gmail, you can turn off the Contacts, Calendars, and Notes settings but leave Mail turned on.
When you turn off an account, don't delete it. You only hide the account from view and stop from searching or checking email until you turn it on.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_271_0.png)

- To delete an email account: Tap the Settings icon on the Home screen, tapping Mail Accounts, and then tap the account name. Scroll to the bottom and tap Delete Account. You're given a chance to reconsider by tapping Delete from My iPhone or Cancel.

The last settings reached the same way: On the Home screen, tapping Mail Accounts, and then tap the name of the account with which you want to work. Next, tap Account — you'll see the email address selected — and then tap Advanced (in the case of iCloud, Mail under the Advanced heading). The settings you see under Advanced and how they vary a little by account. This list describes some of the ones you may need:

- To specify how long until deleted messages are removed permanently from your iPhone: Tap Advanced and then tap Remove. Your choices under iCloud are Never, After One Day, After One Week, and One Month. Tap the choice you prefer.

- To send signed and encrypted messages: In an account that has an Advanced setting, tap S/MIME to turn on the setting and then indicate whether a message must be signed or require a certificate, which might be issued by a systems administrator at your employer. This setting is under Advanced for a reason.

- To choose whether drafts, sent messages, and deleted messages are stored on your iPhone or on your mail server: Tap Advanced if this option is presented. Then, under the Mailbox Behaviors heading, choose various settings to determine whether you're storing such messages on the iPhone or on the server. Your options vary according to your email account. If you choose to store any or all of them on the server, you can't see them unless you have an Internet connection (Wi-Fi or cellular). If you choose to store them on your iPhone, they're always available, even if you don't have internet access.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_272_0.png)

We recommend that you do not change these two items (again, assuming they are even presented) unless you know exactly what you're doing and why. If you're having problems with sending or receiving mail, start by contacting your ISP (Internet service provider), email provider, or corporate IT person or department. Then change these settings if it instructs you to:

- To configure server settings: Tap Host Name, User Name, or Password in the Incoming Mail Server or Outgoing Mail Server section of the account settings and make your changes.

- To Adjust SSL, Authentication, IMAP Path Prefix, or Server Port: Tap Advanced, and then tap the appropriate item and make the necessary changes.

Apple says that the front camera has fine-tuned for FaceTime usage, in photography-speak means the camera has the proper field of view and focal length. At times, you'll want to employ the iPhone's main camera on the rear to show off all your surroundings and give the caller an idea of where you are.

To toggle between the front and main cameras, tap the camera icon.

If you want to mute a FaceTime video call, tap the microphone icon with the slash running through it. The caller won't be able to hear you if you continue to mute.

Although many FaceTime commences with a regular AT&T, Sprint, T-Mobile, or Verizon call, you can't go from FaceTime to an audio-only call without hanging up and redial. In the past, if you dropped a FaceTime call because of a Wi-Fi hiccup or other problem, you had to redial via FaceTime or your provider, depending on whether you wanted the call to be video or only audio.

You can make FaceTime audio-only calls too. Tap the FaceTime icon, contacts, and then tap the icon, which indicates you want to make a FaceTime call without the face. Note, this feature hasn't been named FaceTime. (Tapping the video icon next to a contact listing would make a regular FaceTime video call instead.) From the FaceTime app, you can also designate FaceTime favorites (audio or video), and make FaceTime calls in the Recents log all. Only FaceTime calls will be shown in Recents, the regular calls you make over the cellular network.

To block all FaceTime calls, tap Settings from the Home screen, tap FaceTime, and make sure the FaceTime button or switch is off. If you can't find why you're not getting FaceTime calls, go back to Settings and make sure this option is turned on. While you're in FaceTime Settings, that's when you can list one or more addresses by which a caller can reach you for a video call, along with your iPhone's phone number.

If you want to momentarily check another iPhone while on a FaceTime call, press the Home button and then tap the icon for the app you have in mind. At this point, you can still talk over FaceTime, but you'll no longer see the person. Tap the green bar at the top of the screen to bring the person back in front of you.

And there you it. That's FaceTime, one of the newest features in the iPhone.

## Controlling Control Center

As its name suggests, Control Center is a single repository for the controls, apps, and settings you frequently call upon. We briefly wrestled with holding off on the discussion of Control Center until Chapter 14, where we go deeper into the iPhone settings. We quickly dismissed that idea because we think you're going to take advantage of Control Center as much as we do. Indeed, you will almost certainly spend a lot more time in Control Center than in Settings.

To access Control Center, swipe up from the bottom of the screen — any screen. The beauty of Control Center is that it's always available when you need it.

Now, take a gander at Figure 5–1 and you'll get an immediate handle on all the things Control Center lets you get at right away.

Next are controls for AirPlay and AirDrop. The latter is a way to share pictures, videos, and other files wirelessly with folks nearby who have iOS devices (iOS 7 or later) of their own and dectoth turned on. By contrast, AirPlay is a way to stream music and movies wirelessly via Wi-Fi to Apple TVs and compatible AirPlay–enabled speakers and receivers. We discuss AirPlay in greater depth in Chapter 8.

## A Quick Tour Outside

The iPhone is a harmonious combination of hardware and software. In this section, we take a brief look at what's on the outside. In the next section, we peek at the software.

## On the top and side

The top of iPhone 6 and 7 models is as smooth as a baby's bottom, as shown in Figure 1–1. If you have one of the iPhone 6 models, your sleep/wake button is on the side. For all models, the SIM card tray is on one side, and the ring/silent switch and volume buttons are on the other side. We describe these elements fully in the following list:

- Microphone: Used for FaceTime calls and noise suppression during phone calls.
- SIM card tray: The SIM card tray is where you remove or replace the SIM card inside your iPhone.

A SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) card is a removable smart card used to identify mobile phones. Users can change phones by moving the SIM card from one to another — kind of. The iPhone uses a nano-SIM.

- Sleep/wake button: This button is used to lock or unlock your iPhone and to turn your iPhone on or off. When iPhone is locked, you can still receive calls and messages, but nothing happens if you touch your screen. When your iPhone is turned off, all incoming calls go directly to voicemail.
- Ring/silent switch: This switch, which is on the left side of your iPhone, lets you quickly swap ring mode and silent mode. When the switch is set to ring mode — the up position, with no orange showing on the switch — your iPhone plays all sounds through the speaker on the bottom. When the

Silent mode is overridden, however, by alarms you set in the built-in Clock app, iPod audio, and selecting sounds such as ringtones and alert sounds in the Settings app.

If your phone is set to ring mode and you want to silence it quickly, press the sleep/wake button on the top of the iPhone or press one of the volume buttons.

- Volume buttons: Two volume buttons are just below the ring/silent switch. The upper button increases the volume; the lower decreases it. You use the volume buttons to raise or lower the loudness of the ringer, alert sound effects, songs, and movies. And during phone calls, the buttons adjust the voice loudness of the person you're speaking with, regardless of whether you're listening through the receiver, the speakerphone, or a headset.

## On the bottom

On the bottom of your iPhone, you find a microphone, the Lightning connector, the speaker (or speakers), and the headset jack (all but iPhone 7 models), as shown in Figure 1-2:

- Headset jack (iPhone SE and 6): The headset jack lets you plug in the included iPhone headset, which looks like EarPods with an elliptical shape.

- Microphone: The microphone lets you hear your voice when you're not using a headset.

The iPhone sports three microphones (top front, top back, and bottom). The top ones are for FaceTime calls and also work with the main mic (located on the bottom) to suppress unwanted and distracting sounds on phone calls using dual-mic noise suppression or beam-forming technology.

- Lightning connector: The Lightning connector has three purposes for most iPhones and for iPhone 7 models. One, you can use it to recharge your battery. Simply connect one end of the Lightning connector-to-USB cable to the iPhone and the other to the USB power adapter. Then, you can use the port to synchronize. Connect one end of the port on your iPhone and the other to a USB port on your Mac or PC. Then, you can use the Lightning or dock connector to connect your iPhone to other devices, such as a camera or television using an adapter such as the Camera Kit or one of Apple's AV adapters. Four (iPhone 7 models only), you can use to connect EarPods or the included 3.5mm headphone jack adapter so you can listen to audio or talk on the phone without holding a slab of glass and metal in front of your face.

Little-known fact: The EarPods with Lightning connector that come with iPhone 7 and Plus will work with any iOS device with a Lightning connector.

- Speaker: The speaker is used by the iPhone's built-in speakerphone and plays audio — music or video soundtracks — if no headset is plugged in. It also plays the ringtone you hear when you receive a call. iPhone 7 models have stereo speakers.

## On the front

On the front of your iPhone, you find the following (labeled in Figure 1-3):

- Camera: The camera on the front is tuned for FaceTime, so it has the right field of view and focal length to focus on your face at arm's length.
- Receiver: The receiver is the speaker that the iPhone uses for calls. It naturally sits close to your ear whenever you hold it in the "talking" position.

If you require privacy during phone calls, use the included headset (or any compatible wired or wireless headset — as discussed in Chapter 14).

## On the back

On the back of your iPhone is a camera lens. It's the little circle in the top-left corner. The iPhone also has one or more LEDs next to the camera lens for a flash for still photos, as a floodlight for videos, and as a flashlight (turn it on and off in Control Center). For more on using the camera and shooting videos, see Chapters 9 and 10, respectively; for more on the flashlight and Control Center, see Chapter 5.

Finally, all current iPhone models have a third microphone on the back (refer to Figure 1-1).

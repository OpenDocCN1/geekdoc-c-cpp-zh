## Using digital zoom

When you spread your fingers or chin closer together on the screen, the zoom slider appears. Continuing to pinch or unpinch has the same effect of dragging the slider to the right or left. The zoom features works shooting video too.

> You may not love the results you get zooming in. The iPhone has a digital zoom, not an optical zoom — the 7 Plus is an exception and the quality distinction is enormous. Digital zoom in Photos enables you to get closer to your subject zooming up to 5x on models preceding the 7 and up to 10x on those latter two models. What digital zoom is really is cropping and blowing up part of image, which can cause fuzziness or blurring. Not to say harsh, but a subject's imperfections — and any inadequacies of the photographer's part — may come to light. Despite that, we find that even when using digital zoom, the camera renders some pretty strong pictures.

## Applying filters

The beauty of photo software is that you can edit and doctor up pictures to make them look slimmer, sillier, and funnier — or even from color to black and white. You accomplish these enhancements by using editing tools included in the Photos app, as outlined in this chapter, or in any number of third-party apps.

Apple lets you apply color effects *before* you take your shots. Even better, these handy tools are filters, so you can see the effect of changing from one to another before deciding which works for a given scene.

To apply, tap the three-dot filters at the upper-right corner of the display, and tap any of the eight filters shown in Figure 9-4, left, from a black-and-white Noir image to a Chrome picture somewhat reminiscent, we suppose, of the days of Kodachrome film. Or tap the ninth color right dab in the middle, to go back to a normal image.

You can apply filters after you take a picture as well, as part of your editing suite. That way, you have a normal picture and the filtered one. If you apply a filter before you shoot, the camera will take a picture only in that filtered mode.

## Binging on burst shooting

Even top-notch photographers need help getting that perfect shot or sequence of shots. Burst mode provides that help on the iPhone 5s and later. Capturing pictures rapid fire — at a rate of ten images per second — you won't miss.

When you're ready to shoot, hold the shutter button. Burst mode provides that help on the iPhone 5s and later. Capturing pictures rapid fire — at a rate of ten images per second — you won't miss.

With burst mode, you can capture up to hundreds of photos, especially when you end up with hundreds. We think that burst mode is a great feature. But Apple recognized in most cases, you're probably not going to keep every photo you take.

The software processes the images in real time and suggests the pictures you'll like based on factors such as clarity, sharpness, and even whether a subject's eyes are closed.

You can tell whether a photo is part of a shooting binge in three ways:
- In the first way, word appears in the upper left of the image, with a numerical count of burst photos in parentheses.
- The second way is by visiting the Burst Album that conveniently supplies for your bursts.
- Another way is when you come to what we still refer to as the Camera Roll or the All Photos Album in the Photos app. The thumbnail that represents this sequence of shots will appear as though it's sitting on a stack of photos.

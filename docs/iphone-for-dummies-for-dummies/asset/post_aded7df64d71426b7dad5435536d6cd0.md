## Getting the iMessage

Messages apps let you exchange short text messages with any cellphone that supports the SMS protocol. You can also send and receive MMS messages, which can include pictures, contacts, videos, audio recordings, and locations, with any cellphone that supports the MMS protocol.

SMS is an acronym for the Short Message Service protocol; MMS is the acronym for the Multimedia Messaging Service protocol. Most phones sold today support one or both protocols.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_118_0.png)

In addition to the traditional SMS and MMS protocols offered by the wireless carriers, iOS 5 and higher support iMessage, an Apple-provided service that lets you send and receive unlimited messages with text, pictures, videos, and locations. The good news is that although the wireless operators all charge for SMS or MMS services, iMessages are free when using Wi-Fi. If you use cellular service, there is no per-message charge, but an iMessage may still cost you because you are using data from your plan's allotment.

If you have a limited data plan, you can turn off the Cellular Data option (in the Settings section) to force Messages (and all other data-using apps) to use only Wi-Fi.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_119_0.png)

The bad news is that you can share iMessages only with folks using Apple devices (iPhone, iPad, iPod touch, and Mac) running iOS 5 or higher or macOS 10.8 Lion or higher. And, if no Wi-Fi connection is available, the message will be sent as a standard SMS or MMS message via your wireless carrier (subject to the usual message charges).

You can turn off the SMS message feature if you like, which might be a good idea unless you have an unlimited messaging plan. By setting Settings > Messages and tapping the SMS to switch to off.

# Viewing open pages on other devices

Apple includes a helpful feature called iCloud Tabs that lets you venture into cyberspace to look at web pages on your other iOS devices and computers.

You get to iCloud Tabs by tapping the pages icon and scrolling all the way to the bottom, past the carousel of cool-looking 3D tabs. You'll then see your list of open links, segregated by device, as shown in Figure 11-7. Tap any listing to access that page on your phone.

When in the Pages view, tap Done to return to the last page you were looking at by tapping the pages icon. Tap instead to return to the icons representing your bookmarks and frequently visited pages. Tap Private to close all your existing pages before turning on private browsing, which we get to later in this chapter.

If for some reason you don't see iCloud Tabs after tapping the pages icon, head to Settings, tap iCloud, and make sure the option for Safari is turned on.

Through the Continuity and Handoff features baked into iOS 8 and later, you can now browse a page on your iPad or Mac with OS Yosemite, OS El Capitan, or macOS Sierra, and resume from the same link on your iPhone. Each machine or device must be signed into the iCloud account.

# Letting history repeat itself

Sometimes, you want to revisit a site that you bookmarked, but you can't remember the darn destination or where you left in the first place. Good thing you can study the history books.

Safari records the pages you visit and keeps the logs on for several days. Tap the bookmarks icon, tap History (likely second down on the list), and then tap the day you think hung out at the site. You will see web pages segregated into categories as This Evening, This Afternoon, and Tuesday Morning. When you find the listing, tap it. You're about to make your triumphant return.

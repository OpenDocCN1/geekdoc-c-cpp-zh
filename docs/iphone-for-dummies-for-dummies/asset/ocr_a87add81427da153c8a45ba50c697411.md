# Sky-High Settings

When you first open Settings, you see the scrollable list shown in Figure 14-1.(Not all the settings are visible in the figure.) In all airplane mode (at the top of the list), a greater-than symbol (>) appears to the right of each listing. This symbol tells you that the listing has a bunch of options. Throughout this chapter, you tap the > symbol to check out those options.

If you scroll down to the bottom of the Settings list, you'll see settings that pertain to Twitter and Facebook and settings that pertain to some of the specific apps you added to the iPhone. (See Chapter 15 for the scoop on third-party apps.)

# Airplane mode

First, even though you're reading about airplane mode, we're obliged to tell you that you can turn this setting on or off also in Control Center. Because you're here-anyway, come, fly with us.

> Figure 14-1: Presenting lists of settings.

Using a cellphone to make calls on an airplane is a no-no. But nothing's verbose about using an iPhone to listen to music, watch videos, and peek at pictures—and the rules that make you want to do so until the craft has reached altitude have been relaxed. So how to take advantage of the iPhone's built-in Music player (or other capabilities) while temporarily turning it off, email, and Internet functions? The answer is, by turning airplane mode. To do, merely tap airplane mode on the Settings screen to turn the switch (which is showing green). That act disables the iPhone's wireless radios (Wi-Fi, EDGE, 3G, 4G, LTE) and Bluetooth. While your iPhone is in airplane mode, you can't make receive calls, surf the web, or do anything that requires an Internet connection. The good news is that airplane mode makes your battery running longer—particularly useful if you are flying half-around the world.

The appearance of a tiny airplane icon on the status bar in the top-left corner reminds you that airplane mode is turned on. Just remember to turn it off when you're back on the ground.

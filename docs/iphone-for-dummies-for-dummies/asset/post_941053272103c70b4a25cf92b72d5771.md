
>> Trick 2: Restore from backup. Click the iPhone icon near the top left in the iTunes window, click Summary in the sidebar, and then click Restore Backup. The Restore Backup dialog appears and offers you a choice of backups. Select the one you want, click the Restore button, and let the iPhone work some magic.

If you have more than one backup for a device, try the most recent (undated) one first. If it doesn't work or you're still missing files, restoring from any other backups before you throw in the towel.

Note that these backups include photos in the Recently Added folder or Camera Roll, text messages, notes, contact favorites, videos, sound settings, and more, but not media such as music, videos, or photos. If media is missing, perform Trick 1 again.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_359_0.png)

And finally, if neither Trick 1 nor Trick 2 did the trick, try this:

>> Trick 3: Sync one media type at a time. Start syncing (or downloading from the App Store) apps you use regularly. Test the iPhone for a while and, if everything seems okay, sync another type of media — music, movies, calendars, photos, or whatever. Test the iPhone again, and if everything seems okay, continue until the problem recurs or you've synced all your media. If the problem comes back, you should have a good idea of what media type is to blame.

# Shazam

Ever heard a song on the radio or television, in a store, or a club, and wondered what it was called or who was singing? With the Shazam app, you may never wonder again. Launch Shazam, tap Touch to Shazam, and point your iPhone's microphone at the source of the music.

If you have an iPhone with 3D Touch, you can press the Shazam icon and choose the Shazam Now quick action shortcut (thus skipping the whole “tap Touch to step).

In a few seconds, the song title and artist's name magically appear on your iPhone screen, as shown in Figure 17–1.

In Shazam parlance, that song has been tagged. Now, if tagging was all Shazam could do, would surely be enough. But wait, there's more. After Shazam tags a song you can

- Listen to the song on Apple Music
- Watch related videos on YouTube

# TurboScan

If you travel for work, your pockets are probably littered with business cards, expense receipts, notes, pictures, and documents. If you could only carry a scanner in your pocket to get that photo into some sense of order.

Stop dreaming. TurboScan turns your iPhone into one.

If you've read previous versions of this book you may remember that we listed TurboScan in the very next chapter on favorite paid apps. And there is a paid version of TurboScan called TurboScan Pro, which costs $4.99.

But TurboScan itself is free, and when we asked the app's producer Piksoft what the differences are between the free and paid versions — it's not we were told that such differences are “very minor."

Thus, you now find Piksoft's app here.

TurboScan lets scan and spit out high-quality PDFs. It is fast — processing a page in less than four seconds — and can detect paper edges to help improve accuracy. And by tapping the 3x button, shown in Figure 17-3, you can take three pictures of the same document image, a feature Piksoft calls SureScan. You can process scans in color or black-and-white.

After you've scanned a document, you can choose a name, an appropriate size (such as US letter, receipt, or business card), and add a datestamp. You can email the document to yourself as a PDF or a JPEG, print it, or save it to your All Photos album. You can also open it as a PDF to view in other apps on your iPhone that can read documents in the PDF format (such as CloudReaders, Evernote, or iBooks).

# TripCase

We both travel more than most people and are somewhat set in our ways. In the old days, we used our computers to print boarding passes, hotel and rental car details, and any other info we might need while transit. The printouts were strictly analog, so they didn't notify us (or anyone else) of gate changes or flight delays or cancellations. And, of course, they couldn't remind us to check in.

What we wished and hoped for was a single intelligent repository for travelrelated information, one that was smart enough to alert us of gate changes, weather delays, flight cancellations, and the like, one that was easy to configure, convenient to use, and free. What we found was TripCase, which is all that and more. It's a free iPhone app (and a website) that organizes details of each trip in place, with reminders and flight alerts delivered directly to your iPhone.

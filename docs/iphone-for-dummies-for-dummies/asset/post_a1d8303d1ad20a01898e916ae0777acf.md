# A KEYBOARD FOR ALL BORDERS

Apple continues to expand the iPhone's global reach by supplying international keyboard layouts for more than 50 languages. To access a keyboard that isn't customized for English American, tap Settings ⚙ General ⚙ Keyboards ⚙ Add New. Pop up a list with custom keyboards for Russian, Slovak, Swedish, and other languages. Apple even supplies two versions of Portuguese to accommodate customers in Brazil and Portugal, and four versions of French (including keyboards geared to users in Belgium, Canada, and Switzerland, as well as France). Here's a version of English for Australian English, for Indian English, and one for Singapore English. Here,s an English for Japan. (Go. figure) Keyboards for "right-to-left" languages are also on the list.

Have a multilingual household? You can select, in turn, as many international keyboards as you want. When you're working in an app that summons a keyboard, tap the international keyboard key, with the toggle and dictation keys, you see the keyboard you want. Note that the space momentarily displays the name of the keyboard language and then translates the word itself to the language of the keyboard in use. Tap again to select the next keyboard in the list of international keyboards (well as the Emoji keyboard) that you turned on Settings. If you keep cycling, you eventually come back to your original English layout. Here's an alternative method for summoning a keyboard you've enabled: Press your finger against the international keyboard key to summon a pop-up window that displays all the keyboards that are ready for action. Slide your finger from the list until it lands on the keyboard you want to use, and then release to select that.

You can use handwriting character recognition for simplified and traditional variations of Chinese. Just drag your finger in the box provided. Some Chinese keyboards don't rely on handwriting.

And here's a neat trick with iOS 10: You can type in two languages without switching from one to the other, just type the appropriate word or in whichever language makes sense and the iPhone takes a stab at what it thinks you mean to type next — in the appropriate language. You can type with any pair of the following languages: English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, and Spanish. And this multilingual typing feature is also supported for English and Chinese.

When you're typing notes or sending email and want to type a number, symbol, or punctuation mark, tap the 123 key. The keyboard morphs into an alternative virtual keyboard with numbers and symbols. The 123 key is now the #+= Tap to display keys with additional symbols to return to the alphabetical keyboard. These extra steps are not hard to get to, but some may find them irritating.

As mentioned, you can rotate the iPhone so that its keyboard changes to a landscape mode in certain apps, including Mail, Messages, Notes, and Safari. The keys are slightly larger in landscape mode, a boon to those who do a lot of typing or have large fingers.

# Correcting mistakes

It's a good idea to type with abandon and not get up over mistyped characters. The iPhone's self-correcting keyboard will fix many errors and, as mentioned, will help you reduce mistakes in the first place by predicting which words you have in mind. Still, you may have to make some corrections manually.

FIGURE 2-7:
When the keyboard bails you out.

If the iPhone thinks you've made a mistake while typing, it may underline or highlight the suspect word. For instance, in our earlier example, *nptes* is not a recognized English word, the iPhone will flag that possible error in the body of your message. Tap the word to *see* possible alternatives (like *notes appears*) just below the suspect word.

If you want to decline the suggestion and type your own replacement, you can do so. And of course you keep the word that the iPhone thought you typed in.

Sometimes misspelled words appear with an underline and no suggestion. If you tap the misspelled word, a suggestion for another spelling may appear. Tap the new word to accept it. Sometimes when you tap the underlined word, you instead see the note *No Replacements Found.*

A neat trick when manually correcting text is to hold your finger against the screen to bring up the magnifying glass shown in Figure 2–8. Use it to position the pointer to the spot where you need to make the correction.

# Choosing an alternative keyboard

Apple permits you to choose an alternative keyboard from a third-party app developer, a boon to the producers of Swiftkey, Swipe, and Fleksky keyboards among others, that debated on the Android mobile operating system. Some third-party keyboards let you swipe gestures that is, gliding rather than lifting your finger from one key to the next. If you've tried and liked this input method on other phones, you'll appreciate being able to employ it on your iPhone.

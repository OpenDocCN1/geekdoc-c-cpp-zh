
# Share the Love ...and the Links

Ever stumble on a web page you just have to share with a buddy? The iPhone makes it dead simple. From the site in question, tap the share icon at the bottom of the browser (shown in the margin). Then tap the appropriate button to share via Mail, iMessage (or SMS), Twitter, Facebook, or AirDrop.

What happens next depends on which button you tap. For example, if you tap Mail, a new mail message appears with the Subject line prepopulated with the name of the website you're visiting and the body of the message. Just type something in the message body, supply your pal's email address, and then tap the Send button.

And, by the way, the share icon isn't only for web browsers anymore. These days, in addition to finding it in Safari, you'll find the share icon tracing many apps that create or display content you might wish to share.

# Choosing a Home Page for Safari

You may have noticed that the iPhone version of Safari does not have an option to specify a home page, though the popular option exists on Mac and PC versions of the Safari (and for that matter, every other web browser in common use today). When you tap the Safari icon, you return to the last site you visited.

The trick to having Safari open the page of your choosing rather than the last page you visited is to create an icon for the page you want to use as your Home page. This technique is creating a web clip of a web page. Here's how to do it:

- 1. Open the web page you want to use as your Home page and tap the share icon (shown in the margin).
- 2. Tap the Add to Home Screen button.
- 3. (Optional) Change the name of the icon if you like.
- 4. Tap Add.
- 5. Tap this new web clip instead of the Safari icon, and Safari opens to your Home page instead of the last page you visited.

- 1. Click the Media Browser button to reveal the media browser pane.

- 4. Select the song you want to turn into a ringtone and drag it onto the timeline ("Hello Mudd, Hello Faddah!") in Figure 19-4.

# Getting Apps Out of the Multitasking Screen

Multitasking is great, but sometimes you don't want to see an app's icon in the multitasking screen. Don't worry — it's easy to remove any app that's cluttering your screen.

To get rid of an icon in the multitasking screen, here's what you do:

- 1. Double-press the Home button.
The multitasking screen appears. Don't forget that you can swipe the screen from right to left (or to right) to see additional icons representing other multitasking apps.
- 2. Slide the screen proxy of the app you want to quit upward and off the top of the screen, as shown in Figure 19-6.
The app disappears from the multitasking screen.
- 3. Press the Home button or tap any app to dismiss the multitasking screen.

The most useful application of this trick is to stop an app's running in the background. For example, if iTunes Radio is playing in the background and you decide've had enough music for now, just following the preceding steps, sliding the Music app and off your screen, and it will shut the heck up.

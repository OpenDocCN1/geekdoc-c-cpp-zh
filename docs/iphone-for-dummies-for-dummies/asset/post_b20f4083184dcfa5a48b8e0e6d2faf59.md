## A Brief iCloud Primer

Apple's iCloud service is more than just a wireless hard drive in the sky. iCloud is a complete data synchronization and wireless storage solution. In a nutshell, iCloud can store and manage your digital stuff — your music, photos, contacts, events, and more — and make it available to all your computers and devices automatically.

iCloud pushes information such as email, calendars, contacts, reminders, and bookmarks from your computer and to your iPhone and other iDevices, then keeps those items updated on all devices wirelessly and with any effort on your part. iCloud, iCloud Photo Library, and iCloud Photo Sharing (see Chapter 9), and email (see Chapter 12).

Your free iCloud account includes 5GB of storage, which many users will need. If you have several devices (including Macs and PCs) or like saving a lot of your data in the cloud, you'll probably find yourself needing more storage; 50GB, 200GB, and 1TB upgrades are available for $1, $3, and $10 a month, respectively.

A nice touch is that music, apps, periodicals, movies, and TV shows purchased from the iTunes Store, as well as your photo stream and iTunes Match (see Chapters 8 and 9), don't count against your 5GB of free storage. iBooks don't count against your 5GB either, or audiobooks. You'll find that the things you do count — such as mail, documents, photos taken with your iPhone camera, information, settings, and other app data — don't use much space, so 5GB may last a long time.

If you plan to go PC-free but still want to have your email, calendars, contacts, and bookmarks synchronized automatically and wirelessly (and believe us, here's how to enable iCloud syncing on your iPhone:

2. In the list of settings, tap iCloud.
3. Tap Account, and provide your Apple ID and password (if you haven't provided it previously).
4. Tap Done.

A list of apps appears. Tap any individual on/off switch to enable or disable iCloud sync for Mail, Contacts, Calendars, Reminders, Safari (Bookmarks), Notes, and PassBook.

- Photo Library: Automatically upload and store your entire photo library in iCloud. The benefit is that you can access all your photos and videos on all your Macs and devices. One possible downside: This option could put you over iCloud's 5GB limit.
- My Photo Stream: Automatically upload photos you shoot with your iPhone camera and send them to all your devices when connected to Wi-Fi.
- iCloud Photo Sharing: Create and share albums with other iCloud users, or subscribe to other iCloud users' shared albums.

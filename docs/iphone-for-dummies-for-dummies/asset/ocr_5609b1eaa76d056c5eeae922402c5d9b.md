# WHAT'S IN THE BOX?

Somehow we think you've already opened the elegant box that the iPhone came in. But if you didn't, here's what you can expect to find inside:

+   - EarPods: Use them for music, videos, and, yes, phone calls. iPhone 7 and 7 PlusPods have a Lightning connector; all other models have standard 3.5mm headplugs.
- Lightning-to-USB cable: Use this handy cable to sync or charge your iPhone. You can plug the USB connector into your PC or Macintosh to sync or the included USB power adapter. By the way, if you prefer to have your iPhone standing up on your desk while you sync or charge it, as we do, check out one of the charging/syncing docks from Apple and others.
- USB power adapter: Use this adapter to recharge your iPhone from a standard AC outlet.
- Some Apple logo decals: Of course.
- A quick start guide: Not to use it. You see the names of the buttons; how to turn on the phone; and where to learn more and get support. We save you time of looking; you can learn more at http://www.apple.com/iphone and get help at www.apple.com/support/iphone. You're welcome.
- iPhone info sheet: Here where you find safety warnings, legality, warranty information, and regulatory compliance gobbledook, other things.
- SIM eject tool: Just kidding. Apple once a SIM eject tool in the box but in many years. Now — just use a straightened paper clip or safety pin if you need to remove your SIM card. (See Chapter 16 for more on the SIM card.)
- iPhone: You were starting to worry. Yes, the iPhone itself is in the box.

And what a display it is. We venture that you've never seen a more beautiful scene on a handheld device in your life.
The accelerometer detects when you rotate the device from portrait to landscape mode and adjusts what's on the display accordingly.

The iPhone's built-in also still knock your socks off. A proximity sensor detects when the iPhone gets near your face, so it can turn off the display to save power and prevent touches by your cheek. A light sensor adjusts the display's brightness in response to the current ambient lighting situation. The iPhone even has a gyro-scope for advanced motion sensing and GPS sensors so your phone can determine where in the world you are. Our favorite example of motion sensing is that our iPhones rarely ask if we want to join a Wi-Fi network when we're in a moving car. That's smart smartphone.

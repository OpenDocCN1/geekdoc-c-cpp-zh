When you typed on the iPhone keyboard in the past, you received visual feedback of the key you struck because that letter momentarily swelled to a larger size. If you want the iPhone to follow this traditional ritual, make sure that the new Character Preview switch under Keyboard settings is enabled.

Note the Keyboard settings under Accessibility settings. You can flip a switch to show lowercase keys if you're not pressing the shift key. In this section, you also find dedicated settings if you use a hardware keyboard with your phone.

You'll also find the switch to enable Dictation here.

If you enable a Chinese keyboard, you'll also see a Space Confirmation switch under Handwriting.

# Language & Region

The iPhone is an international sensation. It's sold and used around the world by people of all nationalities. In the Language & Region section, you can set the language you type on (by using a custom virtual keyboard), the language in which the iPhone displays text, and the language in which it speaks. Heck, you can even select a different region format (from among numerous countries) and a different calendar type, among Gregorian, Japanese, and Buddhist, and can choose your preferred language order.

# Dictionary

Tap the Dictionary setting to select the dictionary or dictionaries you want your iPhone to rely on when looking up the meaning of a word. Your choices include the Oxford Dictionary of English, the New Oxford American Dictionary, and an Apple dictionary. Many foreign language dictionaries are also available.

# Accessibility

The ever–comprehensive Accessibility tools are targeted at people with certain disabilities, though anyone can benefit from some of the following:

+   Voice-over: A screen reader describes aloud what's on the screen. The reader can read email messages, web pages, and more. As you dig into these settings, you'll see that you can use phonetics, change the pitch, alter the speaking rate, and so on.

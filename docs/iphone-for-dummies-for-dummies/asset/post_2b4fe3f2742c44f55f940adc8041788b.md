## Launching slideshows

Those of us store lot of photos on computers are familiar with slideshows of those images. It's a breeze to replicate the experience on the iPhone:

- 1. Choose an album in the albums list.

To do so, tap the Photos icon from the Home screen or tap the Camera icon in the Camera app.

- 2. Do one of the following:

- In the Photos app: Select a picture from an album, tap it to summon the picture controls (as shown in Figure 9-13, left), and then tap the share icon, in the bottom left. At the top of the screen, the image you selected as the basis of your slideshow has a check mark, as shown in Figure 9-13, right. Scroll to the left or right to view other pictures in the slideshow and tap any or of those adjacent images to select the slideshow as well. Check marks appear as you do so. Tap any picture second time to deselect and remove the check mark.

- In the Camera app: Tap the image in the lower-corner of the screen to display the most recent image you've shot, and a picture to include in your slideshow. From there, follow the instructions for the Photos app to tap and choose other images for the slideshow.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_200_0.png)

FIGURE 9-13: Summoning picture controls to launch a slideshow.

- 3. Tap Slideshow.

The slideshow starts playing. But we want to have more control over this slide presentation, so tap the screen and proceed to Step 4.

- 4. Tap Options, and then choose the theme and music (if any) to accompany the slideshow.

You have five theme choices (Origami, Magazine, Dissolve, Ken Burns, and Push). Why not try them all, to see what you like? You can choose the music with the theme or select a favorite from your iTunes stash.

- 5. Choose a speed for your slideshow.

Drag the slider under Options. To slow down how quickly one slide moves to the next, drag the slider towards the turtle at the left; to speed up your slidehow, drag to the right, toward the hare. Isn't Apple being clever here?

- 6. Tap the Repeat button so that the slideshow continues to loop until you manually stop it.

Tap Done to return to the slideshow.

- 7. Choose where you get to see the slideshow.

You can see the slideshow on your iPhone itself of course or have it beamed wirelessly to an Apple TV, should you use Apple's $69 set-top box or end with the newer, more-feature Apple TV boxes. The later Apple models start at $149. To make a slideshow appear on Apple TV, tap the icon in the upper-right corner of the screen and then tap Apple TV.

- 8. Tap Done to run the slideshow.

The slideshow ends automatically, unless you've set it to repeat, as explained in the Step 6. Tap the screen to end it prematurely.

That's it! Enjoy the show.

## Storing pictures in the (iCloud)

As mentioned, the iCloud service, any photo you take with the iPhone or other iOS 8, 9, 10 device can be automatically stored in the cloud and pushed to another iPhone, or your PC, Mac, iPad, iPod, Apple TV (third generation or later). The transfer takes place through the magic of iCloud Photo Sharing, the antidote to the endless problem, "I've snapped a picture!" Pictures are uploaded when your iCloud devices are connected to Wi-Fi.

And you no longer fret about storage space when using iCloud Photo Sharing. Apple used to store the last 1,000 pictures you took over a 30–day period in a special (called) Photo Stream album—enough time, Apple for all your devices to connect and grab those images, because a Wi-Fi connection was your only requirement. All the pictures you took on your PC or Mac, because the 1,000–picture limit on iOS devices no longer applies. Again, you can always move images that you have in the cloud into other albums on your iPhone or other iOS devices and computers, which you want to view pictures when you don't have an Internet connection.

![](img/8361dd8dda9818e97bf48313b13b8d6b_201_0.png)

There's a catch to using the iCloud Photo Library: You have to pay for storage. You get 5GB of iCloud storage gratis, but shatterbugs use that amount in a flash. You can get 50GB of storage for $9.9 a month, 200GB for $2.99 a month, 1 terabyte for $9.9 a month, or 2 terabytes for $19.99 a month.

Photos taken on the iPhone are whisked to the cloud until you leave the Camera app. In way, you get a chance to delete pictures that you'd rather have turn everywhere. But after you leave the Camera app, all the pictures are saved in All Photos (found in the list of Albums in the Photos app), including pictures that arrived as email attachments that you saved as well as screen captures on the phone. We found this last feature when writing this book.

You can save pictures in the All Photos album to any other album on the phone. Start by tapping the Select button at the upper-right of the screen. Tap each photo you want to move. Tap Add button at the bottom and pick destination. If some pictures you snap are not uploaded, go to Settings, scroll down to Photos and make sure Upload to Photo Stream is turned on.

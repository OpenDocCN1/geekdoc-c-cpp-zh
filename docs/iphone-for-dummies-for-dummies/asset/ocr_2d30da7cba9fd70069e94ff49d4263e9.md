The screen sports the following icons:

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.126, 0.233, 0.159])

+   * **Rotate, straighten and crop:** Tapping the icon to the right of Cancel (and shown in the margin) summons a number of additional icons and controls. You can

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.185, 0.233, 0.218])

+   * **Rotate the entire image in either direction.** Tap the rotate icon to take this action.

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.227, 0.233, 0.26])

+   * **Straighten a crooked image or vice versa.** Rotate the dial under the photo and watch the effect on the photo. Click Done when you are satisfied with how the image looks.

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.297, 0.233, 0.33])

+   * **Crop the image.** Tap the crop, and choose from the various aspect options. Or press your finger against the image to draw the photo around a crop grid to get it just like you would, pinching and unpinching as you see it further away or closer. When you are satisfied with the result, tap Done. Or Reset to play around with your options or tap Cancel to revert to the original. Even if you tapped Done to create a newly cropped image, you can reopen the photo and tap Reset then revert to Original to go back to where you started.

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.455, 0.233, 0.488])

+   **Red-eye:** Tap this icon, in the upper-left corner of the screen, and tap each red-eye you want to repair. The red-eye tool only corrects a photo with subjects who have a red-eye issue.

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.52, 0.233, 0.553])

+   **Auto-enhance** Let the iPhone take stab at making your image look better. Apple lightens or darkens the picture, tweaks color saturation, and more. Repeatedly tap the icon to turn this on or off. Auto-enhance is improved with iOS 10. Tap Done if you like the result.

![](bbox=[0.206, 0.6, 0.233, 0.633])

+   **Filters:** You may find this feature in the "Applying filters" section, earlier in this chapter. But that section described filters before you shoot the shutter. Here, you can choose a filter — Mono, Tonal, Noir, Fade, Chrome, Process, Transfer, and Instant — after the fact. If you're not satisfied after applying a filter, tap None to go back to the original photo.

The iPhone doesn't apply filters before you shoot a panorama, but you can change the filters during this editing stage.

+   **Adjust light, color, b&w:** Apple provides numerous editing controls to adjust brilliance, exposure, highlights, shadows, brightness, contrast, and black point (Light); saturation, contrast, and cast (Color); and intensity, neutrals, tone, and grain (B&W).

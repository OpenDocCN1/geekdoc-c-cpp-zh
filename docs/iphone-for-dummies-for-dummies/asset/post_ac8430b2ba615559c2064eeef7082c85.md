
# Wallpaper

Choosing wallpaper is a neat way to dress up the iPhone according to your taste. You can sample the patterns and designs that Apple has already chosen for you by tapping the thumbnails in Figure 14–8.

Among your choices are colorful dynamic wallpapers with floating bubbles that add a subtle dizzying effect or live wallpapers that come when you press against the screen with 3D Touch. But stunning as they are, these images may not hold a candle to the masterpieces in your own photo albums, including your live photos (about those in Chapter 9). After making a selection, tap the image, and then Set. You can set wallpaper for the Home screen, the Lock screen, or by tapping the appropriate button. To have the same or different images.

# In General

Certain miscellaneous settings are difficult to pigeonhole. Apple wisely lumped many of these under the General settings moniker. Figure 14–9 gives you a look at them, though not all the settings in this section are under General.

# About

You aren't seeing double. Within Settings is a setting called About, full of trivial (and not-so-trivial) information about the device, from the number of applications on the phone to its serial number.

# Software update

If there's a software update to be had, you can find it under the Software Update setting. Otherwise, you'll be informed that your software is up to date.

# Storage & iCloud Usage

Think of the Storage & iCloud Usage as one of the places to go on the iPhone for statistics about how you employ the device. You find other information in the About setting (under General on the Settings screen). A list of all the apps taking space on your iPhone appears under Manage Storage. Ask yourself if you really need all the apps that are hogging the most space. If not, tap the name of the app and tap Delete App (You can't delete a native iPhone app such as Safari or Mail, not that you'd want to.) You can always sync again (or fetch the app from the App Store) if you find that your total iCloud storage and how much is available, not only on the iPhone, but also across your iCloud-capable devices. If you need to buy more storage, under the iCloud section, tap Manage Storage Change Plan, which brings you to a Buy More Storage screen.

# Background App Refresh

When this setting is on, apps are allowed to freshen their content in the background whenever you have a cellular or Wi-Fi connection. You can cherry-pick apps which get this automatic refresh. Although this feature is a nice convenience, turning it off may bolster the life of the device.

# Siri

Apple's voice assistant (see Chapter 5) gets its own dedicated place in Settings. Choose the language Siri works in, determine whether you always want feedback or only in a hands-free solution, let Siri know your own information, and decide whether Siri will kick in when you bark the words "Hey Siri". On models prior to the iPhone 6s or 6 Plus, you must be connected to power for "Hey Siri" to work. That is not a requirement for later models.

Meantime, in Settings, you also get to choose you want Siri to use a male or female voice, for English speakers, whether or not you want to have an American, Australian, or British accent. We revisit another setting relevant to Siri later in this chapter, in the “Touch ID & Passcode” section.

# Handoff and Suggested Apps

The Handoff feature added with iOS 8 lets you start a task—such as typing an email on your iPhone, on another iOS 8, 9 or device, or on a Mac running OS X Yosemite, the El Capitan, or macOS Sierra, and pick up on another iPhone, iOS 8, 9, or 10, or Mac. All the devices must be running the identical iCloud account. On the phone, you'll be able to resume with the app from your Lock screen or app switcher. On a Mac, you'll see the appropriate app on the dock.

In the Suggested Apps section, you can turn on to showcase installed apps or App Store suggestions that are relevant to your current location. Suggestions-appear on the Lock screen and in the app switcher.

# CarPlay

If the vehicle you drive supports Apple's CarPlay, you can get directions, make calls, and listen to an audiobook while you're at the wheel. You can steer all this activity via the car's knobs, buttons, or dashboard touchscreen, or by tapping into Siri's voice control.

Here in Settings, you can turn on the ignition for CarPlay and choose whether to allow CarPlay to work when the phone (not the car) is locked.

# VPN

A virtual private network, or VPN, is typically a way to securely access your company’s network behind the firewall — using an encrypted Internet connection that acts as a “tunnel” for data. The iPhone software supports the protocols IKEv2 (Internet Key Exchange), L2TP (Layer 2 Tunneling Protocol), and IPSec, which anyway provides the kind of security that satisfies network administrators.

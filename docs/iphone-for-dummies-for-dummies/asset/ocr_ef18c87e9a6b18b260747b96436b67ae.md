# Voice Dialing

Most cellphones of recent vintage you dial a name or number by voice. Bark
out out "Call Mom" or "Dial 212-555-1212," and such handsets oblige. Your engaging
virtual personal assistant, Siri, can do that too. And Siri can return calls too.

You can take advantage of voice calling (and some other functions too) when you
have Siri disabled. It's all part of a feature called Voice Control. You have two
ways to summon the Voice Control:

+   Press and hold the Home button until the Voice Control screen
in Figure 5-5 appears. The screen displays wavy lines that move as you speak,
an you'll see some of the commands you can say loud (Previous Song,
all, and so on). Don't bluff anything until you hear a quick double-beep.
The iPhone will repeat the command
it thinks it.

+   Press and hold down the center button on
the wired headset. Again, wait for an audible
cue and then tell the iPhone what you have
in mind.

Voice Control works nicely with the wired
headset included with your iPhone. It works
also with Bluetooth headsets and car
kits. If you use a wireless headset that's not
supported, you'll have to hold the phone up
to your lips if you want to respond to voice
commands.

You definitely want to wait for voice confir-
mation after you've spoken. In our experi-
ce, Voice Control isn't perfect, especially in a
noisy environment. So if you're dialing a name
or number, make sure the iPhone is indeed
calling the phone you had in mind. There's no
telling what kind of trouble you might get into
therwise.

## Waze

Lots of excellent GPS mapping apps can help you navigate unfamiliar territory or keep up with traffic. The iPhone's built-in Maps app has turn-by-turn navigation, of course. And we're fans of Google Maps (which used to be native to the iPhone).

Waze, which is now owned by Google, takes a different route. It relies on the community—other drivers who have the Waze app to report (by tapping one of the icons shown in Figure 17-13) accidents, speed traps, hidden cameras, and bottlenecks.

And Waze is useful even when individual drivers don't report their or other conditions because the app exploits crowdsourcing, passively leveraging in real time from you and everyone else with the app to determine traffic and how long it will take to get from here to here.

You can request notifications when severe traffic conditions might intrude upon your plans. You can tie Waze to navigate to Facebook events and see if your friends might be heading the same way. You can see each other's ETA. You can also remain invisible if you don't know where it is driving you to.

As you cruise, or provide active reports, you can earn points that increase your level of influence on the road. So you'll not only get where you're going but arrive empowered.

## WebMD

As the old says goes, you have nothing if you don't have your health. Carrying the WebMD app on your iPhone provides a rich repository of health and medical information in your pocket, as the app's Home screen displayed in Figure 17–14 shows.

Let's explore a few of these areas:

- **Symptom Checker:** First let WebMD know your age, zip code, sex, and then you see an illustrated male or female body so that you can tap on the area of concern. Tap the Flip icon, shown on the bottom-left corner of Figure 17-15, to go from a front to a back view and versa. Pinch to zoom in on a specific body part.

Tap the appropriate body part (or tap the List button instead), and WebMD serves up a list of potential symptoms. Tap a symptom to detect possible conditions and find articles (on the WebMD website) that you may help out.

- **RX Medicine:** Among the tools you find here is one that helps you identify drugs by their shape, color, the letters or numbers imprinted on the pill, tablet, or capsule.

- **First Aid Information:** You can search for remedies, or consult a scrollable A to Z list to find treatments for everything from food poisoning to snakesakes.

## Ten Appetizing Apps

If you read Chapter 17, you know that lots of free apps are available for your iPhone. But as the cliché goes, some things are worth paying for. In some cases, the apps in this chapter are free up front, but you have to pay for some to fully take advantage of them. Still, none of the ten pay apps we've chosen as our favorites are likely to break the bank.

As you're about to discover, some of the apps on this list are practical and others are silly. The common theme? We think you'll like these apps around your iPhone.

## Akinator the Genie

Did you play Twenty Questions as a kid? Akinator the Genie ($1.99) is a loose variation that you often have scratching your head. Basically, you think about a character, real or fictional, and Akinator asks you questions: "Is your character's gender female?" "Has your character ever been in a movie?" "Does your character really exist?" "Is your character famous?" Figure 18-1, left, shows one of the questions Akinator asks when trying to solve the puzzle. You respond by tapping Yes, Don't Know, No, Probably, or Probably Not. Several questions in, Akinator takes a stab at who is on your mind. We were thinking of New York Giants quarterback Eli Manning (see Figure 18-1, right) when Akinator asked if our character was famous because of football and also had a well-known brother. Akinator is a bit boastful, too — “Guessed right one time!” and he'll tell you how many times the character has already played. You can turn on a child filter to keep the game safe for the youngest players. Akinator sometimes takes more than 20 questions to get the answer, although this is a rare occurrence. We are always entertained by Akinator and suspect you and your friends will be entertained as well.

## Comics

We really love the Comics app. Not only do we find many of the latest comics and graphic novels in its web-based store on the same day they hit the stores, but the selections include both DC and Marvel, which is Bob's all-time favorite: Spider-Man! Many of the titles classics, including issue #1 of Amazing Spider-Man, released in 1963, a copy in excellent condition goes for at least $2500! Thanks to the Comics app, we can enjoy this out-of-print classic on our iPhones for a mere $1.99. That's just too cool. Other comics are priced from $0.99.

One of the coolest features is Guided View (motion comics), which looks great on any iPhone. It's difficult to describe in words and images, but we're going to try anyway. First, take a look at a full page as it would appear in the next panel of the comic, shown in Figure 18-2. With Guided View, you'd see first Figure 18-3, left, when you tap the right third of the screen to indicate you're ready to read the next panel; panel slides off the left edge of the screen as the next panel (shown in Figure 18-3, right) slides onto the screen from the right. We don't know of any app that makes reading a comic so enjoyable on the small screen. The store offers classic Marvel and DC titles, including Superman, Batman, Wonder and Justice League, as well as new classics like The Walking Dead and Scott Pilgrim.

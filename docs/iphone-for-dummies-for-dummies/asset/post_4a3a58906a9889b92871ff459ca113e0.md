## Managing messages

When a message is on your screen, you can do many tasks in addition to reading it. Check out Figure 12-9 for the location of controls. By tapping the icons labeled in Figure 12-9, you can perform the following actions:

- View the message.
- View the preceding message.
- Flag the message to denote its importance, move it to junk, mark it as unread, or notify when anyone replies to the email.
- File this message in another folder. When the list of folders appears, tap the folder where you want to file the message.
- Delete this message. You have to dig in trash to retrieve the message if you tap the delete icon by mistake. Go to Mail Settings if you want the iPhone to ask before deleting or trashing a message. As mentioned later in this chapter, notably Gmail, the default behavior is to archive mail instead of deleting it.
- Reply, forward, or print this message.
- Create a new email message.

You can delete email messages without opening them in a few ways:

- Swipe left halfway across the message, and then tap the Trash button that appears to the right of the message, adjacent to the Flag and More buttons. If you swipe all the way to the left by keeping your finger pressed against the screen, you'll trash the message immediately. Be careful if that was not your intention. Or, if you swipe in the other direction (to the right), you can mark a message as unread. As well in some email accounts such as Google's Gmail, an Archive button may appear instead of Trash, depending on whether you turned on Archive Mail. In this case, tap the Archive button; it is not deleted. We get to that later in the chapter.

- Tap the Edit button (in the upper-right corner of Inboxes or a mail selection), and then tap the circle to the left of each message you want to remove, as shown in Figure 12-10. Tapping that circle puts a check in it and changes the Trash All button at the bottom of the screen to a Trash button. Tap that Trash button to erase all messages you selected.

- Trash the message (or mark it as read) inside an interactive notification that appears regardless of the screen you are viewing. The advantage of this method is you don't have to leave the screen to delete the message. Just tap the Trash button in the notification.

- When you've opened a message to read it, drag the entire edge of the screen toward the right. Upon doing so, you can sneak a peek at the messages in your inbox, as revealed in Figure 12-11. You haven't trashed the message you just dragged to the right — it remains in the inbox and subfolder where it was — but you did dismiss it for the moment.

# Threading messages

Apple lets you thread messages, or have automatically group related messages. The beauty of this arrangement is that you can easily trace an email conversation.

When you organize messages by thread or conversation, the related messages show up as a single entry in the mailbox, with a box-right arrow pointing that the message is part of a larger ongoing exchange. If a message is not part of a thread, you see a single arrow. Figure 12–12 (left) shows that Bob and Susan are part of the same conversation in Ed—the listing reveals pretty the eight outlying messages that make up the conversation. # Searching emails

You can easily search through a bunch of messages to find the one you want to read away—such as that stock tip from your broker. Tap status bar to scroll through the top of the inbox or manually scroll to search. You can type whichever search term seems relevant. Matching emails that have already been downloaded surface. Scroll to the top of the screen to see your current mailbox. If you have multiple accounts on your phone, you'll also see an option at the top to search all your mailboxes.

What's more, Apple groups search results by people, subjects, unread, flagged messages, messages with VIPs, and attachments

In the stock example, if you have an email to from a person named Stockman, he will show up in the People search results.. if you have a subject line that reads "Your stock portfolio performance," it will appear in the Subjects search results.

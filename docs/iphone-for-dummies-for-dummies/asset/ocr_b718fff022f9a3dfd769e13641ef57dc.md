# Evernote

Before we even talk about the Evernote iPhone app, let's take a quick look at the problem Evernote resolves for us: storing our little bits of digital information text, pictures, scanned images, receipts, bills, email messages, pages, and other info we might want to recall someday — and synchronizing all the data among all our devices and the cloud. The app has plethora of features that help you organize your notes and find them.

Evernote (www.evernote.com) is all that and more, with excellent free apps for iOS, Mac OS X, Android, and Windows, plus a killer interface that works with most browsers.

Notes can be any length, and can include text, pictures, and PDFs, and you can create notes by typing, dictating, or photography. You can also add unlimited tags to a note, and create up to 25 separate notebooks to organize your rapidly collection of notes.

You can annotate images and PDFs in Evernote using another free app from Evernote, called Skitch, which we also recommend without hesitation.

Getting words and images into Evernote couldn't be much easier, but the info will be useless if you can't find it when you need it. Evernote won't let you down, with myriad options for finding and working with your stored data. In addition to the aforementioned tags and notebooks, Evernote lets you search the text inside every note to help you find the note you need quickly.(Only Pro users can search in PDF documents, though.)

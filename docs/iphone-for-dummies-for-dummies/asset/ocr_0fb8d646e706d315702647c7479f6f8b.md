Moreover, Apple has made it so that Wallet is time and location based. In other words, the boarding pass appears when you get to the airport, even (with your permission) on the iPhone Lock screen, and you're notified of any gate changes. Walk into the coffee shop and the appropriate gift card pops up.

You can find Wallet-capable apps in the App Store. You may also see an Add to Wallet link on a merchant's website — just make sure you're signed in your iCloud account to add passes to the Wallet app. And you can scan Wallet bars with the iPhone camera.

To use a pass, tap it in the Wallet app and then point it at the barcode reader or scanner at the terminal, neighborhood multiplex, stadium, hotel, department store, or whatever place you prefer.

To show a pass on the Lock screen, tap the i-in-a-circle at the bottom right of the pass and make sure that the Show on Lock Screen switches is on.

You can share passes using Mail, Messages, or AirDrop. And speaking of which ...

# Dropping in AirDrop

At various points in this book, you encounter AirDrop, a fast, safe, and secure (through encryption) wireless method of sharing photos, videos, contacts, documents, and more with people you are close to physically. You just tap the share when it is available in an app.

AirDrop exploits both Wi-Fi and Bluetooth so sure both have been turned on for it to work properly. No other device setup is required.

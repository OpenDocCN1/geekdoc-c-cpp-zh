#  Create Ringtones for Free in GarageBand

The capability to free iPhone ringtones with Apple's GarageBand application (which is bundled with every Mac) was beyond the purview of the ringtone discussions in previous chapters. Creating those ringtones, however, is relatively easy. Start by launching GarageBand on your Mac and creating a new iPhone ringtone. Then:

+   1. Click the Media Browser button to reveal the media browser pane.

+   2. Click the disclosure triangle to reveal the contents of your iTunes library.

+   3. Click your iTunes music library to reveal its contents.

+   4. Select the song you want to turn into a ringtone and drag it onto the timeline ("Hello Mudd, Hello Faddah!") in Figure 19-4.

You can't use songs purchased from the iTunes Store for ringtones if they're protected by Apple's digital rights management copy protection. GarageBand won't let you drag a song onto its timeline.

Apple stopped copy protection for music files in 2009. If you purchased the song before that, you're good to go. If you pay a small upgrade fee (30c at press time) to convert the song to iTunes Plus, Apple's higher-quality, non-copy-protected format.

The bottom line is that you can make ringtones only from songs you've ripped from CD or downloaded without rights management or other copy protection (such as MP3s from files in Apple's iTunes Plus format) — or sounds you record yourself on your iPhone or computer.

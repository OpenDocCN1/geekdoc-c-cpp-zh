## Using the self-timer

Many physical cameras have a self-timer that lets you be part of a picture, perhaps in a group setting with friends. The self-timer built into the Camera app adds this functionality to your iPhone, whether you're using the front or rear camera. If anything, the addition of the self-timing feature might improve the quality of your selfies.

Tap the timer icon (labeled in Figure 9-1) and choose 3 seconds or 10 seconds as the interval between when you press the shutter and when the picture is captured. You'll see a countdown on the screen.

To turn off the self-timer, tap the Off button. Couldn't be easier than that.

## Tracking Down Your Pictures

So exactly do your pictures live on the iPhone? In the “Binging on burst shooting” section, we give this answer away, partly anyways. As we mention, the pictures you snap on the iPhone first land in a photo album in the Camera app. In the Photos app, photos land in the All Albums.

In the Photos or Camera app — you can find the former by tapping a thumbnail image in the latter — you'll also find pictures you've shared with friends and they've shared through the iCloud Photo Sharing feature. The photos you imported are readily available too and grouped in the same albums they were in the computer.

Moreover, every picture you take with your phone (and other iOS 9 or iOS 10 devices) are stored in an iCloud Photo Library. You can access any of these places if you have a Wi-Fi or cellular connection to the Internet. What's more fretting about images hogging too much space on your phone. The pictures are stored in the cloud at their full resolution in their original formats. (Apple will leave behind versions that are ideally sized for your phone.)

You can download the images that you want available when you're not connected to cyberspace.

In this section, we show you not only where to find these pictures but also how to display and share them with others — and how to dispose of the duds that don't measure up to your lofty photographic standards.

Get ready to let your fingers on the phone (without having to worry about smudging). Open the Photos app by tapping its icon on the Home screen or by going through the Camera app. Then take a gander at the buttons at the bottom of the screen: Photos, Memories, Shared, Albums, as shown in Figure 9–6. ## Making memories

Hidden in almost everyone's photo collection are the crown jewels, those images that trigger the most precious memories. As of the Photos app in iOS 10, Apple added a Memories feature that can help trigger such memories automatically. Tap the Memories tab to take a photographic trip down memory lane.

How does it work? The Photos app smartly scans your picture library and collects images around vacations, birthday parties, or what the app deems are the best pictures you took during the year or a shorter time frame.

In Ed’s Photos, for example, the Memories feature automatically built photo memories around trips he took to Japan, to the state of Maine, and elsewhere, as shown in Figure 9-8, left. Within such memories, you can play a Memories movie that the app automatically generates, complete with theme, titles, and transitions.

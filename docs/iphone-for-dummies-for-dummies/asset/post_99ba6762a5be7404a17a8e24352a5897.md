## Altering bookmarks

If a bookmarked site is no longer meaningful, you can change it or get rid of it:

- To remove a bookmark (or folder): Tap the bookmarks icons and swipe the entry for the bookmark you want to toss off the list from right to left so that a red Delete button appears on the right. Tap Delete. Then Done, unless another bookmark appears to go away. As an alternative, you can tap Edit at the bottom-right of the screen and then tap the rect next to each bookmark you want to delete.

- To change a bookmark's name or location: Tap Edit and then tap the bookmark. The Edit bookmark appears with the name, URL, and location of the bookmark. The fields you want to change. In the Name field, tap the X in the gray circle and use the keyboard to enter a new title. In the Location field, scroll up or down the list until you find a new home for your bookmark, and then tap that new destination.

- To create a new folder for your bookmarks: Tap Edit and then tap New Folder in the bottom left. Enter the name of the new folder and choose to put it.

- To move a bookmark up or down a list: Tap Edit and then drag the three bars to the right of the bookmark's name.

## Letting history repeat itself

Sometimes, you want to revisit a site that you bookmarked, but you can't remember the darn destination or where you left in the first place. Good thing you can study the history books.

Safari records the pages you visit and keeps the logs on for several days. Tap the bookmarks icon, tap History (likely second down on the list), and then tap the day you think hung out at the site. You will see web pages segregated into categories as This Evening, This Afternoon, and Tuesday Morning. When you find the listing, tap it. You're about to make your triumphant return.

To clear your history — nobody else can trace your steps — and just is it you're hiding? — tap Clear at the bottom right of the screen. You can clear your history for the last hour, for the day you're looking at, for the day you're looking at plus the day before, or all time.

Alternatively, on the Home page, tap Settings > Safari > Clear History and Website-Data. In both instances, per usual, you may have to check out without wiping the slate clean.

Folks browsing the web on their iPhones using their employer's Wi-Fi network shouldn't have a false sense of security. Your bosses will still have a history of every website you have visited, even if you clear your history.

## Private Browsing

Don't leave any tracks while you surf? Don't worry, we won't and we won't tell. Turn on private browsing for a “what happens in Safari stays in Safari” tool. Truly bent on staying private will also want to tap Clear History and Website Data, as we mention earlier in this chapter.

To go incognito, tap Pages so you see a tabbed view of open pages, and tap the Private button, in the bottom-left corner. You're given the option to close your existing tabs before turning on private browsing.

After private browsing is on, any traces of your visit to nonstop.com (or wherever) are not visible to other users of your phone. The owner of the Wi-Fi network that you're using or your network administrator, however, may still be able to see them.

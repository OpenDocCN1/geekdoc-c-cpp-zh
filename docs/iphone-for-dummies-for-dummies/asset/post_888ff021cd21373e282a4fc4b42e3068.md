# Happy haptics (iPhone 7 and 7 Plus)

The iPhone 7 and 7 Plus have an especially large haptic engine, the Apple technology that produces the vibrations that lend tactile feedback when using your phone. Here's where you can control what you receive feedback. On each device, tap Settings > Sounds & Haptics and scroll down to System Haptics. # Happy Home button (iPhone 7 and 7 Plus)

The Home button on the iPhone 7 and 7 Plus is not a mechanical button at all rather a customizable solid-state-sensitive button that can deliver feedback via the aforementioned taptic engine. The button doesn't physically move, but it makes you think that it does. And the Home button also course invokes Siri, multitasking, Touch ID, Apple Pay, and other functions.

In Settings General on the 7 or 7 Plus, you'll find a Home Button listing where you can sample one of three touch options. Tap one of the three circular buttons (labeled 1, 2, or 3) and then tap the actual Home button to see how the Home button feels under that option. Tap Done to apply the setting with which you are most comfortable.

# Brightening up your day

Who doesn't want a bright, vibrant screen? Alas, the brightest screens excite a trade-off: Before you drag the brightness slider in Figure 14-7 to the max, remember that brighter screens save the life from your battery more quickly. You'll find the brightness slider under the Display & Brightness setting.

> FIGURE 14-7: Sliding this control adjusts screen brightness.

We recommend tapping the Auto-Brightness switch so that it's on. This control adjusts the screen according to the lighting conditions around the iPhone while being considerate of your battery. You can find a similar brightness slider in Control Center, but you'll have to visit Settings to turn the Auto-Brightness switch on or off.

If the app're spending time in supports dynamic type, you can adjust your type size by dragging a slider. Under Display & Brightness, you'll find a slider for making text bold. Back when you set up an iPhone 6 or later, you will have the option to choose the Display Zoom from a standard (normal-sized) view to a zoomed (larger) icon view that claims more-screen real estate. If you're not satisfied with your first choice, you can change it. You'll have to reboot whenever you make such change.

As of iOS 10, Apple added the Night Shift feature here in Settings. When enabled, the phone automatically alters the colors on your iPhone after dark so that they're at the warmer end of the color spectrum. The promise is of better sleep. You can drag a slider to change the temperature to a warmer setting — give it a try to see which effect warms you up, or perhaps more to the point where we're talking sleep, cools you down. You can manually turn on Night Shift so that it is enabled until the next day. Or you can schedule the hours when you use Night Shift to automatically kick in and cease.

Settings for the Raise to Wake feature that we mentioned way back in Chapter 2 are also found under the Display & Brightness setting. As with Night Shift, the feature arrived with iOS 10.

# Auto-lock

Under Display & Brightness, you will also find Auto-Lock settings, which used to be listed as a stand alone item under General settings. Its functionality is unchanged: You can set the amount of time that elapses before the phone automatically turns off the display. Your choices are 5 minutes, 4 minutes before, and down to 30 seconds. Or you can choose to have the iPhone never lock automatically.

If you work for a company that insists on a passcode, the Never Auto-Lock option isn't on the list when your iPhone shows you.

Don't worry if the iPhone is locked. You can still receive calls and text messages, adjust the volume, see notifications, access Control Center (if you made it here in Settings), take pictures, call upon Siri's services, access the Wallet app and transfer through Apple Pay, and reply with a message when you can't answer an incoming call.

# Report, Sound, and Appearance Settings

The next bunch of settings control what the iPhone reports back to you, what the phone sounds like, feels like, and looks like.

# Notifications

By swiping down from the top of the screen, you can receive a variety of notifications in the aptly named Notification Center, from missed calls and texts to appointments.

Under Notifications Settings, turn on or off notifications from specific apps. After you determine which belong in Notification Center, tap the app list in the roster and choose an alert style (a banner, which appears at the top of the screen momentarily before it disappears, or an alert, which requires you to do something before it goes away). You also decide whether to show specific notifications on the Lock screen, or in some instances, in your vehicle via CarPlay. You may also choose whether to display a badge icon.

App developers can send you alerts to the programs you've installed on your iPhone by exploiting the Apple Push Notification service. Alerts are typically in text form but may include sounds as well. Or they may appear in a little circle affixed to the app icon as numbered badges. You can receive such alerts when the app isn't running.

> Many of the notifications you receive are interactive, you can respond to them on the spot. For example, you can reply to an incoming email or message without having to pay a visit to the underlying app.

You can turn off notifications for individual apps. Simply tap an app in the list, and turn on or off your app's sounds, alerts, or badges. Figure 14–4 shows available notification options for the FaceTime app.

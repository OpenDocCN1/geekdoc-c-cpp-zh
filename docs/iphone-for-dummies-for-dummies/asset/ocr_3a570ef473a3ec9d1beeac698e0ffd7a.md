If you don't send a button, trying one or more characters. That action will replace the microphone icon next to the text-entry field with a Send button.

If your iPhone has 3D Touch, you can press lightly on a sender's name to press harder to pop open the message as if you had it. Or lift your finger after the light press and then-press a different sender.

There's also an easy way to send a voice message instead of text. Just press and hold down the microphone icon to the right of the text-entry field and a gray overlays, as shown in Figure 6–4. When you've finished speaking, you can tap the Play to hear what you just said, swipe up to send your message, or tap the X to cancel.

You can also tap the microphone key to the space bar on the keyboard (the one next to the text-entry field) and speak your reply to Siri, who will translate it to text and type it in the text-entry field for you. (To find out more about Siri, your intelligent assistant, chapter 5.)

Your SMS conversations are saved as a series of text bubbles. Your messages appear on the right side of the screen, in blue bubbles if the recipient is using iMessage or green bubbles if he or she is using SMS/MMS; the other person's messages appear on the gray bubbles, as shown in Figure 6–5.

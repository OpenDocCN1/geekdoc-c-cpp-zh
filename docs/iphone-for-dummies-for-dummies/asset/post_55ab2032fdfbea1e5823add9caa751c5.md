
Be careful not to swipe down from the very top edge of the screen. That gesture pulls Notification Center instead of Spotlight search although with iOS 10, Spotlight is also available at the top of Notification Center.

Press and hold down the Home key for more than a second or so to summon Siri (unless you turn the Siri feature off in Settings) or to summon Voice Control on older models.

## Organizing Icons into Folders

Finding the single app you want to use among apps spread out over 15 screens is a daunting task. But Apple felt your pain and added a handy organizational tool called Folders. The Folders feature enables you to create folders, each holding dozens of apps. You might want to create folders for social apps, photos, travel-related apps, and any other number of categories.

To create a folder, press your finger against an icon until all the icons on the screen jiggle, as shown in Figure 2-13, left. Decide which apps you want to move to a folder, and drag the icon for the first app on top of the second app. In the figure, we're dragging the Withings app on top of the Health app, as shown in Figure 2-13, right. Apple names the folder according to the category of apps inside the folder, but you can easily change the folder name by tapping the X in the bar where the folder name appears and substituting a new name.

To launch an app that's inside a folder, tap that folder's icon and tap the icon for the app that you want to open.

Apps can be dragged into and out of any folder. As of iOS 9, you can stash up to 9 apps per page in a folder and have as many as 15 pages. That's a grand total of 135 (more, we speak, than you'd possibly ever to tuck away in a folder). If you drag all apps outside the folder, the folder automatically disappears.

Folders are a great organizational tool. But if you have numerous apps on the phone, it's all easier to create and arrange folders not from the phone when syncing your iPhone in iTunes.

3D Touch comes into play if you have the 6s, 6s Plus, SE, 7 or 7 Plus and want to get all the icons to jiggle so can rearrange them. You can still make that happen on such models, but you may have to practice. In our experience, extremely gently touch is required. Otherwise, you might bring up a share icon on the icon you touch or Rename on a folder.

## Proactive Searching

With the Safari browser (see Chapter 11), you can search the web using Google, Yahoo!, Microsoft Bing, and DuckDuckGo — as well as Chinese search engines if you add a Chinese keyboard. You can also search for people and programs across your iPhone or within specific apps, using a combination of Spotlight and Siri. We show you how to search within apps in the various chapters dedicated to Mail, Contacts, Calendar, Notes, and Music.

Spotlight can search for news and trending topics, local restaurants, movie times, and content in Apple’s own iTunes Store, App Store, and iBooks Store.

Moreover, with Siri using the Spotlight feature (familiar to Mac owners), you’ll also see circled icons representing the contacts you engage with the most, the people you are scheduled to meet, as well as eateries, shops, and other places of possible interest nearby.

The searches with the iOS are also proactive, meaning that the device gets to know you over time and makes suggestions accordingly. The phone might suggest the News app, for example, if it gets to learn that you turn out early morning (while enjoying your coffee). Or if you’re in a particular area, you might see the news that’s trending in your location.

To access the search screen on an iPhone, swipe down from anywhere. To see Siri suggestions (for people to contact or apps you might want to use at the moment), as well as suggestions based on the time and your location (such as places to lunch, shop, or get gas), swipe from left to right from the Home or Lock screen to summon the today view.

To actively search the main search screen you pull down from the top of the screen, like a window shade, enter your search query using the virtual keyboard. The iPhone starts spitting out results the moment you type a character, and the list grows as you type.

The results are pretty damn thorough. Say you entered Bell as your search term, as shown in Figure 2-14. Contacts whose names contain Bell will show up, along with folks for companies named Bell. If your iTunes library has the song "One Last Bell to Answer" or music performed by Joshua Bell, those will show up. Same for third-party apps with the name and if Bell is mentioned in a note, a message, an event, and more will also appear. Tap any listing to jump to the contact, or app if you're searching.

You have some control over the type of search that Spotlight conducts. From the Home screen, tap Settings > General > Spotlight Search. Make sure the switch for each bundle or third-party app on the iPhone you like is flipped (so that green is showing). Tap to turn off the switch for any of these items that don't want included in your search.

## Notifications and the Today View

The today view displays widgets or notifications pertaining to the day you are viewing, of course, but it also may clue you in what's next on your calendar, say, even if what's next is the day. But today is called today for a reason. You'll know whether to do a raincoat or run out and buy a last-minute birthday gift for your friend who is celebrating the Four-O. You'll know what's on the immediate agenda. And you can also get a read on how the stocks in your portfolio are performing.

If you scroll to the bottom of the today view, you can tap an Edit button that gives you a bit more control over the items and widgets in your today view and the order in which those lists appears. You can remove the notifications you see in this view (or not) rearrange the order in which, among others, the Stocks, Calendar, and Reminders appear. Notifications from some third-party apps can also show up in the today view.

Apple has also opened up Notification Center to third-party developers, enabling you to respond to notifications from some of your favorite apps.

To display Notification Center, drag it down like a window shade. To get the today view, swipe from left to right from the Home or Lock screen. To dismiss an individual notification, lightly press the notification using 3D Touch and the X. If that notification represented an email, your 3D Touch option might be to archive the message or mark it as read. You can also tap an X to clear an entire day's worth of notifications, or press a little harder to Clear All Notifications. You can also dismiss individual notifications by swiping the notification from left to right and tapping Clear.

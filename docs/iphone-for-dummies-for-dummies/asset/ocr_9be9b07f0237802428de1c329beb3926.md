To add an indentation when you forward or reply to a message: As mentioned earlier in the chapter, tap Quote Level and tap slide the switch from off to on.

To set the default email account for sending email from outside the Mail app: On the Home screen, tap Settings > Mail > Default Account. The account you want to use as the default. For example, when you want to send a picture directly from the Photos app, this designated default email account is used. Note that this setting applies only if you have more than one email account on your iPhone.

That's what you need to know about the settings that apply to sending email.

# Checking and viewing email settings

Several settings affect the way you check and view email. You might want to modify one or more, so we describe what they do and where to find them:

To specify how often the iPhone checks for messages: On the Home screen, tap Settings > Mail > Accounts > Fetch New Data. You're entering the world of fetching and pushing. Check out Figure 12-22 to glance at your options. If your program supports push and you have turned on, messages are sent to your iPhone automatically as soon as they reach the server. If you turned off push or your email account doesn't support it, the iPhone fetches instead (that is, messages are batched before they are sent). Choices for fetching are Every 15 Minutes, Every 30 Minutes, Hourly, and Manually. Tap the one you prefer, keeping in mind that fetching less frequently has a positive effect on battery life.

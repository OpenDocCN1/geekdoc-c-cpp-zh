# # Making Photos Come to Life

One of the coolest features added to the 6s and 6s Plus (and beyond) is called Live Photos. Essentially, you snap a still image as always, a snippet of video (and sound) is captured about a second and a half before you tap the shutter. Think of a live photo as an animated GIF.

Models with live photos, you'll see a glyph when you fire up the camera (see Figure 9-1). If the glyph is yellow, the Live Photos feature is enabled, as it is by default. Otherwise, the glyph will be white. You can tap the glyph to toggle the feature on or off. You see a live indicator in a yellow box when the iPhone is capturing a scene.

You can view your live photo on your iPhone or on other devices by leaving your finger pressed down on the screen. You can also use a live photo as your Lock screen iPhone wallpaper.

According to Apple, a live photo claims roughly the same amount of space as two photos with the live feature turned off do. That means storage shouldn't become a major issue for most iPhone owners who snap a decent amount of photos, even of you with the smallest capacity iPhones. Of course, you'll find out in this chapter, photos need not stay resident on your phone; you can store images on the cloud via iCloud.

> TIP We recommend that you think of live photos as photos than videos. The 12-megapixel still image is still at the heart of what you're trying to capture. That said, you may want to think about keeping the camera steady for a second or so longer and after you snap the photo.

# # Keeping Things in Focus

The camera in the iPhone can detect up to ten faces in a scene, placing a rectangle on top of each mug (the focus box in Figure 9-1). Behind the scenes, the camera is balancing the exposure across each face. If you want to take a picture, press and hold your finger against the screen until the rectangle pulses. AE/AF Lock will appear on the screen. Tap the screen again to make AE/AF Lock disappear.

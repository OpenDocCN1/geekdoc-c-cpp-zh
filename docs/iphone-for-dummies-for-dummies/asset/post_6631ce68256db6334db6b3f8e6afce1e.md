## Blocking callers

We don't embrace every caller, not by a long shot. Perhaps the caller is an old-turned stalker. The person may be a crud. Or you keep hearing from the same persistent direct marketer, who doesn't take your “no, thank you” at face value. Fortunately, you get the last word on the iPhone. Go to the Recents list for the caller and tap the i-in-a-circle. Scroll down to the bottom of the screen, tap Block Caller, then tap Block Contact. You will no longer receive phone calls, FaceTime calls, or messages from people on the block list. Although your phone won't ring, the caller will continue to reach your voicemail and you will be able to leave you a message. If the blocked stalker, um, caller returns to your good graces, you can tap Unblock Caller to let the person's calls through.

## Avoiding disturbances

In the middle of the night — the middle of the day when you're playing hooky the last thing you want is to be bothered by a phone call. Apply such potential disturbances in when meant the Do Not Disturb feature. When you turn on the feature in Settings or in Control Center (Chapter 5), your incoming calls and alerts will be silenced. A moon icon rises to the top of the status bar to remind you when Do Not Disturb is enabled.

Of course, you may be reluctant to turn on the feature, feeling perhaps about missing emergency calls or calls from family members or important associates. You need exceptions?

### ITUNES AND RINGTONES

Even with all the ringtones Apple gives you gratis, you may want to add or create your own. After the ringtones you create or download on your computer, you can connect it to your iPhone and synchronize the ringtones back to the device.

## While on a Call

You can do lots of things while talking on an iPhone, such as consulting your calendar, taking notes, or checking the weather. Press the Home button to get the apps that let you perform these tasks.

If you're using Wi-Fi, 3G, or 4G and on an AT&T or a T-Mobile iPhone, you can also surf the web (through Safari) while talking on the phone. You can surf while you talk if your only outlet to cyberspace is, say, the pokier AT&T EDGE network or CDMA network which Sprint relies on.

Here are other things you can do on a call:

- Mute call: From the main screen (shown in Figure 4-8), tap Mute. Now you need not mutter under your breath when the caller ticks you off. Tap Mute again to unmute the sound.
- Tap to display the Contacts list: This option is useful if you want to look up a friend's name while you're talking to another pal or add someone to a conference call.
- Place call on hold: Touch and hold the aforementioned Mute icon. Tap Mute again to take the person off hold. You might put a caller on hold to answer another incoming call or to make a second call yourself.

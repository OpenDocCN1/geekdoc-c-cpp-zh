
If you're using Exchange, iCloud, or certain IMAP-type email accounts, you may even be able to search messages stored on the email provider's servers or out in the Internet cloud, assuming of course that you have an active connection.

The capability to search emails may vary by email account. In some instances, you can search only whole words; in other cases, partial words may vary.

You can delete messages in bulk. In the same way, you can move them to another folder in bulk. Tap Edit and tap the circle to the left of each message you want to move so that a check mark appears. Tap the Move button at the bottom of the screen (refer to Figure 12–10), and tap the new folder where you want those messages to hang out.

## Don't grow too attached to attachments

Your iPhone can even receive email messages with attachments in a wide variety of file formats. Here are some of the most common:

- **Apple Keynote:** .key
- **Apple Numbers:** .numbers
- **Apple Pages:** .pages
- **Contact information:** .vcf
- **Images:** .jpg, .tiff, .gif, .png
- **Microsoft Excel:** .xls, .xlsx
- **Microsoft PowerPoint:** .ppt, .pptx
- **Microsoft Word:** .doc, .docx
- **Preview and Adobe Acrobat:** .pdf
- **Rich Text:** .rtf
- **Text:** .txt
- **Web pages:** .htm, .html

The iPhone can also play many types of audio attachments that turn up in an email, including MP3, AAC, WAV, and AIFF.

> If the attachment is a file format not supported by the iPhone (for example, a Photoshop file), you see the name of the file but you can't open it on your iPhone. In some other instances, you may see an Open Button if one of your apps handle the file type. Often, an attachment will automatically appear in the body of a message — no action is required on your part.

If that doesn’t happen in a given email, here’s how to read an attachment:

- 1. Open the mail message containing the attachment.
- 2. Tap the attachment.

You probably need to scroll down to see the attachment, which appears at the bottom of the message. The attachment downloads to your iPhone (see Figure 12-15, left) and opens automatically (see Figure 12-15, right).

- 3. Read the attachment.
- 4. Tap the back button in the upper-left corner of the screen to return to the message.

## More things you can do with messages

Wait! You can do even more with your incoming messages:

- To see all recipients of a message or their full names, tap Details (displayed in blue) to the right of the visible recipient name. If you don’t see details recipients were kept hidden by the sender.

If all recipients are displayed, you see Hide in blue rather than Details. Tap Hide to hide all names except the sender's and your own.

- > Add an email recipient or sender to your contacts, tap the name or email address at the top of the message and then either Create New Contact or Add to Existing Contact.

- > Mark a message as unread or flag it to call attention to it later, tap the flag (at the lower-left corner of the screen). Tap Mark as Unread or Flag, depending on your preference. If you choose the former, the message is again included in the unread message count on the Mail icon and your inbox has a blue dot next to it in the message list for your mailbox. If you choose flag, an orange flag or orange dot will appear to the message in the message list. (You can switch from flag to dot back in Settings.)

A couple of other options are presented here. Tap Move to Junk for messages that are unwanted pitches, advertising, or dare we suggest, something worse, pornography or spam. Tap Notify Me to receive notifications when someone responds to the email.

Alternatively, you can mark messages in bulk as unread by tapping the Edit button and then tapping the circle next to each message you want to mark as unread. Mark All so that all the buttons are filled in. From here, you can move the marked messages or trash them.

- > To make a sender VIP, tap the name or email address at the top of the message and then Add to VIP. This tool is especially considering that most of us deal with lots of unimportant email messages, which tend to bury the important messages next to the spouse, the kids, the boss, and clients. A star appear next to any incoming messages from a VIP or threads that the designated VIP is part of. The feature is iCloud-enabled, so a VIP on your iPhone will remain a VIP in Mail across all your iOS and Mac computers. You can summon mail from all your VIPs by tapping the VIP folder in the list of mailboxes (refer to Figure 12-6). To demote a VIP to what you rightly refer (translation: not very important person), tap the name or email at the top of the message and then Remove from VIP. Don't worry; she or he will never know.

- > To zoom in and out of a message, employ the pinch gestures.
- > To follow a link, tap the link (links are typically in blue). If the link is a URL, Safari opens. If the link is a number, Phone.

## Sending an email

To compose a message, follow these steps:

- 1. Tap the compose icon (see Figure 12-9), in the lower-right.

The New message screen appears, ready for you to start typing the recipient's name.

- 2. Enter the recipients' names in one of the following ways:
  - Type the names or email addresses in the To field.
  - Tap the microphone key on the virtual keyboard to dictate the names or email addresses.
  - Tap the + to the right of the To field to select a contact from your iPhone.

You can rearrange names in the field by dragging them around, moving, say, a name from the To field to the Cc field.

- 3. (Optional) Enter a name in the Cc field, Bcc field, or both fields. Or choose to send mail from any of your email accounts:
  - a. Tap the field labeled Cc, Bcc, or From. Doing so breaks the single field into separate Cc, Bcc, and From fields. The Cc label stands for *carbon copy*, and Bcc stands for *blind carbon copy*.
  - b. Tap the recipient Cc or Bc field and type the name. Or tap the + icon that appears in one of those fields to add a contact.

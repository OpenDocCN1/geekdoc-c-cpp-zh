If you have a 3D Touch-capable iPhone, you can get a quick start on shooting. Press your finger against the Camera app and choose Record Video Slo-mo, a handy lead-in to the next section.

## Going slow

If you have the iPhone 5s or later, you get another shooting benefit: the capability to capture video in slow motion, which we think is truly nifty. You'll love back in slow motion your kid's amazing catch in the varsity football game, like the TV networks do show super plays by the pros.

On the 6 and 6 Plus, you have the option to shoot slow-mo at 720 and 240 FPS. Choose Settings > Camera & Record Slo-mo, and then choose 720p at 120 FPS or 720p at 240 FPS. On the 6s, 6 Plus, SE, 7, and 7 Plus, you can record slo-mo at 1080p HD at 120 FPS or 720p at 240 FPS.

After shooting, you get to select where to play back the video at quarter speed, and we show you in a moment.

But first things: You have to grab the video before you can play back. To shoot in slow motion, launch the Camera app and select Slo-Mo as your shooting format of choice.

Shoot your slow-motion footage the same way you shoot at regular speeds. Note that the white circle surrounding the red shutter icon has teeny-tiny lines around it.

Tap the slow-motion video you want to watch. The video starts playing at normal speed, and then slows at a point determined by the phone. You can adjust this slow-motion section by sliding the vertical bars above the frame viewer (see Figure 10-5). When the vertical lines are close, the video is playing back at a normal speed. The video plays back slowly when the lines are spread apart. When you're playing back a segment in motion, any accompanying audio is slowed down.

## Sharing video

Unlike other video on your iPhone, you can play back what you've just shot in portrait or landscape mode. And if the video is any good (and why wouldn't it be?), you're likely going to want to share it with others. To do so, display the playback controls by tapping the screen, and tap the share icon, which appears all the way to the bottom left of the screen (in the margin). You have several options: Make the video part of a slideshow (see Chapter 9), email it, include it in a message or in your Notes app, or send it to iCloud Photo Sharing, Facebook, Vimeo, or YouTube. (You have additional options if you've enabled a Chinese keyboard.) You can also add the video to the Notes app, or send it via AirDrop to a person who is nearby and carrying his or own AirDrop-compatible device.

To help you remember a video that's worth sharing, tap the heart icon that appears with the playback controls to designate it a favorite. As noted in Chapter 9, you can also tap the heart for still images.

## Watching video on TV

The Retina display on the iPhone is worth boasting, but sometimes you want to watch videos on your iPhone on a larger television. Apple affords you several ways to do so:

- **Add a cable:** connect the iPhone to TV. The cable you choose depends on your TV setup, the iPhone model. Wired connections include A/V adapter (iPhone 4); Lightning-to-AV adapter. Some older cables no longer available directly from Apple, but you can typically find them online.
- **Use wireless streaming:** You've heard us mention AirPlay. If you're watching video, tap the AirPlay icon and choose Apple TV from the list of AirPlay devices. If all goes well, the video you're watching now appears like magic on your TV. If it doesn't go well, make sure the Apple TV is tapping into the same wireless network.
- **Mirror:** take AirPlay streaming one step further and exploit the AirPlay Mirroring feature. It lets you share, or mirror, to a TV anything on the iPhone screen — not just videos or music, but also games, pictures, web pages, and so on. As you rotate the iPhone from portrait to landscape or back, AirPlay Mirroring follows the big screen.

# Alice's Adventures in Wonderland

by Lewis Carroll

# Chapter I. Down the Rabbit-Hole

Alice was beginning to get very tired of sitting by her sister on the bank, and of having nothing to do: once or twice she had peered into the book her sister was reading, but it had no pictures or conversations in it, 'and what is the use of a book,' 'without pictures or conversation?'

So she was considering in her own mind as much as she could, for the hot day she felt very sleepy and stupid, that the pleasure of making a daisy-chain would be worth it of getting up and picking the daisies, when suddenly a White Rabbit with pink eyes ran by her.

There was nothing very remarkable in that, so Alice thought it was very much out of the place for the Rabbit to say to her; but as she thought it over afterwards, it occurred to her that she ought to have wondered at this, but at the time it seemed natural); when the Rabbit actually took a watch out of his waistcoat-pocket, looked at it, and then hurried on, Alice started to follow, for it flashed across her mind that she had never before seen a rabbit with either a waistcoat-pocket, or a watch to take out of, and burning with curiosity, she ran across the field after, and fortunately was just in time to pop down a large rabbit-hole under the hedge.

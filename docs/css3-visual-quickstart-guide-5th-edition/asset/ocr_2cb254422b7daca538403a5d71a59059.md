Chapter I. Down the Rabbit-Hole

Alice was beginning to get very tired of sitting by her sister on the bank, and having nothing to do once or twice she had peeked into the book her sister was reading; had no pictures or conversations in, what the use of a book, thought Alice "without pictures or conversation"
So she was considering in her own mind (as she was so sleepy, the hot day made her feel very drowsy), that the pleasure of making a walk would be the worth of getting up and walking down the daisies, when White Rabbit-Hole ran so quickly by her
There was nothing so remarkable as that, nor did Alice say it so much when she thought it over afterwards. It occurred to her that she had not so much expected it, but the time it all seemed quite natural, when the Rabbit actually took a watch out of his waistcoat-coat, pocketed it, and then hurried away. Alice started for her feet, for it thought very curious to see a rabbit take a watch out of a waistcoat, or to put a watch in pocket; with burning curiosity, she ran across the field and, fortunately was just in time to pop down a large rabbit-hole under the hedge

# ## CHAPTER V. ADVICE FROM A CATERLLAR

Alice was tired and shut her eyes; for a minute or two the Caterpillar took out of its mouth and yawned.

The Caterpillar looked at her for some time; Alice looked at the Caterpillar. Alice, wanting a conversation, yawned, a long, sleepy yawn.

"WHAT ARE YOU? said Alice.

Now they began to have a conversation. Alice replied, "hardly know, just as I am present—at least as I thought I myself when I got up this morning, I think I must have changed several times since then."

"I can't believe myself," said Alice.

"I'm not very sure myself," said Alice.

"Then who are YOU? said the Caterpillar.

"I'm... Alice," said Alice, "but I don't know what I was, when I said it."

"That's the right answer," said the Caterpillar.

"Why are you so sleepy?" said Alice.

The Caterpillar was blowing smoke in the form of a ring.

Alice thought a little while what to say to the Caterpillar, but as she did not think of any good replies.

"You're very rude," said Alice.

"I'm not," said the Caterpillar.

"Then you are!" said Alice.

"Munch?" said the Caterpillar.

"I didn't say munch!" said Alice.

"You thought your thought," said the Caterpillar.

"I didn't!" said Alice.

"You did," said the Caterpillar.

"Well, I'm glad I'm not you!" said Alice.

The Caterpillar began to smoke again.

"What do you do?" said Alice.

"Some change," said the Caterpillar.

"I don't understand what," said Alice.

"Then do some!" said the Caterpillar.

"I can't!" said Alice.

"Why can't?" said the Caterpillar.

"Because I don't know what!" said Alice.

"That's right," said the Caterpillar.

"But I mean what?" said Alice.

"Everything!" said the Caterpillar.

"You make no no sense," said Alice.

"Some people do," said the Caterpillar.

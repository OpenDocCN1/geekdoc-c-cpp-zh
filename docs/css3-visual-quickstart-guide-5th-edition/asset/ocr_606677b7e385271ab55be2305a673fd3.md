# ALICE'S ADVENTURES IN WONDERLAND

by LEWIS CARROLL

# Chapter I. Down the Rabbit-Hole

Alice was beginning to get very tired of sitting by her sister on the bank, and having nothing to do once or twice she had peeped into the book her sister was reading, and it had no pictures or conversations in, and what is the use of a book, thought Alice, without pictures or conversations?
So she was considering in vain what to do, for the hot day she felt so sleepy and drowsy, whether the pleasure of making a daisy would be worth the trouble of getting up and picking the daisies, when she saw a Rabbit with eyes so close by.

> "Twinkle, twinkle, little bat!
How I wonder what you are!"

There was nothing so remarkable in that, nor did Alice think it so much of an odd thing to hear a Rabbit say to himself ("Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be late!" (when she was so astonished to hear the Rabbit speak aloud, but it all seemed quite natural), that the Rabbit actually took out of his waistcoat-pocket, a watch, and then ran off. Alice started after him, for it was curious to see a Rabbit with a watch, to watch out of it, and burning with curiosity, she ran across the field and fortunately was in time to pop-down a large Rabbit-hole under the hedge.

These simple first-order filters work by moving the pole back and forth across the real axis while holding a fixed zero at Nyquist. When the pole is on the right side of the unit circle, low frequencies are gained up due to their close proximity to the pole. High frequencies are close to the zero and get attenuated (Figure 6.2). This produces the low-pass response in Figure 6.3.

When the pole/zero pair are reversed, the opposite effect happens and the high frequencies are now boosted with low frequencies attenuated. There is also a zero at 0 Hz. This produces the high-pass response. These are simple but very useful designs and are found in many effects including delay and reverb algorithms.

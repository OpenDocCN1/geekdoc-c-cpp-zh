```

# Constructing the Projection Matrix

Suppose our second camera is rotated by a rotation matrix \(\bar{R}\) and then located at a point \(\mathbf{c}_2 \neq \mathbf{0}\). We seek a projection matrix, \(P_2\) that will project a point in 3-space onto the focal plane of this camera. \(\bar{R}\) moves the camera. However, what we need is not “where is the camera?” but rather “where is the world, as viewed by this camera?” Remember, in some sense, the observer is *inside* the camera, and the world is rotating. To take this into account, all we need is a transpose:

```
\(R = \bar{R}^T . \quad (12.10)\)

In this scenario, Blue's rate of fire is twice Red's and Blue wins easily:

\(\frac{\chi_R}{\chi_B} = \frac{.0001}{.0002} = .5, \frac{B_0}{R_0} = \frac{100}{100} = 1, .5 < 1.\) (3.3.28)

# # How Many Units Remain at Time t?

The number of units remaining at a particular time is given by these equations:

\(B = B_0 \frac{\chi_B B_0 - \chi_R R_0}{\chi_B B_0 - \chi_R R_0 \exp((\chi_R R_0 - \chi_B B_0)t)}\)
\(R = R_0 \frac{\chi_R R_0 - \chi_B B_0}{\chi_R R_0 - \chi_B B_0 \exp((\chi_B B_0 - \chi_R R_0)t)}.\) (3.3.29)

Here are the results of this scenario after the first 100 seconds. The situation does not look good for Red.

\(\chi_B B_0 = .0002 \cdot 100 = .02\)
\(\chi_R R_0 = .0001 \cdot 100 = .01\)
\(B = 100 \cdot \frac{.02 - .01}{.02 - .01 \cdot \exp((.01 - .02) \cdot 100)} = 61\)
\(R = 100 \cdot \frac{.01 - .02}{.01 - .02 \cdot \exp((.02 - .01) \cdot 100)} = 23.\) (3.3.30)

# # How Many Units Are Left When One Army Loses?

As explained in the next section, the combat in this scenario always lasts forever, but as time goes to infinity, the number of units in each army approaches a value. If the armies are equally matched, the number of units remaining in both armies approaches 0.

\(B_{t=\infty} = B_0 - \frac{\chi_R R_0}{\chi_B} R_0, \frac{\chi_R}{\chi_B} \le \frac{B_0}{R_0}\)
\(R_{t=\infty} = R_0 - \frac{\chi_B B_0}{\chi_R} B_0, \frac{\chi_R}{\chi_B} \ge \frac{B_0}{R_0}\) (3.3.31)

In this scenario, Red loses and the number of its units approaches 0. The final number of units in Blue approaches 50.

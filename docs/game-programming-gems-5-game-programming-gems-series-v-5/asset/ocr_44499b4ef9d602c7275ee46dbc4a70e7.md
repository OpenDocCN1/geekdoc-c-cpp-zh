13. Given two versors A and B, each defines a subspace of V; the intersection of these subspaces is called their meet and is denoted by M = A ∩ B. It can be computed via A ∩ B = A' _|_ B = (A _|_ J) _|_ B. The dual concept is the join, which is the smallest subspace of V containing the two subspaces, written as J = A ∪ B. For example, the meet of two nonparallel planes in 3D is the line of their intersection, as shown in Figure 2.1.7. The meet of two versors can be thought of as the greatest common divisor of the defining vectors, and the join as the least common multiple. Knowing one allows easy computation of the other via the relations shown in Equation 2.1.16.

```
J = A ^ (M^-1 _|_ B)
M = (B _|_ J^-1) _|_ A
(2.1.16)
```

# References

[A90] "The Performance of Spin Lock Alternatives for Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" by Thomas E. Anderson. IEEE TEDS Volume 1:1, January 1990. A classic paper on how different locking alternatives do and don't scale. By Tom Anderson, very well researcher in both systems and networking. And of a very fine OS textbook, we must say.

[B-10] "An Analysis of Linux Scalability to Many Cores" by Silas Boyd-Wicker, Austin T. Clements, Young Mao, Alexander Pesterov, M. Frans Kabeek, Robert Morris, Nicholas Zeldovich. 2010. A modern paper on the difficulties of scaling to many cores.

[CS09] "Parallel Computer Architecture: A Hardware/Software Approach" by David E. Cull, James P Sinclair, and Anoop Gupta. Georg Kaufmann, 1999. A fine textbook with detailed parallel details and algorithms. As Mark Hill observes on the jacket, the book contains more information than most research papers.

[M11] "Towards Transparent CPU Scheduling" by Joseph T. Meehan. Doctoral Dissertation at University of Wisconsin-Madison, 2011. A dissertation that covers all the details of how modern linux multiprocessor scheduling works. Pretty awesome! But, as co-advisors of lee, we be a bit lost here.

[SHW11] "A Primer on Memory Consistency and Cache Coherence" by Daniel J. Sorin, Mark D. Hill, and A. Wood. Synthesis Lectures in Computer Architecture. Morgan and Clay-pool Publishers. Required for anyone who likes to know too much about a given topic.

[SA96] "Scalable Virtual Domains: A Fast and Accurate Mechanism for Provisional Share Resource Allocation" by Ion Stoica and Hussein Abdel-Wahab. Technical Report TR-95-22, Old Dominion University, 1996. A report on this cool scheduling idea, from Ion Stoica, a professor at U.C. Berkeley and world expert in networking, distributed systems, and many things.

[IT09] "Evaluating the Performance of Cache-Affinity Scheduling in Shared-Memory Multiprocessors" by Josep Torrellas, Andrew Tucker, Anoop Gupta. Journal of Parallel and Distributed Computing, Volume 24:2, February 1995. This is not the first paper on the topic, but it has citations to earlier work, and is a more readable and practical paper than some of the earlier-based analysis papers.

# References

[A11] "VIC: Interrupt Coalescing for Virtual Machine Device IO" by Irfan Ahmed, Ajay Gulati, Ali Mashtashed. USENIX '11. A survey of interrupt coalescing in traditional and virtualized environments.

[A14] "Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces" (Chapters: Crash Consistency: PSCK and Journaling Log-Structured Systems) by Remzi Arpacci-Dusseau and Andrea Arpacci-Dusseau Books, 2014. A description of a file-system checker and how it works, which requires low-level access to disk devices not normally properly provided by the file system.

[C01] "Empirical Study of Operating Systems" by Emery Chao, Jungfeng Yang, Benjamin Chad, Seth Halpern, and David Weiserbaum. The first paper to systematically explore how many features in modern operating systems are used. Along other findings, the authors show that device drivers have seven times more than the kernel code.

[C08] "The x86 Operating System" by Russ Cox, Franz Kaenkaob, Robert Morris, Nicholas Zeldovich. From: http://pdos.csail.mit/6.828/2008/index.html. We set lde, for the device drivers with a few more details included.

[D07] "What Operating Systems Should Know About Memory" by Ulrich Derepp, November, 2007. Available: http://www.akkai.org/derepp/cpumy.pdf. A fantastic read about modern operating systems, starting at DRAM and all the way up to virtualization and cache-optimized implementations.

[E08] "Error-Handling is Costly" by Haridai Gunai, Cindy Rubio-Gonzales, Andrea Arpacci-Dusseau, Remzi Arpacci-Dusseau, Neil Libist. FAST San Jose, CA, February 2008. Our work on building a tool to find code in Linux file systems that do not handle error return properly. We found hundreds and thousands of bugs, of which many have never fixed.

[H17] "Core i7-7700 review: com/remext/241950-intels-core-i7-7700-reviewed-kaby-likeubuntu-desktop.com" by Joel Hruska. January 3, 2017. www.extremech.com. An in-depth review of a current intel chip, including CPUs and the I/O subsystem.

[H18] "Hacker News" by many contributors. Available: https://www.ycombinator.com of the better aggregators for tech-related staff. Once back in 2013, this book became a highly-ranked entry, leading to 1 million downloads in one day! Said, "We have to experience such high",

[L94] "Attachment Interface for Disk Drives" by Lawrence J. Ramirez. Number: ANSI X3.221, 1994. Available: http://ftp.tio.org/e13/project/d07914rc-ata-1.pdf. A right dry document about device interfaces. Read it at your own pace

[RM96] "Receive Live in an Interrupt-driven Kernel" by Jeffrey Mog, K. K. Ramakeshan. USENIX '96. Michael Mog and colleagues made a great deal of pioneering work over network performance. This paper is just one example.

[S08] "Interrupts" by Mark Smotherman. July 2008. Available: http://people.clemson.edu/mark/interrupts.html. A treasure review of information on the history of interrupts, DMA, and related ideas in computing.

[S03] "Improving the Reliability of Commodity Operating Systems" by Michael M. Swift, Brian N. Levy: SOSP '03. Swift's work is reviewed in a more microkernel-approach to operating systems; finally, it found some reasons why address-space protection could be useful in a modern OS.

[W10] "Hard Disk Drive" by Washington State Course Homepage. Available online at this site: http://eecs.wsu.edu/~cs460/cs560/HDDdriver.html. A nice summary of the IDE disk drive's interface and how to build a driver for it.

# # References

[CV65] "Introduction and Overview of the Multics System" by F. J. Corbalo, A. V. Vyssotsky.
Fall Joint Computer Conference, 1968. One of five papers presented on Multics at the Fall Joint
Computer Conference; oh to be on a fly in the room that day!

[DD68] "Virtual Memory, Processes, and Sharing in Multics" by Robert C. Daley and Jack B.
Dennis. Communications of the ACM, Volume 11:5, May 1968. An early paper on how to perform
dynamic linking in Multics, which was as easy as time. Dynamic linking finally its way
into systems around 20 years ago, as the X-less libraries were demanded. It says that
these early libraries were MIT's retrieve for dynamic linking in early years
of Unix!

[Fac62] "Segment" by M. N. Greenfield. Proceedings of the SJCC, Volume 21,
1962. Another early paper on segmentation; so early that it has no references to other.

[Hol61] "Program Organization and Record Keeping for Dynamic Storage" by A. W. Holt. Communications of the ACM, Volume 4:10. An incredible early and easy to read paper
about segmentation and some of time.

[In64] "6 and IA-32 Architecture Software Developer's Manual" by Intel. 2009. Available: http://www.intel.com/products/processors/manuals. Reading about segmentation in
every chapter (Chapter 3 title: it'll hurt your head), like a fine wine,

[Ke68] "The Art of Computer Programming: Volume I" by Donald Knuth. Addison-Wesley,
1968. Knuth is famous not only for his early books on the Art of Computer Programming but for his
typesetting system introduced in October 1983. A true reference on how to build systems. Hard to
d type this very book. His tomes are always a great early references to many of the
algorithms that underlie computing systems today.

[LL83] "Hints for Computer Systems Design" by Butler Lamps. ACM Operating Systems
Review, a remarkable collection of advice on how to build systems. Hard to read in
sinking, take it a little at a time like a fine wine, or a reference manual.

[LV82] "Virtual Memory in the VAX/VMS Operating System" by Henry M. Levy,
P H. Lipman. IEEE Computer, Volume 15:3; March 1982. A classic memory management
system, with lots of sense in its design. We'll study it in more detail in a later chapter.

[RK68] "Dynamic Storage Allocation Systems" by B. Randell and J.C. Kuehner. Communications of the ACM, Volume 11:5; May 1968. A nice overview of the differences between paging and
sysystems with some historical context-

[R69] "A note on storage fragmentation and program segmentation" by Brian Randell. Communications of the ACM, Volume 12:7; July 1969. One of the earliest papers to discuss fragmentation-

[W95] "Dynamic Storage Allocation: A Survey and Critical Review" by Paul R. Wilson, Mark
S. Johnstone, Michael Neel, David Boles. International Workshop on Memory Management,
Scotland, UK, September 1995. A great survey paper on memory allocators.

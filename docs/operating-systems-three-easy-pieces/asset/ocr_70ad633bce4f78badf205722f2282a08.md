# References

[At02] "Cooperative Task Management Without Stack Management" by Atul Adya, John Howell, Marvin Theimer, William Bobsky, R. Douceur. USENIX ATC ’02, Monterey, CA, June 2002. This gem of a paper is the first to clearly articulate the difficulties of event-based concurrency, and suggests some simple solutions, which explores the ever crazier idea of combining the two types of concurrency management into a single application!

[FHR84] "Programming Continuations" by Daniel P. Friedrich, T. Haynes, Eugene E. Kohlbe. In Transformation and Programming Environments, Springer Verlag. The classic reference for many ideas from the world of programming languages. New paradigms are increasingly popular in modern systems.

[N13] "Documentation by those who built node.js. Available: node.js/api. One of the many new frameworks that help you rapidly build web services and applications. Every modern systems hacker should be proficient in most frameworks (and this one, more than one). Syd started by developing T/Tk, a cool scripting language and became an expert.

[O96] "Why Threads Are a Bad Idea for most purposes" by John Outerhout. Invited Talk at USENIX ’99, San Diego, CA, January 1996. A great talk about how threads aren't a great for GUI-based applications (but the ideas are not). Outerhout formed while he was developing many of these opinions and toolkit that made it 100x easier to develop GUI-based applications than the standard. While the TK GUI toolkit lives on it (Python for example), Tcl seems to be dying (un)fortunately).

[PDZ99] "Flash: An Efficient and Portable Web Server" by Steve S. Pai, Peter Drusch, William Zwaenaep. USENIX ’99, Monterey, CA, June 1999. A pioneering paper on how to structure web servers in the thread-burdened internet era. It outlines the basics as well as to see the authors how to build highly scalable asynchronous IO is lacking.

[SR05] "Advanced Concurrency in the UNIX Environment" by Richard Stevens and Stephen A. Rago. Addison-Wesley, 2005. Once again, we refer to the classic must-have-on-your-bookshelf of USENIX systems programming. If there is some detail you need to know, it is in here.

[WCB01] "SEDA: An Architecture for Well-Conditioned, Scalable Internet Services" by Matt Welsh, David Culler, Eric Brewer. SOSP ’01, Banff, Canada, October 2001. A nice twist on event-based serving combines threads, queues, and event-based handling into one streamlined whole. Some of these ideas have found their way into the infrastructures of companies such as Google, Amazon, and elsewhere.

> ASIDE: RTFM — Read THE MAN PAGES

Many times in this book, when referring to a particular system call or library call, we'll tell you to read the **manual pages**, or **man pages** for short. Man pages are the original form of documentation that exist on UNIX systems; realize that they were created before the thing **web** existed.

Some time reading man pages is a key step in the growth of a systems programmer; there are plenty of useful tidbits hidden in those pages. Some particularly useful pages to read are the man pages for whichever shell you use (e.g., tcsh, bash), and certainly for any systems call you make (in order to see what return values and error conditions exist).

As a result, modern systems include a strong confection of the notion of a **user**. The user, after entering a password to establish credentials, login to gain access to system resources. The user may launch one or many processes, and exercise full control over them (pause them, kill them, etc.). Users generally can control their own processes; it is the job of the operating system to parcel out resources (such as CPU, memory, disk) to each user (and their processes) to meet overall system goals.

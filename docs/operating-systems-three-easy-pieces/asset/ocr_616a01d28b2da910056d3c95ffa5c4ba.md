Unfortunately, when the program accesses a[3], we encounter another TLB miss. However, once again, the next entries a[4] ... a[6] will hit the TLB, as they all reside on the same page in memory.

Finally, access to a[7] causes one TLB miss. The hardware again consults the page table to figure out the location of this virtual page in physical memory, and updates the TLB. The final two accesses a[8] and a[9] are the benefits of this TLB when the hardware looks in the TLB for their translations, more than twice.

Let us summarize TLB activity during our ten accesses to the array: miss, hit, hit, hit, hit, hit. Thus, the TLB hit rate, the number of hits divided by the total number of accesses, is 70%. Although this is not too high (indeed, we see rates that approach 100%), it is non-zero, which may be a surprise. Even though this is the first time the program accesses the array, the TLB improves performance due to spatial locality. The elements of the array are packed tightly into pages (i.e., they are close to one another in space), and thus only the first access to an element on a page yields a TLB miss.

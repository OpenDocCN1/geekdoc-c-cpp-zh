**Professor**: Sounds good. Any questions?

**Student**: Oh! Why is it "Three Easy Pieces"?

**Professor**: That's an easy one. Well, you see, there are these great lectures on Physics by Richard Feynman...

**Student**: Oh the guy who wrote "Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman", right? Great book! This is going to be hilarious like that book was

**Professor**: Um... well, no. That book was great, and I'm glad you've read it. Hopefully this book is more like his notes on Physics. It is appropriate, as Operating Systems are about half as hard as Physics.

**Student**: I like physics, so that is probably good. What are those pieces?

**Professor**: They are the three key ideas we're going to learn about: **virtualization, concurrency, and persistence**. In learning these ideas, we'll learn all about how an operating system works, including what program to run on a CPU, how it handles memory, how to manage disks, and even a little about distributed system. **Student**: I have no idea what you're talking about, really.

**Professor**: Well, I think it is sometimes useful to use yourself outside of a narrative and think a bit; these dialogues are those times. So you and I are working together to make sense of all these pretty complex ideas. Are you up for it?

**Student**: So we have to think? Well, I'm up for that. I mean, what else do I have to do anyway? It's not like I have much of a life outside of this book.

**Professor**: Me neither, sadly. So let's get to work!

# Preface

# To Everyone

Welcome to this book! We hope you'll enjoy reading it as much as we enjoyed writing it. The book is called Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces (available at http://ostep.org), and the title is obviously an homage to one of the greatest sets of lecture notes ever created, by Richard Feynman on the topic of Physics [96]. While this book will undoubtedly fall short of the high standard set by that famous physicist, perhaps it will be good enough for you in your quest to understand what operating systems (and more generally, systems) are all about.

The three easy pieces refer to the three major thematic elements the book is organized around: virtualization, concurrency, and persistence. In discussing these concepts, we'll end up discussing many of the important things an operating system does; hopefully, you'll also have some along the way. Learning new things is fun, right? At least, (usually) it should be.

Each major concept is divided into a set of chapters, most of which present a particular problem and then how to solve it. The chapters are short, and try (as best as possible) to reference the source material where the ideas really come from. One of our goals in writing this book is to make the paths of history as clear as possible, as we think that helps a student understand what is, what was, and what will be more clearly. In this case, seeing how the sausage was made is as important as understanding what the sausage is for.

There are a couple devices we use throughout the book which are worth mentioning here. The first is the crux of the problem. We always try to solve a problem, we first try to state what the most important use is; such a crux of the problem is explicitly called out in the text, and hopefully solved via the techniques, algorithms, and ideas presented in the rest of the text.

In many places, we will explain how a system works by showing its behavior over time. These timelines are at the essence of understanding; if you know what happens, for example, when a process page faults, you are on your way to truly understanding how virtual memory operates.

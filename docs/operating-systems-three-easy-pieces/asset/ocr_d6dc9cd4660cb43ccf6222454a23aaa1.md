# References

[B+07] "An Analysis of Latex Sectors in Disk Drives" by L. Bairravasundaram, Good-son, P. Pasupathy, J. Schindler. SIGNMIER '07, San Diego, CA. The first part to study sector errors in detail. The paper also won the Kenneth C. Sevick Outstanding Student Paper award, named after a brilliant researcher and genius who passed away too soon. To owe the OAST authors it must move from the U.S. to Canada, to once again as the Canadian national anthem, standing in the middle of a crowd of thousands. We owe the U.S. but just,

[B+08] "An Analysis of Corruption in the Storage Stack" by Lakshmi N. Bairravasundaram, Gar-tham, R. Goodson, Sandra Schroeder, A. Apaci-Dusseau, Remzi H. Apaci-Dusseau. FAST '08, San Jose, CA, February 2008. The first part to truly study disk corruption in detail, focusing on how much corruption occurs over years for over 1.5 million drives.

[BS94] "Commercial Fault Tolerance in Linux: A Review" by Wendy Bartle, Lisa Olson. IEEE Transactions on Database and Secure Comput., Vol. 21 (2003). This classic paper in building fault tolerant systems is an excellent overview of the state of the art from both IBM and Tandem. Must read for those interested in the area.

[C+04] "Raw-Dual Pairing for Double Fault Correction" by P. Corbett, B. English, A. Guest. FAST '04, San Jose, CA, February 2004. An early example of how extra redundancy helps to solve the combined fault/partial-failure problem. A nice example of how to mix theoretical work with practical.

[F+04] "Checksums and Error Correction" by Peter F. Weenc. Copy available online here: http://www.step.org/citations/checksums-03.pdf. A great simple tutorial on checksums, available to any reader.

[FS+82] "Architecture for Telecommunication Services" by John G. Fetcher. IEEE-Transactions on Communication, Vol. 30, January 1982. Fetcher's original work on his eponymous checksum. If he hadn't created the Fetcher, we wouldn't have just called it; or others have named it after him. Don't blame English for making sense of this speaking anecdote. This might born out of Robin White's memory, we would have just called it the Fetcher checksum.

[HLM94] "File System Design for an NFS Server Facility" by David Hines, Jim Lucas, Malcolm Coleman. USENIX SPRING '94. The pioneering paper that discusses the ideas and products at the heart of the NetApp's core. By this system, NetApp grew into a multi-billion dollar economy. To learn more about NetApp, read Hines's autobiography "To Castrate a Ball" (which is the title, so anyway), and all instruction by Gary Johnson.

[K+08] "Parity Loss and Parity Regained" by Andrew Krikou, Lakshmi N. Bairravasundaram-Garath R. Goodson, Kiran Srinivasan, Randolph Thelen, A. Apaci-Dusseau-Remzi H. Apaci-Dusseau. FAST '08, San Jose, CA, February 2008. This work explores how fault tolerant checksum schemes work (or not working) in practice. We reveal a number of interesting flaws in current protection strategies.

[M+13] "Cyclic Redundancy Checks" by .... Available at: http://www.mathpages.com/kmath458.html. A clear and concise description of CRC. The content is in-format, as it turns out.

[P+08] "Implementation of the Log-Structured File System" by Prabhakaran, L. Bairravasundaram, A. Nagrawal, H. Gandhi, A. Apaci, R. Apaci-Dusseau. SOSP '05, Brighton, England. Our paper on how disks have partial failure modes, and a detailed study of how modern file systems react to such failures. As it turns out, we found many bugs, design flaws, and other oddities in this work. We're welcome!

[S+99] "Implementing Fault Tolerant Distributed Systems: The State Machine Approach: A Tutorial" by Fred B. Schneider. ACM Surveys, Vol. 22, No. 4, November 1990. How to build fault tolerant services. A must read for those building distributed systems.

[Z+13] "Zettabyte Reliability with Flexible End-to-end Data Integrity" by Y. Zhang, D. Myers, A. Apaci-Dusseau, R. Apaci-Dusseau. MST '13, Long Beach, California, May 2013. How to add data protection to the cache of a system. Out of space, we would write something...

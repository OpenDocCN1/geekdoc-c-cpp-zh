# DISTRIBUTED SYSTEMS

# References

[A70] "The ALOHA System – Another Alternative for Computer Communications" by Norman Abraham. The 1970 Fall Joint Computer Conference. The ALOHA network pioneered some basic concepts in networking, including exponential back-off and retransmission, which formed the basis for communication in shared-bus Ethernet networks for years.

[BN84] "Implementing Remote Procedure Calls" by Andrew D. Birrell, Jay Nelson. ACM TOCS, Volume 2:1, February 1984. The foundational RPC system upon which all others builds. Another pioneering effort from our friends at Xerox PARC.

[MK99] "The Effectiveness of Checks for Embedded Control Networks" by Theresa C. Maxwell and Philip J Koopman, IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing, 6,1, January 1999. A nice exercise in deadlock and some performance and robustness comparisons between them.

[LH89] "Memory Coherence in Shared Virtual Systems" by Kai Li and Paul Hudak. ACM TOCS, 7:4, November 1989. The introduction of software-based shared memory via virtual memory, a distributing idea for sake, but not a lasting or good one in the end.

[SK09] "Principles of Computer System Design" by Jerome H. Saltzer and Frank Kaasek. Norman-Kaufmann, 2009. An excellent book on systems, and a must for every bookshelf. One of the few terrific designs on naming we've seen.

[SR84] "End-to-End Arguments in Systems" by Jerome H. Saltzer, David P. Reed, and David D. Clark. ACM TOCS, 2:4, November 1984. A beautiful description of layering, abstraction, and universality must ultimately in computer systems.

[VJ88] "Congestion Avoidance and Control" by Van Jacobson. SIGCOMM '88 . A pioneering paper on how clients should adjust to perceived network congestion; definitely one of the key pieces of technology underlying the Internet, and a must read for anyone serious about systems, and for Van’s relatives because well relatives read all of your pages.

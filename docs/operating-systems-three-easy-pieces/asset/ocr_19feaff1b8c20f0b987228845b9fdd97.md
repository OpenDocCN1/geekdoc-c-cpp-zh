# ASIDE: TYPES OF CACHE MISSES

In the computer architecture world, architects sometimes find it useful to characterize misses by type, into one of three categories: compulsory, capacity and conflict misses, sometimes called the Three C's [H87]. A compulsory miss (or cold-start miss) occurs because the cache is evicted); to begin with this is the first reference to the item; in contrast, a capacity miss occurs because the cache ran out of space and had to evict one to bring a new item into the cache. The third type of miss (a conflict miss) occurs because of limits on where an item can be placed in a hardware cache, due to something known as set-associativity); it does not arise in the OS page cache because such caches are always fully-associative, i.e., there are no restrictions on where a page can be placed. # 22.3 A Simple Policy: FIFO

Many early systems avoid the complexity of trying to approach and employ simple replacement policies. For example, some used FIFO (first-in, first-out) replacement, where pages were simply placed in a queue when they enter the system; when a replacement occurs, the page on the tail of the queue (the “first-in” page) is evicted. FIFO has one great strength: it is simple to implement.

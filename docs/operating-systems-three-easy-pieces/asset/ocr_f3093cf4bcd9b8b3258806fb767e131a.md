We conclude by analyzing I/O latency in RAID-4. As you now know, a single read (assuming no failure) is mapped to a single disk, and its latency is equivalent to the latency of a single disk request. The latency of a single write requires two reads and two writes; the reads can happen in parallel, as the writes, and total latency is about twice that of a single disk (with some differences because we have to wait for both reads to complete and get the worst-case positioning time, but the updates don't incur cost and thus may be a better-than-average positioning cost).

# 38.7 RAID Level 5: Rotating Parity

To address the small-write problem (at least, partially), Patterson, Gibson, and Katz introduced RAID-5 works almost identically to RAID-4, except that it rotates the parity block across disks (Figure 3.7).

| Disk 0 | Disk 1 | Disk 2 | Disk 3 | Disk 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | P0 |
| 5 | 6 | 7 | P1 | 4 |
| 10 | 11 | P2 | 8 | 9 |
| 15 | P3 | 12 | 13 | 14 |
| P4 | 16 | 17 | 18 | 19 |

As you can see, the parity block for each stripe is now rotated across the disks, in order to remove the parity–disk bottleneck for RAID-4.

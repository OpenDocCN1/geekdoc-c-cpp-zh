# INTRODUCTION TO OPERATING SYSTEMS

ASIDE: THE IMPORTANCE OF UNIX

It is difficult to overstate the importance of UNIX in the history of operating systems. Influenced by earlier systems (in particular, the famous Multics system from MIT), UNIX brought together many great ideas and a system that was both simple and powerful.

Underlying the original "Bell Labs" UNIX was the unifying principle of building small powerful programs that could be connected together to form larger workflows. The shell, where you type commands, provided primitives as pipes to enable such meta-level programming, and thus became easy to string together programs to accomplish a big task. For example, to find lines of a file that have the word "foo" in them, and to count how many lines exist, you would type: grep file.txt | using the grep and wc (word count) programs to achieve each.

The UNIX environment was friendly for programmers and developers alike, also providing a compiler for the new C language. Making it easy for programmers to write their own programs, as well as share them, made UNIX enormously popular. And it probably helped a lot that the authors could copy for free to anyone who asked, especially free open-source software.

Of critical importance was the accessibility and readability of the code. Having a beautiful kernel written in C invited others to play with the kernel, adding new and cool features. For example, an enterprising group at Berkeley, by Bill Joy, made a wonderful distribution (the Berkeley Systems Distribution, or BSD) which had some advanced virtual memory, file system, and networking subsystems. Unix later found Microsystems.

Unfortunately, the spread of UNIX was slow a bit as companies tried to assert ownership and profit from it, an unfortunate (but common) result of lawyers getting involved. Many companies had their own variants: SunOS, AIX from IBM, HPUX (a.k.a. “H-Pucks”) from HP, and IRIX from SGL. The legal wrangling among AT&T/Bell Labs and these other players cast a dark cloud over UNIX, and many wondered if it would survive, especially as Windows was introduced and took over much of the PC market...

# CONCURRENCY: AN INTRODUCTION

# References

+   [D65] "Solution of a problem in concurrent programming control" by E. W. Dijkstra. Communications of the ACM, 8(9):569, September 1965. Pointed out as the first paper of Dijkstra's he outlines the mutual exclusion problem and a solution. The solution, however, is not widely used; advanced hardware and OS support is needed, and as well seen in the coming chapters.

+   [D68] "Sequential processes" by Edsger Dijkstra. 1968. Available at: http://www.cs.utexas.edu/users/EDW/ed0123.pdf. Dijkstra has an anonymous number of his papers, notes, and theses recorded (or posted) on this website at the last place he worked, University of Amsterdam. His work, however, earlier while he was at the Technische Hochschule of Eindhoven (THE), includes this famous paper on "sequencing processes", which basically outlines all of the thinking that has to go into operating system multi-threaded programs. Dijkstra discovered much of this while working on an operating system named "THE", and last appeared in 1969.

+   [GR92] "Transaction Processing: Concepts and Techniques" by Jim Gray and Andreas Reuter. Morgan Kaufmann, September 1992. This book is the bible of transaction processing, written by one of the legends of the field, Jim Gray. It is, also considered Jim Gray's "brain dump", in which he wrote down tragically a few years back, and many of us lost a friend and great mentor, including the co-authors of said book. Gray died during the turmoil of the 1990s.

+   [LM93] "Atomic Transactions" by Nancy Lynch, Michael Merritt, Wilhelm Weih, Alan Stone. Morgan Kaufmann, August 1993. A nice text on the theory and practice of atomic transactions for distributed systems. Perhaps a bit formal for some, but lots of practice is found here.

+   [NM92] "What Are Race Conditions? Some Issues and Formalizations" by Robert H. B. Netzer and Barton P. Miller. ACM Letters on Programming Languages and Systems, Volume 1:1: March 1992. An excellent discussion of the different types of races found in concurrent programs. In this chapter (and the next few), we focus on data races, but later we broaden to general races as well.

+   [SR05] "Advanced Programming in the UNIX Environment" by W. Richard Stevens and Stephen A. R. Rago. Addison-Wesley, 2005. As we've said many times, buy this book, and read it, and in little chunks, preferably before going to bed. This will make you fall asleep more quickly; importantly, you learn a little more about how to be a serious UNIX programmer.

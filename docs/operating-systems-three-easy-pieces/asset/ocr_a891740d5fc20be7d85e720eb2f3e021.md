# BEYOND PHYSICAL MEMORY: MECHANISMS

# References

[CS94] "Take Our Word For It" by F. Corbato, R. Steinberg, www.takeourword.com/TOW146 (Page 4). Richard Steinberg writes: "Someone asked me the origin of the word daemon as it applies to computing. Best I can tell on my research, the word was first used by people on your team at Project MAC using the IBM 7094 in 1963." Professor Corbato replies: "Our use of the daemon was inspired by Maxwell's daemon of physics and thermodynamics (my background is in physics). Maxwell's daemon was an imaginary agent who helped sort molecules of different speeds and worked tirelessly in the background. We used the daemon to describe background processes

[D97] "Before Memory Was Virtual" by Peter Denning. In the Beginning: Collections of Software Pioneers, Wiley, November 1997. An excellent historical piece by one of the pioneers of virtual memory and working sets.

[G95] "idleness is not lost" by Richard Golding, Peter Bosch, Carl Staelin, Tim Sullivan, John Wilkes. USENIX ATC '96, New Orleans, Louisiana. A fun and easy-to-read discussion of how idle time can be better used in systems, with lots of good examples.

[LL82] "Virtual Memory Management in the VAX/VMS Operating System" by Hank Levy, P. Lipman. IEEE Computer, Vol. 15, No. 3, March 1982. Not the first place where page clustering was used, but a clear and simple explanation of how such a mechanism works. We cite this paper a lot!

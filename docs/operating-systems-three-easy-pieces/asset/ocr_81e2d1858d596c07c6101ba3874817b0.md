# A DIALOGUE ON THE BOOK

**Professor:** Excellent query! Well, each person needs to figure this out on their own, of course, but here is what I would do: go to class, to hear the professor introduce the material. Then, at the end of every week, read these notes, the ideas sink into your head a bit better. Of course, some time later (hint: before the exam!) Oh read the notes again to firm up your knowledge. Of course, your professor will not assign some homework and projects, so you should do; in particular, doing projects where you write code to solve real problems is the best way to put the ideas within these notes into action. As Confucius said...

**Student:** Oh, I know! 'I hear and forget. I see and remember. I do and I understand.' 'Or something like that'

**Professor:** (surprised) How did you know what I was going to say?!

**Student:** It seemed to follow. Also, I am a big fan of Confucius, and an even bigger fan of Xunzi, who is actually a better source for this quote':

**Professor:** (stunned) Well, I think we are going to get along just fine! Just fine indeed.

**Student:** – just one more question, if I may. What are these dialogues for? Mean, isn't this just supposed to be a book? Why not present the material directly?

**Professor:** Ah, good question, good question! Well, I think it is sometimes useful to use yourself outside of a narrative and think a bit; these dialogues are those times. So you and I are going to working together to make sense of all of these pretty complex ideas. Are you up for it?

**Student:** So we have to think? Well, I'm up for that. I mean, what else do I have to do anyway? It's not like I have much of a life outside of this book.

**Professor:** Me neither, sadly. So let's get to work!

> ^According to http://www.barrypopik.com (on, December 19, 2012, entitled "Tell and I forget; teach me and involve me and I will learn") Confucian philosopher Xunzi said "Not having heard something is not as good as having heard it; having heard is not as good as having seen it; having seen it is not as good as knowing it; knowing is not as good as putting it into practice."

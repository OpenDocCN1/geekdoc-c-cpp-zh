# # References

[BH70] "The Nucleus of a Multiprogramming System" by Perch Hansen, Communications of the ACM, 13:4, April 1970. The first paper to suggest that the OS, kernel, should be a minimal and flexible substrate for building customized operating systems; this theme is revisited throughout OS research history.

[CV65] "Introduction and Overview of the Multics System" by F. J. Corbato

[DV66] "Programming Semantics for Multiprogramming Systems" by Jack B. Dennis, Earl C. Van Hoorn, 1960. Communications of the ACM, Volume 9, Number 3, March 1966. An early paper that was not the first but

[LI60] "Man-Computer Symbiosis" by J. C. R. Lickider. Transactions on Human Functions in HFE, E-1. A fascinating paper about how computers and people are going to enter into a symbiotic age; well ahead of its time to be a fascinating reading nonetheless.

[M62] "Time-Sharing Computer Systems" by M. McCarthy. Management and Control of the Future, MIT Press, Cambridge, MA 1962. Perhaps McCarthy's earliest recorded paper on time sharing. He had been thinking of the idea since 1955. McCarthy left the systems behind and went to become a terrific advocate of the LISP programming language. See McCarthy's home for more info: http://www-formal.stanford.edu

[M75] "A System Design for a Small Computer" by J. McCarthy, S. Bollen, E. Fredkin, J. C. R. Licklider. A system that swapped program to the "drum" when the program was not running, and then back into "core" when it was about to run.

[M83] "On the History of Time Sharing" by John McCarthy, 1983. Available: http://www-formal.stanford.edu/history/time-sharing/time-sharing.html. A terrific on how the idea of time sharing might have come fazhtorm, including some doubts towards those who chose sharing [59] by the pioneering work in this article.

[NS07] "Valgrind: A Framework for Heavyweight Dynamic Instrumentation" by N. Nethercke, J. Seward. PLDI 2007, San Diego, California, June 2007. Valgrind is a lifesaver of a program for those who use languages like C. Read this paper to learn about its very cool instrumentation techniques - "A quite impressive."

[S89] "A System Design for Microkernels" by R. Rashid, D. Jullin, D. Orr, R. Sanzi, R. Baron, A. Forin, D. Golub, M. Jones. CONPC'89, February 1989. Although not the first proposal for microkernels per se, the Mach project at CMU was well-known and influential; it still lives today in the bowels of Mac OS X.

[S99] "Time Sharing in Large Computers" by C. Strachey. Proceedings of the International Conference on Information Processing, UNESCO, June 1999. One of the early references on time sharing.

[S03] "Improving the Reliability of Commodity Operating Systems" by M. M. Swift, B. N. Bershad, H. M. Levy. SOSP '03. The first paper to show how micro-kernel technique is touching on operating system reliability.

# References

[804] "Implementing Condition Variables with Semaphores" by Andrew Birrell. December 2004. An insight into how difficult CVs on semaphores really is, and the mistakes the author and co-workers made along the way. Particularly relevant because the group had done a ton of concurrent programming; Birrell, is known for (among others) writing various thread-programming guides.

[808] "Real-world Concurrency" by Bryan Cantrill, Jeff Bonwick. ACM Queue. Volume 6, No. 5, September 2008. An article by some hackers from a company formerly known as Sun on the problems faced in concurrent world..

[807] "Concurrent Connection with Readers and Writers" by P.J. Courtney, F. Heymans, D.L. Parnas. Communications of the ACM, 14:10, October 1971. The introduction of the reader-writer problem, and a simple solution. Later work introduced more complex solutions, skipped here, but they are pretty complex.

[829] "On Two-Nodes Problem in Connection with Graphs" by E.W. Dijkstra. Mathematical Enquiry, 2:6079. Available: http://www-l3.tum.de/~pub/rh0056/WebHome/dijkstra.html. Can you please read about algorithms in 1959? We can't forget everything, but please read that they...

[868] "Go-To Statement Considered Harmful" by E.W. Dijkstra. ACM, volume 11:3, March 1968. http://www.utex.edu/users/EDW/ED023xx/ED215.pdf. Some thoughts about the field of structured programming.

[868] "The Structure of the Multiprocessing System" by E.W. Dijkstra. ACM, volume 11:5, 1968. One of the earliest papers to point out that works in computer science is an engaging intellectual endeavor. Also another undoubtedly in the formal layer of systems.

[872] "Information Sharing Shifting a Buffer" by E.W. Dijkstra. Information Processing Letters 1, 1972. http://www.utex.edu/users/EDW/ED023xx/ED329.pdf. E.W. Dijkstra. The first paper to clearly state down what the problems were in concurrent however. However, practitioners in OS design know many of the problems posed by Dijkstra, perhaps having him claim too much was a misrepresentation.

[871] "Hierarchical ordering of sequential processes" by E.W. Dijkstra. Available here: http://www.utex.edu/users/EDW/ed03xx/ED310.pdf. Presents numerous concerns regarding concurrency, including philosophers. The webpage about this problem is also useful.

[892] "Transaction Processing: Concepts and Techniques" by Jim Gray. Andreas Reuters. Morgan Kaufmann, September 1992. The first quote that we find particularly humorous is found on page 48, at the top of the Section 8.1. "The worst thing about it" ... probably the OS implementers worked out the appropriate algorithms, although Dijkstra is generally credited with inventing semaphores years later." Oh, snap!

[808] "The Little Book of Semaphores" by A.B. Downey. Available at the following site: http://green.example.com/semaphores/, a nice (and free!) book. Lots of semaphores to solve, if you like that kind of thing.

[813] "Hits for Semaphore Design and Instruction Buffer" by Mark L. Hill. Ph.D. Dissertation. U.C. Berkeley, 1987. For this work which was done with caching in systems employs. A great sample of the field.

[813] "Hits for Semaphore Design" by Buster Lampson. ACM Operating Systems Review, 15:5, October 1983. Lampson, a famous semaphore-user, used hits in the history of computer systems. A hit is something that is often correct but can be wrong; in this case, a spill) is following thread that it is because some way was going out, but not that the condition will be in the desired state when the waiting wakes up. In this paper about hits for designing systems, one of Lampson's facts is that you should use hits. It is not as confusing as that is.

[799] "RE: NT kernel guard playing with Linus Torvalds. June 27, 1999. Available: https://archive.org/comp/linux/semaphores.html. A response from Linux about the utility of semaphores, including the throttling we mention in the text. Always, Linux is slightly insightful but quite informative.

# References

[AC97] "Extending Proportional-Scheduling to a Network of Workstations" by Andrea Araci-Dousseau and David E. Muller. PDTA97, June 1997. A paper by one of the authors how to extend proportional-scheduling to work better in a clustered environment.

[B-18] "The Battle of the Schedulers: FreeBSD ULE vs. Linux CFS" by J. Bourbon, S. Chevalvay, B. Lepers, W. Zwaenen, R. Gouicam, J. Gallard, M. Joper. SENUSIC ATC 18, July 2018, Boston, Massachusetts. A recent, detailed work comparing Linux CFS and the FreeBSD schedulers. An excellent overview of each scheduler is provided. The result of the comparison: in some cases CFS was better, and in others, U (the BSD scheduler). In life there are no easy

[B72] "Symmetric binary B-Trees: Data Structure And Maintenance Algorithms" by Rudolf Bayer. Acta Informica, Volume 1, December 4, 1972. A cool tree introduced before you were born (most likely). One of many trees used out; study your algorithms book for more information!

[D82] "Number Should Start At Zero" by Edsger Dijkstra, August 1982. Available: http://www.cs.utas.edu/users/ED/EDS82.pdf. A note from E. Dijkstra, one of the pioneers of computer science. We'll be hearing much more about this in the section on Concurrency. [K15] "A Warehouse-cluster Scheduler" by S. Kanev, P. Ranganathan, J. Parago, K. Hazelwoud, T. Moseley, G. Wei, D. Brooks. ISCA '15, June 2015, Portland, Oregon. A fascinating study of how we go on in modern data centers, which are increasingly where most of computing happens. 20% of CPU time is spent in the operating system; 5% by the scheduler alone

[I09] "Inside The Linux Completely Fair Scheduler" by M. Tim Jones. December 15, 2009. http://ostep.org/citations-cfs.pdf. A simple overview of CFS from its earlier days, was created by Inge Molhar in a short burst of creativity which led to a 100K kernel patch in 60 days.

[L88] "A Fair Scheduler" by J. Kay and P. Paulder. CACM, Volume 31 Issue 1, January 1988. An early reference to a fair-scheduler.

[W94] "Lottery Scheduling: Flexible Proportional-Share Resource Management" by Carl A. Waldspurger and William E. Weihl. OSDI '94, November 1994. The landmark paper on Waldspurger's lottery scheduling gets the systems re-engaged about scheduling, fair sharing, and the power of randomized algorithms

[W95] "Lottery and Stride Scheduling: Flexible Proportional-Share Resource Management" by Carl A. Waldspurger. Ph.D. Thesis, MIT, 1995. The award-winning thesis of Waldspurger's outlines lottery and stride scheduling. If you are thinking of writing a Ph.D. dissertation at some point, you should always have a good example around, to give something to strive for: this is such a good one.

[W02] "Memory Resource Management in VMware ES Server" by Carl A. Waldspurger. OSDI '02, Boston, Massachusetts. The paper to read about memory management in VMMs (a.k.a., hypervisors). In addition to being relatively easy to read, the paper contain numerous ideas about this new type of VMM-level memory management.

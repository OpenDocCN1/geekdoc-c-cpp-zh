# 44.12 Summary

Flash-based SSDs are becoming a common presence in laptops, desktops, and servers inside the datacenters that power the world’s economy. Thus, you should probably know something about them, right?

Here’s the bad news: this chapter (like many in this book) is just the first step in understanding the state of the art. Some places to get more information about the reality include research on actual device performance (such as that by Chen et al. [CK+09] and Grupp et al. [A+08], Gurpta et al. [CG], Huang et al. [H+14], Kim et al. [KK+02], Lee et al. [L+07], and Zhang et al. [Z+12]), and even distributed systems of flash (including Gordon [CG+09] and CORFU [B+12]). And, if we say so, a really good overview of all the things you need to extract high performance from an SSD can be found in a paper on the “unwritten read contract” [HK+17].

Don’t just read academic papers; also read about recent advances in the popular press (e.g., [V12]). Therein’ll learn more practical (but still useful) information, such as Samsung’s use of both TLC and SLC cells within the same SSD to maximize performance (SLC can buffer writes quickly) as well as capacity (TLC can store more bits per cell). And this is, as they say, just the tip of the iceberg. Dive in and learn more about this “iceberg” of research on your own, starting with Ma et al.’s excellent (and recent survey) [M+14]. Be careful though; icebergs can sink even the mightiest of ships [W15].

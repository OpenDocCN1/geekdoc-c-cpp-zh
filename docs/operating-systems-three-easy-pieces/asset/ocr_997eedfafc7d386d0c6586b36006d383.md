# References

[BS+09] "Tolerating File-System Mistakes with EnvyFS" by L. Balairasundaram, S. Sundararaman, A. Apaci-Dusseau, R. Apaci-Dusseau. USENIX ’09, San Diego, CA, June 2009. A fun paper about using file systems at once to tolerate a mistake in any one of them.

[BH100] "Computer Operating Systems" by P. Brinch Hansen. 'Classic Operating Systems: From Processing to Distributed Systems.' Springer-Verlag, New York, 2000. This essay provides an intro to a well wonderful collection of papers about operating systems.

[B2+72] A Paged Time Sharing System for the PDF-10" by D. Dobson, J. Burchfield, D. Murphy, R. Tomlinson, CACM, Volume 15, Number 3, March 1972. TENEX has much of the machinery found in modern operating systems; read about it to see how much innovation was already in the early 1970s.

[B75] "The Mythical Man-Month" by F. Brooks. Addison-Wesley, 1975. A classic text on software engineering; well worth the read.

[BH011] "Computer: A Programmer's Perspective" by R. Bryant and D. O'Hallen. Addison-Wesley, 2010. Another great intro to how computer systems work. It has a bit of overlap with this book – if you like it, you can skip the first few chapters of it, or simply read them to get a different perspective on some of the material. After all, one good way to build your own knowledge is to hear from many perspectives as not the best read, and then you develop your own thoughts on the matter. You know, by thinking!

[GS85] The Mechanics of Unix by P. Maurice, 1985. www.gnu.org/gnu/manifesto.html. A large part of Linux's success is no doubt the presence of an excellent compiler, acc, and other free pieces of software that is not the best read. The GNU Hallstall is a visionary when it comes to open source, and this manifesto lays out his hopes and turns.

[K+61] "One-Level Storage System" by T. Killburn, B.D.G. Edwards, M. Ligan, F.H. Summer. IRE Transactions on Electronic Computers, April 1962. The Atlas presented much of what you see in modern systems. However if there were only one read, you would need to read the historical perspective of [78].

[M78] The Manchester Atlas: A Historical Perspective" by S. H. Livingston. Communications of the ACM, Volume 21:1, January 1978. A nice piece of history on the early development of computer systems and the pioneering efforts of the Atlas. Of course, one could go back and read the [78] papers كلهم, but this paper provides a good overview and some historical perspective.

[O72] "The Multics System: An Examination" by Elliott. MIT Press, 1972. A great overview of. Many good ideas, and it was an over-designed system, shooting for touch, and this was much really needed. A classic of Fred Brooks would call it the "secundum effect (1973).

[P03] "Introduction to Computing Systems: From Gates to C and Beyond" by Yale N. Patt, Sananjay J. Patel. McGraw-Hill, 2003. One of our favorite intro to computing systems books. Starts at starts and ends when you get to the C: the material is really quite easy.

[RT74] "The Unix-Time Sharing System" by Dennis M. Ritchie, Kevin Thompson, AC AC, Volume 17:6, July 1974. A general summary of UNIX is what was taking over the world of computing, by the people who wrote it.

[S68] "940 Time-Sharing System" by Scientific Data Systems. TECHNICAL MANUAL, SDS 90 11668, August 1968. Yes, a technical manual we best we could. But it is fascinating to read these old system documents, and see how much was already in place in the early 1960s. Of the minds behind the Berkeley project vertically became the (SDS) system was Butter Launcher, who won a turning award for his contributions in systems.

[SS+10] "Membrane: Operating System Support for Reliable Systems" by S. Sundararaman, M. Subramanian, A. Rajimwale, A. Apaci-Dusseau, R. Apaci-Dusseau, M. Swift. FAST-10, San Jose, CA, February 2010. The great thing about writing your own class notes: you can advertise your own research. But this paper is actually pretty neat – when a file system hits a bug and crashes, Membrane auto-magically restores it, all applications or the rest of the system being affected.

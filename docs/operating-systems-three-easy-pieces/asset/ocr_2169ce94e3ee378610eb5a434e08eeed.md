# BEYOND PHYSICAL MEMORY: POLICIES

# References

[AD03] "Time-Adaptation in River" by Remzi H. Arpaci-Dasseau. ACM TOCS, 21:1, February 2003. A summary of one of the author's dissertation work on a named system, where he learned that comparison against the ideal is an important technique for system designers.

[B86] "Study of Replacement Algorithms for Virtual-Storage Computer" by Laslo A. Bellamy. IBM Systems Journal 5(2): 78-101, 1966. The paper that introduces the simple way to compute the behavior of a policy (the MIN algorithm).

[BS69] "An Anomaly in Space-Time Characteristics of Certain Programs Running in a Machine" by C. L. Berry, A. D. Nelson, and G. S. Shedler. Communications of the ACM, 12, June 1969. Introduction of the little we know about Belady's anomaly. How do Nelson and Sheldon feel about this name, we wonder?

[CE85] "Evaluation of Buffer Management Strategies for Relational Databases" by Hong-Tai Chou, David J. DeWitt VL85, Stockholm, August 1985. A database paper on buffering strategies you should use under a number of common database access patterns. The paper suggests that for your workload, you can tailor policies to do better than the general-purpose ones used in this.

[CP96] "A Paging Experiment with Multiple Systems" by F.J. Corbato. Included in a Festschrift published in honor of Prof. P.M. Morse. MIT in Press, Cambridge, MA, 1996. The original (and hard to find) reference to the algorithm, not the first use of a set of bits. How to Belady's algorithm was applied, we wonder?

[D70] "Virtual Memory" by Peter J. Denning. Computing Surveys, Vol. 2, No. 3, September 1970. Denning's early days and survey on virtual memory systems.

[EF78] "Self-cache vs Warm-start Miss Policies" by Malcolm C. Easton, Ronald Fagin, Communications of the ACM, October 1978. A discussion of cold-start vs. warm-start policies.

[EH89] "Electrically Induced Nuclear Deflection" by Martin Fleischman, Stanley Pons. Journal of Electrolytic Chemistry Volume 26, Number 2, April 1989. The famous paper that showed the use of an electric field in an attempt to generate nearly-pure particles from atoms of water with little metal in them. Unfortunately, the results of these two well-known scientists were doubted (and later, discredited). The only one really happy about this was Marcus Hannemann, who published a paper even though he was not involved in the work, thus (published by) Fleischman and Pons avoiding association with one of the greatest scientific frauds of the 20th century.

[HP06] "Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach" by John Hennessy and David Patterson. Gordon-Kaumann. A remarkable book about cache architecture. It is the work which later gained wide popularity with its inclusion in HP06? The "we failed to partition it into three components intuitively because of the misses (page 49)?

[KE61] "One-Level System Storage" by T. Turnbull, D.B.G. Edwards, M.J. Liang, FH. Summer. IBM Transactions. EC-11-2, 1962. Altos had a bit, but only a small number of memories and the scanning of the use in large memories was not a problem the authors solved.

[M70] "Evaluation of Techniques for Storage Hierarchies" by R. L. Matson, J. Geccsei, D. R. Slutz, I. L. Taylor. IBM Journal, Volume 9, 1970. A paper that is mostly about how to simulate cache hierarchies efficiently; a classic in that regard, as well as for its excellent discussion of the properties of a tariff. Can you figure out why the stack policy might be useful for simulating a lot of different-sized caches at once?

[MM03] "A Self-Tuning, Low Overhead Replacement Cache" by Nimrod Megcardo and Dharmendra S. Modha. FAST 2003, February 2003, San Jose, California. An excellent modern paper about replacement algorithms, which includes a new policy, AC, which is now used in systems. Recognized in 2014 as a "Test of Award" won by the storage systems community at the "FAST 14" conference.

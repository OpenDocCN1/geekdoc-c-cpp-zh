$IDO_2 = CO_{2a} \times CI;$ (2)

$IVO_2 = (CO_{2a} - CO_{2v}) \times CI,$ (3)

Where: $CI$, l/(min · m²) - cardiac index; $CO_{2a}$, ml/l - oxygen content in arterial blood; $CO_{2v}$, ml/l - oxygen content in venous blood.

Oxygen content (concentration) in arterial and venous blood were determined by the equations (Naylor-Sherfed, e.a., 1990):

$CO_{2a} = SO_{2a} \times Hb \cdot 1{,}39\ (100 \cdot a) / Patm \times pO_{2a}$ (4)

$CO_{2v} = SO_{2v} \times Hb \cdot 1{,}39\ (100 \cdot a) / Patm \times pO_{2v}$ (5)

where Hb - hemoglobin; $SO_{2v}$ % - oxygen saturation in arterial (a) and venous (v) blood; $pO_{2a}, pO_{2v}$, mm Hg - partial pressure of oxygen in arterial (a) and venous (v) blood; $a = 0.23$ - a coefficient that depends on the temperature at which the blood oxygen content was measured, $t = 37^\circ C$; $Patm = 760$ mm Hg - atmospheric pressure.

For the data processing were used the special algorithms of cluster analysis (Nastenko, 1996), correlation and regression analysis and variation statistics as well.

A detailed analysis of the dependencies and the corresponding regression equations are given in (Nastenko, e.a., 2000, 2001).

It is interesting the behaviour of traffic pressure in the limits $n\to0$, $n\to1$, and $n\to n_c(v_{\max})$. For the first two limits, respectively, the Eq. (22) gives

$$\frac{P}{T}\approx n \qquad \frac{P}{T}\simeq \ln\left(\frac{1}{1-n}\right) \tag{23}$$

The result of the first limit ($n\to0$) resembles the well-known equation of state of an ideal gas. In the second one ($n\to1$), depending on the $s$ of temperature, $P\to\pm\infty$; this result resembles the behaviour of pressure-density relation in a condensed phase. For high energies ($\beta\to-\infty$), the same as with entropy, traffic pressure and chemical potential have a peculiar behaviour in the limit $n\to n_c(v_{\max})$. This is shown in Fig. 17 for speed limit $v_{\max}=2$. In both cases, this behaviour is a result of the singularity these properties have at $\beta=0$.

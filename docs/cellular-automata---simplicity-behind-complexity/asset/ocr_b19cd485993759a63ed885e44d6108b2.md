# ### 3.5 Mobility and diffusion factors

The circulation part of the transport model is written in the pair of equations (20). For deriving the mobility and diffusion factors one can consider that the system has reached a stationary situation where $J_p(t=0) \simeq 0$ and $J_r(t=0) \simeq 0$, then:

$$\frac{D_p}{\mu_p} \simeq \rho_0\left|\frac{dV}{d\rho_0}\right|; \qquad \frac{D_r}{\mu_r} \simeq \rho_0\left|\frac{dV}{d\rho_0}\right|$$  
(23)

As the urban potential $V$ is approximated by Thomas-Fermi, one gets a generalized expression of $D_p/\mu_p$ as a function of space⁵:

$$\frac{D_p}{\mu_p} \simeq \frac{2\,\varepsilon_p(x,y)\left|1-(d r/d\rho_p)\,\rho_p/m_p\right|}{3\,\rho_p\left|1-(r/\rho_p)\,m_p/m_r\right|^{1/3}}$$  
(24)

where $\varepsilon_p(x,y) = (\tilde{a}_p^2/2m_p)(3\pi\rho_p(x,y))^{2/3}$ can be considered the isolated contribution of $\rho_0(x,y)$ to Fermi's level. The former applies for genuine stationary conditions, but for a quasi stationary frame there should be a limiting trend as follows:

$$\frac{D_p}{\mu_p} \simeq \frac{D_r}{\mu_r} \simeq \frac{2}{3}\,\varepsilon_p(x,y)/\rho_p$$  
(25)

Once the $D/\mu$ quotient has been specified for each pixel, a numerical value of each parameter can be found by estimating mobility factors, as in solid state theory:

$$\mu_p = q_p\,\tau_p/m_p; \qquad \mu_r = q_r\,\tau_r/m_r$$  
(26)

The characteristic time parameters $\tau_p$ and $\tau_r$ can be interpreted as the average free time periods *between relocations* of inhabitant and repersons. In the case of $\tau_p$, its value is representative of the average time invested daily per inhabitant in terms of displacements (in one direction) for different activities, which for the case study was about 25 min in 1990⁶.

An average measure of $D/\mu$ quotient is given by Einstein’s equation $D_p/\mu_p = D_r/\mu_r = kT/q_p [J/(m^2)]$, being for the case study $\mu_{p0}=1.15\,10^{-04}$, $\mu_r=2.59\,10^{-05}$ for the effective mobility factors [J.(m.)²/sec/kg] and $D_{p0}=5.91\,10^{-5}$, $D_{r0}=1.51\,10^{-5}$ for the effective diffusion factors [m2 /sec]. For this case the average values of the parameters are taken as the city average “thermal” expansion, gives a relative surface expansion of 1.65. Since this is practically $(1+\beta_0)$, where $\beta_0 \simeq 0.65$ is the city average quality factor, it gives a net relative expansion of $\beta_0$ per inhabitant in excess, which is in accordance to the fact that excess concentration will be rearranged trying to conserve the former average quality factor.

The mobility and diffusion factors link the daily commutation regime to the expansion regime, in a way that the city structure depends directly on the average relocation time period and vice versa.

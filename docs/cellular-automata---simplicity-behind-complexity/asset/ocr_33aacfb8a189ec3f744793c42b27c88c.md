# ### 5.1 Rule 35

If we assume uniformity, Eq. (19) becomes

$$\begin{aligned}
p(000)&=p(1100)+p(111), & p(001)&=p(100)+p(1101),\\
p(010)&=p(101)+p(1001), & p(011)&=p(1000),\\
p(100)&=p(011)+p(0100), & p(101)&=p(0101),\\
p(110)&=p(0001), & p(111)&=p(0000).
\end{aligned}\tag{22}
$$

The sixth equation means $p(1101)=0$, so we must have $p(110)=0$ or $p(101)=0$. In the
former case, the first and second equations mean that $p(000)=p(111)$ and $p(001)=p(100)$.
Then, $p(0001)=p(1000)=0$ is obtained from the eighth equation. Therefore, we have
$p(000)=p(011)=p(111)=0$ and the remainings are determined as

$$p(001)=p(100)=1-\rho,\quad p(010)=\frac{\rho}{2},\quad p(101)=\frac{3}{2}\rho-1,\tag{23}$$

where $\rho$ is the expectation value of $E(xy)$ and this solution makes sense only when $\frac{2}{3}\le \rho \le 1$.
In the latter case, using approximation $p(0000)=\frac{p(000)^2}{1-\rho}$, we arrive at

$$\begin{aligned}
p(000)&=\frac{2-3\rho}{4}, & p(001)&=\frac{\rho}{2}, & p(010)&=\frac{\rho^2}{2-\rho}, & p(011)&=\frac{\rho(2-3\rho)}{2(2-\rho)},\\
p(100)&=\frac{\rho}{2}, & p(101)&=0, & p(110)&=\frac{\rho(2-3\rho)}{2(2-\rho)}, & p(111)&=\frac{(2-3\rho)^2}{4(2-\rho)}.
\end{aligned}\tag{24}
$$

This is the solution for $0\le \rho \le \frac{2}{3}$.

The connection condition (20) is written as

$$\begin{aligned}
p_1(000)&=p_1(1100)+p_1(111), & p_1(001)&=p_1(100)+p_1(1101),\\
p_1(010)&=p_1(101)+p_1(1001), & p_1(011)&=p_1(1000),\\
p_1(100)&=p_1(011)+p_1(0100), & p_1(101)&=p_1(0101),\\
p_1(110)&=p_1(0001), & p_1(111)&=p_1(0000).
\end{aligned}\tag{25}
$$

As in Eq. (21), $p_0$ is written as $p_0(xyzw)=(a\delta_{x,1-y}+(1-a)\delta_{x,y})p_1(yzw)$. Then substitution of
the solution for $0\le \rho \le \frac{2}{3}$, (23), into $p_1$ satisfies the above equations if $\rho=\rho_L=\frac{2a}{2+a}$. Notice
that as $a$ varies from 0 to 1, $\rho_L$ varies from 0 to $\frac{2}{3}$. The stationary solution for $\rho\ge \frac{2}{3}$, (24),
cannot satisfy the above equations. At the right boundary $p_{N-2}(x_{N-2}x_{N-1}x_N)$ must satisfy

$$\begin{aligned}
p_{N-2}(000)&=p_{N-2}(1100)+p_{N-2}(111), & p_{N-2}(001)&=p_{N-2}(100)+p_{N-2}(1101),\\
p_{N-2}(010)&=p_{N-2}(101)+p_{N-2}(1001), & p_{N-2}(011)&=p_{N-2}(1000),\\
p_{N-2}(100)&=p_{N-2}(011)+p_{N-2}(0100), & p_{N-2}(101)&=p_{N-2}(0101),\\
p_{N-2}(110)&=p_{N-2}(0001), & p_{N-2}(111)&=p_{N-2}(0000).
\end{aligned}\tag{26}
$$

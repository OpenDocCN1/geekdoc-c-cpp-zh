Similar arguments can be made with respect to coronary heart disease, when the rate of development of coronary artery lesions determines the rate of progression of heart failure. Conversely, the intensity of revascularization after acute myocardial infarction may determine the degree of restoration of the functional ability of the heart.

### 3.3 Dynamic properties of capillary blood flow and tissue oxygenation. Simulation results

A cellular automaton belongs to the critically self-organized systems (Bak et al., 1987, 1996), with varying degrees of dynamic self-organization.

Depending on the computational experiment conditions, the capillary network demonstrates one of three types of functional self-organization:

- *Subcritical*: the capillaries are opened and closed at random time moments (self-organization is absent);
- *Critical*: joint regular and random distribution of open capillaries (the system goes from a self-organized state into a chaotic state and vice versa);
- *Supercritical*: open capillaries form regular lattice structures, in which the capillaries are either always open or rhythmically oscillate (the system is stably self-organized).

The forms of oscillations of capillary flow and tissue oxygen saturation obtained from CA simulations are presented in Fig. 7. The wide range of its oscillation properties allows attributing it to the fourth class, characterized by the most diverse and most complex behavior (Wolfram, 1984) and consequently the most adaptive to changes in external and internal conditions.

### 3.4 Thresholds of opening and closing of capillaries

In available information resources, we did not find any data about the level at which tissue oxygen saturation and at what concentration of tissue metabolites the activation and deactivation of the capillaries occur, or whether these values are constant or variable. CA simulation allows us to objectify the whole range of capillary network behavior and to compare the results with the real functioning of the capillary system.

The **purpose** of this section was to study the influence of differences between opening and closing capillary thresholds on the formation of the oscillatory properties of capillary blood flow at different levels of tissue metabolic activity.

It should be noted that there is no physical analog of the quantity "opening threshold" or "closing threshold" of a capillary. Physically, these values are represented by a combination of factors and various physical characteristics that affect the opening and closing of precapillary sphincters. In general, these values may change over time. In ongoing studies, we identify only one value that determines the state of a capillary (opened/closed): the number of metabolites accumulated in the tissues in the neighborhood of a given capillary.

During the study, we investigated the static properties of the CA—the average oxygen saturation of tissue cells and the percentage of opened capillary cells (capillary blood flow)—as well as dynamic properties: the percentage of capillary cells that opened or closed during each iteration.

Tissue metabolic activity was calculated as a percentage of the maximum possible value.

Differences between thresholds of capillary opening and closing were calculated as a percentage of their average values.

The average percentage of opened capillaries (of the total), depending on tissue metabolic activity and the difference between the opening and closing thresholds, is presented in Fig. 8.

Fig. 9. Fraction of flickering capillaries, % depending on: a — tissue metabolic activity; b — opening and closing threshold difference.

When the difference between the thresholds is equal to 10% of the average number of active capillaries, only 50% of the capillaries are opening and closing. With a further increase in the threshold difference, 22% and 16% of the capillaries are flickering.

With increasing threshold difference, the number of flickering capillaries decreases according to a hyperbolic law. Zero values indicate that flickering stops and active capillaries become permanently open.

Thus, an increase in the threshold difference may cause deterioration of capillary flow redistribution.

![](img/55ff3770fcfe64effe3c90a4d4692fa6_327_1.png)

Fig. 10. The dependencies of average tissue oxygen saturation, % on: a — tissue metabolic activity; b — opening and closing threshold difference.

The system "capillaries–tissue" is characterized by the following peculiarity (Fig. 9a). At a constant threshold difference, the number of flickering capillaries is constant, regardless of tissue metabolic activity.

For a small threshold difference, almost all capillaries are flickering. With an increase in the threshold difference up to 10% of the average number of active capillaries, the fraction of flickering capillaries varies between 42% and 55% of tissue activity. With a further increase in the threshold difference, the number of flickering capillaries is reduced to zero. That is, the redistribution of nutritive (capillary) blood flow deteriorates with an increase in the difference between the thresholds. This is confirmed by the graphs presented in Fig. 9b. The number of flickering capillaries and, correspondingly, the fraction of redistributed nutritive flow rapidly decrease with an increase in the threshold difference.

Some analogy is observed with acute heart failure, accompanied by tissue hypoxia. In order to saturate the tissues with oxygen and remove tissue metabolites, more time is required in which the capillaries remain open. This situation may correspond to a large difference between the opening and closing thresholds of capillaries.

Let us consider how the above-described functional peculiarities of the capillary network influence tissue oxygen saturation (Fig. 10a). At a small threshold difference, tissue oxygen saturation remains approximately constant over the entire range of tissue metabolic activity. There is a tendency toward some increase in the zone of medium metabolic activity, with a gradual decrease in the area of high metabolic activity values.

With an increase in the threshold difference, a tendency is observed for tissue oxygen saturation to increase with increasing tissue metabolic activity. This dependency is even more pronounced at higher magnitudes of threshold difference.

If the real capillary network has properties similar to those simulated in our investigations, under more severe conditions of the organism, the conditions of oxygen delivery to tissues can be improved due to the activation of mass transfer mechanisms (Fig. 10b).

This peculiarity was clearly expressed for low and medium levels of tissue metabolic activity and gradually decreases with an increase in this parameter.

## 6. Cellular automaton simulations of arterial and capillary flow interactions

The cardiovascular system (CVS) can be considered as an interaction of a combination of continual (arterial flow) and discrete (capillary flow) mechanisms (Chernukh & Alexandrov, 1984; Little, 1989; Rushmer, 1976), whose interactions have been studied insufficiently at the present moment.

The relative regulatory autonomy of the peripheral circulatory system can be simulated in a simpler way using cellular automata models.

We based our investigation on the assumption that the microvascular arterial bed is a critically self-organized system working on the “edge of chaos.” The microvascular arterial network increases or decreases the diameter of arterioles and opens more or fewer capillaries depending on fluctuations of blood flow and pressure.

The high sensitivity of the capillary network to changes in internal and external conditions, as well as a wide range of its oscillatory properties and also experimental data, allows us to assume that oscillations of peripheral and systemic blood flow can co-interact.

**The purpose** of this part of the investigation was to simulate the interactions of capillary blood flow and systemic blood flow during a single cycle of heart contraction.

### 6.1 Conditions of simulation

- 1. The current level of systemic flow determines the quantity of capillaries that can work simultaneously at a given moment. However, the cellular automaton opens as many capillaries as necessary to satisfy the oxygen debt, but not more than permitted.
- 2. A single iteration of CA corresponds to one time unit.
- 3. The quantity of potentially opened capillary cells is constant during diastole.
- 4. Tissue metabolic activity changes from 0% to 100% in discrete steps in each numerical experiment.
- 5. CA opens a capillary when average tissue oxygen saturation is lower than the “opening threshold.” The number of opened capillary cells is determined by conditions 1 and 3.
- 6. Opened capillary cells close if the average charge of surrounding tissue cells reaches the value of the “closing threshold.”
- 7. The properties of all capillary cells and tissue cells were homogeneous throughout the CA.

# Shh! Keep It Quiet — Anonymous Methods

And you have the gist of using delegates, take a quick look at Microsoft’s first cut at simplifying delegates in C# 2.0 a couple of years ago.

To come out of the delegate rigamole, you can use an anonymous method. Anonymous methods are just written in more traditional notation. Although the syntax and few details are different, the effect is essentially the same whether you use use a “raw” delegate, an anonymous method, or a lambda expression. You can find out about lambda expressions in your book’s companions Web site.

An anonymous method creates the delegate instance and the method it “points” at the same time — right in place, on the fly, tout de suite. Here are the guts of the DoSomethingLength() method again, this time rewritten to use an anonymous method (boldfaced):

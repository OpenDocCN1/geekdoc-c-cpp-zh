# What is a collection, really

Take a moment to compare the little collection in this with an elaborate collection. Whereas LinkedList has a complex structure of nodes connected by pointers, this months is based on a simple array with canned content, at that, I expanding the collection notion a bit, and expand it even more before this chapter concludes.

(Your collection may not contain called content — most collections are designed to hold things you put them via Add() methods and the Array Ways. The KeyedArray class in the earlier section "Accessing the Collections Ways," for example, uses the [ ] indexer to add items. Your collection could also provide an Add() method as well as an iterator block so it can work with foreach.)

The point of a collection, in the most general sense, is to store multiple objects and to allow you to iterate those objects, retrieving one at a time sequentially — sometimes randomly, or apparently randomly, as well, in the Indexer example. (Of course, an array can do that, even with the extra apparatus of a class such as MonthDays, but iterators go beyond the MonthDays example, as I'll show you.)

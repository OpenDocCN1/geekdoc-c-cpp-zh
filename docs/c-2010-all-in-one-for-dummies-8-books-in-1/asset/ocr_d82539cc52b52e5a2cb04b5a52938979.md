This produces a list of even-numbered integers from 10 down through 4. Also notice how the foreach is specified. You have to instantiate an EvenNumbers object (the collection class). Then, in the foreach statement, you invoke the named iterator method through that object:

```
EvenNumbers en = new EvenNumbers();
foreach(int even in en.DescendingEvents(nTop, nStop))
```

**DescendingEvents()** were static, you wouldn't need the class instance. You would call it the class itself, as usual:

```
foreach(int even in EvenNumbers.DescendingEvents(nTop, nStop))
```

**It's a regular wetland out there!**

If you can produce a "stream" of even numbers with a foreach statement, think of all the other things you may produce with special-purpose collections like these: streams of powers of two or terms in mathematical series as prime numbers or squares — or something exotic such as Fibonacci numbers. Or, how about a stream of random numbers (what's the Random class already does) or of randomly generated objects?

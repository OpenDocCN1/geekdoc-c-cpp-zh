## About the Authors

Hi, my name is Bill, and I've been a software developer for over 20 years. I started my career with C++ and since then, I've stayed at the forefront of new technologies. I have a degree in computer science from Ohio State University and I've written several books on C# and development.

My name is Chuck, and I'm a software architect and technical writer. I have worked on a wide variety of projects, from web development to cloud infrastructure. I'm passionate about making complex technical concepts easy to understand.

My name is Stephen Randy, and I'm a software engineer with more than a decade of experience in Windows programming and .NET. I live with my wife and son near Dallas, Texas.

## Acknowledgments

A lot of people work to make a book of this size. Don't think, just because the authors are listed on the front page, that they conceived every idea in the book. It takes a community.

First, thanks to Chuck Sphar and Randy Davis for the fantastic source material that is the backbone of this book. I learned much editing the first minibooks for the scope of this All-in-One. Also, thanks to Katie Feltman and Chris Morris for their editorial expertise.

A number of community members had a huge part in the creation of this book. Carey Payette and Phil Japiske built Book V (about WPF) basically from the goodness of their hearts, and I couldn't have completed it without them—my WPF skills weren't worth writing it. These two get the award for Biggest Contribution, and I thank them both.

The developers at Information Control Corporation were essential for formulating the initial scope of this book and fact-checking the details. Webb, Stephen Gibbs, John Hann, Larry Bell, Michael Birchmeyer, and Azher Muhammad had a big part, especially in the information related to C# 4.0. Thanks to all and the other ICC experts gave me ideas and tips.

Kevin Polch-Bisson at Microsoft provided some C# clarity via Twitter the scope of this book. Steve Andrews provided the structure for the T4 chapter in Book Iv. Madd Togersen reviewed the table of contents, and I thank him for the "It looks delicious" phrase, which I think was my most quoted phrase of the year.

Lars Christensen provided a few choice pointers for the book, and Julie Leman's Entity Framework writing was the basis of my own additions to the ADO.NET chapter.

As always, to my wife, Gabrielle, for her support. Sometimes I just can't believe lucky I am.

# Accessor methods

If you look more carefully at the BankAccount class, you see a few other methods. One, GetString(), returns a string version of the account fit for presentation to any Console.WriteLine() for display. However, displaying the contents of a BankAccount object may be difficult if the instances are inaccessible. The class should have the right to decide how it is

In addition, you see two "getter" methods, GetBalance() and GetAccount Number(), and one "setter" method, SetAccountNumber(). You may wonder why I would bother to declare a data member such as _balance private to provide a public GetBalance() method to return its value. I have two reasons:

+   - GetBalance() doesn't provide a way to modify _balance — it merely returns its value. The balance is read-only. To use the analogy of an actual bank, you can look at your bank time you want; you can't withdraw money from your account without using the bank's withdrawal mechanism.

+   - GetBalance() hides the internal format of the class from external methods. GetBalance() may perform an extensive calculation by reading peptides, adding account charges, and accounting for any other amount your bank may want to subtract from your balance. External methods don't know and don't care. Of course, you care which fees are being charged — you just can't do anything about them, short of changing banks.

Finally, GetBalance() provides a mechanism for making internal changes to the class without the need to change the users of BankAccount. If the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) mandates that your bank store deposits differently, the mandate shouldn't change the way you access your.

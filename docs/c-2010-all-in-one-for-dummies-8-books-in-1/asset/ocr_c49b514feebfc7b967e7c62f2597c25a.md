# Hiding Behind an Interface

In this book, I discuss what you write (but) someone else (a client) uses in her programs (you may be the client yourself, or of course). Sometimes, you have a complex or tricky class for which you would rather not expose the whole public interface. For various reasons, it includes some dangerous operations that nonetheless have to be public. Ideally, you would expose a subset of your class's public methods and hide the dangerous ones. C# interfaces can do that too.

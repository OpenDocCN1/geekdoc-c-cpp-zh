
# 418 Finding Your Files

## How iPad stores files

Though there's no Mac Finder or Windows File Explorer on one place, you can view all your stored files in one place. Open the Photos app, for example, and you see lots of photos, each one contained in a separate file. The Music app contains audio files, Videos movie files, iBooks contains e-books, and if you own Pages, it contains word-processing documents (as shown in Figure 1-1), and so on.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_435_0.png)

**Figure 1-1**: Documents stored by Pages.

Though there's no Windows File Explorer feature on iPad, when you connect your iPad to a PC, you can use the Windows File Explorer feature on your computer to view the files stored on your iPad.

How did those files get there? You may have created them in an app on iPad; you may have downloaded them from the Internet, as with music or videos from iTunes; you may have synced them from your computer or pushed them to all your iOS devices from iCloud, as with files or photos. Perhaps you grabbed a document from a website or from an online service such as Dropbox.

Depending on the app you open, you may see a Documents button to display thumbnails of documents (refer to Figure 1-1); libraries of photos, as in Photos; albums of music or individual song files, as in Music (see Figure 1-2); or bookshelves or lists of e-books, as in iBooks. Each app offers a slightly different way to find whatever you've created on, downloaded to, or synced to your iPad.

## Going with iCloud

iCloud isn't so much as a place as it is a storage service. If you choose to back or share files using iCloud, you can't go to a website and organize those files. They're automatically backed up from your devices, and new files created or downloaded to one device are automatically pushed to your other devices. In essence, this is invisible to you. You still access these files by opening the associated app.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_436_2.png)

Note that iCloud Drive, which released just as this book was going to press, will eventually replace simple iCloud storage. iCloud Drive includes storage, contact and syncing capabilities, email, location services, and app sharing.

# Sharing Files

As mentioned in the previous section, iPad does not let you get all the way your computer does with a series of folders and files. You open files in individual apps, as Photos and Videos. You cannot save files to a storage medium, such as a flash drive that you can remove and replace in another computing device. Therefore, managing and sharing your files with other devices or other people works a bit differently than on your iPad.

# 420 Sharing Files

You have a few options for getting files out of your iPad:

- Sync them via iCloud, which automatically pushes them to other iOS devices.
- Email them.
- Sync via iTunes with a computer to share things like calendar events, contact records, music, or photos, which is covered in Book I, Chapter 5.
- Share them over a network.
- Share them with somebody in close proximity using AirDrop.

Also consider using wireless devices from manufacturers such as Kingston or Maxell for external storage.

## Relying on good old email

You can use tools in individual applications to email files to yourself or others. If you're working on a document on your iPad and then want to get a copy of it on your computer, you can email it to yourself. This is a simple way to move a single file without needing extra setup.

## Getting close with AirDrop

To use AirDrop, you need to be in close proximity with another device. When you use AirDrop, you can send files, photos, and contacts instantly. It is one of the fastest ways to share something with someone right nearby.

## Going through the cloud

In addition to iCloud, there are other cloud-based services such as Dropbox. These services allow you to store files on remote servers and access them from any device where you log in. This provides more flexibility for managing files across different platforms and devices.

# 422 Sharing Files

Dropbox is a cloud-based service that allows you to store files on servers and access them on any device. It supports OS X, Windows, Linux, Android, and of course, iOS.

## Setting Up Home Sharing

With Home Sharing, you can stream content from any Mac or PC to your iPad directly. You simply set up Home Sharing on your iPad, turn on the feature in iTunes on your computer, and you're good to go.

Here are the steps for setting up Home Sharing when you're connected to a Wi-Fi network:

- 1. On your computer, open iTunes.
- 2. Click the File menu and choose Turn On Home Sharing. The Home Sharing dialog appears.
- 3. Enter your Apple ID and password and then Create Home Share. A message appears, telling you to use the same account you just signed in to on devices you want to share with.
- 4. Click Done.
- 5. On your iPad, tap Settings and then tap Music.
- 6. In the Home Sharing section, shown in Figure 1-7, enter your Apple ID for the same account you used in iTunes on your computer, and beneath the ID, enter a password.

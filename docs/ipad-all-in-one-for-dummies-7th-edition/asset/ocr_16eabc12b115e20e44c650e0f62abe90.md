# Turning On and Working with AssistiveTouch

Why would you want iPad to tell you whenever an auto-correction has been made? If you have vision challenges and you know that you typed ain't when writing dialogue for a character in your novel, but iPad corrected it to isn't, you would want to know. Similarly, if your son's friend's name isSUNSHine (don't worry, he'll break up with her soon) and auto-capitalization corrects it (incorrectly), you need to know immediately so that you can change it back.

The AssistiveTouch Control panel helps those who have challenges working with buttons to provide input to iPad using the touchscreen.

To turn on AssistiveTouch, follow these steps:

+   1. Tap Tap Settings on the Home screen, and then tap General and Accessibility.
2. In the Accessibility panel, scroll down and tap AssistiveTouch.
3. In the panel that appears, tap the On/Off switch for AssistiveTouch to turn it on (see Figure 3-9).

A gray square (called the AssistiveTouch Control panel) then appears on the right side of the panel. This square now appears in the same location as whatever Home screen or apps display on your iPad, though you can move it around the screen using your finger.

+   4. Tap the AssistiveTouch Control panel to display options, as shown in Figure 3-10.

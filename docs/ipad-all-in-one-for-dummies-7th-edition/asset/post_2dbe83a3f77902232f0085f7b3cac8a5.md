## Connecting with Friends on FaceTime

iPads (with the exception of the original iPad) ship with front- and rear-facing cameras, which unleash all possibilities. One of the coolest is using an app called FaceTime. The FaceTime video call lets you use the iPad's video cameras to talk face-to-face with someone who has an iPad (except an original iPad); Mac (running OS X 10.6.6 or later); fourth- or later-generation iPod touch; iPhone 4 or later. (See Figure 4-4.) You can call someone via her iPhone cellphone number or address.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_88_0.png)

**Figure 4-4:** Talk face-to-face using FaceTime.

FaceTime even works directly over a cellular connection, though you'll pay for it by eating up your data allotment.

You can also hold audio-only FaceTime sessions. This feature is handy when you don't want to drain battery power, when you want to use less bandwidth, or when you're having a bad hair day.

You'll want to start trying FaceTime with all your friends who own a compatible Apple device, so head straight to Book II, Chapter 4 to get up to speed on FaceTime features.

## Connecting to the Internet

To browse the web, access Mail, and shop online, you first have to be connected to the Internet, so I'm putting this information up front. How you connect to the Internet depends on which iPad model you use:

- The Wi-Fi-only iPad connects to the Internet via a Wi-Fi network. You may have already set up this type of network in your home using your computer and some equipment from your Internet provider. You can also connect through public Wi-Fi networks (hotspots). You have probably noticed how many hotspots your town or city has: Internet cafes, coffee shops, libraries and transportation centers such as airports or bus stations. In fact, after you start you will notice lots of signs alerting you to Wi-Fi locations—they are everywhere.

- If you own a 3G or 4G-enabled iPad, you can use a cellular connection (which is usually much faster), but you can also use a paid data network through AT&T, Sprint, T-Mobile, or Verizon to connect via cellular network just anywhere. If you have a 3G or 4G model, you don't have to do anything with a contract for coverage, the connection is made automatically wherever cellular service is available, just as on your cellphone.

TIP: See Chapter 1 in this minibook for the capabilities of different iPad models and the costs associated with cellular network.

When you're in range of a hotspot, a pop-up may display automatically; if it doesn't, follow these steps:

1. Tap Settings on the Home screen.
2. Tap Wi-Fi.
3. Be sure that Wi-Fi is set to On (see Figure 5-1) and choose a network to connect to.

Network names should appear automatically when you're in range of networks. When you're in range of a public hotspot, if access to several nearby networks is available, you may see a message asking you to tap a network name to select it.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_92.png)

**Figure 5-1:** Turning on Wi-Fi.

4. After you select a network (or if only one network is available), you may see a message asking for your password; if you do, enter the password.

You may need to ask the owner of the hotspot (for example, a hotel clerk or business owner) for this password or enter your home network password.

5. Tap the Join button and you're reconnected.

Free public Wi-Fi networks typically do not require passwords. However, it's possible for someone to track your online activities over unsecured networks. No matter how much you might be tempted, you want to avoid accessing financial accounts or sending unencrypted sensitive information when connected to a public hotspot.

## Setting Up iTunes

Think of iTunes as Apple's version from which you can manage your iPad settings for syncing, and a big online store from which you can buy content and apps for your Mac or PC, iPod touch, iPhone, and iPad. It's also the place where you can make settings for several of these devices to control how they download and share content. Even if you find other sources of content for your iPad, it's worth having an iTunes account, if only to use the settings it provides.

## Making Settings and Syncing

Before you can use iTunes to manage your iPad, you have to download the latest version of iTunes by going to www.apple.com/itunes. You should also create an iTunes account, providing a payment method that you can use iTunes on your computer to purchase apps and content.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_93.png) (Note: Filename corrected based on OCR context)

You can use an iTunes account, or an Apple ID, to register your iPad when you first buy it. In my case and perhaps in yours, I set up my iTunes account the first time I wanted to buy a movie title.

Remember that you can also sync content using iCloud. See the section “Understanding iCloud,” later in this chapter, for more about that.

## Making iPad settings using iTunes

When you plug your Lightning to USB Cable (for third-generation iPads and earlier) into your iPad and computer and then open iTunes, a group of settings shows how content will sync between the devices. Note that you can sync wirelessly to your computer by tapping Settings on the Home screen, tapping General, and tapping iTunes Wi-Fi Sync. In the settings that appear, tap Sync Now. This works only if your computer is connected to the Wi-Fi network.

## Playing Music

How do you find that you don't have the time or free hands to handle the iPad screen, open Music, search for music, and tap to play it? You'll find worse problems in the world, but if this is one that drags at you, you just press and hold the Home button and tell Siri your favorite tune. You can use Siri to play a song from the Music app or play a station on iTunes Radio.

To use music with Siri, follow these steps:

- 1. Press and hold the Home button until Siri appears.

- 2. To play music, speak a command, such as “Play music” or “As Time Goes By” to play a specific song or album.

- 3. When the music is playing, use commands such as “Pause music, “Next track,” or “Stop music” to control playback.

> One of the best things is that you don't have to follow a specific command format as you do with other voice-command apps. You could say, “Play the next track” or “Next track” or “Jump to the next track on this album, and Siri gets your meaning.

## Getting directions

You can use the Maps app and Siri to find your current location, find nearby businesses such as restaurants or a bank, or get a map of your location. Be sure to turn on Location Services to allow Siri to know your current location. (Go to Settings and tap Privacy: Location Services to make sure that the Location setting and Siri are turned on, both are in the same dialog.)

Here are some of the commands you can try to get directions or a list of nearby businesses:

- *Where am I?*

Displays a map of your current location. If you have a Wi-Fi-only iPad, this location may be approximate.

- *Where is Apache Junction, Arizona?*

Displays a map of that city.

- *Find restaurants.*

Displays a list of restaurants near your current location, as shown in Figure 6-9; tap one to display a map of its location.

Displays a map with the location of that business (or in some cases, nearby locations, such as a bank branch and all ATMs) indicated.

## Searching the web

Siri can use various resources to respond to specific requests like "Who is the Queen of England?" For more general requests, however, Siri searches for the web.

For example, if you speak a phrase, such as "Find a website about," Siri can respond in a couple of ways. The can simply display a list of search results using the default search engine in Safari, or "If you like, I can search the web." In the first instance, just tap a result to go to. In the second instance, you can confirm you want to search the web or cancel.

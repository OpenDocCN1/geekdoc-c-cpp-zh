# 422 Sharing Files

Dropbox is a cloud-based service that allows you to store your files on its servers and access them on any device that's connected to the Internet. (Figure 1-5 shows what the Dropbox interface looks like if you're surfing Safari.) It directly supports (in the form of dected software) OS X, Windows, Linux, Android, BlackBerry, and of course, iOS.

![](bbox=[0.25, 0.238, 0.722, 0.526])

**Figure 1-5:** Accessing Dropbox through Safari.

When you're on a computer, Dropbox works like any other folder on your hard drive. When you open it with your browser, you have instant access to all your files. When you drop something into your folder, it's copied to the company's servers, and pushed out to anywhere you've set up with your Dropbox account, including your iPad or others you're sharing folders

After you download the Dropbox app, you can open a file on iPad and print or view it, but not edit it, unless you choose the option of opening the document with an app you have on your iPad that be a logical fit. Figure 1-6 shows the iCloud app for iPad with shared photos from iPad's Photos app available; this feature is offered to you the first time you log in to Dropbox from the Dropbox app on your iPad.

Dropbox folders can be selectively shared with other Dropbox users, making it a great tool for any sort of collaborative project.

# # Chapter 3: Apps for Road Warriors

# In This Chapter

+   - Arranging travel
- Finding the best hotels
- Locating restaurants
- Using maps and travel guides
- Tracking your expenses
- Staying connected

Pad is practically perfect for people who travel a lot for business or pleasure. It is lightweight, slender, and stays powered up for about ten hours at a time. Depending on the model, you can connect around the world using 3G/4G or Wi-Fi (or both) to stay in touch or browse the Internet for whatever you need. You have an onscreen keyboard, so you do not have to drag around a physical keyboard. Robust business apps such as those in yours let you get work done as you travel. You may never drag a lap on a trip again!

That's why, in this chapter, the focus is on how people on the road can use the iPad. I start by pointing out the installed apps that work for travelers and provide some general advice and specific suggestions for making travel arrangements, finding great hotels and restaurants, getting maps and travel guides, and keeping track of what you spend as you travel. I also provide a reminder about using Personal Hotspot to get online using your smartphone connection, as well as using FaceTime, a way to connect with others to face face.

You can use Siri with an iPad mini or third-generation or newer iPad to ask for answers and information about many of the things handled in this apps in chapter, such as the time of the next flight to Dallas or the highest-rated hotel or restaurant in town. See Book V, Chapter 6 for more about Siri.

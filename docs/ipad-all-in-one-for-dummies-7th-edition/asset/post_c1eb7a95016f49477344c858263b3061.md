
## Presenting the iWork for iOS

REMEMBER In this chapter, the focus is on the three iWork apps, including how to use their common interface elements and find differences between them in cases where it matters. iWork suite includes three apps that are similar to Microsoft Office (but cooler):

- Pages: A word-processing and page-layout application (similar to Word)
- Numbers: A spreadsheet application (similar to Excel)
- Keynote: A presentation application (similar to PowerPoint)

All three apps are optimized for iPad models with a brilliant Retina display, plus they have new templates you can base your presentation on. Each program you download from the App Store is free and automatically migrates documents with other Apple devices. (See Book 1, for more information.) In the following sections, you take a quick look at these three iWork apps, all of which have been upgraded with the release of iOS 8.

For many people, word processing is the core of it; many people don't get beyond it. Pages for Mac adds desktop publishing to that type of structure you see in newspapers and magazines. Instead, an article on Page 1 may continue on Page 4, another article on the first page may continue on Page 8. Also, objects can be anchored to associated text or placed in specific positions, and they don't move around as text is added or deleted.

iWork provides you with a variety of tools to create documents. These include advanced font handling, color, tables, and charts, as well as the ability to place QuickTime movies and hypertext links in your documents. iWork applications also provide a variety of template options for your documents. ## Numbers

As with spreadsheets, Numbers enables you to enter data in rows and columns. One of the most useful features of spreadsheets is that they perform calculations by means of formulas. For example, if you have a column listing your groceries by week, the addition of another grocery bill will cause the program to recalculate the column's total. Spreadsheets are about data (usually numbers) and fast calculation updates, but they can also help you organize data as address lists and generate charts to show data trends.

Spreadsheets can go beyond the common grid interface, and Numbers excels at this approach. Take a look at the Numbers document shown in Figure 1-2, which is based on the Travel Planner template.

## Keynote

Call them lectures, classes, sermons, dog-and-pony shows, but presentations are all much the same: Someone stands in front of a large or small group of people and explains, teaches, inspires, entertains, or persuades them. In some cases, a presentation can run on its own or be run by the viewer in person or online.

Today, a presentation often includes multimedia elements: slides of buildings in an architecture class; slides with music in a presentation about the community theater's latest musical production; movies of good times on the beach in a talk about Uncle Charlie's summer vacation.

Keynote was the original iWork application. Built by Apple engineers for Macworld and Worldwide Developers Conference keynote speeches delivered by Steve Jobs, Keynote has refined over the years to become the powerful tool it is today. (See Figure 1-3.)

## Starting Out with iWork Apps

The first time you run an iWork app, you're greeted by a Welcome screen. The next time you open an app, you find yourself exactly where you left it. This Welcome screen contains basic information and instructions to get you started. You will find that each of apps has a similar look and feel. One of the coolest aspects of iWork is that major features—not just small operations such as changing a font—are the same in all apps. iWork apps are updated periodically. If an update is available, you see the App Store on your iPad screen with a number indicating that downloads are available. Updating your iWork when updates are available is generally a good idea so that their interfaces remain in sync.

## Creating a new document

You create a new document in the same way for each iWork app. Follow these steps:

- 1. Tap any of the iWork app icons to launch the application.

The Documents screen appears. This screen is titled Pages in the Pages app (see Figure 1-5), Spreadsheets in Numbers, and Presentations in Keynote.

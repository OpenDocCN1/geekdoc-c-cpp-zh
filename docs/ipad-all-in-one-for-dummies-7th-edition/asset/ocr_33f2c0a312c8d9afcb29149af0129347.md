The iTunes Store has free items that you can download and play around with the features in Music, including music and podcasts. You can also sync content on your computer or Apple devices to your iPad and play it using the Music app. See Book I, Chapter 5 for more about syncing.

Figure 2-2: View stations by tapping the Radio button.

Apple offers a service called iTunes Match (www.apple.com/itunes-match). You pay $24.99 per month for the capability to match the music you've bought from other providers or ripped from CDs (and stored on your computer) to what's in the iTunes Store. If there's a match (and often there), content is added to your iTunes library at 256 Kbps. If there's no match, it's uploaded to iCloud and synced to your iPad. Then, using iCloud, you can sync the content among all your Apple devices. The upward limit of 25,000 tracks works for most users.

You can use the iTunes Summary tab on your computer to make a setting to sync music at 128 Kbps. This saves space on your iPad if you're an avid music downloader, though the audio quality is lower. You can also make a setting for whether to download album covers in iTunes.

# Creating playlists

Everyone loves playlists. They give you a way to compile your very own music to match your mood or the occasion. You can easily create your own playlists with the Music app to put tracks from various sources into collections of your choosing.

# Deciding how much memory is enough

You know that computer memory is a measure of how much information — for example, movies, photos, and software applications, or apps — you can store on a computing device. Memory also affect your iPad's performance when handling tasks, such as streaming TV shows from the World Wide Web or downloading music.

With audio and video streaming, you can enjoy a lot of content online without downloading the full content to your hard drive or iPad flash memory, and given that the iPad has a relatively small amount of memory in any of its models, that's not a bad idea. See Book II, Chapters 1 through 3 for more about getting your music and movies online.

Your memory options on an iPad Air 2 or iPad mini 3 are 16, 64, or 128 gigabytes (GB). You must choose the right amount of memory because you can't open the unit and add to it, as you usually can with a desktop computer. There is no way to directly insert a flash drive (also known as a USB stick) to add backup capacity iPad has no USB port — but Apple has thoughtfully provided iCloud, a service you can use to save space by backing up content to the Internet. (You can read more about that in Chapter 5 of this minibook.)

With an Apple Digital AV Adapter, you can plug into the iPad to an HDMI-enabled device. See Book II, Chapter 3 for more about using these features. For example, ViewSonic offers several HDMI projectors, DOVO offers you an HD Travel Kit for smartphones and tablets, and Belkin has introduced a new line of tools for HDTV streaming.

So how much memory is enough for your iPad? Here is a good rule: If you like lots of media, such as movies or TV shows, and you want to store them on your iPad (rather than accessing this content online on sites like Hulu or Netflix from your Mac or PC using an app like Air Video), you might need 64GB or more. For most people who make a reasonable number of photos, download some music, and watch heavy-duty media as movies online, 64GB is probably sufficient.

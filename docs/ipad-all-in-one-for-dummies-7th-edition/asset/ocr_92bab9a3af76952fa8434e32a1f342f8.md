After you get your hands on an iPad, you should explore what's in the box and get an overview of the buttons and slots you'll encounter — luckily, there are few of them.

You also need to get comfortable with the touchscreen. If you have a smartphone or computer with a touchscreen, you're ahead of the game here, but even if you do, you should take a little time to get comfortable working with your iPad screen.

iPads (with the exception of the original iPad) have two cameras and a customizable side switch. In addition, iPad has native printing ability, all of which I introduce to you here.

Finally, after a touch of playing with your new gadget, you need to know how to put it to sleep. I cover all these iPad bases in this chapter.

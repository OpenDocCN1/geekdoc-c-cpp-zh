## Getting Support

As you may already know if you know another Apple device, Apple is known for its great customer support, so if you're stuck, I definitely recommend that you try them. Also, be aware that every new iPad comes with a year's coverage for repair of the hardware and 90 days of free technical support.

Here are a few options that you can explore for getting help:

- **The Apple Store:** Make an appointment online or by phone and then go to your local Apple Store if it is handy to find out what the folks know about your problem.

- **The iPad Support website:** Visit this site at www.apple.com/support/ipad (see Figure 9-6) or check the iTunes Store. Here you find online manuals, discussion forums, downloads, and the Apple Expert feature, which enables you to contact a live support by phone.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_178_0.png)

**Figure 9-6:** Don't forget to check the manuals and discussions for help.

- **The iPad User Guide:** You can visit http://manuals.info.apple.com/en_us/ipad_user_guide.pdf to view your iPad user guide. You can also download the manual and read it in popular e-reader programs.

- **The Apple battery replacement service:** If you need repair or service for your battery, visit www.apple.com/batteries/replacements.html.

Note that your warranty provides free battery replacement if the battery level dips below 50 percent and won't go any higher during the first year you own it. If you purchase the AppleCare service agreement, this is extended to two years. Also Apple recommends that the iPad battery be replaced only by an Apple Authorized Service Provider.

## Finding a Missing iPad

You can take advantage of the Find My iPad feature to pinpoint the location of your iPad. This is a very handy feature if you left your iPad or somebody walks away with it. Find My iPad lets you not only track down the culprit but wipe off the data contained in it if you have no way to get the iPad back.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_179_0.png)

If you're using Family Sharing, your family can help each other if someone in your family loses an iOS device. Anyone in the family can find the device and play a sound without having to sign into the missing device with its password. This works even if the ringer on the iPhone is turned off. See Book II, Chapter 1 for more Family Sharing.

Follow these steps to set up this feature:

1. Tap Settings on the Home screen.
2. Tap iCloud.
3. In the iCloud settings that appear, tap Find My iPad.
4. Tap the On switch for the Find My iPad setting (see Figure 9-7) that appears to turn the feature on if it isn't already.
5. Tap the Last Location On so that Find sends your iPad's location to Apple if your battery is almost out of juice, if you'd like.

If your iPad is lost or stolen, go to www.icloud.com on your computer and enter your ID and password. Click the Find My button to display a map of its location, as shown in Figure 9-8. Click the circle representing your iPad; then click the Information button (the small i) in the toolbar that appears. In the dialog that appears, to erase all information from your iPad in a process called wiping, click the Erase iPad button. Remember that this action erases all content, including contacts, music, notes, and so on, for good. To lock the iPad from access by others, click the Lost Mode.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_180_0.png)

**Figure 9-7:** Turn the feature on or off to locate your iPad from your computer.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_180_1.png)

**Figure 9-8:** Find My iPad can pinpoint your iPad's location.

**TIP** You can also tap Play a Sound to send to whoever has your iPad saying how to return it to you if it has been stolen! If you choose to play a sound, it plays for two minutes, helping you track down your iPad or anybody holding it.

## Backing Up to iCloud

You need to be able to back up your content only using iTunes, but with iCloud, you can back up via Wi-Fi network to iCloud storage. Note that whereas iTunes backs up everything, iCloud backs up what Apple considers most important content — videos you get from sources other than iTunes, for example. Note that you can back up to iTunes or iCloud, but not at the same time. However you can always sync to iTunes which copies apps and documents. You get 5GB of storage (not including iTunes-bought music, apps, and electronic books) for free, or you can pay for increased levels of storage (10GB for $20 per year, 20GB for $40 per year, or 50GB for $100 per year).

To perform a backup to iCloud, follow these steps:

1. **Tap Settings on the Home screen.** See Chapter 5 in this book for details on creating an iCloud account.
2. **Tap iCloud and then tap Backup (see Figure 9-9).**

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_181_0.png)
3. **In the pane that appears (see Figure 9-10), tap the iCloud Backup On/Off switch to enable automatic backups, or to perform a manual backup, tap Up Now.**

A progress bar shows how your backup is moving.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_182_0.png)

Figure 9-10: Automatic backups ensure that your data is saved.

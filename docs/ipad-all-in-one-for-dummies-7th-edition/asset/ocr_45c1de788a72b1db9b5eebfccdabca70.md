# 216 Getting Face to Face with FaceTime

If you have a Wi-Fi-only iPad and use FaceTime over a Wi-Fi network, that limits the places from which you can make or receive video calls to your home wireless network or a public hotspot. On the other hand, you avoid costly usage over a 3G or 4G network with this setup unless you own a 3G/4G iPad model.

![](bbox=[0.146, 0.135, 0.218, 0.198])

You have to use the appropriate method for placing a FaceTime call, depending on the kind of device you're calling. If you're calling iPhone 4 or later, use the phone number the first time you're calling, and after that, you use the phone number or email address; if you're calling an iPad 2 or later, an iPad touch, or a FaceTime for Mac user, you have to make the person's email address.

![](bbox=[0.146, 0.34, 0.218, 0.403])

You can't adjust audio volume from the app or record a video call. Nevertheless, on the positive side, though its features are limited, this app is very straightforward to use.

Follow these steps to use FaceTime:

+   1. Connect to a Wi-Fi or cellular (with iOS 6 or later) network.
   See Book I, Chapter 5 for details on connecting.
2. If you know the person you're calling has FaceTime on an iPhone 4 or later, an iPad 2 or later, or a Mac, add the person to your iPad Contacts.
   See Book V, Chapter 5 for how to do this if person isn't already in Contacts.
3. Tap the FaceTime app icon on the Home screen.
   The first time you use the app, you may be asked to select the phone number and email you want to use for FaceTime calls and then click Next. The FaceTime screen appears.
4. Tap the Contacts button in the bottom-right corner of the screen.
5. Scroll to locate and tap a contact's name.

![](bbox=[0.183, 0.677, 0.246, 0.727])

The contact's information displays (as shown in Figure 4-8).

You can also place a FaceTime call using Siri. Just say something like “Make a FaceTime call to Joey” and confirm Joey's contact information, and the call is placed.

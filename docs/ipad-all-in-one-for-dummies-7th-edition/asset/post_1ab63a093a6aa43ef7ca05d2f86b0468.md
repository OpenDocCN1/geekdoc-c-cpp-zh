## Managing Other Hearing Settings

A couple of hearing accessibility settings are simple on/off settings, including

- **Mono Audio.** Using the stereo effect in headphones or a headset breaks up sounds that you hear a portion in one ear and a portion in the other, to simulate the way your process process sounds (unless there is only one channel of sound, in which case sound is sent to both ears). If you're hard of hearing or deaf in one ear, you're hearing only a portion of the sound in your hearing ear, which can be frustrating. If you have hearing changes and want to use iPad with a headset, you should turn on Mono Audio. When it's turned, all sound is distributed and to both ears.

- **Have iPad Auto-text.** The Speak Auto-text feature, which you access through the Speech selection under Accessibility, speaks autocorrections and auto-capitalizations (you can turn on both these features using Keyboard settings). When you enter text in an app such as Notes or Mail, the app then makes every type of change, while Speak Auto-text lets you know what change was made.

## Turning On and Working with AssistiveTouch

Why would you want iPad to tell you whenever an auto-correction has been made? If you have vision challenges and you know that you typed "ain't" when writing dialogue for a character in your novel, but iPad corrected it to "isn't," you would want to know. Similarly, if your son's friend's name is Sunshine (don't worry, he'll break up with her soon) and auto-capitalization corrects it (incorrectly), you need to know immediately so that you can change it back.

The AssistiveTouch Control panel helps those who have challenges working with buttons to provide input to iPad using the touchscreen.

To turn on AssistiveTouch, follow these steps:

1. Tap Settings on the Home screen, and then tap General and Accessibility.
2. In the Accessibility panel, scroll down and tap AssistiveTouch.
3. In the panel that appears, tap the On/Off switch for AssistiveTouch to turn it on (see Figure 3-9).

A gray square (called the AssistiveTouch Control panel) then appears on the right side of the panel. This square now appears in the same location as whatever Home screen or apps display on your iPad, though you can move it around the screen using your finger.

4. Tap the AssistiveTouch Control panel to display options, as shown in Figure 3-10.

## Managing Home Speed

Sometimes if you have challenges, it's hard to use Home Speed.

1. Tap Settings > General > Accessibility.
2. Tap Home Speed.
3. Choose slow or fastest.

## Focusing Learning with Guided Access

Guided Access is a feature that you can use to limit an app.

1. Tap Settings > General > Accessibility.
2. Tap Guided Access (see Figure 3-11), turn it on.
3. Open the app you want to limit.
4. Press the side button three times.
5. Enter a passcode and Start.
6. Press the Home button three times.

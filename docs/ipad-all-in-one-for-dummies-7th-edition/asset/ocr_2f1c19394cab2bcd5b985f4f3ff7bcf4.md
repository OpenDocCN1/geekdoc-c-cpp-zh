Getting on the Internet with your iPad is easy using its Wi-Fi or 3G/4G capabilities. After you're online, the preinstalled browser, Safari, can take you around the web. Safari will be familiar to you if you've used an Apple device before or the browser on your Mac or PC. You're actually using a hybrid of the mobile version of Safari, also used on iPhone and iPod, and the desktop.

If you've never used Safari, this chapter helps you get up to speed. In this chapter, you discover how to open Safari and navigate among web pages using tabs browsing, as well as how to use iCloud to share browsing history among devices. Along the way, you learn about All Tabs view, a version of which has been available in iPhone and now comes to the iPad. All Tabs lets you view your open pages as thumbnails.

You see how to create a bookmark for a favorite site or web clip on your Home screen. You can also view your browsing history, save images to your Photo Library, post photos from within Safari, and email a hotlink to a friend. Two useful features are Shared Links as URLs posted to your Twitter and iCloud Keychain, for storing passwords and credit card information in one handy, safe place online. You explore the Safari Reader and Safari Reading List features, including the ability to save clips and web pages to Reading List, and find out how to keep yourself safe while using private browsing. I also show you the simple steps in printing what you find.

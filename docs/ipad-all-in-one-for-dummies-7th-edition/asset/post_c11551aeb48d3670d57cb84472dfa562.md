## Knowing what you need to use your iPad

Before you head off to buy your iPad, you should know what other devices, connections, and you'll need to work with optimally. At a bare minimum, you need to be able to connect to the Internet to take advantage of most iPad's features. You can open an iCloud account (Apple's online storage and synchronization service to store and share content online, or you can use a computer to download photos, music, or applications from non-Apple online sources as stores or sharing sites like your local library, and then transfer them to your iPad through a process called syncing. You can also use a computer or iCloud to register your iPad the first time you start, although if you have an Apple Store nearby, you can handle the registration for you.

Can you use iPad without owning a computer and just public Wi-Fi hotspots to go online (or a 3G/4G connection if you have one of them)? Yes, to be able to go online using a Wi-Fi-only iPad and to use many of its built-in features at home, you need a home Wi-Fi network available or be lucky enough to live in a town that has town-wide Wi-Fi (or at least Wi-Fi in your neighborhood). You also need to use iCloud or sync your computer to get updates for the iPad operating system.

For syncing with a computer, Apple's iPad User Guide recommends that you have:

- A Mac or PC with a USB 2.0 or USB 3.0 port and one of the following operating systems:
    - Mac OS X 10.6.8 or later
    - Windows 8, Windows 7, Windows Vista, Windows XP Home Professional with Service Pack 3 or later
    - iTunes 11.0 or later, available at www.apple.com/it
    - An Apple ID and iTunes Store account
    - Internet access
    - An iCloud account

Apple has set its iTunes software and the iCloud service to give you two ways to manage content for your iPad — including movies, music, or photos you've downloaded — and how to sync your calendar and contact information. Chapter 4 in this minibook covers those settings in more detail.

## Considering iPad Accessories

If you get your iPad from Apple, either at one of its retail stores or through its online store, there's the difference in the buying experience.

The brick-and-mortar Apple Store advantage is that the sales staff will help you unpack your iPad and make sure it's working properly, register the device (which you have to do before you use it), and help you learn the basics. There are occasional workshops offered to help people learn how to use iPads, and Apple employees are famous for being helpful to customers.

However, Apple Stores aren't on every corner, so if visiting one (or you just prefer to go alone), you can go to the Apple Store website (http://store.apple.com/us/browse/home/shop_ipad/family/) as shown in Figure 1-2, and order one to be shipped to you (and get it engraved, if you wish). Standard shipping is typically free, and if there's a problem, Apple's online-store customer service reps are very helpful— they help you solve the problem or possibly replace your iPad.

### Apple's stable of accessories

At present, Apple offers a few accessories you might want to check out when you purchase your iPad (or you can purchase them down the road), including:

- iPad Smart Case/Smart Cover: Your iPad isn't cheap, and unlike a laptop computer, it's vulnerable to damage. The Smart Case protects the front and back of the iPad, while the Smart Cover protects the screen and can be used to wake or sleep the iPad. The Smart Case costs $79, and the Smart Cover costs $29.

- Apple EarPods with Remote and Mic: This device offers in-ear listening and remote control of your audio for about $29.

- Apple Digital AV Adapter: To use the HDMI technology that allows you to connect devices to output high-definition media through HDMI ports in TVs, monitors, and projectors, you can use this adapter (Figure 1-3) for $49.

- AirPrint Printers: There are printers available from HP, Canon, Brother, Lexmark, and Epson that support Apple's AirPrint feature. Prices range from $129 to $399. HandPrint and Printopia are Macintosh apps that can print any printer shared over a network to your iPad.

- iPad Camera Connection: Because there's no USB port on an iPad, you can use a USB connection to upload photos from your camera or media card. If you want to add this functionality, you can use a MicroUSB camera and a MicroSD reader, each of which will set you back $30.

- Stands: The Apple Store offers several stands, including the Origami Workstand at $29.95 and Twelfth Mobile Upstand at $49.95. Twelfth Hover Stand for iPad costs $16.95, and attaches to your computer. Twelfth SouthBook is another good option at $29.99. For a truly special handcrafted stand, visit http://www.webskit.com/accessories.html to find a one-of-a-kind tablet stand that will make your choice.

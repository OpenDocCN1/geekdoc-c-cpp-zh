# 12 Choosing the Right iPad for You

You can't expand memory in an iPad. Apple is banking on your wanting to stream and sync content via iTunes or iCloud. Only you can decide whether that will work for you.

What's the price for more memory? For the iPad Air 2, a 16GB WiFi (see the next section for more about Wi-Fi) costs $499, 64GB adds another $100. But doubling that to 128GB adds $100, boosting the price to $699. If you buy an iPad mini 3, you're looking at $399, $499, or $599 for the three levels of memory it offers.

# Determining whether you need Wi-Fi only or Wi-Fi and 3G/4G

Another variation in price and performance for the iPad is whether your model has Wi-Fi or 3G/4G. Because the iPad is great for browsing online, shopping online, emailing, and so on, having an Internet connection for your device obviously is pretty essential. That's where Wi-Fi and 3G/4G come in. Both are used to connect to the Internet, and in case you need a refresher course, here's a quick summary:

+   Wi-Fi is what you use to connect to a wireless network at home, at work, or at public locations as your local coffee shop or an airport. This type of network uses short-range radio to connect devices to the Internet; its range is reasonably limited — if you leave walk out of the coffee shop, you can't use it.

+   3G and 4G cell phone technologies allow an iPad to connect to the Internet via a cellular network that's widespread. You can use it in the same way that you make calls from just anywhere using your cellphone. 3G is available as of iPad 2 and all later models. The latest cellular connection technology, may not be available in every location. You'll still need to connect to the Internet when 4G service isn't available, but without the advantage of the technology.

You can buy an iPad with Wi-Fi or both with Wi-Fi and 3G (or 3G and 4G) capabilities. A 3G/4G iPad costs an additional $130, but it also includes GPS so you can get more driving directions. You can buy an iPad model for your preferred data connection provider — AT&T, Sprint, T-Mobile, or Verizon in the United States, as of writing.

Also, to use your 3G/4G network, you have to pay AT&T, Sprint, T-Mobile, or Verizon a monthly fee. The good news is that none of these carriers requires a long-term contract, as you probably had to commit to your cellphone and its data connection — you can pay for a connection the month travel to Hong Kong and get rid of it when you return home.

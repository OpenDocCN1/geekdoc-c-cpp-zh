Apple designed its iTunes desktop application as a way to manage how your iPad syncs with your computer. Using iTunes, you can share information and content such as calendar events, pictures, music, and contacts. Before you use iTunes to sync from a PC, you have to download the software, and if you want to make purchases from the store, you need to open an iTunes account, both of which I cover in this chapter. You also use the iCloud service to store and share content online using a Wi-Fi connection. In this chapter, I cover how to set up iCloud account and make settings back up and share content in the cloud.

But iTunes isn't just for syncing content. Some apps, as as Contacts and Videos, come preinstalled on your iPad. But as you know if you are an iPhone user, a world of other apps is out there, many that are designed specifically for your iPad. After you set up iTunes, you can buy apps (or download free ones) in the App Store using iTunes account. Some are free and come for a price (typically from 99€ to about $10, though few go to $50 or more).

In this chapter, I tell you how to connect to the Internet (you need an Internet connection to access the App Store). Then I tell you how to set iTunes and iCloud to sync your iPad with other devices and how you can acquire apps for your iPad through the App Store.

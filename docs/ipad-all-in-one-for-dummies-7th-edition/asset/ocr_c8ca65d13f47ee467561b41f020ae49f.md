+   2. **Enter a recipient's address in the To field either by typing it or by tapping the Dictation key on the iPad's onscreen keyboard (fourth-generation iPad and later) and speaking the address.**

If you have saved addresses in Contacts, tap the plus (+) in an address field to choose an addresse from the Contacts list that appears.

+   3. **If you want to copy other people on the message, tap the Cc/Bcc field.** Both Cc and Bcc fields are displayed. Enter addresses in either or both. Use the Bcc field to specify of blind carbon copies, which means that no other recipients are aware that that person received this reply.

+   4. **Enter a subject for the message in the Subject field.**

+   5. **Tap in the message body and type your message.**

+   6. **If you want to check a fact or copy and paste some part of another message into your draft message, swipe down the top of the email to display your Inbox and other folders. Locate a message and if you want to copy, text; when you're ready to return to your draft, tap the Subject of the email, which is displayed near the bottom of the screen with any other open folders, and paste any text you might have copied into the new message.

+   7. **Tap (you gone it) Send).**

You can also use Siri to address an email to a person for whom you've created a record in your Contacts app that includes an email address. Just press and hold the Home button and say something like "Joe Smith," confirm the contact, and then fill in the form that appears are using Dictation or the keyboard.

Want to shoot somebody in an email (not, of course, a practice advice)? You can activate Caps Lock when the onscreen keyboard on your iPad by double-tapping Shift key. To turn off Caps Lock, tap either Shift key once. To use this functionality, be sure to tap Settings:General:Keyboard and enable Caps Lock.

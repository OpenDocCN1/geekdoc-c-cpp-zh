# Watching non-iTunes videos

Are you limited to iTunes content on iPad or viewing your downloaded content on the smaller screen? Not at all. You can connect iPad to television, so can watch videos on the more vision-friendly larger screen. To do this, you must have the Digital AV Adapter at the Apple Store. The Digital AV Adapter is a $39.95 accessory. Apple TV costs $99. You also need any other appropriate cables for your TV to complete the connection. (Or you can use AirPlay via an Apple TV).

Note that iPad supports the following video formats: H264 video up to 1080, 30 frames per second; MPEG-4, up to 2.5 Mbps, by 480 pixels, 30 frames per second; and Motion JPEG (M-JPEG), up to 35 Mbps, 1280 by 720 pixels, 30 frames per second. You can set up widescreen viewing options in the Videos section of Settings. For more about using Settings, see Book I, Chapter 8.

Note that if you watched a video and stopped it away, the next time you open the video in iPad, it opens, by default, at the last location that you were viewing. To start a video from the beginning, just as most players, simply tap and drag the circular button on the Progress bar all the way to the left. You can also change the default setting from starting where you left off to starting from the beginning in iPad's settings under Video.

# Turning on Closed-Captioning

If you have hearing challenges or are a fan of foreign languages, you'll be glad to know that iPad offers support for closed-captioning and subtitles. This feature requires that content supports closed-captioning (not all shows or movies do) and that you go to Settings to turn the feature.

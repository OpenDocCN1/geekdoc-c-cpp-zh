# Mark Email as Unread or Flag for Follow-Up

With a new feature in iOS 8, you can use a simple swipe to access tools that either mark an email as unread after you've read it, which places a blue dot before the message, or flag an email, which places an orange circle before it. These methods help you remember to reread an email that you've already read or to follow up on a message at a later time.

- 1. With Mail open and an Inbox displayed, swipe to the left to display three options: Move, Flag, and Trash. Note that you should swipe only halfway across the message; swapping all the way to the left deletes the message.
- 2. Tap More.

On the menu shown in Figure 7-16, you're given several options, including Mark as Unread and Flag. Tapping either command applies it and returns you to your Inbox.

- 3. Tap Mark as Unread.

Note that you can also get to the Mark as Unread command by swiping to the right on a message displayed in your Inbox.

- 4. Swipe to the left on another email and then tap Flag.

An orange circle appears before the email.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_147_2.png)

You can either mark an email as unread or flag it; you can't do both. Either action provides a unique visual clue indicating that you need to revisit this message before deleting it.

# Create an Event from Email Contents

Invitations sent to you in email that you accept have always been placed in the Calendar app automatically. In addition, Mail will create a Calendar event from certain information contained within an email such as a flight reservation.

To check this feature out, follow these steps:

- 1. Create an event to yourself mentioning a reservation on a specific date and time.
- You can also mention another type of reservation, such as for dinner, or just a phone number. 
- 2. Send the message to yourself and open Mail.
- 3. In your Inbox, open the mail. Note that pertinent information is displayed in blue, underlined text.
- 4. Tap some underlined text; in the menu shown in Figure 7-17, choose to Create Event.

A New Event form from Calendar appears.

- 5. Enter additional information about the event and then tap Done.

# # Printing Emails

iPad has native printing capabilities that can be used by certain apps, including Mail. You need an AirPrint-compatible wireless printer set up to use this feature. For more about options for printing from your iPad, see Book V, Chapter 1.

With an email message open, follow these steps to print:

- 1. **Tap the Reply button and then Print.**
- 2. **In the Printer Options dialog that appears (if you haven't used this feature with your printer before), tap Print Select.**
   The iPad searches for any available printers.
- 3. **Tap your printer to select it.**
- 4. **Tap Back to return to the Printer Options dialog and use the plus or minus buttons (as necessary) in the Copies field to adjust the number of copies. If you use a printer that supports two-sided printing, you can use the Double-Sided switch to choose that option.
- 5. **Tap Print.**

Your print job is on its way to your printer.

# Deleting an Email

I have friends who never delete emails, but that, frankly, drives me nuts (plus, at some point, their mailbox fills up and new emails are rejected). When you no longer want an email cluttering your Inbox, you can delete it. When you delete an email on your iPad, it's gone from your Inbox including the Inbox you access through your mobile phone or computer. For a time, you can retrieve it if your email provider offers a Trash folder for your email account and if your settings for your account with that provider don't cause emails to be deleted from their server on download.

Here's how to delete those emails no longer want:

- 1. With the Inbox displayed, tap the Edit button.

Circular check boxes display to the left of each message (see Figure 7-18).

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_150_0.png)

Figure 7-18: Delete several messages at one time using the Delete feature.

- 2. Tap the circle next to the message you want to delete.

You can tap multiple items if you have several emails to delete. Messages marked for deletion show a check mark in the circular check box. (refer to Figure 7-18.)

- 3. Tap the Trash button.

The message(s) moves to the Trash.

# 134 Organizing Email

Depending on your email provider, Mail may keep a copy of deleted messages for a time in a Trash folder. To view deleted messages, go to the Mailboxes.

You can also delete an email by tapping the Trash button on the toolbar that runs across the top or by swiping left or right on a message displayed in Inbox.

In most email accounts your ability to create folders is dependent on the email program. Some providers don't offer much support for managing messages in folders. Assuming you have access to folders (for example, an Outlook.com account) displayed, tap the Edit button. Circular check boxes display to the left of each message.

Follow these steps to move any message into another folder:

- 1. Tap the circle next to the message you want to move.
- 2. Tap the Move button.
- 3. In the list that appears (see Figure 7-19), tap the folder where you want to store the message.

The message is moved.

If you have an email open, you can move it to a folder by tapping the Folder icon on the toolbar that runs along the top. The Mailboxes list displays; tap a folder to move message.

## Getting Online with iPad

iPad would be kind of a very expensive calendar, address book, and music player if you couldn't go online to browse things, get email, stream video and audio. Two bundled apps, Safari and Mail, help connect your iPad to many of the Internet's resources.

## Going on Safari

Safari is Apple's web browser. If you've owned a Mac computer, iPhone, or iPad touch, you've already used Safari (see Figure 4-1) to navigate around the Internet, create and save bookmarks of favorite sites, and add web clips to the Home screen so you can quickly visit sites from there.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_79_1.png)

If you've been a Windows user in the past, you may also have used Safari, or you may be more familiar with browsers such as Internet Explorer, Chrome, or Firefox. If you haven't used Safari, don't worry; the browser should be pretty easy for you to get the hang of, with its familiar integrated address-search fields, tab navigation, and bookmarks.

Using a browser on iPad is a lot of fun because of the touchscreen functionality gives you the ability to zoom in or out of a page by flipping two fingers inward or outward. Tabbing browsing allows you to move easily among known pages, and the All Tabs view with iOS 8 for iPad lets you see your open tabs as thumbnails on a single screen. You can read more out using Safari in Chapter 6 of this minibook.

www.it-ebooks.info

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_80_0.png)

**Figure 4-1:** Find what you need on the web with Safari.

Be sure to check out iCloud connectivity with a feature called Handoff. If you have several Apple devices, you can use iCloud tabs to pick up where you left off or viewing tabs on one device on another.

You can get done much of what you want to do on your iPad by using Siri. This personal assistant, which you learn more about in Book V, Chapter 6, can help you go to a web page, create and send an email, an event in Reminders or Calendar, and more.

## Getting Mail

iPad lets you get all mail in one place through the Mail app — the program you use to access email accounts you set up in iPad. You can set up accounts that you have with popular email providers such as Gmail, Yahoo, AOL, and Outlook.com. You can also access accounts through iCloud or Microsoft Exchange/365. (Your work account might use Microsoft Exchange, for example.) Almost any IMAP- or POP3-type account (the two most mail protocols) is supported, which lets you access the email accounts you have with your Internet service provider (ISP).

After you set an account, when you tap the Mail icon, your email will display without having to browse to a site or sign in. Then you can use tools to move preset mail folders, read and reply to mail, and download attached photos to the iPad. You can also use the Print feature in Mail to print your email messages to a printer that supports AirPrint. You can read more about setting up and using email accounts in Chapter 7 of this minibook.

www.it-ebooks.info

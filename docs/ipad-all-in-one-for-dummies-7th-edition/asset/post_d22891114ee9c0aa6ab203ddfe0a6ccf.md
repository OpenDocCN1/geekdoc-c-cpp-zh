## Resizing a row or column

The two vertical lines (known as the Cells button) in the top-right corner of the column frame (refer to Figure 3-14) and the two horizontal lines in the bottom-left corner of the row frame (refer to Figure 3-12) let you resize the selected rows or columns. You always resize a column to its right and to its bottom, whether you're making it larger or smaller. The contents are automatically adjusted, and the adjoining rows or columns are moved aside.

## Moving a row or column

Sometimes, you want to rearrange the rows or columns in a sheet. With one or more rows or columns selected, press and drag it (or them) to a new position. All selected rows or columns move as a unit, and the other rows or columns move aside.

## Resizing a table

You can resize a table in two ways: add or remove rows or columns to from the table, or leave the number of rows and columns the same and change the overall size of the table. You may even want to both.

## Adding or removing rows and columns at the right or bottom

Tap the Cells button in the top-right corner of the column frame to add a new column at the right. To add a new row at the bottom, tap the Cells button in the bottom-left corner of the row frame, and a new row is added to the bottom of the table.

To remove a blank column or row at the right side or bottom of a table, drag the appropriate Cells button to the left or to the top of the frame — the extra rows or columns disappear.

## Adding or removing rows and columns inside the table

Select the row or column below or to the right of the new row or column. From the selection buttons, tap Insert to add a new row above the selected row or a new column to the left of the selected column.

## Changing a table's size

Select the table so that the handles (small blue dots) are visible at the right and bottom. These handles work like other resizing handles in Numbers: Simply drag them horizontally or vertically to change the table's size. The content of the table automatically resizes to fit the new table size. The numbers of rows and columns remain unchanged, but their sizes may be adjusted.

## Entering and editing data

The keyboard for entering data into a Numbers cell is a powerful and flexible tool — it's four keyboards in one. Above the keyboard, the Formula bar lets you quickly and easily enter formulas. (Refer to Figure 3-15.) On the left of the Formula bar are four buttons that let you switch from one keyboard to another. You can choose these keyboards, starting from the:

- Numeric (42 icon): Lets you use the numeric keyboard.
- Date and Time (clock icon): Lets you enter dates and times.
- Text (T icon): Displays the standard keyboard.
- Formulas (= icon): Opens the formula-editing keyboard.

Whichever keyboard you're using, you see the characters you're typing in the Formula bar above the keyboard. To the right of the oblong area is a button labeled Done (or a check mark, depending on which keyboard you're using) that moves your typing into the selected cell and then hides the keyboard.

The Dictation feature on iPad works in Numbers and the other iApps. Tap the Dictation key on the onscreen and speak your text; tap the key to insert text in your document.

You can bring up the keyboard when you double-tap a cell or another text field (as a tab name). That means that the cell is selected and the keyboard knows where the data should go when you tap OK or Done.

Note that some of these features are unavailable on a Bluetooth keyboard, which may or may not have a number pad and special symbols when entering formulas.

To the right of the keyboard in the numeric and date-and-time keyboards are three large keys:

- Delete: This is the standard keyboard Delete key.
- Next (adjacent): The key with the right-pointing arrow inserts the typed data into the selected cell and moves to the next cell to the right.
- Next (next line): The key with the hooked arrow pointing left and ups the typed data and moves to the left and to the leftmost cell in the section of cells you're entering. For example, if you start in the third column of the fourth row, the Next (next line) key moves you to the fifth row, not the first column.

## Customizing check boxes

A check box can indicate, for example, that an item is in or out of stock (yes/no). However, you can also use it to represent numeric data. Computers and programs often represent zero values as true/false. If you show the in-stock inventory count as a number and sometimes the check box, Numbers handles the conversion for you.

Here's how check box customization can work:

1. Double-tap a cell to start editing; tap the 42 icon and tap the Checkbox button when the keyboard opens. You see a check box in the selected cell; in the area above the keyboard, you see the word *false*, as shown in Figure 3-17. The green outline distinguishes the word *false* from text you type. It has the value *false* because, before you type anything, its numeric value is zero.

![](img/2a8a8d89daeb1b3896bd38ebf18af126_399_0.png)

2. Type a nonzero value. This value can represent the number of items in stock, for example.

3. Tap the Checkbox button again. You see the value you changed to *true* (see Figure 3-18) and that the check box is selected in the table.

Because the formatting is separate from the data value, you sometimes display the in-stock inventory count as a number and sometimes the check box. Customers are likely to care only about the check box, but the inventory manager cares about the number.

Everything in Numbers is linked, so you don't have to worry about a sequence for most things. If you've followed these steps, you've seen how to enter a number and have control it check box, but that's only one of at least three ways of working with a check box.

With a selected cell and the Checkbox formatting selected, you see the true/false value above the keyboard and the check box itself in the table. Tap the check box in the table: The true/false value is reversed, and it is the state of the check box itself.

Likewise, if you tap the word *true* or *false* in the display above the keyboard, the check box flips its value and true/false reverses.

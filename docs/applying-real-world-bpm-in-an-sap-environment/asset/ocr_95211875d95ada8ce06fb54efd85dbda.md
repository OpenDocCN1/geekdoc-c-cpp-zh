Whereas approaches 1 and 2 are more top-down focused approaches, thereby being more value management focused, approaches 3 and 4 illustrate a more a bottom-up approach, focusing on performance management. However, in all of these approaches the goal is to identify not only value drivers, but also value gaps. The core weakness identification approach, which we know as the new ASAP methodology framework (see details in Part III, Chapter 14, Section 14.3), focuses on weakness cluster identification and mapping. Such a weakness cluster mapping is based on the current pain point situation (PPS) and any weaknesses within the processes. A current weakness cluster view with a pain/gain impact estimation (P/G-IE) is used to map and then analyze the PPS with the resulting pain point effects (PPEs) or undesirable effects (UDEs) in terms of results and the possible root problems of the organization and thereby determine the impact to the business model. A PPE or UDE is any major issue that prevents the organization from achieving its goal and is therefore a great source of value potential, as shown in Table 3.2.

As Table 3.2 illustrates, many of the PPEs and UDEs include problems and thereby weakness cluster symptoms that were revealed by the other approaches, as well as detailed or new UDEs. The distinction between situation, problems, and symptoms is crucial, because the real value creation potential lies in solving the core problems, not the situation or the symptoms.

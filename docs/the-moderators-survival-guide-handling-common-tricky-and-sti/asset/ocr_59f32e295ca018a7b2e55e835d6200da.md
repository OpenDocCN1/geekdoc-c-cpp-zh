# SURVIVAL STORY: "HE REFUSES TO LEAVE"
CLIFF ANDERSON

I was at my desk when I got a call from the lab (another usability engineering was running a study that week. Was from our lab assistant, who seemed more than a little excited—“Cliff, there is a man here, and he refuses to leave, and he is threatening us, and we don't know what to do." After calling him, I learned that he had expected to get his $100 compensation in cash, was basking because we were offering Visa gift cards—something that was new for us and for our regular recruiting agency.

I told her "I'll be right down," then hit the stairs to get to the lab. When I got there, I found my assistant, the usability engineer was running our test, and the user. Things seemed pretty under control, but I noticed that the user seemed a little perturbed.

I went right up to him, introduced myself, shook his hand, and asked him what was the matter. He went into his story, and I did my best job at active and empathetic listening, using all the tricks and tropes that I had learned over the years working with users and doing research.

I could tell he was calming down, but I still hadn't come up with a way to address his problem. So, I put my hand on my chin, knotted my brows, and it came to me! "Why don't you give him that gift card, and we can go to the ATM in the lobby downstairs, and I can withdraw your $100"

He agreed, we withdrew the $100, shook hands, and parted amicably. I went back upstairs shaking my head, giving myself credit for keeping cool under pressure (in general, one of the best skills a usability engineer can have) and also making sure that our screener (and recruiting agency) made sure to mention this new form of compensation ahead of time.

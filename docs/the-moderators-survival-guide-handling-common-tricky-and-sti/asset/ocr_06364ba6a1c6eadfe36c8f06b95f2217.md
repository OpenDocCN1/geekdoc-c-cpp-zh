Keep your facial expression friendly and neutral. An occasional smile is okay, and you may notice the participant smiling back in return! Check your tone, remembering to sound genuinely interested in what she has to say.

If the participant seems disengaged with the product she is using, try to establish a personal connection between the participant and what she is doing. Encourage her to talk about herself or her experiences, and tie her response to a rephrasing or refocusing of the task. For example, if you're running a usability study on a mobile device that the participant seems to find uninter, ask her to tell you mobile games she does enjoy. After hearing her response, ask her to compare the experience of her favorite game to the one she is using

A bonus about making the personal connection is that the participant may forget her initial emotional response (nervousness, etc.). But be careful to keep your efforts off building engagement linked to the session. This pattern is not about making friends with the participant; it's about turning a topic or task she finds boring, uninspiring, or irrelevant into something that she can provide feedback on.

In having this conversation with the participant, you may discover other reasons for the lack of engagement, such as low literacy or computer skills or a mismatch with your screening criteria. If this is the case, you may decide to shift the focus of the session to accommodate her unexpected skill level or criteria mismatch, or you may need to end the session early.

The Friendly Face and Inquisitive Mind moder styles discussed in Chapter 2 are most appropriate to use during this pattern.

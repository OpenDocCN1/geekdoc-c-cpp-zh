# THE MODERATOR'S SURVIVAL GUIDE

If you feel that you have a strong relationship with the person you'd like to give feedback to and aren't worried about his reaction, you can try a more direct approach. But don't just giving him feedback; start by asking if he'd like to hear it:

+   - I had a chance to watch one of your sessions, and thought that you did a good job. There were a couple of things that I noticed while you were moderating. You have a lot going on, would you be interested in hearing my feedback?>

+   - <Hey <moderator>, great session! I know if it were me, I'd want to hear any feedback that other moderators have for me (and please let me know what you have for me!). So I have a couple of ideas to bounce off what I noticed watching you moderate, if you're up for hearing them.

Of course, a direct way of giving feedback is to present it in a funny way. For example, "Heh, when you were upset, 'Are you having a problem?' and the participant got upset and was like, 'Um-NO!' We received feedback veiled in humor before we've given the message and were glad to hear it! But course of using humor can be touchy, so if the person you're giving feedback doesn't respond, responds negatively, avoid using that approach him in the future.

Also, keep in mind everything you've learned when presenting user research findings: discussing what worked help the more negative feedback be received smoothly.

Another way to exchange feedback is to set up a process where you and your colleagues can submit feedback. For example, if you work on a large user research team, you could create an anonymous survey to fill out each study. Or the survey could "suggestion box" ideas, collecting generalized feedback that is not specifically targeted to any one researcher or study. The entire team can then get together and discuss the comments. It's a great way to continuously moderating techniques and improve everyone's skills.

If you're hiring a consultant to work with or for you on a project, he might solicit feedback on his performance after the project has ended. This mechanism is in place, make sure to include feedback on his moderating. If something like that is not in place, consider working with the agency to instantiate a feedback loop for that purpose.

# 2.5 Use careful language and tone to probe the situation\and begin to resolve it

Once you've established that you're not causing or magnifying an issue, you'll probably
need to talk with the participant try to understand more about, and hopefully resolve,
t situation. One of the most important principles in moderating is to let the participant
do most of the talking, but when you do speak—whether it's to provide direction, probe for
detail, or give neutral reassurance—you need to choose your words, and your tone,
carefully.

To choose the appropriate language, we suggest reviewing and memorizing some
refabricated responses that represent moderating best practices. Prefabricated memories
are valuable for interacting with participants while maintaining neutrality and consistency.
By memorizing at least these phrases and reviewing a list before your study,
save the cognitive effort of improvising appropriate responses on the spot. As we
start reviewing specific situations in Part 2, you'll be amazed how flexible these short
phrases can be.

The phrases we use most often include:

+   * What would you expect?
* What are you trying to do now?
* What are you thinking right now?
* How did this compare to your expectations?
* What are your thoughts on how that worked?

+   * That link isn't working for today's session. What you would expect to happen if you
clicked it and it was working?
* “Thank you for that feedback, but for the sake of time, please move on to ..."

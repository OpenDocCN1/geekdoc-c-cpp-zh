| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Friendly Face | ➤ Centers on being comforting and encouraging to participants. | ➤ Participants are timid and self-blaming. | ➤ Talking too much or letting the casual, conversational style become influential. | ➤ Be genuine. If participants feel that you're disengenuous, they may close up and give feedback, or make snarky remarks or facial expressions. |
| | ➤ Comes as relatively casual and responsive to participants, often using nonverbal feedback as smiling and nodding, and can help participants feel comfortable and happy to be part of the research activity. | ➤ Participants are participating out of obligation. | ➤ Slipping into the role of a buddy, saying biasing things like, "I know, that seems weird," or "Don't worry, everyone's had trouble with that" | ➤ Steering topic so they become more interested in laughing or chatting with you giving the feedback. |
| | | ➤ Participants seem disengaged. | | ➤ Becoming too formal or quiet, which can make participants feel nervous or shy. This nervousness may prevent them from interacting naturally with a product or opening up as much as they would otherwise. | Monitor your tone. If it becomes overly serious or academic, participants may become more nervous. |
| Down to Business | ➤ Treats the session with a serious, professional tone. Often limits interaction with the participant and minimizes probing or follow-up questions. | ➤ Participants act too casually, or otherwise seem need structure. | | Avoid condescension. Participants may view a serious tone as condescending, especially if they are colleagues at the same organization or participants who are high profile (e.g. VIP customers). |
| | ➤ Shows the participant that you're in control of the session and have an important agenda to cover. | ➤ Participants continue asking for assistance. ➤ Participants need constant reinforcement that they're the "driver" and you're mostly a silent observer. ➤ Your study is using a quantitative method where you're measuring aspects of the experience. | | |

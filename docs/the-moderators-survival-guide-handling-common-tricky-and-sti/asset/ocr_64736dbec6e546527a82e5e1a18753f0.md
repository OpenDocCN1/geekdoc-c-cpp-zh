+   - If the prototype can be completely functional, make it adequately functional. In the real world, participants will try lot of crazy paths to an answer and sometimes you can't anticipate all of them. So "adequately functional" means that the prototype includes more than just linear flow of clicks or steps, and more than just the correct paths are working. The prototype should provide alternative and anticipated paths or steps that the participant can explore and attempt.

+   - We recognize that most teams have time and rescuing constraints. If you can't get more than the correct paths or steps working, provide the *illusion* that the prototype is functional. One example of this is, in a website, make all of the links look clickable even if they're just null links that don't go anywhere. Doing this prevents the mouse "drive-by," where participants can catch onto what is working based on where they get the hover-hand icon.

# Test and document the technical setup

To avoid, or at least minimize, technical difficulties during your study, do a full walkthrough of your technical setup for the session. This walkthrough should include turning on and off any cameras, screen-sharing applications, and recording devices. Test the order in which each item needs to be turned on or document the order that works best. Depending on the complexity of your study, consider a checklist of your setup steps in your study so you can easily reset if anything changes during a session.

If you're running a remote session:

+   - Familiarize yourself with options to mute others on the call or eject them from the screen-sharing session to avoid observers interrupting the session.

+   - Do a dry run with participants to ensure that your screen-sharing software works on their computers, which will save time during their sessions.

+   - In your technical setup checklist, include screenshots of what the participant will experience as they see the remote session. These screenshots will help you provide instructions to the participant to perform basic troubleshooting.

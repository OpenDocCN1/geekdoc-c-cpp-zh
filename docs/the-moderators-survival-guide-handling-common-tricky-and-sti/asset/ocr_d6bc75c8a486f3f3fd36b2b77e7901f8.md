The best style to use depends on how you need to interact with the participant as discussed in Table 1.1 and where your study goals fall on the quadrant shown in Figure 1.2. Each style falls somewhere within the range of rigid fluid (based on your research goals and how much you need to follow a prescribed structure of your study plan) and engaged versus disengaged (how you need to interact with the participant at any given time).

You've read about the differences between moderating styles in Table 1.1, but let's take a more detailed example here. Consider a contextual inquiry where you have a participant who you're trying to keep focused on demonstrating your workflow. Contextual inquiries are typically fairly fluid, and you're fairly friendly and casual moderator, so you've started with a style similar to Inquisitive Mind. However, the participant's talkativeness, can be so informal and off-topic, pulling her away from the natural workflow you're trying to observe. The session may quickly snowball into an interview or conversation. The best way to adapt is to transition to a Down to Business style with the goal of slightly disengaging from the participant. You don't want to disengage so much that the participant becomes uncomfortable, just enough so that she knows you're there for the serious business of watching her work.

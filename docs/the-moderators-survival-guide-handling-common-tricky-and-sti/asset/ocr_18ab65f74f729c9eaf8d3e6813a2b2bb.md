# 10.5 Participant gives vague responses to questions

The participant responds to your questions with vague answers. You may not be sure he doesn't understand your questions, isn't articulate, is shy, or just wants to collect the promised compensation.

+   Method(s): Any
Frequency: Occasional
Pattern(s) to apply: Take responsibility; Clarify the task/question; Build engagement

# What to do

+   Try clarifying or reordering your questions in case the participant didn't understand what you are asking. If necessary, try to back up to broader, easier questions and then, once you've built up that foundation, lead into the harder questions. If he seems frustrated or unfazed, take responsibility for “not being clear” about your question.

+   Ask “Why?” to prompt the participant to dig deeper into his response. For example, if you ask a participant about his experience using a remote control and he says it is okay, you follow up with, “Tell me more—why do you say it is ‘okay’?” You can ask “Why?” multiple times until you feel you've reached the level of detail needed.

+   If the participant is being vague because he is unfamiliar with the subject matter, move on to the next task/question that doesn't cover the topic. Familiarity with topic is a deal-breaker, section 4.1.

+   If the participant is shy and reluctant to share feedback, try to adapt your moderating style as discussed in section 1.5 to a more informal and approachable one such as Friendly Face or Inquisitive Mind.

+   If the participant doesn't seem interested in the topic or seems eager to get through the session as quickly as possible, see 8.6 for ideas on how to inspire his interest.

# What to say

To clarify:

+   “I’m sorry, I don't think I’m being clear with my question. Let me rephrase....”

+   “Let me back up a little, and first ask you this....”

To build engagement:

+   “Thanks so much for participating. I know this is taking time out of your busy day, but your feedback is very helpful.”

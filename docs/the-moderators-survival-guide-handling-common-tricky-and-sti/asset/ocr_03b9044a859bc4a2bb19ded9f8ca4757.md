# 9.1 Participant is running late (cont.)

# What not to do or say

+   - Don't blame the participant for being late, no matter how frustrated or irritated you are.
- Avoid making veiled comments about how much you had planned to get through if the participant had been on time.

# How to avoid

+   - Ask the recruiter to ensure participants arrive 15 minutes early to fill out forms or to "check in." If there are typically traffic or parking issues in a particular area, or a known event or construction is happening, the recruiter warn participants about scheduling extra travel time and suggest alternative methods of transportation.
- Obtain the participants' cell phone numbers so you can call them directly if they're running late. Ensure that the participants also have a contact phone number for you and your recruiter in case they're running late.
- Schedule backup or floater participants for sessions when it's extremely important not to have a late or no-show participant (discussed more in section 15.1).
- If budget is a concern for your study, consider working out an arrangement with the recruiter where, if the participant is not at your location within a certain number of minutes of her scheduled time, she'll not receive her compensation. If you do explore this option, we recommend recommend having a backup or floater system so you don't end up with empty timeslots. Include the use of floaters in your proposal to the stakeholders who manage your research budget, although keep in mind that this system probably won't reduce your compensation costs.

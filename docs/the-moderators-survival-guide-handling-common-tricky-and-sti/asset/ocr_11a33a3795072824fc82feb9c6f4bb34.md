# 5.5 Participant treats a contextual inquiry like an interview

You arrive onsite to perform a contextual inquiry but the participant expects that she just needs to tell you about what she does with a product and what she thinks of it. For example, a call representative anticipates an interview and schedules special time off her phone queue to chat with you. Your goal was actually to take her calls and ask her questions in between calls. If you're doing the session remotely, the participant may just work about the inquiry instead of showing it to you through the screen-sharing software.

**Method(s):** Contextual inquiry
**Frequency:** Occasional
**Pattern(s) to apply:** Take responsibility; Clarify the task/question; Shift the focus

# What to do

This situation may happen because the participant was given vague details when recruited for the study, or made the wrong assumption when asked to participate. Most people are familiar with the idea of being interviewed, but a contextual inquiry is not a very common method outside of the user research community.

+   - Clarify your expectations as soon as you realize what is happening, and apologize for not being clear enough ahead of time what you were planning. Hopefully the participant will be able to adapt and you can continue the session as planned. If you can't continue, see if you can reschedule or shift the focus to some other way to get feedback.
- If she seems embarrassed or feels bad about the mix-up, take responsibility for the miscommunication (even if someone's fault).
- Prepare a list of questions for when you can't watch the participant work. This may come up without a misunderstanding. For example, the participant's system may be down, or she may have completed a project early and have nothing to show you.

# What to say

+   - "I was actually looking to do today is to watch you in the way that you would typically if I weren't here. I have some questions for you along the way, or things I ask to show me, but in general, you should be working and my role is mostly to be a wallflower and watch quietly"
- "Are you able to get set so that I can quickly just watch while you work?"
- "I'm sorry for the mix-up—I must not clearly communicated my intent for this session. If it isn't too much trouble, could you go ahead and show me what you're describing?"

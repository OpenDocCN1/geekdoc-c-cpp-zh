# THE MODERATOR'S SURVIVAL GUIDE

# When to use

Use this pattern when you need to take the participant's focus and emotions and redirect them in a direction more appropriate for your study. This pattern may be appropriate:

+   - If the participant seems fixated on something other than you need to receive feedback on.
- If the participant seems to be going down a path that will not be useful on your study goals, and you want to bring her attention back to your original intent.
- If you need to give the participant an assist so she is able to continue a task during a usability study.
- If the participant is obviously disoriented or unhappy with the organization/product, and you can tell that her complaints will get in the way of your feedback.
- If the participant seems overly enthusiastic with the organization/product and loses focus.

You will see redirection referenced in many situations throughout the book, but it is especially key in situations where participants are uncertain. These situations are listed in Chapter 8.

# What to do

This pattern redirects the participant toward an outcome more appropriate to the research you're performing. The most common application of this pattern is the moderate staple of turning a question back around the participant. For example, if she asks, “Where would I go to that?” you respond, “Where would you expect to go?” If the participant asks a question that is more difficult to test around—“Am I doing this right?—this pattern lets you provide a vague answer that redirects her to a topic more relevant to the study. For example, “Like I said, we are definitely not testing you. Let me ask you about..."

You may also need to provide a usability study participant with an assist if she struggles with a task that you need to get feedback on, or if she is unable to complete a future tasks that depends on. In these situations, you're redirecting the participant from the path she is currently taking to the one that you need to use so you can accomplish your study goals.

An assist can mean anything from a gentle prompt to pointing out what the participant hasn't noticed on her own. For the different types of assists, see section 6.2 and the sidebar "The Diversionate Assist."

This pattern also lets you provide some space for the energy exiting from a disoriented or overly enthusiastic participant. One way to do this is, at the beginning of the session, let a disoriented participant spend a amount of time venting to get the negativity out of her system. For example, if you're interviewing her about how she chooses to purchase a product and she immediately starts running around an e-commerce website, interrupt: “My goal for this session is to talk at a higher level over your decision process.

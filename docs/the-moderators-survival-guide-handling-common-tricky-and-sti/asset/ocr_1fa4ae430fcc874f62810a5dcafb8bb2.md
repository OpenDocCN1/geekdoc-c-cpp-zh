# Listen to stories from other moderators

The great thing about listening to stories from other moderators is that you can learn without needing to go through the experience yourself. It's not as powerful as experiencing it yourself, even in a practice setting, but learning vicariously gives you a framework for thinking about how you might deal with similar situations. While this book is helpful in this respect, we encourage you to reach out to others in your research community so you can learn from each other.

# 16.2 Integrating tips and feedback

Of course, becoming a better moderator isn't as simple as taking feedback and tips and just doing it. These techniques won't immediately make you a superstar moderator—like most skills, moderation takes lots of practice, over a long period of time.

Once you've sought out available resources, practiced, and received feedback on your moderation, take an incremental approach rather than trying to change everything at once. You have a lot to think about during research sessions, and you don't want 80% of your brain power going to generalizing your performance. Revisiting the metaphor of improvisational comedy, the best scenes come when the players are not "in their heads." In other words, if you're thinking too hard, you're blocking the passageways for the instincts formed by all of training, practice, and feedback you received up until that point. The same is true for moderating—don't get stuck on acting and reacting perfectly, or you'll freeze up.

Instead, plan the incremental changes that you can make. When you run your next session (even if it's just for practice), focus on one or two small things that you want to improve about your moderation. Make a note for yourself about it in your study plan so you have an additional reminder. Aside from those small things, free yourself to think on your feet and use your instincts to respond genuinely to the participant and acquire solid research findings. Focus on the thing across a few sessions or even an entire study. Then, for the next session, focus on another area you want to improve.

Each change is a baby step, but those steps are incredibly effective.

As an example of how to plan out changes, let's say you've received feedback from a colleague saying you tend to cut off the participant, answer questions too soon, and unconsciously tap your pen on your clipboard.

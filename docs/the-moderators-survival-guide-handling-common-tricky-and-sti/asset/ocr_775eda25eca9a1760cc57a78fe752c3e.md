# 6.4 Participant is not able to complete a necessary task (cont.)

+   - We also like to use a stealthier tactic in situations like this. For a detailed description, see the Chapter 3 sidebar “The Diversionary Assist.
- Another option is to set the product to the necessary state yourself while the participant’s not looking. For example, you can pass the screen-sharing with a remote participant, or have an in-person participant look away briefly so you can do whatever needs to be done. This avoids the complications of a direct assist, which can be awkward, especially if you need to ask the participant to look away. Gentle humor and a smile can make this less awkward, for example, “I know this sounds strange, but I need you to look away for a few seconds while I reset the prototype for your next task"

# What to say

For an initial vague assist:

+   - “What if I were to tell you that...”
- “I’ll just let you know there is a way from here to do that....

For a direct assist:

+   - “Let me draw your attention to this area. What are your thoughts on it being here?”
- “I’ll point out that you can find functionality. Talk to me about this versus your expectations.”
- “It’s good to see your thought process in doing that. I will just let you know there is actually a different way of doing that task. I am curious to know what you think of it” <point him to correct way>.

For a redirect as part of a diversion assist:

+   - “Let’s move on now. I wanted to ask you about what you did earlier.”
- “Why don’t we stop this here, because I wanted to ask you about...."
- “Let’s stop this here and we can come back to it. Read the next scenario....”

# What not to do or say

+   - Try avoid asking, “Did you notice this link?” when the participant obviously hadn’t. It can work in a pinch with nothing to say, but it often makes the participant feel a little uncomfortable.
- Avoid saying anything implying there is a correct answer. For example, “What you’re looking for is here” or “This is the right answer.”

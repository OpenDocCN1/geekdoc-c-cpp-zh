# 8.1 Participant looks for affirmation (cont.)

+   - In a usability study, avoid letting the participant know whether he is doing a task correctly
or not.

+   - Avoid using phrases like “You’ve completed this task” or “This task is complete
because it implies correct and incorrect ways of approaching tasks. Similarly, avoid leading
questions that might imply or suggest an expected answer. For example, “So you do this every day,
right?”

# How to avoid

+   - Keep your body language consistent throughout the session so participants, you might be extra
sensitive to being observed, don’t feel like you’re affected by your performance. For
ample, avoid leaning forward when the participant is taking an unexpected path during a
usability study if you’re being leaned back the rest of the session. Similarly, avoid
frowning or shaking your head if you disagree with feedback provided during an interview.

+   - Encourage the participant by periodically telling him that his feedback is helpful. If you take
this approach, be sure to not just provide this encouragement after the participant provides
ypes of feedback (for more on this technique, Chapter 3 sidebar “Should You Tell a
Participant That Her Feedback Is Helpful?”).

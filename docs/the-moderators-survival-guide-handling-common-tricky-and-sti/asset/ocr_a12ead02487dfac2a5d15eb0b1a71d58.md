# 13.1 Participant curses or makes inappropriate comments (cont.)

+   - In conjunction with these tips, think about your body language and moderating style. If you're relaxed and friendly in disposition, now is the time to adapt to a more formal, less responsive moderating style (as discussed in section 1.5) so that the participant knows you're serious.

+   - If asking the participant to stop doesn't help, or her language is still out of control, either take a break to assess the situation with colleagues, or just end the session early. Try to do so in a way that doesn't blame the participant (e.g., use a pretext and blame a technical failure or pretend that you're done with the tasks.

# What to say

If the participant is cursing and you feel comfortable using slight humor:

+   - "I just to let you know that this is a curse-free zone!"

+   - "If you don't mind, let's keep our words PG-13."

+   - "I like your spirit, I'll ask you not to curse in this setting if you can help."

If the participant is cursing, and you want to keep the tone relatively formal:

+   - "<Participant>, I appreciate your feedback so far, but since we're being recorded and there may be others listening in me, I need you to use clean language for the rest of the session. Thank you So, let's go back to..."

If the participant is making discriminatory remarks, and you need to redirect:

+   - "Okay, let's move onto..."

If the participant is making discriminatory remarks and you need to be more explicit:

+   - "I know that you're free to give any feedback, but I'll ask you that you do so in a way that is respectful to others. Thank you in advance. Now let's get back to..."

+   - "As I said earlier, we have some observers and the session is being recorded, I'll ask you to avoid <cursing, using profanity, making personal statements about 'kinds of people'> during the rest of the session."

If you need to end the session, use a pretext such as:

+   - "I'm sorry

# What not to do or say

+   - Don't laugh in response to offensive or controversial jokes, even if you want to feel like the participant is looking for you to respond in some way.

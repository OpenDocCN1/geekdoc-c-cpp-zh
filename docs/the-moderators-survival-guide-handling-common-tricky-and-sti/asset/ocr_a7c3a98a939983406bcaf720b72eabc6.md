# 12.2 Something personal, inappropriate, or confidential is visible (cont.)

+   Any time you remind the participant to close out of sensitive materials, try to do so in a way that isn't scolding or embarrassing. Take the responsibility for not being clear enough about these precautions earlier in the session and remind him that it's for his privacy.

+   Remind your observers about maintaining participant confidentiality. Ask them not to laugh or discuss what they've seen with other observers or anyone outside of the test.

# What to say

+   «Participant», I just want to remind you that your screen is currently being shared, please make sure you've closed anything you don't want to see. Can I bring your attention back to...?

+   Before we continue, would you mind closing the other applications and windows that are open on your computer (that aren't relevant to <task>research>?) Since you're sharing your screen, I need to be sensitive of your privacy and make sure you're only sharing what you intend to share with us. If you need a moment to close things that you don't want to see, you can click Stop Sharing, and Start Again when you're ready.

+   «Participant», I'm just going to stop the sharing for a second. Bear with me. I stopped the sharing because I noticed there was potentially personal information on your screen. I should have warned you before, sorry. We respect your privacy so I'll ask you to close or hide anything you wouldn't want a wider audience to see.

To refresh the research:

+   «I'm sorry to have you start sharing and then stop it abruptly. What we're going to do now is switch modes lightly. I'm going to start by asking you some questions.

To observers:

+   «I could this make for a good story, but we take participant confidentiality and comfort seriously. So I'll ask you not to make fun of the participant or discuss what you saw, either in or out of this room. Thanks for understanding.”

# What not to do or say

+   Don't blame or accuse the participant of anything based on what you saw. It's best to pretend as nothing happened.

+   Don't come across as condescending or judgmental based on what you see. For example, if the participant has computer wallpaper that you would consider not safe for work, ask him to hide anything he doesn't want everyone to see, or if he needs more direction, ask him to change his wallpaper to something less distracting. Use directives more than descriptions—for example, say “your wallpaper is inappropriate.”

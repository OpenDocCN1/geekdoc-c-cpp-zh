# 13.7 Participant makes request during a site visit

> You're conducting a study at a participant's house and asks you politely if you'd like a beverage, or if she can make you some food. Maybe the request is more personal (e.g., asking you to take off your shoes), or it's just plain unexpected (e.g., asking you to take out garbage).

Method(s): Any in-person method
Frequency: Occasional
Pattern(s) to apply: Take responsibility; Disengage from the participant

# What to do

When you visit a participant's home, keep in mind that you're a guest and need to be polite and accommodating. Establishing an amicable yet professional relationship will set the tone for a fruitful feedback session. However, it's a slippery slope from being polite to getting too involved or friendly with the participant. Doing so can bias the results of your study or get you into a sticky or dangerous situation.

+   - If the participant asks you for a beverage, feel free to accept as long as it's nonalcoholic, doesn't create a burden on your host, and doesn't take too much away from the session. Be gracious and thank her for the offer, and remember that the participant is meeting social norms for having a visitor.

+   - Whether you accept a beverage from the participant or bring your own bottle of water, be careful where you place it. Some people are picky about beverages on their computer desks, or on tables without coasters. Ask the participant where she'd prefer to place your beverage.

+   - If the participant asks you you want some kind of food, try to avoid having her out of the way unless she's really excited or about serving you and it seems offensive to turn her down. Thank her for the offer. However, be aware that if a participant is, cooking you a steak dinner, it sets a precedent for a more friendly relationship, and it may become harder to stay neutral and in control during the session.

+   - If the participant has particular customs or rules like taking off shoes, be considerate and follow her rules. As a rule of thumb, it's always a good idea to go to a home visit with socks and shoes rather than without socks. Make sure your socks are ready for public viewing with no holes or inappropriate illustrations.

+   - If the participant has a really specific request, be accommodating but try to avoid denying tasks for her. For example, out of respect you may take a few minutes of silence for her religious ritual such as praying, but you don't have to take out her, answer the phone, or fix her dishwasher in return for her feedback!

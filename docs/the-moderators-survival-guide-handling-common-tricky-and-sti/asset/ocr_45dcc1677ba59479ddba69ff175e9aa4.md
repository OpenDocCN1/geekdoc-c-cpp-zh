# "YES, AND ..."

The "Yes, and ..." principle is central to improvisation. Let’s imagine that you start a scene with “Happy birthday. I made you a *cake,* and my response is, “That is not a cake. That is a dinosaur and my birthday was last month." This response makes it harder to respond with something that builds the scene along smoothly. I’ve probably set you up for an awkward situation.

You say, “Happy birthday. I made you a *cake.* Now my response is, “Thank you so much! Mmm, the cake frosting is my favorite part.” Once again, the goal is to build upon the scene, however, instead of controlling it. Rather than saying, “No, it’s not bacon frosting! It’s buttercream,” you might say, “Yeah. It goes well with the wasabi filling. This is saying—”Yes, and ...!” You took weird and made it weirder—which makes an exciting and (potentially) hilarious scene.

We’re not saying if the participant starts a bizarre conversation, how would follow it if it goes (the topic relates to your goals). But if unexpected happens, roll with it. If a prototype breathes, troubleshoot-on-the-fly or find a way to get the participant’s feedback out. If an observer barges in, address and resume the session. If the participant talks about cake frosting, keep a poker face and bring the scene back on track (again, unless bacon frosting is in scope!). Flexibility and creativity to smoothly move the “scene” along.

# 1.4 The science and art spectrum

You’re conducting a usability study. The participant is attempting a task that asks her to book a flight on a travel website. She gets to a page where she is considering the fares and pays, “I don’t usually just book a flight. I typically explore the flight-plus-hotel combination deals and purchase one of them,”

Do you ask the participant to continue the original task of picking a flight? If you did, you’d be able to compare all participants’ data fairly. Or do you go off-script and follow the participant into whatever they would typically explore? You’d get some valuable information about other areas of the website that you might not get from other participants.

You’ve just stumbled into one user’s great debates: Is moderating a science or an art?

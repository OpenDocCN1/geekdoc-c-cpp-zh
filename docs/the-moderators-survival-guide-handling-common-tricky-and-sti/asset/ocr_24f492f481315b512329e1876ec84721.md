# SURVIVAL STORY: "SHE TIPPED ME ... BIG!"
MHAEL ROBBINS

It started as a routine usability study. Observing behind a huge one-way mirror my consultancy's director, my boss, a couple टाक्या/equipment operators, and the client. The participant and I were seated side-by-side in front of a PC in a cramped, bare-walled testing room. She was a cheerful woman in her fifties and I was a thirsty-something-year-old guy.

It well into the hour-long session before it dawned on me that she might be flirting. She was getting progressively more complimentary and effusive. She cleared up doubts when she began lightly touching my arm and her eyes shot some special twinkle. From behind the huge mirror I felt amused grins getting wider. The study wasn suffering so I kept moving around and beside, she was amusing me too! Everything was fine ... until she craned up after the study ended.

She started with waves of praise about how well I had done, how professional I was, etc.\ I thanked her for her kind words as I handed payment to her—an envelope of cash. Before I knew what happened, she stuffed $20 into my hand and showered me with a tsunami of praise. She tipped me ... big!

My first thought was "Oh no! What do I do now?" Then a mental flash of our unseen transfixing on this rapidly unfolding, live drama. I quickly interrupted her and handled the cash back, asking that she keeps it. She refused. I put the cash down on the table in front of her and rationalized that she earned it and belonged to her. She wasn't wanting it. I insisted. Back and forth it went. Changing tactics, I stuffed the $20 deep into her open purse, got close to her, and explained in an earnest and disarming whisper that, just as an architect is, I wouldn't feel right as a professional accepting her money and I wanted her to keep it ... really. And, for some extra oops, I also assured I was doing alright with money ... really. It worked! She left perhaps even more cheerfully than she arrived. Pewhew!

I appreciated the knowing smile my director was wearing as she stepped out of the observation room and the good-natured ribbing from my colleagues.

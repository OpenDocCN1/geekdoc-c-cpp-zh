# What to do

This pattern means that you take responsibility for anything that, from the participant’s perspective, is unexpected during the session. For example, if you need to skip tasks or end a session due to something about the participant’s behavior (e.g., she brings a baby with to the session or misrepresents a question), you can blame yourself for not having the setup to support her situation or for not choosing the best words. Putting the responsibility on you keeps the participant from feeling badly herself.

This pattern also means that if something in the session seems to be wrong, you should take, or absorb, responsibility for it. If the participant is complaining, don’t agree or disagree with her—just absorb and accept her feedback. If something goes wrong, don’t blame players, developers, recruiters, or the participant—even if something is objectively their fault. As far the participant is concerned, the buck stops with you.

Take responsibility is a pattern that can be used in almost any kind of session. It is a good principle to keep in mind as unexpected situations arise.

You also need to take responsibility for the participant when it comes to her physical comfort and safety, especially in life-threatening situations such as fires or earthquakes. Especially if she is at your facility, it’s your job to watch over, and direct her until she is safely on her way home.

Keep in mind that you may also need to use this pattern with observers, if your observers are in the same room as you and the participant. If the observers are distracting or causing problems, you must take responsibility and resolve the situation to ensure the comfort of the participant.

# 3.2 Clarify the task/question

By clarifying what you mean by a task/question, or asking the participant to reiterate understanding of what you asked her to do, you’ll reduce misunderstandings and improve the odds of getting the kind of feedback you need.

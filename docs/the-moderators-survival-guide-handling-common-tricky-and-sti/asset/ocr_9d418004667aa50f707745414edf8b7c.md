+   If the participant is a very important person (VIP) or customer, or a colleague hooked in your project team, you probably can't get away with the technical failure pretext. They may encourage you to proceed anyway so the participant feels like he has a chance to provide feedback that will be taken seriously, even if it isn't in line with your research goals. One way to get this feedback is to adjust your study plan to learn more about what the participant does or is interested in doing.

# What to say

+   Before we continue, I just want to verify a few things about your <experience/expertise/role>. According to the information provided by our recruiter..."

+   I'm sorry, <participant name>, but for the purpose of this research, we need to talk to people who <criteria>. Thank you much for your willingness to come in today, and here is your agreed-upon compensation.

Ending early:

+   "I'm sorry, it looks like the prototype I wanted to get your feedback on isn't working. I have a few questions for you, but after that, we'll end the session early. You'll still get your full compensation!"

# What not to do or say

+   Don't be accusatory of the participant's qualifications (or lack thereof). He may take it personally. When verifying the participant criteria, do so in a subtle and nonjudgmental way.

+   Don't assume that the participant lied about his qualifications. The screener could have been poorly worded, or your recruiter may have been more lenient than he should have been. After the session, go back and review the requirements with the recruiter.

+   Likewise, do not assume that the recruiter messed up. If you're able to look into what happened, you won't know the truth of the situation. When interacting with the participant, always take the responsibility for the misunderstanding yourself, even if you're pretty sure the fault lies with your recruiter.

# How to avoid

+   Ensure that the recruiter has detailed information about each of your desired criteria and how to evaluate whether or not the participant matches the criteria. Avoid using "give-away" questions

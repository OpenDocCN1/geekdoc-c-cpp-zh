+   - Consider whether intended questions stem from useful information for the research, or just being curious.

+   - Make participants feel heard. Since you may not have follow-up questions to reassure participants that you feel heard, use eye contact and a friendly tone to make them feel and valued.

+   - Avoid redundancy. Make sure you're not eliciting feedback on topics participants already addressed in the session, just because it's the script. If you don't want to cover those topics again, preface your questions with phrases such as "You may have already answered this, ..."

# By the Book

+   - Takes great lengths to create a plan that follows a prescribed order and purpose, and tries not to deviate from the plan. If you didn't create the plan but are in a session, you'll probably follow it closely.

+   - Tends to be obtuse of an inquisitive mind style, and is usually inherent to newer moderators.

+   - Participants are experts in a highly technical domain and you're not.

+   - Your study is using a quantitative method where order and timing are important.

+   - Your plan is complex and carefully thought out, with limited flexibility and lots of dependencies between tasks or questions.

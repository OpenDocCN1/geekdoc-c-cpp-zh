# Nat. Commun. 14, 1234 (2023)

# **Fine-Grained Scene Graph Generation with Data-Enhanced Knowledge Distillation**

Yuyu Guo, Lianli Gao, Xuanhan Wang, Yuxuan Zhai, Xing Xu, Heng Tao Shen

School of Computer Science and Engineering, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China

**Abstract.** Scene graph generation (SGG) aims to identify objects and their relationships in an image. Although recent SGG models have achieved significant progress, their performance is still limited by the long-tail problem in predicate classes. To alleviate this issue, we propose a novel data-enhanced knowledge distillation framework. Specifically, we first construct a knowledge base by extracting high-confidence relation triplets from the training data. Then, we design a knowledge-guided data enhancement strategy to generate additional training samples for rare predicate classes. Finally, we employ a teacher-student framework to transfer the knowledge from a complex teacher model to a lightweight student model. Extensive experiments on the Visual Genome dataset demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, achieving state-of-the-art performance.

**Keywords:** Scene Graph Generation, Knowledge Distillation, Data Enhancement, Long-Tail Problem

# ## 1 Introduction

Scene graph generation has attracted increasing attention in recent years due to its wide applications in image captioning, visual question answering, and image retrieval. A scene graph provides a structured representation of an image, where nodes correspond to object bounding boxes and edges represent the pairwise relationships between objects. Despite significant progress, existing SGG methods often suffer from the long-tail distribution of relationship classes, where a few common predicates dominate the training data while many other predicates appear only rarely.

To address this challenge, some works attempt to re-balance the data distribution via resampling or re-weighting strategies. However, these methods may introduce bias or lose important information. Another line of research focuses on utilizing external knowledge sources or incorporating linguistic priors, which can provide additional semantic information. Nevertheless, these methods often require extra annotations or external data, limiting their applicability.

In this paper, we propose a novel framework that combines data enhancement and knowledge distillation to address the long-tail problem in SGG. Our main contributions can be summarized as follows:

+   • We construct a high-confidence knowledge base from the training set and design a knowledge-guided data enhancement strategy to generate effective training samples for rare predicates.
+   • We propose a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework to transfer the enhanced knowledge to a compact student model, achieving a good balance between performance and efficiency.
+   • We conduct extensive experiments on the Visual Genome dataset, demonstrating that our method significantly outperforms existing SGG approaches, especially on rare predicate classes.

# ## 2 Related Work

**Scene Graph Generation.** Early SGG methods typically use a two-stage pipeline: object detection followed by relationship classification. Recent works have introduced more sophisticated architectures, such as graph neural networks and transformer-based models, to capture contextual information. However, these models are often biased towards frequent predicates due to the long-tail distribution.

**Knowledge Distillation.** Knowledge distillation (KD) is an effective model compression technique, which transfers knowledge from a large teacher model to a small student model. KD has been successfully applied in various tasks, including image classification, object detection, and scene graph generation. Different from existing KD-based SGG methods, we focus on leveraging the distilled knowledge to enhance the training data itself, rather than only aligning the outputs.

**Data Augmentation.** Data augmentation is a widely used technique to alleviate data scarcity. In the context of SGG, a few works have explored generating synthetic relationship samples. However, these methods often ignore the semantic correlation between objects and predicates. Our data enhancement strategy explicitly incorporates the knowledge base to generate more reasonable and diverse samples.

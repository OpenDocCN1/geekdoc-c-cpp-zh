### Evidence for the portfolio

Representatives from each discipline, nominated by their peers, were invited to a two-hour workshop to discuss and agree priorities for improvement over the next 12 months. A summary of the PCT's priorities, NSFs and the National Primary Care Collaborative (community needs, see Box 5), together with the outcome of the SWOT analysis (practice needs, see Box 6), was presented to the team. Emerging from this data was a long list of potential areas for improvement. Armed with this information and with the help of a facilitator, the team split into two smaller groups to choose three areas we considered to be a priority for the practice and its population.

- To improve and increase the use of IT (in particular data input for chronic disease management). Chosen because we were aware data were being input in a variety of ways for chronic disease management and each surgery's results were unreliable. We also had a problem storing increasing amounts of paper and had just had a system upgrade. Our aim would be for 100% of computerised patient records to be complete, accurate and up to date.
- To improve patient access. Chosen because we were already actively participating in the National Primary Care Collaborative and 'access' had been identified as a priority for improvement by the PCT. Our aim would be to offer patients a routine appointment with GP or nurse within 48 hours of request. (Urgent appointment requests were already met on the same day).
- To address the NSF for mental health. Chosen because we were experiencing an increasing number of patients with acute episodes of mental illness requiring medication and who did not come under the care of the community mental health team. These patients require frequent monitoring and support, and the counselling service we provided, with a waiting time of 15 weeks, was inadequate. Our aim would be to provide practice-based support for patients experiencing an acute episode of mental illness.

## Stage 3: Agreeing specific actions for improvement

### Introduction

This is the stage where the ideas for improvement are translated into action. The CQI approach (see Part Four) ensures that there is a clear aim and measures that will show if the action is having the hoped for effect. The first steps to bringing about improvement are planned, tested out and evaluated (see Boxes 8 and 9).

### PDSA 1

Plan: Reception staff will record and monitor the third* available appointment for each appointment nurse, in order to assess the practice's capability of doctor and nurse appointments.

The third available appointment is a measure of access used by the National Primary Care Collaborative.

Do: When a patient requests an appointment the receptionist will record on a form, the appointment date the patient required and the date of the next available appointment. The receptionist will analyse the data weekly and monitor the length of wait for an appointment for each doctor and nurse.

Study: Results showed that although the monitoring exercise created additional pressure on reception staff, at times it was possible to identify peaks in demand, e.g. Monday and Friday afternoons.

Act: To consult about the benefits of introducing one session of nurse triage on Monday mornings.

### PDSA 2

Plan: To carry out a survey to seek patient views and assess potential use of nurse triage and to distribute a small patient questionnaire to patients attending the surgery.

### Evidence for the portfolio

Project groups were set up across all clinical staff groups and included 'expert' nurses who were involved in the PDSA process. The aim of the project group was to improve access to appointments within 48 hours, and included a GP and nurse practitioner. They agreed that a change would be:

- The number of patients ringing for routine appointments
- The 'how to' appointment
- The time of routine appointments
- The skill mix of the nursing team

Each project group tries repeatedly to agree the project scope, what are the key tasks and what evidence they will gather. We found that there were usually several cycles of PDSA, e.g. see the box on the right.

## Stage 4: Agreeing learning and development needed to achieve improvements

### Introduction

This section is about linking learning with improving care:

- what skills do we need to develop to bring about an improvement in the areas we have identified as a priority
- what do we need to learn, as individuals and as a team, to enable us to deliver the planned improvements?

### 4 Personal development needs

This refers to individual learning needs that may be unique to an individual, or may be a shared need. The needs identified should relate to the personal needs and the needs of the practice, team and organisation. Examples of personal learning needs may be identified from appraisals or performance review, and include recognition and understanding of own needs, opportunities to learn new skills and knowledge, and other development needs.

### 11 What specific educational action can we take? How can we measure the effect of each action?

This refers to specific educational actions taken to meet the improvement needs identified. In addition to formally accredited opportunities, you may want to consider:

- Processes for facilitating learning and setting up the appropriate opportunities.
- Learning opportunities, including the use of different resources.
- Specific training sessions, for instance communication skills or computer skills.
- Peer support and discussion groups.
- Individual education to attain a specific skill or knowledge.
- Personal learning may come out of an individual reflection or a particular incident, event, or need for personal development.

You may also want to report any benefits you feel have resulted from the educational actions. Providing evidence of this is not easy, especially when the benefits relate to the practice culture. However, an appraisal may be worthwhile. This may be worthwhile to the rest of the team with implications for the practice.

### 10 Learning and development needs of practice and individuals

This will be a list of learning and development needs with respect to the individual. It may include the different professional opportunities available, opportunities for learning and development, and the needs of the organisation and practice.

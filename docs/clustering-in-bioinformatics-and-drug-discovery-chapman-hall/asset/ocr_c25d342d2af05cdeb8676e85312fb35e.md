# 1.2 Bioinformatics and Drug Discovery

A large component of drug discovery involves searching for, and subsequently modifying or designing, small molecules; namely, ligands that bind to protein targets. Small molecule ligands are most often identified through *High Throughput Screening* (HTS), virtual studies in computational chemistry, or databases containing prior ligand activity data for a given target. Searching for drugs, or *lead discovery*, and modifying and designing drugs has traditionally been the domain of medicinal chemistry and computational chemists. The analysis and storage of this data has come to be known as cheminformatics. Targets are typically identified by biologists, geneticists, and bioinformaticians, often via systems biology. However, within drug discovery the study of drug design incorporates the study of the ligand-target interaction. Thus, there are certain areas of intersection between bioinformatics, cheminformatics, and even medicinal informatics within the domain of drug discovery.

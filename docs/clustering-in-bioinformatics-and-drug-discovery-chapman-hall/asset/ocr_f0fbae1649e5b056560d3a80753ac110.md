A nagging doubt remains however: are the groups that we find quantitatively meaningful or useful? A data set may have faint structure that may be simply indicative of a purely random process, and this is an important point throughout the study of cluster analysis, namely, one of validity. Is there strong reason to believe that the data show meaningful structure? and at what scale (e.g., local galaxies or the entire universe)? Can we make conjectures and develop hypotheses about possible classes within the domain of study? Or, are we simply grouping randomly produced structure? Take for example, the scatter of rain drops as they first appear on a flag stone or square slab of concrete in the open air: if we assume that the pattern as it develops is random, we nevertheless will see regions where the density of drops is slightly greater, and other regions where it is slightly less dense overall.

A formal proof of this theorem can also be an interesting extension to the formalization. Finally, formalizing the PDF allows writing the statistical properties of random variables, like the expectation, in terms of the density function and hence can be useful in the evaluation of continuous systems.

Some of the interesting future directions for the DTMC formalization include the formalization of continuous time Markov chains (CTMC), the formalization of Queuing theory foundations and Markov decision procedures. These extensions would further expand the scope of using theorem proving for analyzing the probabilistic aspects of systems.

The evaluation of the performance of protocols, like Crowds or Tor, can be an interesting application of the information theory related formalization reported in this book. The measures of information leakage, given in Chapter 8, can be used to quantify the leakage of information for different configuration of these networks.

Building upon the Reliability Block Diagram (RBD) formalization, presented in this book, other commonly used RBDs, including series-parallel and K-out-of-N, can be formalized. Moreover, the formalization of RBDs can lead to analyzing many safety-critical applications, including cloud computing, automotive systems and medical devices. Likewise our formalization of the WSN properties of coverage and detection leads to many interesting formal analysis case studies, such as underwater monitoring and Body Area Sensor Networks (BASNs).

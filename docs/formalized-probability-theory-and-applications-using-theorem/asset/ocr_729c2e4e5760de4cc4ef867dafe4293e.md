The work is presented using the HOL4 theorem prover and its existing theories of Boolean algebra, sets, lists, positive integers and real numbers have been extensively utilized. The formalization, presented in this book, can therefore be regarded as a useful indicator of the state-of-the-art in theorem proving. The presented work clearly shows that formalizing mathematics in a mechanical system is a tedious work that requires deep understanding of both mathematical concepts and theorem-proving. It was often the case that lemmas that are commonly known to be true are found to be quite difficult to verify using theorem proving as all the required reasoning of truth should be explicitly provided to the verification. The automated reasoners aid somewhat in the proof process by automatically verifying some of the first-order-logic goals but most of the times the user needs to guide the tool by providing the appropriate rewriting and simplification rules. On the other hand, theorem-proving was found to be very efficient in book keeping. For example, it is very common to get confused with different variables and mathematical notations and make human errors when working with large paper-and-pencil proofs, which leads to the loss of a lot of effort. In the case of mechanical theorem provers such problems do not exist. Another major advantage of theorem proving is that once the proof of a theorem is established, it is guaranteed to be valid and the proof can be readily accessed, contrary to the case of paper-and-pencil proofs where we have to explore the enormous amount of mathematical literature to find proofs. Thus, it can be concluded that theorem-proving is a tedious but promising field, which can help mathematicians to cope with the explosion in mathematical knowledge and to save mathematical concepts from corruption. Also, there are areas, such as security critical software, military, space travel and medicine, where using formal methods will become a dire need.

Thanks to the sound probabilistic foundations available in the HOL theorem prover, the resulting analysis is certified to be absolutely accurate. We have been hence able to provide a rigorous formalization of the k-set randomized scheduling and its coverage performance through an appropriate modelling of its randomness. Indeed, previous simulation works are mainly based on pseudo-random modelling. In addition, compared to probabilistic model checkers, a major novelty provided in this chapter, is the possibility to achieve formal and precise analysis of the statistical properties of the problem. It has been thus possible to formally verify the network coverage intensity as a statistical measure of the coverage intensity for a specific point. Moreover, the generic nature of the theorem proving technique and the high expressibility of higher-order logic allows us to set an approach which is much more flexible. Finally, because missing a critical assumption can lead to verification failure within the theorem prover, the current approach is distinguishable by its completeness regarding the minimum set of assumptions.

On the other hand, the formal performance analysis of the coverage behavior of the forest fire application clearly shows the usefulness of our theoretical developments. It has been thus possible to formally provide a generic asymptotic analysis for all possible values of the design parameters, and in the specific case of the considered forest fire application. Furthermore, such verification enables reliable asymptotic reasoning of the deployed WSN. At the end, it is also important to note that the presented application is a simple case study illustrating the practical interest of our work, but the claimed generic results can be obviously valuable for any other WSN application as well (McIver & Fehnker, 2006).

The above mentioned additional benefits, associated with the theorem proving approach, are attained at the cost of the time and effort spent, while formalizing the randomized scheduling algorithm and formally reasoning about its coverage properties, by the user. We believe that the main challenges incurred in our work can be divided into two main categories. Firstly, although the higher-order-logic modelling seems to depend on simple discrete random variables, the major difficulty was to map a probabilistic model of a real WSN algorithm, which is far from a pure mathematical problem, into higher-order logic. Effectively, since the mathematical modelling of real-world systems is commonly very intuitive, the initial theoretical model, which we used in this work, hence included many hidden steps with few attached explanations either when considering the random variables or when applying the probability rules. In addition, the assumptions of the original model are never presented exhaustively. We have thus to reason correctly about all missing steps so that we can first understand the flow of the theoretical analysis, and achieve then the higher-order-logic formalizations of the coverage attributes. At this stage, a good background on probability and a detailed knowledge of the WSN context, are usually required for a deep understanding of the probabilistic reasoning.

## 13.4 CONCLUSION

This chapter presents a reliable approach for the formal analysis of the coverage performances of wireless sensor networks using the k-set randomized scheduling to save energy. In particular, we formalized the main foundations of the randomized scheduling and expected values of the coverage intensity using the measure theoretic formalization of probability theory described in Chapters 4 and 5. This formalization allowed us to formally verify the coverage related characteristics of most WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling. In order to illustrate the practical effectiveness of our foundational results, we utilize them to perform the formal probabilistic analysis of a WSN application for forest fire detection. Such application is considered as one of the most safety-critical applications of WSN. The achieved results are absolutely generic, i.e., valid for all parameter values; a result which cannot be attained in simulation or probabilistic model checking based approach. Moreover, it has been possible to provide precise formal reasoning on the statistical coverage using expectation. Finally, unlike most of the existing work that focuses on the validation of the functional aspects of WSN algorithms, our work is distinguishable by addressing the performance aspects.

This work lays also an interesting foundation for our future work on the higher-order logic formalizations of the detection properties of WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling. In the next chapter, we formally check the relationship between coverage and detection showing that coverage reflects detection. On the other hand, once the formal reasoning support of the detection aspect is developed in the HOL theorem prover, the performance of other interesting applications, such as underwater monitoring, can also be formally analyzed.

# 13.4 CONCLUSION

This chapter presents a reliable approach for the formal analysis of the coverage performances of wireless sensor networks using the k-set randomized scheduling to save energy. In particular, we formalized the main foundations of the randomized scheduling and expected values of the coverage intensity using the measure theoretic formalization of probability theory described in Chapters 4 and 5. This formalization allowed us to formally verify the coverage related characteristics of most WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling. In order to illustrate the practical effectiveness of our foundational results, we utilize them to perform the formal probabilistic analysis of a WSN application for forest fire detection. Such application is considered as one of the most safety-critical applications of WSN. The achieved results are absolutely generic, i.e., valid for all parameter values; a result which cannot be attained in simulation or probabilistic model checking based approach. Moreover, it has been possible to provide precise formal reasoning on the statistical coverage using expectation. Finally, unlike most of the existing work that focuses on the validation of the functional aspects of WSN algorithms, our work is distinguishable by addressing the performance aspects.

This work lays also an interesting foundation for our future work on the higher-order- logic formalizations of the detection properties of WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling. In the next chapter, we formally check the relationship between coverage and detection showing that coverage reflects detection. On the other hand, once the formal reasoning support of the detection aspect is developed in the HOL theorem prover, the performance of other interesting applications, such as underwater monitoring, can also be formally analyzed.

# # Conclusion

This book presented an alternative approach for conducting probabilistic analysis. The idea is to use higher-order-logic theorem proving as a complementary approach to state-of-the-art simulation and probabilistic model checking based techniques. For this purpose, the book presented a formal framework of all the tools needed to describe the system under consideration and its desired properties in a formal language that can be used within the theorem prover to verify that the system implementation satisfies its specification. Because of the formal nature of the models, the analysis will be free of approximation and precision errors and due to the high expressive nature of higher-order logic a wider range of systems can be analyzed. Thus, this kind of an analysis approach can prove to very useful for the performance and reliability optimization of safety critical and highly sensitive engineering and scientific applications.

# ## 1. SUMMARY

The book provided a higher-order-logic formalization of measure theory that is based on the extended-real numbers and includes the formalization of the Borel sigma algebra defined on the open sets. This allowed us to define measurable functions defined over arbitrary topological spaces. The book also presented the formalized product measure spaces that are required to formalize the operators involving multiple measurable functions.

The formalization of measure theory is then used to define the Lebesgue integral and prove its properties. This formalization is also based on the extended-real numbers allowing us to work with non-integrable functions and to prove the Lebesgue monotone convergence theorem without assuming the integrability criterion. This result is later utilized in the proof of the important Radon Nikodym theorem, which is a theorem that would not have been possible to verify without the use of extended-real numbers.

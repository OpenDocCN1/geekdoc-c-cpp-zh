The work is presented using the HOL4 theorem prover and its existing theories of Boolean algebra, sets, lists, positive integers and real numbers have been extensively utilized. The formalization, presented in this book, can therefore be regarded as a useful indicator of the state-of-the-art in theorem proving. The presented work clearly shows that formalizing mathematics in a mechanical system is a tedious work that requires deep understanding of both mathematical concepts and theorem-proving. It was often the case that lemmas that are commonly known to be true are found to be quite difficult to verify using theorem proving as all the required reasoning of truth should be explicitly provided to the verification. The automated reasoners aid somewhat in the proof process by automatically verifying some of the first-order-logic goals but most of the times the user needs to guide the tool by providing the appropriate rewriting and simplification rules. On the other hand, theorem-proving was found to be very efficient in book keeping. For example, it is very common to get confused with different variables and mathematical notations and make human errors when working with large paper-and-pencil proofs, which leads to the loss of a lot of effort. In the case of mechanical theorem provers such problems do not exist. Another major advantage of theorem proving is that once the proof of a theorem is established, it is guaranteed to be valid and the proof can be readily accessed, contrary to the case of paper-and-pencil proofs where we have to explore the enormous amount of mathematical literature to find proofs. Thus, it can be concluded that theorem-proving is a tedious but promising field, which can help mathematicians to cope with the explosion in mathematical knowledge and to save mathematical concepts from corruption. Also, there are areas, such as security-critical software, military, space travel and medicine, where using formal methods will become a dire need.

## 2. FUTURE DIRECTIONS

The formalization and verification results, presented in this book, open new avenues in using theorem proving for the precise analysis of systems that exhibit randomness. Building on these results, more features can be added to strengthen the capabilities of the theorem proving based probabilistic analysis framework. Some of the future extensions are outlined below:

The formalization of probability theory can be extended by defining the Probability Density Function (PDF). The PDF is defined as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the cumulative distribution function with respect to the probability measure. Formalizing the PDF allows to analyze the class of systems that can be described by standard continuous distributions such as the normal distribution. The latter can be used to model a wide class of systems due to the central limit theorem. A formal proof of this theorem can also be an interesting extension to the formalization. Finally, formalizing the PDF allows writing the statistical properties of random variables, like the expectation, in terms of the density function and hence can be useful in the evaluation of continuous systems.

Some of the interesting future directions for the DTMC formalization include the formalization of continuous-time Markov chains (CTMC), the formalization of Queuing theory foundations and Markov decision procedures. These extensions would further expand the scope of using theorem proving for analyzing the probabilistic aspects of systems.

The evaluation of the performance of protocols, like Crowds or Tor, can be an interesting application of the information theory related formalization reported in this book. The measures of information leakage, given in Chapter 8, can be used to quantify the leakage of information for different configuration of these networks.

Building upon the Reliability Block Diagram (RBD) formalization, presented in this book, other commonly used RBDs, including series-parallel and K-out-of-N, can be formalized. Moreover, the formalization of RBDs can lead to analyzing many safety-critical applications, including cloud computing, automotive systems and medical devices. Likewise our formalization of the WSN properties of coverage and detection leads to many interesting formal analysis case studies, such as underwater monitoring and Body Area Sensor Networks (BASNs).

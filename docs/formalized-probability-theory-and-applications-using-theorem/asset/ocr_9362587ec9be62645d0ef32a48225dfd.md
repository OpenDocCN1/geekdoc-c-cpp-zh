\int_X f d\mu = \int_X f^+ d\mu - \int_X f^- d\mu (4.6)

# Definition 4.27

\vdash \forall m f. integral m f = pos_fn_integral m (fn_plus f) - pos_fn_integral m (fn_minus f)

The Lebesgue monotone convergence is arguably the most important theorem of the Lebesgue integration theory and it plays a major role in the proof of the Radon Nikodym theorem and the properties of the integral. Let $(f_j)$ be a monotonically increasing sequence of non-negative measurable functions such that $\forall x. f(x) = sup_n f_n(x)$, then

\int_X f d\mu = sup_n \int_X f_n d\mu (4.7)

The higher-order-logic formalization of the Lebesgue monotone convergence is the following:

# Theorem 4.10

\vdash \forall m f fi. measure_space m \land i x. 0 \le f i x \land \forall i. fi i \in measurable (m_space m, measurable_sets m) borel \land \forall x. mono_increasing (\lambda i. fi i x) \land \forall x. x \in m_space m \Rightarrow f x = sup (image (\lambda i. fi i x) UNIV) \Rightarrow pos_fn_integral m f = sup (image (\lambda i. pos_fn_integral m (fi i)) UNIV)

The definition of the Lebesgue integral, based on the extended-real numbers, ensures that the integral is always defined for non-negative functions even when the integral is infinite. Let $(X.A, \mu)$ be a measure space then a measurable function f is integrable iff $\int_X |f| + d\mu < \infty$ or equivalently iff $\int_X f^+ d\mu < \infty$ and $\int_X f^- d\mu < \infty$.


## 14.4 CONCLUSION

This chapter presented an approach for the formal analysis of the detection performance of wireless sensor networks using the k-set randomized scheduling to preserve energy. In particular, we formalized the notions of intrusion period, detection probability and delay using the measure theoretic formalization of probability theory in the HOL4 theorem prover. This formalization allowed us to formally verify the detection related characteristics of most WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling. In order to illustrate the practical effectiveness of our foundational results, we utilized them to perform the formal probabilistic analysis of a WSN application for border surveillance. The obtained results are exhaustive and completely generic, i.e., valid for all parameter values; a result which cannot be attained with simulation or probabilistic model checking based approach. Moreover, unlike most of the existing work that focuses on the validation of the functional aspects of WSN algorithms, our work is distinguishable by addressing the performance aspects. Finally, the proposed approach described in this chapter can be generalized to tackle the formal analysis of the same randomized scheduling under other assumptions, or even other probabilistic problems in the WSN context. Indeed, the presented formalizations can be valuable for formally verifying the same algorithm with, for example, a modified shape of the intrusion object. In addition, the higher-order-logic formalizations of some common random variables such as Bernoulli or Binomial can be very useful for the formal analysis of any probabilistic analysis problem.

This work lays also an interesting foundation for our future work on the higher-order-logic formalization of the lifetime properties of WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling. Similarly, once the formal reasoning support of the lifetime aspect is developed in the HOL4 theorem prover, the performance of other interesting WSN applications, such as underwater monitoring, can also be formally analyzed.

## KEY TERMS AND DEFINITIONS

**Average Detection Delay**: The average detection delay of a WSN is the expectation of the time elapsed from the occurrence of an intrusion event to the time when this event is detected by some sensor nodes.

**Detection Probability of a WSN**: Detection probability is the probability of detecting an occurring event within the monitored area of the given WSN.

**Detection**: Detection or intrusion detection is the process of detecting inappropriate, incorrect or suspicious intruders in the WSN. One of the foremost requirements of a WSN is to be able to detect all interesting events.

## Conclusion

This book presented an alternative approach for conducting probabilistic analysis. The idea is to use higher-order-logic theorem proving as a complementary approach to state-of-the-art simulation and probabilistic model checking based techniques. For this purpose, the book presented a formal framework of all the tools needed to describe the system under consideration and its desired properties in a formal language that can be used within the theorem prover to verify that the system implementation satisfies its specification. Because of the formal nature of the models, the analysis will be free of approximation and precision errors and due to the high expressive nature of higher-order logic a wider range of systems can be analyzed. Thus, this kind of an analysis approach can prove to be very useful for the performance and reliability optimization of safety critical and highly sensitive engineering and scientific applications.

### 1. SUMMARY

The book provided a higher-order-logic formalization of measure theory that is based on the extended-real numbers and includes the formalization of the Borel sigma algebra defined on the open sets. This allowed us to define measurable functions defined over arbitrary topological spaces. The book also presented the formalized product measure spaces that are required to formalize the operators involving multiple measurable functions.

The formalization of measure theory is then used to define the Lebesgue integral and prove its properties. This formalization is also based on the extended-real numbers allowing us to work with non-integrable functions and to prove the Lebesgue monotone convergence theorem without assuming the integrability criterion. This result is later utilized in the proof of the important Radon-Nikodym theorem, which is a theorem that would not have been possible to verify without the use of extended-real numbers.

The above mentioned theories are then used to develop a higher-order-logic formalization of the main concepts of probability, like the probability spaces and random variables, as well as their statistical properties, like the expectation and variance. Some classical results of probability theory, like the Shannon and Markov inequalities and the Weak Law of Large Numbers, have also been verified in a theorem prover.

The book also provided a higher-order-logic formalization of Discrete-Time Markov Chains (DTMC) and verification of most of their important properties. These formalizations provide the capability formally evaluate the performance, maintainability and reliability of a wide variety of systems which are described as DTMCs. For illustration purposes, the book provides the analysis of the binary communication channel and the tag-collection process in an Automatic Mail Quality Measurement (AMQM) protocol. The formalization of the DTMC theory is further used to formally define classified states and classified DTMCs and the formal proofs of their significant properties. As an example, we used the formalization of the aperiodic and irreducible DTMCs to validate the LRU stack model in higher-order logic. These applications highlight the benefits of the formalization of DTMCs and the formal verification of their properties using a higher-order-logic theorem prover.

Next, the main measures of information are also formalized starting with the Radon-Nikodym derivative, which is used to define the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence as a unified measure of information. The KL divergence is then used to define the most commonly used measures of information, like the Shannon entropy, the mutual information and the conditional mutual information.

The book also described the formalization of various measures of reliability and how reliability-engineering problems can be modeled and analyzed in the sound core of a theorem prover. The reliability analysis notions of the cumulative distribution function and the survival function are presented. Various useful properties of these measures are also verified. These theorems facilitate reasoning when constructing formal reliability proofs. Some examples for this work has also been presented in this book.

Finally, the book presents a reliable approach for the formal analysis of the coverage and detection performance of wireless sensor networks using the k-set randomized scheduling to save energy. In particular, the main foundations of the randomized scheduling and expected values of the coverage intensity and intrusion period, detection probability and delay properties are presented. This formalization allows to formally verify the coverage and detection related characteristics of most WSNs using the k-set randomized scheduling.
